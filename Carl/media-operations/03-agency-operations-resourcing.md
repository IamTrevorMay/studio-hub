# Creative Agency Operations: Traffic, Resourcing & Utilization

## TL;DR

- **The whole agency business is one equation:** People's time (capacity) × how much of it hits client work (utilization) × what each hour actually earns (Average Billable Rate) = Agency Gross Income. Delivery margin, overhead, and profit fall out of that chain. If you can't see all four numbers, you're flying blind.
- **Target utilization by role, not blanket:** producers (designers/editors/devs) 75–85% weekly, PMs 60–70%, account/strategy 50–60%, leadership 30–50%. Whole-org **annual** utilization lands much lower — 50–65% is healthy — because PTO, admin, sales, and culture time are real. Above ~85% sustained = burnout and quality decay; below ~60% for producers = overstaffed or under-sold.
- **Utilization without realization is vanity.** Realization (what you actually invoice/collect vs. hours worked at standard rates) should be 85–95%. High utilization + low realization = the team is busy giving work away (scope creep, write-offs, underpricing).
- **Use the 55/25/20 rule on Agency Gross Income:** ~55% to delivery labor, ~25% to overhead, ~20% net profit. Deviations tell you exactly where the problem is. Healthy delivery margin: 50–60% at the P&L level, 70%+ per project.
- **Overservicing is the silent killer:** ~38% of agencies chronically overservice; 10% overservicing = one month of free work per year, 30% = a whole free quarter. Fix it with time tracking, scoping accuracy reviews (target ±10–20%), and a real change-order process.
- **Retainers beat projects on almost every operational metric** (18% vs 42% annual churn, 56- vs 24-month client lifespan, +8pp net margin), but only with hard scope boundaries and monthly hours-vs-fee reconciliation. Hybrid (retainer base + scoped projects + performance kicker) is where sophisticated shops are landing.
- **AI is compressing delivery hours 3–4× on repeatable work (2024–2026),** breaking hourly billing. The winning move is to keep pricing on value/outcomes/productized fees and let utilization math track *cost*, not *price*.

## 1. The agency operating model in one page

Every services business — ad agency, video production shop, post house, design studio — monetizes the same asset: **people's hours**. The operational chain:

```
Headcount × working hours        → Gross Capacity
Gross Capacity × Utilization %   → Delivery Hours (time on client work)
Delivery Hours × ABR             → Agency Gross Income (AGI)
AGI − Delivery Costs             → Delivery Profit (gross margin)
Delivery Profit − Overhead       → Operating Profit
```

Five levers, in order of typical leverage:

1. **Price / ABR** (what each delivered hour earns) — highest leverage, least used.
2. **Scope discipline / realization** (stop giving hours away for free).
3. **Utilization** (more of the hours you already pay for hit client work).
4. **Delivery cost mix** (seniority-to-task matching, freelancers for peaks).
5. **Overhead control** — lowest leverage; most agencies over-focus here.

Key insight from Parakeeto (the leading agency-profitability consultancy): **utilization and ABR must be read together.** Driving utilization up while ABR falls (discounted overflow work, overservicing) makes the agency *busier and poorer*. The pair is the health check; either alone misleads.

Relevance note: this model applies directly to any team selling creative labor — including a creator-led media company selling sponsor integrations and branded content (each sponsored deliverable has an implied ABR), and a training facility selling coach hours (a lesson calendar is a utilization problem: coach hours × booked % × rate).

## 2. Core vocabulary and formulas

Get these definitions exact — most agency financial confusion is definitional.

### Agency Gross Income (AGI)
`AGI = Gross Revenue − Pass-Through Expenses`
Pass-throughs: media/ad spend, white-labeled services, project-specific freelancers billed onward, project-specific software, stock/licensing. AGI is the *real* top line — every ratio below should be computed against AGI, not gross revenue. An agency "billing $3M" that passes $1.8M to ad platforms is a $1.2M business.

### Delivery Margin (gross margin)
`Delivery Profit = AGI − Delivery Costs` ; `Delivery Margin % = Delivery Profit / AGI`
Delivery costs = payroll for client-facing work, delivery freelancers/contractors, shared delivery tooling.
**Benchmarks:** 50–60%+ at the P&L level; 70%+ on individual projects (project view excludes shared costs). Below 50% at the P&L = underpricing, overservicing, or bad estimation. Sobering datapoint: UK £1M+ agencies hit a *record-low 39% median* delivery margin in 2024 — only 24% of them reached 50%.

### Utilization Rate
`Utilization = Delivery Hours / Gross Capacity`
- **Delivery hours** = hours on client work regardless of billing model (count retainer and fixed-fee work — this is not "hours invoiced").
- **Gross capacity** = contracted hours (40/wk × 52 = 2,080/yr per FTE; some use 220 working days × 8 = 1,760 "available hours" — pick one convention and never mix them, the two conventions differ by ~15 points).
- Distinguish **weekly utilization** (production management number, excludes PTO weeks) from **annual utilization** (true economic number, includes PTO/holidays/training). Annual is always dramatically lower. See §3.

### Realization Rate
`Realization = Revenue Actually Billed / Potential Revenue at Standard Rates`
Measures leakage between hours worked and money collected: discounts, write-offs, unbilled scope creep. **Benchmark: 85–95%. Below 80% = systemic pricing or scope problem.**

### Average Billable Rate (ABR)
`ABR = AGI / Delivery Hours`
The single best profitability proxy because it works across all pricing models — a fixed-fee project that came in fast has a high ABR; the retainer client eating extra hours has a collapsing ABR. Set the target from cost: if fully-loaded average cost per delivery hour (ACPH) is $45 and you want a 70% delivery margin, target ABR = $45 / (1 − 0.70) = **$150/hr**. Rule of thumb: ABR should be ~3× ACPH.

### Scoping Accuracy
`Estimated Hours / Actual Hours` per project. Target within **±10%** consistently; ±20% is the outer bound of healthy. Track per project type and per estimator — this is the input that makes every forecast trustworthy.

### Client Dilution
Productivity loss from one person spread across too many simultaneous clients/projects (context-switching tax). Symptoms: utilization looks fine, throughput and quality drop. Fix with client/task batching and fewer concurrent assignments per person.

## 3. Utilization: benchmarks, targets, and traps

### Role-based weekly targets (converging view across Parakeeto, Bennett Financials, Forge, Mosaic)

| Role | Weekly billable/delivery target |
|---|---|
| Producers (designers, editors, developers, writers) | 75–85% (some shops run 80–90%) |
| Project managers | 60–70% |
| Account managers / strategists | 50–60% (relationship + planning time is structural) |
| Creative directors | 50–65% |
| Leadership / principals | 30–50% |
| Biz dev / ops | 0–50%, often fully non-billable |

**Whole-agency annual utilization: 50–65% is healthy; 55–60% is a good target** (Parakeeto). Don't panic when the annual number looks "low" next to weekly targets — PTO, holidays, sales, admin, and internal work are in the denominator.

### Guardrails
- **Below 60% (producers, weekly):** overstaffing, weak pipeline, or too much non-billable process. Diagnose before cutting — sometimes it's a traffic problem (work exists but isn't flowing).
- **Above 85% sustained:** burnout, no slack for revisions/rush work, quality decline, zero development time. Agencies at 70–75% report *higher client satisfaction* than those pushing 85%+. Marketing-industry burnout runs near 70% — utilization policy is a retention policy.
- **Consistent 80%+ with stable billing rates = understaffed** — that's the hiring trigger, not a victory lap.
- Healthy non-billable allocation for client-facing roles: **20–25%** (training, internal, admin) — budget it explicitly rather than pretending it doesn't exist.

### Industry trend (SPI Research Professional Services Maturity Benchmark, ~400+ firms)
- 2014–2023: hovering near the historic ~75% benchmark for billable consultants.
- **2024: 68.9% — lowest since 2019. 2025: 66.4% — new record low**, 3.6 points under SPI's 70% "minimum healthy" line. The whole industry is carrying slack (demand softness + AI compression + retained headcount). Healthy target range for billable consultants remains 74–84%, optimum ~75%.
- Only **18% of agencies always hit their internal utilization targets**; only ~1 in 4 hit industry-standard benchmarks. Every point of utilization recovered is worth roughly €2,500–4,000 per consultant per year at typical rates.

### The utilization × realization 2×2 (fastest diagnostic Carl can run)

| | High realization | Low realization |
|---|---|---|
| **High utilization** | Healthy — protect it | **Busy but broke**: scope creep, write-offs, underpricing. Fix scope + price, not effort. |
| **Low utilization** | Well-priced, under-sold: pipeline/sales problem | Existential: fix pricing AND demand |

If both look strong and profit is still weak → the problem is overhead or cost structure, not delivery (check the 55/25/20 split, §7).

### Measurement notes
- Manual time entry captures only ~67% of billable work vs 91%+ with automated capture — under-tracked time silently deflates utilization AND realization and distorts every downstream metric. If timesheets are hated, shorten the categories (client / internal / sales / admin is enough to run the whole model).
- If time tracking truly doesn't exist, Parakeeto's proxy is **billable efficiency** = estimated billable hours sold ÷ gross capacity — it measures compensation received, not work done, so overservicing hides inside it. Treat as a stopgap only.

## 4. Traffic management: the flow function

**Traffic management = managing the flow of work through the agency** — intake → scheduling → production → review → delivery. Distinct from project management: the PM owns one project end-to-end (scope, budget, client, quality); the **traffic manager owns the whole board** — cross-project resource allocation, workload balance, and throughput. Small shops merge the roles; the *function* must exist either way.

### What the traffic function does
- Gatekeeps intake: every new request gets a capacity check and a realistic date *before* anyone promises the client.
- Assigns work to balance load and match skill/seniority to task (senior people doing junior work torches delivery margin).
- Maintains the single source of truth for who's doing what, when — visibility is the product.
- Spots bottlenecks and deadline risk early; reshuffles before the fire, not during.
- Feeds leadership the forward view: are we over/under capacity in 2, 4, 8 weeks? Hire, contract, or push dates?
- In ad/media contexts, may also handle placement logistics (broadcast traffic).

Drew McLellan (Agency Management Institute): agencies need more than creatives and managers — "bring back the production manager to do what technology alone cannot." Tools give visibility; humans make the prioritization and conflict calls.

### When the function must become a person
Multiple concurrent clients + recurring deadline misses + resource conflicts + nobody can answer "can we take this on?" within an hour. Typically justified somewhere in the 10–25 headcount range; before that, it's a hat the ops-minded founder or senior PM wears with a real weekly ritual behind it.

### Implementing traffic management (7-step sequence, adapted from Function Point / practitioner consensus)
1. **Inventory all work in flight** — every project, task, owner, due date, in one system. No shadow work.
2. **Standardize intake** — one request form/channel; requests without brief, deadline, and budget owner bounce back.
3. **Define capacity per person** — weekly available hours net of PTO and standing non-billable commitments.
4. **Schedule against capacity, not hope** — book people to tasks with hours, not just names on a list.
5. **Institute the weekly resourcing meeting** (see §8 rituals) — 30–45 min, traffic + PMs + leads: next 2 weeks locked, next 4–8 sketched.
6. **Add a daily 10-minute flow check** — blockers, today's must-ships, any reshuffles.
7. **Close the loop with actuals** — time tracked vs scheduled feeds scoping accuracy and next week's plan.

## 5. Capacity planning and forecasting

Capacity planning answers one question ahead of time: **do we have the right people and hours for the work that's coming?**

### The three capacity numbers (Parakeeto)
- **Gross Capacity** = Σ (each person's weekly hours × weeks in period).
- **Delivery Capacity** = (Gross Capacity − culture/internal hours) × utilization target. This is what you can actually sell.
- **Revenue (AGI) Capacity** = Capacity × Utilization Target × ABR. This is the revenue ceiling of the current team — the most clarifying number an agency owner can compute. If the sales target exceeds AGI capacity, the plan requires hiring or price increases, full stop.

### Demand forecasting (what to load against capacity)
Forecast in three layers, **by role/discipline separately** (design, edit, dev, strategy, PM — a team can be simultaneously over-capacity in editing and idle in design):
1. **Committed** — signed projects + retainers + recurring delivery, at scoped hours.
2. **Weighted pipeline** — open deals × stage probability × likely scope and start date.
3. **Historical baseline** — average unplanned/reactive work (it always comes; budget for it).

Compare demand vs delivery capacity per role per week on a rolling 8–12 week horizon. Gaps >±15% for 3+ consecutive weeks trigger action:
- **Over capacity (demand > supply):** push dates at intake, bring in freelancers (pre-vetted bench — see below), then hire if the overload is structural (the 80%+ sustained-utilization signal).
- **Under capacity:** pull pipeline forward, invest bench time in training/process/IP/marketing (planned non-billable beats idle), and only then consider cost action.

### The flexible-capacity layer
Mature shops run a **core + flex** staffing model: full-time staff sized to the *trough* or steady-state of demand (protecting utilization), with a pre-vetted freelancer bench absorbing peaks. Freelancer cost on a project is a pass-through or delivery cost — either way it converts fixed-cost risk into variable cost. The failure mode is scrambling to find freelancers during the peak; the bench must be built and warm *before* it's needed.

### Cadence
Capacity planning is not an annual exercise. Practitioner standard: **Monday morning workload review** (spot imbalances before they become fires), weekly rolling reforecast, monthly hiring-plan check against the 8–12 week view.

## 6. Scope management and the overservicing epidemic

### The numbers
- Scope creep affects **37–52% of projects** (PMI); average cost overrun attributable to scope changes ≈ **27% per project**.
- **~38% of agencies frequently overservice clients**; most that measured it found it costing thousands per month.
- Escalation math (Synergist): **10% overservicing = one month of free work per year; 20% = three months; 30% = your entire Q4 worked for free.**
- Scope creep on retainers silently erodes effective hourly rates by **15–30%** when nobody reconciles hours vs fee.

### Why agencies over-service (Synergist's five root causes + fixes)
1. **Poor estimating** → estimate from *recorded actuals of past similar projects*, not optimism; make creatives understand what the estimate assumed.
2. **Under-capacity resources (idle staff gold-plate)** → set utilization targets (~75%), compare invoiced vs actual hours to quantify the giveaway.
3. **Wrong resource assignment** → phase projects, match seniority to task, plan freelance support at kickoff rather than mid-crisis.
4. **Brief misunderstanding** → client-signed brief, kickoff meeting, regular internal progress reviews; missed complexity is unpaid complexity.
5. **Client brief changes without commercial adjustment** → detailed SOW as the reference document; assess time impact of every change; communicate revised estimates *before* doing the work; build buffer into estimates.

### The change-order playbook (projects)
1. SOW defines deliverables, revision rounds, timelines, and assumptions in granular detail — revision rounds are the #1 leak in creative work; cap them explicitly (e.g., "two rounds of consolidated feedback; further rounds at $X").
2. Any request outside SOW → written change order with cost/time impact → client approval *before* work starts. Make the form frictionless (template, same-day turnaround) so the team actually uses it.
3. Track "unbilled scope" as a weekly report line even when you choose to eat it — a deliberate goodwill investment is fine; invisible leakage is not.
4. Empower AMs to say "yes, and here's the change order" — the phrasing that preserves both relationship and margin.

### Retainer hygiene
- Define retainers in measurable units (deliverables or hours), never "ongoing support."
- Reconcile actual hours vs retainer assumption **monthly** (weekly for big accounts). When actuals exceed assumption by >10% for 2–3 consecutive months, force one of three moves: **raise the fee, cut the scope, or spin the excess into a separate project.** Presenting the client with data ("you're consistently using 130% of scope") converts an awkward conversation into an upsell.
- Watch under-delivery too: chronically delivering 60% of a retainer's hours is realization gold short-term and a churn bomb long-term.

## 7. Retainers vs projects, and agency profitability benchmarks

### The economics of each model (Forge 2026 benchmark data)

| Metric | Retainer | Project-based | Hybrid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual client churn | **18%** | 42% | 28% |
| Avg client lifespan | **56 months** | 24 months | — |
| Net margin effect | **+8pp** vs project peers | baseline (~13%) | — |

Adoption: **78% of digital agencies now use retainers as primary model** (up from 64% in 2023); ~90% have retainers somewhere in the mix; ~50% no longer bill hourly at all; 28% of top agencies run retainer + performance bonus.

**Retainer pros:** predictable cash flow → confident hiring; compounding client knowledge; smoother utilization planning. **Cons:** "all-you-can-eat" drift without boundaries; harder initial sell; can mask realization rot.
**Project pros:** easy client yes; big revenue events; fits one-offs/launches. **Cons:** perpetual pipeline refill; scope-balloon risk; lumpy utilization (feast/famine whiplash on staffing).
**The standard mature structure:** retainer base covering recurring services + separately scoped projects for everything else + (increasingly) a performance kicker on measurable outcomes.

### Profit benchmarks (Forge, Swydo, Promethean Research, 2025–2026)

**Net profit margin:** industry average **13%** (2025, down from 14%); healthy **15–20%**; high performers **25–30%**; elite top-3% ≈ **43%**. By size — studios (0–9 people) 19%, small (10–24) 12%, medium (25–49) 9%, large (50+) 8%: *margins compress as generalist agencies scale; only specialization or systems restore them.* Agencies that **narrowed** services: 30% margin; those that expanded: 10%. By type: niche/specialist 25–40%; PPC/paid/social/creative 15–25%; SEO 11–20%; full-service 10–20%. Specialization is the single strongest margin predictor in every dataset — David C. Baker's core thesis (The Business of Expertise): premium pricing follows tight positioning, and expertise emerges from work you've already done, not invention.

**The 55/25/20 rule on AGI** (industry-standard allocation): 55% delivery labor / 25% overhead / 20% net profit. Overhead detail (Parakeeto): admin 8–12%, sales & marketing 8–14%, facilities 4–6% — total 20–30% of AGI. Diagnosis by deviation: labor >55% → overstaffed or underpriced; overhead >25% → audit spend; profit <20% with the other two in line → delivery leakage (scope/realization).

**Revenue per FTE:** industry average ≈ **$163–172K**; healthy 150–200K; specialists 250K+; excellent 300–500K; **<$120–150K = structural problem** (over-hired, underpriced, or drowning in non-billable).

**Client concentration:** no single client >25% of revenue; top three ≤50%. (Directly relevant to creator businesses where one platform or one sponsor can dwarf everything.)

**7-figure vs 8-figure agency profile:** net margin 18–22% vs 25–32%; retention 78% vs 92%; 6+ months cash reserves 31% vs 73%. Scale done right buys *resilience*, not just size.

**Client profitability skew:** ~80% of profit from ~20% of clients; in most agencies a meaningful slice of clients are outright unprofitable and being subsidized. Per-client P&L (client AGI − hours × loaded cost) is the report that finds them; the remedy menu is reprice, reduce scope, or fire.

## 8. Ops tooling and the ritual calendar

### Tool landscape (2025–2026)
One integrated system beats five disconnected ones — the value is time, schedule, and money in the same place.
- **Productive.io** — purpose-built agency all-in-one (budgets, resourcing, utilization, profitability); low learning curve; strong forecasting.
- **Scoro** — strongest financial linkage (budgets ↔ planned hours ↔ actuals ↔ invoicing); steeper setup, top features gated to higher tiers.
- **Workamajig** — creative-agency veteran (since 2003): traffic, production schedules, proofing, billing in one; dated UX.
- **Kantata (Mavenlink)** — enterprise resource management, skills-based matching, real-time margins; rigid for day-to-day shuffles.
- **Float / Resource Guru** — lightweight dedicated resource scheduling; pair with a PM tool; the right entry point for small teams.
- Minimum viable stack for a small shop: PM tool + time tracking + a capacity spreadsheet (people × weeks, hours committed vs available) reviewed weekly. Parakeeto's advice: **start with the questions you need answered, keep the core financials clean first, and clean data before automating dashboards** (ETL, not live-wire everything).

### The ritual calendar (the system is the meetings, not the software)

**Daily (10 min)** — traffic/flow check: today's must-ships, blockers, reshuffles.
**Weekly (30–45 min) — the resourcing meeting.** The single highest-leverage agency ritual. Inputs: next 2 weeks of scheduled work vs capacity per person, incoming requests, pipeline heads-up from sales. Outputs: locked 2-week schedule, flagged conflicts, freelancer calls. Monday morning is the practitioner standard.
**Weekly — project health:** budget burn vs completion % per active project; anything >10% hot gets an action (change order, scope talk, replan) *this week*.
**Monthly — the metrics review:** utilization by role vs target; ABR trend; realization; per-client profitability; retainer hours vs fees reconciliation; scoping accuracy on closed projects; pipeline vs 8–12wk capacity.
**Quarterly:** pricing review (raise for new clients as default); client portfolio review (fire/fix bottom decile); capacity/hiring plan vs growth targets; rate-card vs ACPH drift.
**Annually:** full benchmark self-audit against §7 numbers; positioning review (Baker: is our expertise narrower and more valuable than last year?).

## 9. 2024–2026: AI compression and the repricing of agency work

The most important structural shift since the 2008-era move to digital:

- **Delivery compression:** AI is cutting timelines 3–4× on repeatable work (content batches, SEO copy, reporting, first-pass edits); marketing teams report ~13 hours/person/week saved. A 20-hour deliverable done in 5 hours destroys 75% of its revenue *if billed hourly* — the efficiency accrues to whoever the pricing model says it does.
- **Client pressure:** ~33% of agencies have received explicit "AI discount" requests; ~50% expect them; 29% report hourly-rate pushback citing AI.
- **Industry response:** 38% of US digital agencies moved at least one service line off hourly to retainer-plus-performance or outcome pricing by 2026; WPP now has 20–25% of net sales performance-linked and its CFO publicly committed to exiting time-and-materials; McKinsey ties ~25% of global fees to outcomes. Meanwhile ~31% of agencies report minimal pricing change and 29% are undecided — the middle is getting squeezed.
- **The utilization paradox:** SPI's record-low billable utilization (66.4% in 2025) partly reflects AI doing hours that used to be billable. Utilization remains essential as a **cost/capacity** metric but is dying as a **pricing** basis.
- **Decision framework (repeatability × outcome-measurability):** repeatable + measurable → productize or price on outcome (margins expand most); repeatable + fuzzy → flat productized subscription (keep the efficiency gain); bespoke + measurable → value-based pricing (10–20% of quantified client outcome); bespoke + fuzzy → hybrid retainer + escalator. Guardrails while migrating: ~50% gross-margin floor, ~$200K revenue per producer, quarterly price reassessment.
- Operating implication: track ABR religiously — it's the metric that shows whether AI gains are landing in your margin or leaking to clients as free speed.

## 10. Common mistakes

1. **No time data at all.** Every metric in this doc needs delivery hours. Even 4-category tracking (client/internal/sales/admin) unlocks the model. Manual tracking undercounting ~33% of billable work is still infinitely better than nothing.
2. **Computing ratios on gross revenue instead of AGI.** Pass-throughs inflate everything and hide the real business.
3. **One blanket utilization target.** Uniform targets across roles create gaming and unrealistic expectations; role-based or nothing.
4. **Chasing utilization while ABR collapses.** Filling the team with discounted work is busier-and-poorer. Read the pair.
5. **Confusing weekly and annual utilization conventions** (or 2,080 vs 1,760 denominators) — apples-to-oranges comparisons produce fake crises or fake comfort.
6. **Overservicing as culture.** "Going above and beyond" without measurement = 38% of agencies quietly donating a month-to-a-quarter of labor per year. Measure unbilled scope weekly; make generosity a decision, not a leak.
7. **No change-order muscle.** A change process that exists on paper but embarrasses the AMs to invoke is the same as no process.
8. **Retainers with no monthly reconciliation.** Both failure modes: eating 130% of scope (margin) and delivering 60% (churn).
9. **Hiring for the peak.** Full-time headcount sized to the busiest month guarantees troughs of sub-60% utilization. Core + freelance flex.
10. **Senior people on junior tasks.** Torches delivery margin twice: expensive hours on cheap work AND seniors unavailable for premium work.
11. **Ignoring per-client profitability.** Agency-wide averages hide the 20% of clients being subsidized by everyone else.
12. **Fixing overhead first.** The 25% bucket gets obsessive attention while the 55% delivery bucket (and pricing) — where the real money moves — goes unexamined.
13. **Expanding services to grow.** Data says the opposite: narrowed-service agencies run 30% margins vs 10% for expanders. Growth by specialization, not by menu length.
14. **Treating capacity planning as an annual event.** It's a weekly rolling ritual or it's fiction.
15. **Still billing hourly on AI-compressed work in 2026.** Donating the productivity gain to the client by contract design.

## 11. Questions Carl should ask

**Visibility & measurement**
- "Do you track time? What % of the team's hours can you account for last week?" (No data → start here; nothing else is computable.)
- "What's your AGI — revenue net of pass-throughs? Do your margin numbers use it?"
- "Can you tell me, per client, whether you made money last quarter?"

**Utilization & capacity**
- "What's utilization by role right now — and is that a weekly or annual figure, against what denominator?"
- "Who's over 85% right now, and for how long has that been true?" (Burnout audit.)
- "If a great $100K project landed today, could you tell me within an hour whether you can staff it, and when?" (Tests whether a traffic function exists.)
- "What revenue can your current team actually produce at target utilization and your real ABR? Is your sales goal above that ceiling?"

**Pricing & realization**
- "What did each delivered hour actually earn last quarter (ABR), and what does a delivery hour cost you fully loaded (ACPH)? Is the ratio ~3:1?"
- "Of the hours the team worked at standard rates, what % turned into invoiced revenue?" (Realization; <85% → find the leak.)
- "When did you last raise prices? What happened?"

**Scope & retainers**
- "How do your estimates compare to actuals on the last five projects?" (Scoping accuracy; >±20% → estimation is the fire.)
- "When a client asks for 'one more small thing,' what literally happens next?" (Change-order muscle test.)
- "For each retainer: hours delivered vs hours assumed, last three months?"

**Model & portfolio**
- "What % of revenue is recurring vs project? What's your biggest client as a % of revenue?" (Flags >25% single / >50% top-three.)
- "Which service lines has AI made 2–4× faster, and who's capturing that gain — you or the client?"
- "If you fired your least profitable client tomorrow, what would you do with the freed capacity?"

**Rituals**
- "Walk me through your Monday. Is there a meeting where next two weeks' work is matched against people's hours?" (No weekly resourcing ritual → institute it before buying any tool.)
