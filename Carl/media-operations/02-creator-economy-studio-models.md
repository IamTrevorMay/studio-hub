# Creator Economy Business & Studio Models

How creator businesses become media companies: the models, the math, the team structures, and the failure modes. Reference layer for advising a small creator-led media company.

## TL;DR

- **Products beat media at scale.** MrBeast's chocolate brand (Feastables) made $20M+ profit on ~$250M revenue in 2024 while his 400M+ subscriber media business *lost* ~$80M the same year. Content is the customer-acquisition engine; owned products are the profit engine. Advise clients to treat the channel as top-of-funnel, not the business.
- **The creator middle class is real but thin.** ~45.6% of monetizing creators earn $10K–$100K/yr; only 4–6% clear $100K; half earn under $15K. Concentration is worsening — top 1% of creators took 21% of ad-payment volume in 2025 (up from 15% in 2023). A client doing $500K–$5M/yr is already in roughly the top 1–2% of the ecosystem and should behave like a business, not a channel.
- **Sponsorships are still the backbone (~59–70% of creator revenue)** per Goldman Sachs and 2026 industry surveys — so a media company's first professionalization move is usually sales/partnerships ops, not more production.
- **Team scaling has a known ladder:** solo → freelance editor → producer/ops person ("content market fit" trigger per Colin & Samir) → small studio (5–10) → departmentalized company (25+) → holding company. Each rung has a distinct owner-bottleneck to break. Skipping rungs (hiring for prestige before revenue) is the classic killer.
- **The 2024–2026 capital wave rewards "professionalized org charts."** Dude Perfect ($100M+ raise at ~$250M implied valuation, ~5x forward revenue), Omaha Productions ($750–800M valuation), New Heights ($100M+/3yr Wondery deal). Buyers/investors now demand real financials — the market moved "from vibes to standardized metrics" (RockWater), with creator services trading ~5–9x EBITDA and infrastructure/software 5–15x revenue.
- **The two canonical failure modes:** (1) licensing your name to an operator you don't control (MrBeast Burger / Virtual Dining Concepts — brand damage lands on the creator regardless of fault), and (2) pivoting the audience into a paywall they didn't ask for (Watcher's $5.99 streaming service — instant subscriber and Patreon exodus). Diversify *with* the audience, never *against* it.
- **Diversification into physical/local businesses (relevant to a training-facility play):** creators and athlete-media companies increasingly bolt on location-based ventures, but margins are thin (theme/experience venues run 3–6% operating margin per Dude Perfect deal analysis). The media arm's job is to make the physical business's CAC ~zero, the same way Feastables spends ~0% of revenue on awareness vs. 15–25% for normal CPG.

## 1. The core thesis: from channel to company

The defining shift of 2020–2026: the most valuable creator businesses stopped being "channels with sponsors" and became **diversified media companies where content is the marketing department**.

The pattern (consistent across MrBeast, Dude Perfect, Smosh, Chamberlain Coffee, Prime, Omaha Productions):

1. **Audience accumulation** — a differentiated format compounds an audience on a rented platform (YouTube usually).
2. **Rented monetization** — AdSense + sponsorships. High margin, zero ownership, capped by CPMs and creator's personal output.
3. **Owned monetization** — products, subscriptions, events, licensing, courses, facilities. Requires operators, capital, and inventory risk, but converts attention into enterprise value.
4. **Institutionalization** — the business survives without the founder on camera daily: other talent, formats-as-IP, a real org chart, eventually outside capital or M&A.

Scott Manson (The Chernin Group), on right-sizing ambition: not every creator business needs a billion-dollar path — "a $10 million business is a meaningful business that you should be proud of." The advisor's first job is deciding *which stage the client is actually at* and *whether they even want stage 4*.

### Content-as-CAC: the single most important number

Traditional CPG spends **15–25% of revenue on marketing**. Feastables spends effectively **~0% on awareness** because the founder owns the distribution channel. This is the entire structural advantage of a creator-led business, and it generalizes:

- Media company → newsletter/merch: near-zero CAC.
- Media company → local training facility: the channel is the facility's lead-gen; every video is an ad the audience *chooses* to watch.
- Corollary: if a creator-launched venture still needs paid acquisition to grow, the audience-product fit is wrong (see Prime's collapse, §6).

## 2. Case studies with numbers

### MrBeast / Beast Industries — the maximalist blueprint

- **Scale:** 350+ employees; 460M+ subscribers across platforms; valuation ~$5B (after $300M from Alpha Wave Global, 2024); ~$5.2B cited pre-IPO; S-1 anticipated late 2026.
- **Revenue:** ~$250M media revenue 2024 (+125% YoY); company projects $899M total 2025 → $1.6B 2026. **Still unprofitable overall.**
- **The killer stat:** media division production costs consume 90%+ of media revenue; media lost ~$80M in 2024 while **Feastables made $20M+ profit on ~$250M revenue** and reached 80,000+ retail doors (Walmart, Target, 7-Eleven). The channel is a loss-leading marketing machine for CPG.
- **Playbook elements worth stealing (from the leaked 36-page onboarding doc):**
  - Single, explicit goal: "make the best YOUTUBE videos possible" — not the best-produced or funniest. Every business needs its equivalent one-sentence definition of quality.
  - Three metrics only: CTR, average view duration, average view percentage. Ruthless metric focus beats dashboards.
  - Video structure with assigned responsibilities per segment (min 0–1: prove the thumbnail; 1–3: "crazy progression"; 3–6; 6–end each have jobs).
  - **A/B/C player doctrine:** A-players are obsessive and coachable; C-players ("poisonous" average performers) exit immediately with severance. Harsh, but the underlying idea — small teams can't carry passengers — holds for any 5–25 person studio.
  - **"Critical Components":** any single point of failure for a video gets daily check-ins, backups, courier shipping. "Never coin flip a Critical Component." Generalizes to any production business (a shoot, an event, a facility opening).
  - **Consultants are "cheat codes"** — buy expertise that collapses development time rather than learning everything in-house.
  - Budget visibility rule: spend over $10K should be visible on camera; invisible spend gets challenged. I.e., production cost must map to audience-perceptible value.
- **Cautionary sub-case — MrBeast Burger:** licensed his name to Virtual Dining Concepts' ghost-kitchen network; VDC prioritized rapid expansion (to pitch the model to other celebrities) over quality; reviews hit "inedible"; MrBeast sued in 2023 to shut it down. Lesson: **in the customer's mind, the creator IS the product.** A license with no quality-control rights is a reputational blank check. By 2026 the burger business was restructured to ~400 company-owned/curated locations.

### Dude Perfect — the growth-equity path

- **Trajectory:** revenue $20M (2022) → $25M (2023) → ~$50M (2024); 60M subs, 17B views, ~50% international audience; **only ~25 team members** at time of raise — remarkable revenue-per-head (~$2M).
- **Deal (April 2024):** $100–300M from Highmount Capital, first outside capital ever. RockWater's math: assume ~40% stake ⇒ ~$250M valuation ⇒ ~5x forward revenue ("high for media"). For Highmount to hit a 25% IRR over 5 years, exit must reach ~$750M, requiring ~$150M revenue (3x in 5 years) — "aggressive across all monetization channels."
- **Use of funds:** hired a CEO; location-based entertainment HQ in Frisco, TX ($100M+ facility with trick-shot tower, museum, retail); content expansion (animation, TV, streaming app); live tours; consumer products.
- **The stated why (Coby Cotton):** "we want this to be something much bigger than just the five of us" — capital raised explicitly to outlive the founders' on-camera careers, including developing *other* talent.
- **Analyst skepticism worth internalizing (Mike Booth, via RockWater):** location-based entertainment is a margin trap — Cedar Fair ~6% and Six Flags ~3% operating margins; the Dallas facility needs ~1.75M annual visitors and 6+ years payback vs. 2–3 years for a Topgolf. **Directly relevant to any creator considering a physical facility: model visitor throughput and payback period honestly, and let the media arm subsidize CAC, not opex.**

### Smosh — die, get acquired, buy yourself back

The best longitudinal case on ownership and creative control:

- 2018: parent Defy Media collapses; Smosh is orphaned.
- Feb 2019: **Mythical Entertainment (Rhett & Link) buys Smosh for <$10M cash** — off its own balance sheet, no VC, no debt. Creator-owned holding company acquiring distressed creator IP at a fire-sale price.
- Under Mythical: creative freedom restored; first CEO (Daniel Tibbets, 2021), EVP Programming (2022) — creators installing professional management under a friendly owner.
- June 2023: founders **Anthony Padilla and Ian Hecox buy back majority ownership**; Mythical retains a minority stake and advisory role; hired a CEO (Alessandra Catanese) and EVP of Production on day one.
- Post-buyback results: viewership surge, **~4x revenue growth by 2025**, **2x+ employee count**, moving into a 32,000 sq ft LA studio; sponsors including Google and Adidas; revived live events (Summer Games, tours).
- Lessons: (1) creator IP under an indifferent corporate parent decays; the same IP under founder control with professional ops compounds; (2) a *creator-run holding company* (Mythical) can be a better steward/buyer than PE or a media conglomerate; (3) founders came back **with** a CEO — creative control and professional management are complements, not substitutes.

### Colin & Samir — the small-studio existence proof

- ~6 full-time employees, physical office, standard hours; ~700K+ YouTube subs; revenue = YouTube + podcast + newsletter (The Publish Press) + brand partnerships (a Samsung annual ambassador deal was the 2020 stabilizer).
- History matters: sold a previous company (The Lacrosse Network), then were "$18,000 in the hole" in 2019 before finding the creator-economy beat. Even sophisticated operators nearly died before finding **content-market fit**.
- Their operative framework: *"bringing in some level of operational support is required when you actually find content market fit... when you're actually developing into a business, it's required."* — i.e., don't staff up hunting for fit; staff up to *exploit* fit.
- Colin on the ceiling of founder-led quality control: he "couldn't manage a company with 300 people" because he wants to oversee everything that ships. Creators must eventually choose: stay small and personally excellent, or build review systems and let go. Both are valid businesses; pretending you've chosen the second while behaving like the first is the trap.
- This is the most relevant comp for a small creator-led media company: **6 people, multiple owned distribution surfaces (video + podcast + newsletter), anchored by one or two recurring brand relationships.**

### Athlete-creator empires — Kelce, Manning, McAfee

- **New Heights (Jason & Travis Kelce):** 3-year, **$100M+** deal with Amazon's Wondery (Aug 2024) for exclusive ad sales + global distribution of all audio/video including back catalog and international adaptations. Wondery's CEO's rationale: the show transcended its category into "the cultural zeitgeist." Model: athletes keep creative control, sell *distribution and ad-sales rights* — a licensing model, not an acquisition.
- **Omaha Productions (Peyton Manning):** sold ~10% to Patrick Whitesell/Silver Lake at **$750–800M+ valuation** (March 2025); reportedly ~$60M/yr from ESPN alone. Model: personality-anchored *production company* — ManningCast, Netflix's Quarterback/Receiver/Starting 5, NFL Honors, ESPN+ Places franchise, first scripted series (Chad Powers). Manning appears in a fraction of the output; the company produces around a sensibility, not a face.
- **Pat McAfee:** ~$60M/yr ESPN talent deal. Model: talent-for-hire at platform scale, retaining show identity.
- The athlete lesson stack: (1) credibility in a niche → media property; (2) three distinct exits: sell distribution (Kelce), build a studio (Manning), or rent yourself out (McAfee); (3) legacy media now pays creator-economy prices for audiences it can't build itself. Traditional-media partnership is a monetization *event*, not a business model — the audience relationship must stay owned.

### Creator brands — Feastables vs. Prime vs. Chamberlain Coffee

- **Feastables:** $0 → ~$250M revenue in <3 years; profitable; key hire was a former RXBAR executive who knew grocery retail. Formula = creator supplies audience + operator supplies CPG infrastructure + retail-first (Walmart) over D2C-first.
- **Prime (Logan Paul & KSI):** ~$1.3B global revenue peak (2023) → ~$300M projected 2025, a ~76% collapse; profits down 90%+; reduced to bargain bins in some markets. Post-mortem (Marketing Week): "you can't cheat the fundamentals" — availability, sustained brand-building, right price, real proposition. Virality created a demand spike collectors and hype-buyers drove; there was no durable consumption habit underneath. **Hype is a launch strategy, not a retention strategy.**
- **Chamberlain Coffee (Emma Chamberlain):** ~$20M revenue by 2023 — the *normal-scale* success story: lifestyle-adjacent product, authentic founder use, modest but real business. Most creator brands should aspire to this shape, not Prime's.
- Selection criteria for a creator product: daily-consumption category, genuine founder association, an experienced category operator as partner/hire, and retail economics that work *without* the hype spike.

## 3. Team structure by tier (the scaling ladder)

Synthesized from Colin & Samir, the MrBeast doc, Smosh, Dude Perfect, and creator-ops literature. Subscriber counts are rough proxies — **revenue and output cadence are the real triggers.**

### Tier 0 — Solo (pre content-market fit; typically <$100K/yr)
- Creator does everything. Correct at this stage — hiring before fit burns cash on the wrong content.
- Only acceptable spend: freelance help on the single task the creator is worst at / hates most / that eats the most hours (usually editing).
- Exit criteria: repeatable format, predictable views, first steady revenue (AdSense milestone or recurring brand deals).

### Tier 1 — Creator + freelancers (~$100K–$500K/yr; often 100K–1M subs)
- Core: freelance editor (first hire in the vast majority of cases), then thumbnail designer, maybe a scriptwriter/researcher.
- Structure: one strategist (the creator) + rotating trusted freelancers. This hybrid model scales surprisingly far — CareerFoundry-style channels have passed 250K subs on it.
- Bottleneck broken: creator's post-production hours.
- Watch: freelancer dependency and quality drift; build an editing style guide / example library early.

### Tier 2 — Small studio (~$500K–$3M/yr; 3–8 people)
- The "content market fit → operational support" moment (Chaudry). First *operations* hire: producer or channel/business manager who owns calendar, briefs, sponsor logistics, invoicing.
- Typical shape: creator + producer + 1–2 editors + part-time design/strategy. Colin & Samir run essentially this at 6 FTE across video + podcast + newsletter.
- Sponsorships are ~59–70% of revenue economy-wide at this tier — a dedicated partnerships function (even fractional) usually pays for itself before another editor does.
- Bottleneck broken: creator as project manager.

### Tier 3 — Departmentalized company (~$3M–$20M/yr; 10–40 people)
- Departments emerge: content/production, post, partnerships/sales, ops/finance, audience/community. Roles like Director of Video, creative producers, videographers, strategist.
- Dude Perfect ran ~$50M revenue on ~25 people (lean because talent = owners); Watcher ran 25 people on a much smaller revenue base — **headcount must trail revenue, and Watcher is what happens when it doesn't** (their cost structure pushed them into the fatal paywall pivot).
- Key hires: a real GM/COO or CEO (Smosh and Dude Perfect both did this), head of sales, finance lead.
- Bottleneck broken: creator as manager-of-managers; founder moves to editorial quality bar + face of the brand.

### Tier 4 — Media company / holding company ($20M+ / 50–350+ people)
- Multiple brands/channels, owned products, possibly other talent under the roof. Beast Industries (350+), Mythical (owns/incubates other creator brands), Flight Group ($425M valuation: media + ventures + tech), Whalar ($400M: agency + campuses + gaming + venture arm).
- Requires outside capital or years of retained earnings; requires the founder to accept Colin's dilemma — you cannot personally QC 300 people's output; you build systems (MrBeast's onboarding doc *is* such a system) or you stay smaller.
- The 2026 M&A market explicitly prices this: RockWater notes a widening gap between "YouTubers" and "media companies," with premium valuations for **professionalized org charts (business, content, operations teams)** and hybrid models stacking talent rep + production + agency functions ("the hybrid premium").

### Rules of thumb across tiers
- Hire against the *current* bottleneck, in this canonical order: editing → production/ops → sales → strategy/audience → management.
- Revenue per employee: healthy creator businesses run $250K–$2M/head; below ~$150K/head, the org is ahead of its business.
- Every tier transition changes the founder's job. Name the new job explicitly or the founder unconsciously keeps the old one.

## 4. The creator middle class: economics and distribution (2024–2026 data)

- **Market size:** Goldman Sachs — TAM ~$480B by 2027 (roughly doubling); RockWater cites ~$530B by 2030. Global creator economy ~$255B (2025) → ~$323B (2026, +26.5%). US creator ad spend ~$37B (2025) → ~$43.9B (2026).
- **Population:** 50M+ people identify as creators; only ~2M are full-time; ~10–20% CAGR in creator population expected.
- **Income distribution (the sobering table):**
  - ~50% of creators earn under $15K/yr; ~48.7% under $10K.
  - ~45.6% earn $10K–$100K ("creator middle class," Influencer Marketing Factory 2026).
  - Only ~4–5.7% earn over $100K.
  - **57% of full-time creators earn below the $44K US living wage.**
- **Concentration is increasing:** top 1% captured 21% of creator ad-payment volume in 2025 (15% in 2023); top 10% captured 62% (was 53%). The middle is getting squeezed — mid-size creators must differentiate on owned audience (email, community, events) and owned products, because platform ad economics increasingly flow to the head.
- **Revenue mix (2026 creator average):** sponsored content ~59%, platform payouts ~24%, products/merch ~8.4%, affiliate ~8.2%. Goldman pegs brand deals at ~70% of creator revenue. Implication: sponsorship sales excellence is the highest-leverage skill for the middle class; product revenue is where margin and enterprise value live.
- **Platform payouts:** YouTube paid $70B+ to creators over the trailing 3 years; Meta ~$3B in 2025 (+35%); TikTok Shop US GMV $15.8B (+108%) — social commerce is the fastest-growing monetization surface.
- Advisory framing: a client at $1M+ revenue is not "small" in creator-economy terms — they're statistically elite, and the right benchmarks are Tier 2–3 studios, not headline mega-creators.

## 5. Capital, M&A, and valuation (RockWater's "sophomore year" market)

What creator businesses are worth and who buys them, per 2026 deal data:

- **Multiples:**
  - Creator services businesses (agencies, production, talent rep): **~5–9x EBITDA**.
  - Creator infrastructure/software: **5–15x revenue** (SocialPilot ~5x ARR at $50M; Captiv8 ~5.5x net revenue at $150–175M; Dotdigital/Social Snowball ~6.7x revenue).
  - Talent-anchored media cos with diversified revenue: ~5x forward revenue is *high* (Dude Perfect) — most trade below it.
- **Buyer classes expanding:** legacy media (Paramount bought The Free Press for $150M), B2B software, tech aggregators (Bending Spoons/Vimeo $1.38B), PE (Carlyle→3 Arts, Sienna→Independent Talent Group $100M+), and roll-ups (Fixated–Studio71 merger, Forbes: "creator economy roll-ups have arrived").
- **New capital stack for creator IP:** licensing deals (Tubi–Audiochuck $150M multi-year; Netflix–iHeart video windowing), creator-sports raises (Good Good Golf $45M led by Creator Sports Capital + Omaha Productions), and A24 greenlighting a YouTuber-directed feature. Traditional media is now a *customer* of creator companies.
- **The transparency shift:** public acquirers now disclose prices and earnouts; the anticipated MrBeast S-1 will publish creator-company financials at unprecedented depth; expected surge in **$10M–$100M lower-middle-market deals** as "bid-ask spread" narrows.
- **Ecosystem model (successor to MCNs):** Flight Group and Whalar stack media + ventures + tech/services. RockWater warns of "large variance in outcomes" — the MCN failure repeats if the stacked services are low-margin and don't share real synergy.
- **What raises value for a small media company** (assembled from the above): recurring revenue (retainers, subscriptions) > one-off deals; owned IP/formats that work without the founder; documented financials; a management layer; product revenue alongside media revenue. What kills value: founder-dependence, sponsorship concentration in 1–2 accounts, no financial hygiene.

## 6. Common mistakes (failure modes with named bodies)

1. **Licensing the name without controlling the product** — MrBeast Burger. The operator's incentives (VDC: expand fast to sell the model to other celebrities) diverged from the creator's (quality = brand). Any license must include quality-control rights, audit rights, and termination triggers, because reputational damage is non-delegable.
2. **Paywalling the existing audience** — Watcher (2024): 25 employees, ~3M subs, announced a $5.99/mo exclusive streaming service; instant backlash, Patreon and subscriber exodus, apology and reversal within a week; views/Patreon never fully recovered. Compound errors: no credit/discount for existing Patreon supporters, "it's just a coffee a month" framing to a cost-of-living-squeezed audience, justifying it with *production costs the audience never asked for*. Rules: additive premium tiers, never subtractive; grandfather existing supporters; the audience doesn't owe you your burn rate.
3. **Headcount ahead of revenue** — Watcher again (25 people forced the pivot), and half the dead MCN generation. Revenue per head below ~$150K is a flashing light.
4. **Confusing a hype spike with a business** — Prime: $1.3B → ~$300M in ~2 years. Scarcity-driven virality recruited collectors, not consumers; when hype normalized, fundamentals (price, proposition, repeat purchase) were absent.
5. **Corporate parent misalignment** — Smosh under Defy Media: creator IP treated as inventory, "money-first" management, company collapse nearly took the brand with it. Creators selling majority control should price in the risk that the parent dies or stops caring.
6. **Founder can't let go, but scales anyway** — the Colin dilemma unresolved: org grows past the founder's personal QC capacity with no delegated quality system, output degrades, culture sours. Either build the MrBeast-style codified quality doctrine or cap the org size deliberately.
7. **Physical-venue romance without throughput math** — the Dude Perfect caution: experience venues run 3–6% operating margins; payback can run 6+ years. A creator's audience makes marketing cheap but doesn't repeal facility economics. Model local drive-time population, visit frequency, and payback before pouring concrete. (Directly applicable to any training-facility buildout: the media arm derisks demand, not fixed costs.)
8. **Chasing the mega-creator playbook at middle-class scale** — Beast Industries loses money on media at $250M revenue; imitating spectacle economics at 1/100th the revenue is suicide. Small studios win on margin discipline and owned-audience depth, not production budgets.
9. **Over-reliance on platform payouts** — payouts are ~24% of creator revenue and concentrating toward the top 1%. Any plan that assumes AdSense growth as the primary line is fragile by construction.

## 7. Playbooks and checklists

### Playbook: channel → media company (staged)
1. **Prove content-market fit** (repeatable format, predictable baseline views, inbound sponsor interest) before any structural spend.
2. **Buy back the founder's hours**: freelance editor → thumbnail/design → researcher.
3. **Install ops** at fit: producer/manager owning calendar, briefs, sponsor delivery, invoicing.
4. **Professionalize revenue**: rate card, sponsorship pipeline, 2+ anchor recurring partners (the Colin & Samir Samsung move), then add an owned surface (newsletter, community) to de-platform-risk the audience.
5. **Add a product line** only in a category with genuine founder association + an experienced category operator (the RXBAR-exec pattern) + economics that survive without hype.
6. **Institutionalize**: hire GM/CEO-type when the founder is the management bottleneck (Smosh, Dude Perfect precedent); codify the quality bar in writing (the MrBeast doc pattern); develop second-line talent so the brand outlives the founders' on-camera years.
7. **Optional capital event**: raise growth equity / sell distribution rights / sell a minority stake — only once financials are documented and the growth use-of-funds is specific (Dude Perfect raised *for* named ventures, not "growth").

### Checklist: before launching a creator product
- [ ] Daily/weekly consumption category with real repeat purchase?
- [ ] Authentic, pre-existing founder association with the category?
- [ ] Operator with category experience hired or partnered (with aligned equity)?
- [ ] Quality-control and termination rights if any licensing/co-manufacture involved?
- [ ] Unit economics work at post-hype steady state (assume the launch spike halves, then halves again)?
- [ ] Retail vs D2C decision made on category norms, not ideology (Feastables won by going Walmart-first)?

### Checklist: before any audience-monetization change (paywall, price, platform move)
- [ ] Is it additive (new value) rather than subtractive (removing free access)?
- [ ] Are existing supporters (Patreon/members) grandfathered or credited?
- [ ] Does the messaging avoid citing *our costs* as the audience's problem?
- [ ] Has the announcement been red-teamed against the audience's economic reality?
- [ ] Rollback plan if sentiment breaks in the first 72 hours?

### Checklist: physical venue / facility bolt-on
- [ ] Throughput model: local population within drive time × realistic visit frequency × capture rate ≥ required visits?
- [ ] Payback period ≤ 3–4 years (Topgolf-class benchmark: 2–3; theme-park class 6+ is a warning)?
- [ ] Media arm's role defined: demand generation + content flywheel (facility as set), with CAC assumed near zero but opex fully funded by facility revenue?
- [ ] Operating margin modeled at experience-venue norms (single digits) not media norms?

## 8. Questions Carl should ask a client

**Stage diagnosis**
- What's your revenue per full-time-equivalent? (Under $150K/head = org ahead of business.)
- Which tier are you on the ladder (§3), and what specific bottleneck would the *next* hire break?
- Do you have content-market fit — a repeatable format with predictable baseline performance — or are you still searching? (Hiring rules differ completely.)

**Revenue architecture**
- What % of revenue is sponsorships, and what % of *that* is your top two sponsors? (Concentration in 1–2 accounts is the quiet killer.)
- What do you own outright — email list, community, product, formats that work without your face?
- If YouTube halved your RPM tomorrow, what's the plan?

**Founder role**
- What is your job title *this* year, and what did you stop doing from last year's job?
- Are you willing to let output ship that you haven't personally reviewed? If no — is the org sized for that answer?
- Does the business have any value if you stop appearing on camera? What's the 5-year plan to make that answer "yes" (or the deliberate decision that it's fine as "no")?

**Product/venture decisions**
- For any product or facility idea: who is the operator with category scars, and what do they own?
- What does this venture look like when the launch hype is gone — Prime at $300M is still bigger than most companies, but its cost structure was built for $1.3B. Which cost structure are you building?
- If someone else runs your name (license, franchise, ghost operation): what quality-control and kill-switch rights do you hold?

**Capital & exit posture**
- Are you building a lifestyle-scale studio (Colin & Samir shape), a growth business (Dude Perfect shape), or a holding company (Mythical/Beast shape)? Different answers mean different hiring, margin, and documentation standards *today*.
- If a buyer or investor asked for three years of clean financials tomorrow, could you produce them?
- What would you sell: the company, a minority stake, distribution rights (Kelce model), or nothing? Structure the business now for the answer.
