# Scaling Applied to Mayday Media + Neptune Performance

Carl's translation of the business-scaling domain into a playbook for Trevor's actual situation: a
creator-led media company (More Mayday + clips, sponsorships through an agency, Substack, Fourthwall,
Twitch, TikTok), a baseball development facility in buildout (Neptune), and an internal ops app
(Mayday Studio) that already does half the instrumentation work most companies never get to.

## TL;DR — the highest-leverage moves

1. **Pre-sell Neptune before you spend on it.** Founding memberships + 2–3 travel-org winter
   contracts covering 20–30% of prime winter capacity, sold through the Mayday audience, *before*
   the lease/buildout money commits. If the audience can't pre-sell it, that's the market talking
   (see business-scaling/11-training-facility-business.md).
2. **Separate the entities and the books now, while Neptune is young.** Two OpCo LLCs, separate bank
   accounts, a written cost-sharing agreement, and Neptune paying (at least on paper) market rate for
   Mayday's content engine. Restructuring after a liability event or a lease signing is 10x harder
   (see business-scaling/07-multi-business-holdco.md).
3. **Stand up a 13-week rolling cash forecast and negotiate 50% upfront on every sponsorship.**
   Sponsor money is net-30/60 with approval lag; a buildout is the classic "profitable on paper, dead
   on payroll" setup. This is a weekend of setup and 45 min/week forever
   (see business-scaling/05-financial-ops-cashflow.md).
4. **Install the weekly operating meeting: EOS-style scorecard + Rocks + IDS, 90 minutes, same slot,
   with separate Mayday and Neptune lanes.** Teams that review weekly complete ~43% more of their
   goals. The Business Dev page tracks the buildout; nothing currently forces a weekly *decision*
   rhythm (see business-scaling/06-strategic-planning-okrs.md).
5. **Treat Trevor's on-camera hours as the drum.** Batch shoot days, a 2–4 video publish buffer, and
   a hard rule that nothing on the founder's plate below his Buyback Rate survives the quarter.
   Founder review/approval is the classic policy constraint in a content pipeline
   (see business-scaling/10-bottlenecks-operational-excellence.md, 04-founder-leverage-delegation.md).
6. **Position Neptune as a development lab, not a cage barn.** Assessment-first funnel, memberships +
   seasonal programs (87% 1-yr retention) over credit packs (32%), annual option always offered, coaches
   W-2, September–October opening. The lab model commands 3–10x the price per athlete and is the only
   positioning that matches the media flywheel (see business-scaling/11-training-facility-business.md).
7. **Cap concurrent bets at one.** Neptune *is* the expansion bet — a true Ansoff diversification (new
   product, new customer). Only ~1 in 5 growth initiatives succeeds; don't run a second major bet
   (app productization, new channel, new format) until Neptune has a GM and positive cash flow
   (see business-scaling/12-growth-strategy-expansion.md, 01-scaling-frameworks-growth-stages.md).
8. **Mayday Studio is a moat, not a product — for now.** The Basecamp/Mailchimp bar is *external
   pull*: other creator teams actively asking to pay for it. Until then, audit founder hours spent
   building the app vs. hours it saves; internal tooling is the classic Zone-of-Excellence trap for a
   technical founder (see business-scaling/08-productization-scalable-models.md).

## Where you are on the map (stage diagnosis)

Every framework in the domain says the same thing about this exact configuration:

- **Mayday Media** is a founder-led creator business where the founder is also the product (on-camera
  talent, taste, sponsor relationships). That's Greiner Phase 1 / McKeown "Fun→Whitewater": the point
  where "make great stuff and hustle" stops scaling and the founder starts experiencing systems as the
  enemy of the magic. The counter: systematize the *container* (pipeline, publishing, invoicing,
  freelancers), protect the creative core with principles + exemplars, never checklists
  (see business-scaling/03-sops-systems-process-documentation.md).
- **Neptune** is a capital-hungry, long-cash-conversion-cycle diversification. EOS-grade simplicity
  fits the team size, but EOS has *no cash tools* — so the prescription is a hybrid: EOS meeting and
  accountability mechanics + Scaling Up's cash conversion cycle discipline bolted on
  (see business-scaling/01-scaling-frameworks-growth-stages.md).
- **The binding constraint is founder attention**, not talent or ideas. The delegation ceiling hits
  at 7–10 people in normal companies; in creator businesses it shows up at 3–8 because the founder is
  also the talent. Every recommendation below is downstream of protecting and reallocating Trevor's
  hours.
- **Structural asset you already have:** Mayday Studio syncs daily metrics from every platform, runs
  an Automations engine, tracks the buildout in the BD page, and manages freelancers. Most sub-$5M
  companies would kill for this instrumentation. What's missing isn't data — it's *cadence, owners,
  and targets* wrapped around the data (see business-scaling/09-kpis-dashboards-metrics-management.md).

## NOW — next 30 days

### 1. Cash architecture before buildout dollars move

- **Build the 13-week cash flow forecast** (direct method, weekly columns, receipts not revenue).
  Inflows by *deposit date*: AdSense (reliable, monthly), sponsor payments (model actual historical
  lag — if a brand pays net-60 plus two weeks of approval, model 74 days), Substack, Fourthwall
  settlements, Stripe. Outflows by actual due date: freelancer bi-weekly payroll periods (the app
  already tracks 1st–15th / 16th–EOM), rent, software, tax dates. 30–60 min/week to maintain. The
  finance-tab data in Mayday Studio (Tiller sync, Accounting page) is the feed; the forecast is the
  missing forward view (see business-scaling/05-financial-ops-cashflow.md).
- **Change sponsorship terms starting with the next deal.** Script: "We work on 50% upfront, balance
  net-30 after delivery — does that work on your end?" Most brands accept. Deals >$10K: 50/30/20
  milestones. Add a 1.5%/month late fee clause. Cap approval windows in the contract. Route this
  through the agency partner as *your standard terms*, not a negotiation. This single move halves
  receivable float and is worth more than any new revenue initiative this quarter.
- **Three bank accounts per entity, minimum:** operating, tax (15% of real revenue swept on the 10th
  and 25th), profit/reserve. Skip full five-account Profit First ceremony; keep the load-bearing idea
  (segregated tax and reserve money can't be accidentally spent).
- **Pay Trevor a market wage in the books.** Price the on-camera role + the CEO role at replacement
  cost. Until that's in the P&L, "Mayday is profitable" is an untested claim (Crabtree's cardinal
  rule). Target: 10–15% pretax profit *after* that wage.

### 2. Entity separation and intercompany hygiene

- Mayday Media and Neptune are different liability universes: a defamation/IP claim vs. a kid getting
  hurt on premises. **Separate LLCs before the lease is signed**, ideally under a simple holdco.
  Consider an AssetCo for expensive durable gear (cages, mounds, TrackMan/HitTrax, cameras) leased to
  the OpCos — with CPA review of the self-rental rule.
- **Write the intercompany agreements now**, while they're trivial: cost-sharing for Mayday Studio
  (the app is a shared-services asset — own it at holdco level, allocate its cost), a services
  agreement for any shared staff, and — critically — **Neptune pays market-ish rates for Mayday's
  content/marketing work**, even if it's just bookkeeping entries at first. Subsidized synergy hides
  Neptune's true CAC and makes both P&Ls lie (Tiny's hard-won rule)
  (see business-scaling/07-multi-business-holdco.md).
- Allocate buildout-phase shared costs on **budget/headcount, not revenue %** — revenue allocation
  makes a pre-revenue Neptune look free.

### 3. The weekly operating meeting (the cheapest intervention in the whole domain)

- 90 minutes, same day/time forever, L10 format: 5 min scorecard (on/off-track only, zero
  discussion), 5 min Rocks check, 5 min headlines, 5 min to-dos, then **60 minutes of IDS** on the
  top 3 issues. Off-track anything drops to the Issues List; solved issues never reappear.
- **Separate scorecard sections and Rock lanes for Mayday vs. Neptune, one shared meeting.** Mixing
  them guarantees the noisier business (media) starves the newer one (facility) — or vice versa
  during buildout panic (see business-scaling/06-strategic-planning-okrs.md).
- Set **3 company Rocks max per quarter**, written as outcomes ("Neptune LOI signed with TI allowance
  ≥$X by Sep 30"), each tagged **committed or aspirational** (Google's split). Buildout milestones
  with hard dates are committed; membership pre-sale targets are aspirational, graded on the 0.7 curve.
- Mayday Studio angle: the BD page already has phases, milestones, and countdown — that's the
  quarterly layer. The gap is a **weekly scorecard view**: 5–15 numbers, one owner each, RAG status,
  13 trailing weeks. This is a high-value, low-effort app feature — the data feeds mostly exist.

### 4. Founder decision audit

- Two-week log of every decision routed to Trevor. Bucket: only-founder-can (strategy, key hires,
  brand, on-camera, taste), founder-by-habit, founder-by-vacuum. Kill the vacuum bucket with named
  owners; kill the habit bucket with written thresholds ("anything under $500, just decide";
  "clips approved unless flagged in 24h").
- Compute the **Buyback Rate** (income ÷ 2,000 ÷ 4) and price every recurring task on Trevor's plate
  against it. Anything below the rate — scheduling, asset wrangling, routine sponsor logistics,
  invoice chasing — gets camcordered (record once while doing it) and handed to a freelancer or an
  Automation. The app's Automations engine is literally rung 5 of the maturity ladder; each new
  automation should trace to a documented process (see business-scaling/04-founder-leverage-delegation.md,
  03-sops-systems-process-documentation.md).

## NEXT — this quarter

### 5. Neptune launch discipline (the diversification run with diversification rules)

- **Fund it like an outside investor would:** approved budget, tranche gates ("next $X releases when
  founding members ≥Y"), and **written kill criteria** ("if founding memberships <N by [date], we
  delay phase 2"). Pre-committed kill criteria are the best defense against escalation of commitment
  on a capital project (see business-scaling/12-growth-strategy-expansion.md).
- **Pre-sale as the gate.** Bottom-up market sizing first: count travel orgs, LL/PONY registrations,
  and HS programs within a 20–25 minute drive; median household income in the top feeder zips
  ($90K+ concentrations); competitor prime-time availability two weeks out. SOM = 5–15% of that
  serviceable market — if that number doesn't carry the lease, resize the footprint.
- **Financing sequence:** contractor walkthrough of the shell *before* LOI (HVAC/electrical/sprinkler
  surprises run $100–250K); negotiate a TI allowance into the lease; SBA 7(a) for buildout + working
  capital cushion in one facility (60–120 days — start pre-qualification now); separate equipment
  financing for gear (closes in days, collateralized); **6 months of fixed costs in reserve on top**.
  Never bridge with MCAs. The buildout is a complexity trigger that justifies **fractional CFO hours
  (10–15/month) at any revenue level** — but only after the books are clean and split by entity
  (see business-scaling/05-financial-ops-cashflow.md).
- **Product design from the Baseline dataset:** memberships + seasonal programs as the foundation
  (87% and 82% 1-yr retention for seasonal/annual vs. 32% for credit packs); always offer annual at
  ~8% discount (~25% of members take it; 1.9x paid months over 2 years); build a real top tier at
  ~2.5–3x base (27% of members buy the top tier — that's where HitTrax/TrackMan access lives);
  assessments as standalone paid products ($150–400) feeding programs, Driveline-style. Rentals are
  filler, never the foundation.
- **Coaches W-2 from day one** for anyone on your schedule and curriculum (costs ~10–12% more than
  1099 and removes the industry's most common legal landmine). Facility owns the client relationship —
  booking, billing, CRM in facility software — or you're a landlord watching a coach walk out with 40
  clients. Hybrid comp: W-2 base + split on privates + retention bonus. This maps to the misclassification
  logic already relevant to Mayday's long-tenured freelancers: any contractor at >80% income share,
  on your tools and hours, past ~12 months, should be on the conversion path
  (see business-scaling/02-org-design-hiring-sequence.md).
- **Open September–October**, not spring. August is the #1 join month (index 372); Nov–Mar carries
  60–70% of indoor revenue. An April opening eats 6+ months of fixed costs before the market shows up.
- **Utilization is the whole model:** ±10 points of prime-time utilization ≈ ±$45–60K revenue at ~85%
  incremental margin on the reference 8-cage model. Book a daytime strategy before opening
  (homeschool PE, winter pro/college guys, PT subleases, birthday parties) — 9am–4pm is ~40% of open
  hours (see business-scaling/11-training-facility-business.md).

### 6. Mayday hiring ladder (buyback order, not org-chart fantasy)

The sequence for a Mayday-shaped business: **editor(s) → EA/ops assistant → producer → ops/finance
generalist → channel manager → revenue lead** (see business-scaling/02-org-design-hiring-sequence.md).
Applied:

- Editing/clipping capacity exists (David Korn + freelancer pool). The likely next rung is the
  **EA/ops-assistant** — inbox, calendar, sponsor logistics, scheduling. Operator-grade remote EAs
  start ~$3K/month and pay back in weeks. Don't skip this rung to hire a "manager."
- The **producer seat** ("content ships on time" as a reliability metric) is the second rung — it's
  what makes the publish buffer real instead of aspirational.
- Every hire gets a **scorecard (mission / 3–8 outcomes / competencies), not a job description**, and
  you call the references yourself asking "would you enthusiastically rehire?" Hold 9–12 months of
  fully loaded cost (salary × 1.25–1.4) before committing to a permanent role; flex spikes with
  contractors.
- **Neptune needs a named GM-track person within ~12 months of opening.** The 12-month rule: a venture
  still requiring the founder's daily presence after a year needs a hired leader, a smaller scope, or
  a shutdown decision. Trevor cannot be facility GM and the channel's face; the facility has fixed
  hours, so founder-as-bottleneck caps revenue directly there.

### 7. Pipeline flow: Drum-Buffer-Rope on the content machine

- **Declare the drum:** Trevor's shoot capacity (e.g., 2 batch shoot-days/month → X videos/month
  max). Publish the number. Scripts, sets, thumbnails, and sponsor integrations schedule around drum
  dates, never the reverse.
- **Buffers:** 2–4 fully-ready items staged before each shoot day (scripts locked, assets pulled);
  2–4 finished videos banked downstream so cadence survives a blown shoot. Consistency failures are a
  buffer problem, not a discipline problem.
- **Hunt policy constraints before hiring:** if edits wait days for founder notes, the fix is a fixed
  daily review window + structured feedback template + "approved unless flagged in 24h" for low-risk
  items — not editor #2. Most orgs run their constraint at <50% of real capacity; exploit before you
  elevate. Little's Law check: if the pipeline finishes 2 videos/week with 12 in flight, everything
  takes 6 weeks regardless of effort — cap WIP near team size
  (see business-scaling/10-bottlenecks-operational-excellence.md).

### 8. Scorecard content (what actually goes on the weekly view)

Majority **controllable inputs**, one owner, weekly targets. Candidate rows:

- *Mayday:* uploads published vs. plan; CTR × avg % viewed on new uploads (paired — Goodhart
  guardrail); sponsor pitches/proposals out; sponsor deliverables shipped on time (feeds the agency
  portal anyway); newsletter verified clicks (not opens — MPP inflates them); IG stories vs. daily
  goal (already tracked by the "Do this more" widget — promote it to the scorecard); cash balance
  (the one output everyone sees).
- *Neptune (buildout phase):* founding memberships sold; team-contract conversations held; buildout
  milestones on/off-track; committed pre-sale $ vs. gate. *(Post-open: trial sessions booked, tours
  given, new-member week-1 visits — <4 visits in month 1 ≈ 80% cancellation odds — failed payments
  recovered, prime-time utilization %.)*
- Never let Shorts/clips views share a bucket with long-form anywhere in reporting (~100x RPM
  difference) (see business-scaling/09-kpis-dashboards-metrics-management.md).

## LATER — 6–18 months out

- **Shift revenue mix up the recurrence ladder.** Creator average is ~31% sponsorship / ~18%
  subscription; every point moved from sponsor to subscription (Substack paid tier, memberships,
  Neptune remote programming) is a point moved from fragile to durable. Enforce the 15% cap: no
  single sponsor over 15% of revenue (see business-scaling/08-productization-scalable-models.md).
- **Neptune remote/hybrid programming** is the product that survives summer (when athletes play
  outside and indoor revenue craters) and scales past the walls — the Driveline retention engine.
  Build it once the in-facility assessment→program→reassessment loop has receipts.
- **License the curriculum only after receipts.** The Gym Launch arc is do-it-yourself → do-for-others
  → teach → license. A proven Neptune assessment protocol + coach training is licensable to other
  facilities in year 2–3, never at launch.
- **Mayday Studio productization stays parked** until other creator teams are asking to pay
  (external-pull test). If that day comes, budget for rebuilding 60–80% of it (onboarding, docs,
  permissions, billing) and accept the identity shift it implies. Middle path if pull appears early:
  white-label to 2–3 peer teams before any product-org commitment.
- **Second Neptune location:** not until location #1 is *boring* — 12+ months positive cash flow,
  churn <5%, 85%+ prime capacity with waitlists, SOPs documented, and a GM running it without Trevor.
  Then cluster (within staff/reputation transfer distance), never jump metros
  (see business-scaling/12-growth-strategy-expansion.md).
- **Integrator/COO conversation** opens at ~10–25 total heads across both businesses, or the moment
  Trevor is the only cross-business coordinator. Note the 40% first-year Integrator failure rate:
  promote a proven operator or go fractional first, and write Trevor's *future* role (Visionary job
  description) before any handoff.

## Standing rules (Carl will enforce these in every session)

1. **One expansion bet at a time.** Neptune is the slot. New ideas take the stop-doing test: name
   what gets killed to fund it, or the answer is no.
2. **Committed vs. aspirational, always tagged.** Rent-critical = committed (binary, post-mortem on
   miss). Growth swings = aspirational (0.7 is a win).
3. **No goal scores wired to comp.** Instant sandbagging otherwise.
4. **Exploit before elevate.** No new hire or purchase for a bottleneck until the exploitation
   checklist (starvation, delegable work, rework, interruptions) comes back clean.
5. **The founder's calendar is the real strategy.** Quarterly calendar audit against stated
   priorities; the gap is the first agenda item.
6. **Charge internally at market rates.** Neptune buys Mayday's content engine on paper; the app is a
   holdco asset with allocated cost. Truth in the P&Ls beats warm feelings about synergy.
7. **Systemize the container, protect the core.** SOPs and automations for pipeline/finance/ops;
   principles + exemplars (never checklists) for taste, packaging, and coaching cues.

## What I'd need to know (facts that would change this advice)

1. **Actual revenue by stream and total** (AdSense / sponsorship / Substack / Fourthwall / Twitch /
   Stripe). The stall-point playbook differs sharply below vs. above ~$1M, and the Profit First TAPs
   band depends on it.
2. **Neptune's committed numbers:** target footprint (sq ft), lease vs. buy, all-in budget, launch
   date, financing already secured, and how much (if any) is pre-sold. Everything in §5 assumes a
   leased warehouse conversion in the $300–600K envelope.
3. **Current cash position and reserve months** — determines whether the buildout needs external
   financing at all and how aggressive the tranche gates must be.
4. **Team census:** who is W-2 vs. contractor, tenure, income-share exposure per freelancer, and who
   (if anyone) could hold the Neptune GM or Mayday producer seats internally.
5. **Sponsor concentration:** largest sponsor as % of trailing-12 revenue, and current payment terms
   per deal. Above 30% changes the reserve sizing and the urgency of subscription mix.
6. **Trevor's honest hours split** — content / app development / Neptune buildout / admin, per week.
   The app-building line especially: if it's >8 hrs/week, we need the tools-built-vs-hours-saved audit.
7. **Local market data for Neptune:** drive-time radius counts (travel orgs, registrations), top-zip
   household income, and competitor prime-time booking density. The whole facility model keys off this.
8. **Whether entity separation already exists** (one LLC or several, whose name is on what), and
   whether the personal guarantee question on the lease has been faced.
9. **The trademark/IP home:** where the More Mayday brand, channel, and content library legally live
   today.
10. **Trevor's own intent:** is Mayday Studio a moat, a future product, or a hobby with commit
    history? The answer changes where its hours sit on the DRIP matrix.
