# Instagram Organic Growth (2025–2026)

## TL;DR

- **Instagram is now two separate games**: unconnected reach (Reels, Explore, SEO — how strangers find you) and connected depth (Stories, broadcast channels, DMs — how followers become customers). Run distinct content strategies for each; most accounts fail by playing one game with the other's content.
- **Three metrics decide distribution** (Mosseri, Jan 2025): watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach. For reaching non-followers, **sends (DM shares) per reach is the single most weighted signal**. Design content people forward to a specific friend.
- **Format roles are settled**: Reels = reach (~30% avg reach rate), Carousels = engagement + saves (0.55% ER, highest of any format), Stories = retention/community (only 1–8% of followers see them — that's fine, they're your warmest audience), Images = lowest everything (0.37% ER, down 17% YoY).
- **Baseline engagement is structurally declining** (avg 0.48% ER, down ~24% YoY; follower growth rates roughly halved across every size tier in 2025). Don't let a client panic-diagnose a platform-wide decline as a personal shadowban.
- **Posting frequency has a real dose-response**: Buffer's 2.1M-post study shows 3–5 feed posts/week ≈ 2× the follower growth of 1–2/week; 10+/week ≈ 5.5×. Biggest marginal gain is moving from 1–2 to 3–5. Zero-post weeks actively cost growth.
- **DM automation is the highest-leverage conversion tool on the platform**: 80–90% open rates, 20–28% CTR (vs 2–3% email). Comment-keyword → automated DM funnels convert 85% of commenters to registrants in documented cases (Porterfield, Kutcher). Strict Meta rules apply (24-hour window, 200 DMs/hr).
- **Hashtags are dead as a growth lever; keywords are not.** Caption/bio/on-screen keywords drive Instagram search, and since July 2025 public professional-account content is indexed by Google. Write captions like meta descriptions: keywords in the first 125 characters.

## 1. Mental model: how distribution actually works now

Instagram is **multiple independent ranking systems**, one per surface (Feed, Reels tab, Stories tray, Explore, Search). A piece of content is scored separately for each surface it's eligible for. There is no single "the algorithm."

### Connected vs. unconnected reach

Mosseri's own framing, and the most useful strategic split:

- **Connected reach** — people who follow you. Ranked heavily on *relationship signals*: how often this person interacts with you (DMs, story replies, profile visits, likes). Stories and Feed are dominated by connected reach.
- **Unconnected reach** — people who don't follow you (Reels tab, Explore, Search, recommendations in Feed). Instagram has no relationship data, so it relies almost entirely on **content-level signals**: watch time, send rate, save rate, early engagement velocity.

**Implication**: a follower who never opens your Stories is functionally a non-follower. And a Reel's performance with strangers says nothing about your relationship with your community. Diagnose reach problems by surface, not in aggregate.

### The three metrics that matter (confirmed by Mosseri, Jan 2025)

1. **Watch time** — #1 for initial distribution of Reels. The 0–3 second hold is the gate; if viewers bail before 3s, nothing else matters.
2. **Likes per reach** — matters more for *connected* distribution (showing followers more of you).
3. **Sends per reach** — matters more for *unconnected* distribution. A DM share is the strongest possible endorsement signal ("this is worth a specific person's attention"). Buffer's analysis and Instagram's own guidance both identify sends as the most heavily weighted Reels signal.

Practical translation: for growth content, the design question isn't "will people like this?" — it's **"who would someone send this to, and why?"** (relatable pain, insider humor for a niche, genuinely useful save/send reference).

### What suppresses reach (the real "shadowban")

Instagram denies shadowbans as users imagine them; what exists is **recommendability**. Content can be fully visible to followers but excluded from Explore/Reels recommendations if it trips recommendation guidelines: engagement bait ("comment YES if..."), misleading claims, borderline sexual content, watermarks from other apps (TikTok logos demonstrably downrank), reposted/aggregated non-original content, and spam patterns (mass follow/unfollow, engagement pods, 30-hashtag stuffing, bot activity). Check Account Status → "Content you can't recommend" before assuming anything mystical.

### Account-type neutrality

Business, creator, and personal accounts get identical algorithmic treatment. Switching account type neither helps nor hurts reach — pick based on tooling needs (API access for DM automation requires Business/Creator).

## 2. Format roles — the job of each surface

| Format | Primary job | Avg engagement rate (2025–26, Socialinsider) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reels | Non-follower reach | 0.50–0.52% ER; ~30% reach rate | ~45% more comments than carousels; reach machine |
| Carousels | Follower engagement + saves | 0.55% ER (highest) | Highest saves of any format; also earns Reels-feed distribution if audio added |
| Stories | Retention, community, conversion | n/a (reach: 1–10% of followers) | Seen mostly by existing followers; the trust surface |
| Images | Filler | 0.37% ER (−17% YoY) | Lowest everything; use only for grid aesthetics/announcements |
| Broadcast channel | Owned-ish audience, superfans | n/a (opt-in) | Push notification to subscribers; no algorithm between you and them |
| DMs | Conversion | 80–90% open rate | Where money actually changes hands |

**The funnel reading**: Reels acquire → Carousels + Feed deepen → Stories retain and warm → Broadcast channel/DMs convert. Every audit should map the client's content against this and find the missing stage. The most common gap: all reach, no retention layer (viral Reels, dead Stories, no DM capture — followers accumulate but never monetize).

### Context benchmarks by account size (Socialinsider 2025–26, brand accounts)

- **Overall ER trending down**: 0.48% average, −24% YoY. Structural (saturation + engagement shifting private into DMs/shares), not a bug.
- **Follower growth rates 2024 → 2025**: 1–5K accounts: 38% → 22%; 5–10K: 35% → 20%; 10–50K: 34% → 17%; 50–100K: 30% → 14%; 100K–1M: 27% → 11%. Roughly halved across the board — set client expectations accordingly.
- **Median views for a 10–50K account**: Reels ~2,460, Carousels ~4,275, Images ~2,340. (Note carousels out-viewing reels at brand accounts — carousel re-serving of unseen slides inflates this, but it's real distribution.)
- **Saves for a 50–100K account**: Carousels ~35 vs Reels ~22 vs Images ~10 per post. Carousels are the save format.
- Brands posted 33% more Reels YoY in 2025; typical brand mix is now ~8 Reels / 5 carousels / 7 images per month.

## 3. Reels playbook (the reach engine)

### Anatomy of a Reel that travels

1. **0–3 seconds**: visual + textual hook. State the payoff or the tension immediately. Instagram heavily weights the 3-second hold for further distribution.
2. **Text on first frame**: ~50% of video is watched muted (some studies say 85% for Stories). Every Reel needs on-screen text that works with sound off — this text is also now an SEO signal (Instagram reads on-screen text and speech).
3. **Length**: 30–90 seconds is the engagement sweet spot. Reels up to 3 minutes are now eligible for non-follower recommendation, but shorter still outperforms for completion rate; only go long when the content demands it.
4. **Send-worthiness**: build in the "tag/send a friend who..." moment *implicitly* (relatable specificity) — explicit "tag a friend" text is engagement bait and can suppress recommendation.
5. **Audio**: trending audio still boosts discovery (Instagram tracks audio momentum); original audio is fine for talking-head content.
6. **No third-party watermarks**: TikTok logos are confirmed downranking triggers. Re-export clean.
7. **Momentum stacking**: after a Reel outperforms, post a follow-up within 1–2 days — the algorithm extends the audience it just built for you.

### Trial Reels (launched Dec 2024 — use them aggressively)

- Available to public accounts with 1,000+ followers. Toggle "Trial" before posting → the Reel is shown **only to non-followers** (followers won't see it in Feed/Reels tab).
- ~24 hours later you get views/likes/comments/shares vs. your previous trials. You can manually "share with everyone," or enable **auto-share** (Instagram promotes it to followers automatically if 72-hour performance is strong). Since Feb 2026, trial reels can be scheduled — batch a week of tests in one sitting.
- Instagram's own data: 40% of creators who try trials post more Reels, and 80% of those see increased non-follower reach.
- **Strategic uses**: (a) test new formats/topics without training followers to expect them, (b) test hooks — same content, different first 3 seconds, (c) de-risk pivots (e.g., a media brand testing a new vertical, or a facility testing training content vs. entertainment content, before committing the main grid).

### Reels cadence

4–7 Reels/week for growth mode; minimum 3/week to stay in the recommendation pool. Pair with the Buffer frequency data (§8): the reach gain from frequency is real but sublinear — never trade hook quality for volume.

## 4. Carousels playbook (the engagement + save engine)

- **Highest ER of any format (0.55%) and the strongest save rates** — and saves are a heavyweight ranking signal, especially for Explore.
- **Second-serve mechanic**: if a follower doesn't engage with slide 1, Instagram can re-serve the post starting at slide 2. Carousels effectively get two at-bats in Feed. Make slide 2 a second hook, not a continuation that assumes slide 1 was read.
- **Add audio/music to a carousel** → it becomes eligible for the Reels feed. Free incremental distribution; do it by default.
- Structure that works: Slide 1 = bold claim/hook (readable at thumbnail size), Slides 2–8 = one idea per slide, escalating, final slide = CTA (save this / comment keyword / follow for X).
- **Carousel dimensions**: build at 3:4 (1080×1440) since the 2025 grid change (see §9) — 3:4 renders uncropped both in feed and on the profile grid.
- Best uses: educational breakdowns, step-by-step, before/after sequences, photo dumps (personal brands), case-study storytelling. Carousels are the format where expertise-led brands (coaching, training, B2B, local services) over-index.

## 5. Stories as the retention & community engine

**The reframe Carl should push**: Stories are not a reach channel and never will be — Mosseri has confirmed Stories are ranked for existing relationships, not discovery. Only **1–8% of followers** watch a typical account's Stories. That sliver is your warmest audience: the people who buy, show up, and refer. Stories are a retention/CRM surface wearing a content costume.

### Stories ranking signals

Tray position per viewer is predicted from: how often *that viewer* watches your stories, DM/reply history, and predicted likelihood of tap/reply vs. skip. **Story replies and DMs are the most valued signal** — every reply you provoke moves you left in that person's tray permanently. This compounds: interactive stories → replies → better tray position → more views → more replies.

### Michelle Gifford's 50-30-20 framework (Social Media Examiner, 2025)

- **50% Value** — teach, demonstrate, answer questions, tips tied to your niche.
- **30% Engagement** — polls, emoji sliders, question boxes, "DM me X" prompts. These exist to farm the reply/interaction signal *and* to do market research.
- **20% Connection** — behind-the-scenes, personal moments, opinions, failures. This is what makes followers feel they know you; it's the trust layer that converts.

Gifford's example weekly rhythm: Sunday question box → Monday answer the questions (value from engagement) → Tuesday value tied to your long-form content → Wednesday product/offer highlight → Thursday connection content. She tripled her own story views on this system.

### Daily story mechanics (numbers)

- **Frequency**: 5–7 story frames/day, 5–6 days/week is the practitioner consensus for engaged accounts; Socialinsider's brand data suggests smaller accounts (1–5K) can hold reach with ~3/week scaling to 2–3/day at 50K+. Post frames in a clustered session (a narrative arc), not scattered singles all day.
- **Exit-rate curve**: slides 1–3 have the highest drop-off (≈24% → 18.5% exit), stabilizing ~13–15% through slide 9. **Your first three frames carry the sequence** — front-load the hook.
- **Optimal sequence length**: reach holds well through ~6–13 frames and declines after 13. 5–10 frames is the working range; 20-frame days train people to skip you.
- **Video > image** at every account size for reach and hold (image stories get 56–67% tap-forward vs 50–62% for video). But keep videos 20–30 seconds per frame, captioned (≈85% watch muted).
- **Story reset**: after a 24-hour gap with no stories, the first story back typically sees roughly doubled engagement. Deliberate 1-day pauses are a legitimate tactic, and it means a missed day isn't a catastrophe.

### Sticker tactics

- Polls: include a low-commitment 4th option ("just show me the answers") — it captures lurkers and still counts as an interaction.
- Question boxes weekly: engagement + a free content-ideas pipeline + voice-of-customer research.
- **Always close the loop**: share poll results and best question-box answers the next day. It rewards participants and creates tomorrow's content.
- "Add Yours" stickers are the only story element with meaningful viral spillover.

### Selling in Stories — the 3-slide conversion sequence (Gifford)

1. **Slide 1** (image, not video): name the audience's pain point precisely.
2. **Slide 2**: deliver a standalone piece of value — a real answer, not a teaser.
3. **Slide 3**: CTA — "DM me the word ___ and I'll send you ___." Routing to DMs (not link stickers) both converts better and feeds the ranking signal. Wire the keyword to DM automation (§7).

Run a sell sequence 1–2× per week inside the 50/30/20 mix; daily pitching burns the tray position you built.

## 6. Broadcast channels (the owned-ish audience layer)

One-to-many opt-in group chat in the DM inbox: you post text, voice notes, photos, polls, prompts; subscribers can react/vote/reply (nested replies since Dec 2024) but not post. Messages hit the DM inbox — **no algorithm between you and the subscriber**, which makes this the closest thing to email that lives inside Instagram.

### Why it matters

Stories reach 1–8% of followers; a broadcast message reaches everyone subscribed with a notification. It's a superfan filter — small numbers, extreme intent. Even large brands run channels of only a few thousand: Shake Shack ~4,000 members; Cava's "Tzatziki Town Hall" ~2,400. They treat them as insight panels and VIP rooms, not reach channels.

### What brands actually do in them (Marketing Brew, Oct 2025)

- **Product feedback loops**: Shake Shack polls members on burger names and menu innovations; Tony's Chocolonely crowdsourced a chocolate-bar name; 818 Spirits gauges demand for product variants; Cocokind asks for product feedback.
- **VIP access**: early launches and previews (Parke, Set Active), exclusive codes, giveaways ("we can afford a bigger prize because it's a smaller group — and they appreciate it more" — Shake Shack), in-person event invites.
- **Niche segmentation**: Netflix runs multiple channels for specific fandoms rather than one megachannel.

### Playbook

1. Create from DMs (pencil icon → Create Broadcast Channel); choose all-followers or paid-subscribers audience. Promote it via a story with the join link + a pinned post; Instagram auto-prompts followers once when you first post.
2. Pin a welcome message stating what subscribers get and how often.
3. **Cadence: 2–4 messages/week.** Under that, people forget they joined; over that, they mute. Mix formats (text, voice note, poll, photo). Voice notes over-index for parasocial connection — cheap and unfaked.
4. Use **Reminders** (attachable to posts/Reels) to drive event/launch attendance — subscribers get pinged at 15 min / 3 min / live.
5. Recurring rituals (AMA Fridays, weekly wins, "vote on next week's video topic") beat sporadic announcements.
6. Include external links regularly — this is your bridge from rented audience to owned (newsletter, community, booking page). Analytics are thin (a known weakness — even Shake Shack complains), so tag links with UTMs.

**Fit check**: broadcast channels are worth it once an account has a genuinely engaged core (watch story-viewer counts, not follower counts). For a creator-led media brand or a local training facility, a channel is the natural home for drop announcements, session availability, and insider votes.

## 7. DM automation (the conversion engine)

DMs are where Instagram monetizes for creators/SMBs. The core play is **comment-to-DM**: post says "comment WORD," a tool (ManyChat et al., via the official Instagram Graph API) detects the comment and instantly DMs the resource/link, usually capturing email en route.

### Why it beats everything else

| Metric | Instagram DM | Email |
|---|---|---|
| Open rate | 80–90% | 20–35% |
| CTR | 20–28% | 2–3% |
| Reply rate | 50–60% | <10% |

- The comment itself is engagement that boosts the post's distribution — **the funnel feeds the reach engine that feeds the funnel.**
- **Amy Porterfield**: 85% of commenters registered for her masterclass (vs. a typical 30–40% landing-page conversion); 83% of signups over 10 months came via DM automation, zero ad spend.
- **Jenna Kutcher** (ManyChat case study): 12,489 webinar signups, 55.6% from chat funnels; comment-to-registration at 85%; ad-driven webinar signup conversion rose from 30% to 75% when routed through DMs; ~$900K attributed launch revenue.
- ManyChat claims $65M+ in attributed sales across digital-product/e-comm customers.

### The rules (Meta policy — violating these gets accounts restricted)

- Business or Creator account required; tools must use **official OAuth API** (never give a tool your password).
- **24-hour messaging window**: automated messages only to users who engaged (comment/reply/DM) within the past 24 hours.
- **200 automated DMs/hour hard cap** (since Oct 2024); one automated DM per user per 24h from comment/story triggers; new accounts should ramp gradually (<100/day at first).
- Identical messages at scale trigger spam detection — rotate 3–5 template variations. Offer a "STOP" opt-out.

### Funnel design

1. **Trigger**: one memorable keyword per campaign ("PLAYBOOK", "SPEED", "TRYOUT"). Announce it in the Reel's spoken content, on-screen text, caption, and pinned comment.
2. **Instant DM**: deliver exactly what was promised + one tappable button. No bait-and-switch — the promised thing must be one tap away.
3. **Email capture**: ask for email *inside the DM thread* to deliver the asset ("where should I send it?"). This is the step that converts rented audience to owned; Kutcher/Porterfield-style funnels live or die here.
4. **Follow-up**: one contextual follow-up within the 24h window ("did you get a chance to look? the workshop is Thursday"). Then human/AI takeover for questions; escalate real buying conversations to a person.
5. Also wire triggers to **story replies** (the 3-slide sell sequence in §5) and **DM the word X** CTAs in broadcast channels.

Tools: ManyChat (from ~$15/mo, market standard), Chatfuel, Respond.io. Differences are mostly pricing model and AI follow-up quality.

## 8. Non-follower reach mechanics — the full checklist

How strangers find you, ranked by leverage:

1. **Reels recommendations** (Reels tab + recommended posts in Feed) — driven by watch time, sends/reach, saves/reach, early velocity. The main engine. Use trial reels to test without follower risk.
2. **Explore** — popularity-velocity weighted more than any other surface (likes/comments/shares/saves accumulating fast); save-heavy carousels do disproportionately well here.
3. **Instagram Search / SEO** — Instagram ranks by **keywords in captions, bio, alt text, on-screen text, and speech**, not hashtags. Put the primary keyword in the first 1–2 lines of the caption, in your name field ("Name | What You Do" — the name field is searchable), and write alt text on every image.
4. **Google indexing (since July 10, 2025)** — public content from professional accounts (posts, Reels, captions, alt text) is indexed by Google/Bing. Captions now function as meta descriptions; the first ~125 characters can appear as the Google snippet. For local businesses (e.g., a training facility), this makes Instagram posts rank for "[service] + [city]" queries — treat captions like micro landing pages.
5. **Hashtags** — demoted to minor search metadata. Hashtag *following* was removed Dec 2024. Use 3–5 relevant ones max; zero is defensible. Never 30.
6. **Sends & tags from your community** — every share is unconnected reach you didn't pay for; collabs (co-authored posts) put the post natively in both audiences' feeds and are the highest-ROI cross-promotion mechanic on the platform.
7. **Recommendability hygiene** — check Account Status; avoid engagement bait, watermarks, reposted content, and borderline topics that quietly remove you from all of the above.

### Posting frequency (Buffer, 2.1M posts / 102K accounts, 2025–26)

| Feed posts/week | Follower growth/week | vs. 1–2/week | Reach per post |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | negative (−0.08 SD below baseline) | — | — |
| 1–2 | +0.12% | 1× | baseline |
| 3–5 | +0.26% | ~2.2× | +12% |
| 6–9 | +0.44% | ~3.7× | +18% |
| 10+ | +0.66% | ~5.5× | +24% |

Reads: (1) frequency helps *per post*, not just in aggregate — more at-bats also improve the average at-bat; (2) the biggest jump is 1–2 → 3–5, so that's the first prescription for any under-posting client; (3) quality still gates everything — volume of weak hooks compounds nothing. Space posts out; back-to-back posting suppresses feed delivery. Buffer's timing data favors mid-week (Wed 12pm/6pm, Thu 9am) but per-account Insights beat generic windows.

## 9. Grid & profile strategy

The profile is the conversion page: a visitor decides to follow (or not) in **under ~7 seconds**. Reach content (Reels) earns the profile visit; the profile closes it.

### 2025 grid changes that alter the playbook

- **Profile grid thumbnails changed from 1:1 square to 3:4 vertical** (early 2025) — the biggest grid change in the platform's history. Legacy square posts get cropped on the grid. **Create everything at 3:4 (1080×1440)** — it's the only ratio that renders uncropped in both feed and grid; Reels covers should place key text in the 3:4 safe zone.
- **Grid reorder**: you can now drag-and-drop reorder existing grid posts without losing engagement or reposting. The grid is no longer chronological-by-force — it's a curated storefront. Front-load offers, proof, and current launches at will.

### Profile conversion stack (top to bottom)

1. **Name field**: "Name | searchable keyword" (e.g., "Neptune | Baseball Player Development"). Searchable; the handle alone is wasted SEO.
2. **Bio**: who it's for + outcome + one CTA. Not a mission statement. One link (or link-in-bio page) mapped to the current priority; "DM me WORD" as a secondary CTA works better than a second link.
3. **Pinned posts (3)**: the manual merchandising slots — (a) best-performing "what we do" Reel, (b) proof/results/testimonial carousel, (c) current offer or start-here post. Refresh with launches.
4. **Highlights**: 4–6 max, treated as a mini website nav: Start Here, Results/Proof, Offer/Services, FAQ, About/BTS. Custom covers; ruthless pruning of stale ones.
5. **Grid (first 9–12 posts)**: with reorder, curate the visible window. Column layout (repeating Education / Proof / Offer columns) suits expertise sellers; checkerboard (photo/graphic alternating) suits product brands; personal brands can stay looser but should keep face-forward covers — faces stop the scroll on a grid too.

### Brand account vs. personal brand

- **Personal brand**: face on most covers, first-person captions, connection content on the grid is allowed (it's the differentiator). Recognizability > polish; over-designed templates actively hurt perceived authenticity.
- **Company brand**: consistency system (2–3 cover templates, fixed palette/type), but 2025-era best practice is to look *less* like a brand — founder/staff faces, lo-fi BTS, and customer-content perform better than studio polish. For a two-sided business (e.g., media brand + facility), keep separate accounts per audience; a mixed grid confuses the follow decision and the algorithm's audience model alike.

## 10. Operating cadence — a weekly system Carl can prescribe

For a small team (1–2 people on social, ~4–6 hrs/week):

- **Reels**: 3–5/week, at least 1 run as a trial reel testing a new hook/format. Batch-shoot one session/week.
- **Carousels**: 1–2/week (educational/proof), built at 3:4 with audio attached.
- **Stories**: 5–7 frames/day, 5–6 days/week on the 50/30/20 mix; question box weekly; 3-slide sell sequence 1–2×/week wired to a DM keyword.
- **Broadcast channel**: 2–4 messages/week — one poll/vote, one insider update, one link out to owned property.
- **DM automation**: one live keyword campaign at all times; review flows monthly; capture email inside every flow.
- **Monthly review**: sends/reach and saves/reach on Reels (not likes), story completion + reply counts, profile visits → follows conversion, DM keyword → email conversion, channel membership growth. Reorder grid + refresh pinned posts on every launch.

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Diagnosing the 2025 platform-wide engagement decline as a personal failure** (or shadowban) and thrashing strategy. Check benchmarks by size tier first.
2. **Chasing reach with Stories or engagement with Reels** — using formats against their role. Stories don't grow audiences; Reels don't build trust by themselves.
3. **Optimizing likes instead of sends/saves.** Likes are the vanity signal; sends drive unconnected reach, saves drive Explore.
4. **A weak first 3 seconds** — the single most common Reel failure. Test hooks via trial reels, not opinions.
5. **Engagement bait** ("comment YES!", "tag 3 friends!") — actively suppresses recommendation. Keyword CTAs tied to a real deliverable ("comment GUIDE and I'll send it") are the compliant version.
6. **Hashtag stuffing / hashtag-first strategy** in a keyword-first era. Also: skipping alt text and caption keywords now that Google indexes content.
7. **TikTok watermarks** on cross-posted Reels — confirmed downranking.
8. **20-frame story days and scattered singles** — exit-rate data says 5–10 clustered frames; the first 3 frames decide the sequence.
9. **No DM/email capture layer** — building reach that dies on the platform. Rented audience with no bridge to owned is the classic creator-business failure mode.
10. **Broadcast channel as a megaphone** — announcement-only channels get muted. Polls, votes, and insider access are the retention mechanics.
11. **Password-based automation tools, engagement pods, bought followers** — spam-detection triggers with account-level consequences. Only OAuth/official-API tools; respect the 200/hr and 24h-window limits.
12. **Square (1:1) creative in a 3:4 grid world** — cropped thumbnails and lost grid real estate.
13. **Posting in bursts then vanishing** — zero-post weeks measurably shrink growth; back-to-back same-hour posts suppress each other.
14. **Judging Stories by view count** rather than replies/completion — 1–8% viewership is normal; the metric is depth, not breadth.

## 12. Questions Carl should ask a client

**Diagnosis**
1. What % of your reach last 30 days came from non-followers? (Insights → Reach) — tells you which game they're losing.
2. What are sends/reach and saves/reach on your top 5 Reels? Anything above ~1% sends/reach is share-worthy DNA worth replicating.
3. What % of followers view a typical Story, and how many *replies* does a day of Stories generate? (Views without replies = broadcasting, not community.)
4. Check Account Status: any "not eligible for recommendation" flags?
5. How many profile visits → how many follows? (The profile-conversion rate; if reach is fine but follows are low, the problem is the grid/bio, not the content.)

**Strategy**
6. Which format role is missing: reach (Reels), depth (carousels/Stories), or conversion (DM/keyword funnels)?
7. Who would send your best content to a friend, and what would they say when they sent it? (If there's no answer, there's no unconnected-reach strategy.)
8. What happens after someone follows? Is there any path from follower → DM → email/owned list?
9. Could you sustain 3–5 feed posts + daily stories for 90 days at current resourcing? (If not, cut formats until the cadence is sustainable — consistency beats bursts.)
10. What keyword would a stranger type to find a business like yours, and does it appear in your name field, bio, and recent captions?

**Community & conversion**
11. Do your 100 truest fans have a home (broadcast channel, close friends, community) or are they treated identically to cold followers?
12. What's the one keyword campaign you could run this month, and what asset does it deliver?
13. Are you testing hooks with trial reels or arguing about them in meetings?

- **Mayday Media (creator/YouTube-first)**: Instagram's job is clip-driven discovery (Reels from YouTube content — re-exported clean, native captions, no watermarks) plus a Stories/broadcast-channel layer that drives episode drops, merch, and newsletter signups.
