# SEO & Search Discoverability (Web + YouTube + AI Search)

## TL;DR

- **Search didn't die; clicks did.** Google still routes ~90% of searches and sent 345x more referral traffic than ChatGPT+Gemini+Perplexity combined (Ahrefs, 82k sites), but AI Overviews now suppress position-1 CTR by ~58% and ~60% of Google searches end with no click. Plan for *visibility and citation*, not just traffic.
- **SEO is a portfolio decision, not a default.** For a local facility (Neptune), local SEO is close to mandatory and pays fast — 76-78% of "near me" mobile searchers visit a business within 24 hours. For a media brand (Mayday), classic blog SEO is usually the *wrong* first investment; YouTube search + brand + AI citations matter more.
- **Local SEO is 80% Google Business Profile + reviews.** Complete profiles make customers 70% more likely to visit and 50% more likely to buy; review *velocity and response rate* beat raw count. A new facility can rank in the local pack within weeks, not the 6-12 months web SEO takes.
- **YouTube search is the most durable creator SEO channel.** Metadata gets a video *considered*; retention, CTR, and watch time make it *rank*. A deliberate slice of evergreen, search-intent videos ("how to increase throwing velocity") compounds for years while browse-driven videos decay in days.
- **AI search (AEO/GEO) converts absurdly well.** AI-referred visitors convert at 5-16% vs ~1.76% organic average (up to 23x in Ahrefs' own data). Citations skew to comparison content, statistics, quotes, fresh pages, and *earned media* — 82% of AI citations are earned, not owned, content.
- **Brand mentions now beat backlinks.** Ahrefs' 75,000-brand study: brand-mention correlation with AI visibility 0.664 vs backlinks 0.218. YouTube presence has the single strongest correlation (~0.737) with AI visibility — a huge structural advantage for a YouTube-first company.
- **Topical authority is the unifying strategy** across Google, YouTube, and LLMs: pick a narrow topic universe you can own, cover it completely with hub-and-spoke clusters, add information gain (original data, first-hand experience), and interlink. Sites with strong topical authority appear in AI Overviews ~3x more often.
- **Budget/timeline honesty:** competent SEO retainers run $1,500-3,000/mo with 6-12 month payback. If a client needs leads in 60 days, the answer is Google Ads + GBP + reviews, not content SEO.

## 1. The 2025-2026 search landscape (what actually changed)

### The numbers that frame every SEO conversation

| Fact | Number | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Google share of worldwide searches | ~90% | Ahrefs |
| Google referral traffic vs ChatGPT+Gemini+Perplexity combined | 345x more (82k-site sample) | Ahrefs |
| AI assistants' share of total website traffic | 0.1-0.5% (but growing fast) | Ahrefs |
| Google searches ending with zero clicks | ~60% (72% when an AI Overview shows) | Multiple studies |
| AI Overviews' presence in US searches | ~16% and roughly doubling year over year | Ahrefs |
| Position-1 CTR suppression when AI Overview present | -58% (Dec 2025; was -34.5% in Apr 2025) | Ahrefs, 300k keywords |
| CTR suppression by position (AIO present) | P1 -58%, P2 -51%, P3 -46%, P5 -33%, P10 -19% | Ahrefs |
| Users clicking any external link when AIO present | 8% vs 15% without | Pew Research, 900 US adults |
| Clicks on a source cited *inside* an AI Overview | ~1% | Pew Research |
| Gen Z searching on TikTok/Instagram instead of Google | ~45% | Ahrefs (citing platform data) |
| New web content created with generative AI | ~74% | Ahrefs (Originality data) |

### What this means in practice

1. **Rankings no longer predict traffic.** You can hold position 1 and lose half your clicks to the AI Overview sitting above you. Measure *impressions, citations, branded-search growth, and conversions* — not just sessions.
2. **Informational content is the most cannibalized.** Definitional, "what is X," simple how-to queries get fully answered in the Overview. Transactional, local, comparison, and tool/calculator queries still earn clicks.
3. **The traffic you do get is higher-intent.** AI-referred visitors convert at 5-16% (ChatGPT 15.9%, Perplexity 10.5%, Claude ~5%) vs ~1.76% organic average. Ahrefs saw AI visitors deliver 12.1% of signups from 0.5% of traffic — a ~24:1 efficiency ratio. Small traffic, disproportionate revenue.
4. **Search behavior fragmented.** Google + YouTube + TikTok + ChatGPT + Perplexity + Reddit are all "search engines" now. The right question is no longer "how do we rank on Google" but "where does *our specific buyer* ask *this specific question*, and are we the answer there?"

**Carl's framing:** SEO in 2026 = *discoverability engineering*. One body of authoritative work, optimized for four surfaces: Google web, Google local, YouTube, and LLM answers. The tactics differ per surface; the underlying asset (topical authority + brand) is shared.

## 2. Decision framework: when SEO is worth it

### The four-question qualifier

Before recommending SEO spend, answer:

1. **Is there existing search demand?** If nobody searches for the problem you solve (new category, novel product), SEO can't harvest demand that doesn't exist — that's a job for social/video/PR (demand *creation*). Check with a keyword tool: if the whole topic universe is <5-10k monthly searches, SEO is a side dish.
2. **Can you afford the timeline?** Content SEO: 3 months of coverage-building, ranking movement at months 4-6, real payoff months 6-12. Local SEO: weeks. Google Ads: day one. Businesses needing revenue in <90 days should buy ads first and build SEO behind them.
3. **Can you realistically win?** Look at who ranks now. If the SERP is Healthline/Wirecutter/decade-old competitors with big content teams, a small business fights for scraps. Look for *whitespace*: queries where no strong competitor ranks top 5 — you can own those by default. Long-tail + local + niche-specific beats broad head terms every time ("baseball velocity program for 14u pitchers near Nashville" beats "baseball training").
4. **Does the economics support it?** Competent retainers run $1,500-3,000/month; DIY costs founder time. LTV must justify it. A facility selling $200+/mo memberships with multi-year retention clears that bar easily; a $15 merch item does not.

### Media brand vs local facility — different answers

**Local facility (Neptune Performance type):**
- Local SEO is near-mandatory and cheap. 46% of all queries have local intent; 80% of US consumers search for local businesses weekly; 76-78% of nearby mobile searchers visit within 24 hours; local searches convert vastly better than display advertising.
- The work is bounded: GBP + reviews + citations + a handful of local pages. A solo owner can execute the core in ~20 hours plus ongoing review hygiene.
- Blog-style content SEO is *optional* for a facility — useful for building trainer authority and feeding AI answers, but the local pack is where the members come from.

**Media/creator brand (Mayday type):**
- Traditional website SEO is usually a *poor* first investment: the product is video, audience lives on platforms, and informational blog traffic is exactly the traffic AI Overviews eat.
- The high-ROI search plays for a media brand are: (a) **YouTube search** (section 5), (b) **branded search protection** — own your name's SERP with site, channel, socials, so sponsors and fans doing due diligence find you, not a competitor or stale press, (c) **AI citation presence** (section 6) — sponsors and partners increasingly "ask ChatGPT" about creators and niches, and (d) a lean site with the pages money touches: sponsorship/media kit page, about, key products.
- Non-branded blog SEO for a media company only makes sense once the site has a conversion path (newsletter signup, merch, facility leads) that justifies it.

### Branded vs non-branded

- Branded searches convert at multiples of non-branded; a growth-mode company typically wants a 60/40-70/30 split favoring non-branded *content* while never losing the branded SERP.
- Watch **branded search volume as a KPI** — it's the best free proxy for brand growth and correlates with AI visibility. Great content/video/PR grows it; SEO merely harvests it.
- Defensive rule: rank #1 for your own name + "reviews," "pricing," "vs" variants before a competitor or affiliate does.

## 3. Core small-business web SEO playbook

### The topical authority model (the master framework)

Topical authority = a site's demonstrated, comprehensive expertise on a *specific* subject as recognized by search engines and LLMs. It's topic-scoped, not site-wide — which is precisely why small focused sites outrank giant generalists. Three pillars:

1. **Breadth** — cover every meaningful subtopic in the niche.
2. **Depth** — each page genuinely answers, with original insight.
3. **Structure** — pillar pages + cluster pages + deliberate internal links.

Sites with strong topical authority show up in AI Overviews ~3x more often and gain traffic ~57% faster — this framework is the shared foundation for Google *and* LLM visibility.

**7-step build (Keyword Insights framework, condensed):**

1. **Define the core topic domain.** Narrow enough to dominate, broad enough for 30+ subtopics, aligned with what the business actually sells and genuinely knows. (For a baseball facility: "youth baseball player development," not "fitness.")
2. **Map the topic universe.** People Also Ask, Reddit/Quora threads, competitor sitemaps, YouTube search suggestions, customer questions from sales calls. Aim for an exhaustive question list before writing anything.
3. **Cluster keywords by SERP overlap,** not by semantic similarity. If two keywords share 70%+ of their top-ranking URLs, they share intent → one page. (Benchmark: SERP-based clustering tools scored 70-95/100 accuracy vs 33-47 for pure semantic/AI clustering.)
4. **Architect hub-and-spoke.** One pillar page per major theme (comprehensive guide), 4-15 cluster pages per pillar on subtopics, all interlinked. Example: Pillar "Pitching velocity development" → clusters on mechanics, weighted balls, mobility, in-season vs off-season, age guidelines, common injuries.
5. **Gap analysis with priority tiers:**
   - *Critical:* volume >1,000, difficulty <40, multiple competitors ranking → build now.
   - *Whitespace:* any volume, no strong competitor in top 5 → own by default, high priority.
   - *Opportunity:* volume 500-1,000, difficulty 40-60 → next quarter.
   - *Low:* volume <100 + difficulty >60 → monitor only.
   Also: before creating a new page, check if an existing top-3 page overlaps >45% with the gap keyword — expand it instead (avoids cannibalization).
6. **Write for information gain.** The market is drowning in AI-consensus content (74% of new content is AI-generated). The only durable moat is what a model can't synthesize: your data, your client results, your gym-floor observations, your opinionated frameworks. Litmus test per page: "Is there anything here a reader can't find anywhere else?"
7. **Internal linking discipline.** Pillar → all clusters; clusters → pillar + related clusters; descriptive anchor text; zero orphan pages. Established pages pass authority to new ones — link new content from your strongest pages at publish time.

### Search intent — the single most common failure point

Every keyword has a dominant intent visible in what already ranks: informational, commercial-comparison, transactional, local, or video. **Match the format the SERP rewards.** If the top 10 are product pages, a blog post will not rank no matter how good. If the SERP shows a map pack, you need GBP, not an article. If video carousels dominate, make the video.

### Technical minimum (don't over-invest here)

For a <500-page small-business site, technical SEO is a checklist, not a program: fast mobile load (60%+ of traffic is mobile), HTTPS, one indexable URL per page, XML sitemap, sensible titles/H1s/meta descriptions, image alt text, schema markup (LocalBusiness, Organization, FAQ, VideoObject where relevant), no accidental noindex. Do it once, audit twice a year, spend the rest of the budget on content and links.

### Links & digital PR (2026 flavor)

- Classic guest-post link building is low-yield. What works: **being citable** — original stats, local sponsorships, event partnerships, expert quotes to journalists, niche directory presence.
- The strategic shift: brand *mentions* (linked or not) now matter more than raw backlinks for AI visibility (0.664 vs 0.218 correlation), and distributing content across multiple publications increases AI citations up to 325% vs publishing only on your own site. Digital PR is now simultaneously a links play, a brand play, and a GEO play.

### Measurement that survives zero-click

Track: (1) impressions + average position by topic cluster (Search Console), (2) branded search volume trend, (3) share of voice on your top 50 target queries, (4) AI citation checks (ask ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini your top 20 buyer questions monthly, log mentions), (5) conversions from organic + AI referrals — not raw sessions.

## 4. Local SEO (the facility playbook)

### How local ranking works — Google's own three factors

1. **Relevance** — how well the profile matches the query. Driven by categories, services, description, and on-site content.
2. **Distance** — proximity to the searcher. You can't change it; you *can* widen relevance so you appear for more query types within your radius.
3. **Prominence** — reviews (count, rating, velocity, responses), links, citations, general web presence. GBP completeness/activity is estimated to drive ~32% of local ranking power; link signals ~16.5%.

### Google Business Profile: the highest-ROI 10 hours in local marketing

- **Claim + complete everything.** Complete profiles: 2.7x more likely to be considered reputable, 70% more likely to be visited, 50% more likely to convert to purchase.
- **Primary category is the single biggest lever.** Be specific: "Baseball training facility" / "Sports complex" / "Athletic club" — the most precise available category, not generic "Gym." Add 2-4 secondary categories (e.g., batting cage center, sports school, personal trainer).
- **Pre-launch trick:** set the profile live with a "coming soon"/opening date before the facility opens — builds review runway and founding-member demand before day one.
- **Photos:** 20+ authentic photos (facility, equipment, coaches, athletes training — with permission); no stock. Photo-rich profiles get dramatically more engagement.
- **Services & products sections:** list every program (lessons, camps, memberships, assessments, team training) with prices where possible — these are matchable text for queries.
- **Posts:** weekly-ish updates (events, results, offers). Signals an active business.
- **Q&A:** seed it yourself with the 10 questions every parent asks (age ranges, pricing, walk-ins, parking).
- **UTM-tag the website link** so GBP traffic is visible in analytics.

### Reviews: the second ranking factor and the first conversion factor

- 97% of consumers read local reviews; 71% read them on Google; 54% visit the website after reading positive ones.
- **Velocity + response rate > total count.** A steady drip (2-6/month) with owner responses to every review beats a stale pile of 200. Recency is weighted.
- Operationalize it: ask at the moment of a win (post-assessment, post-PR on a velocity test, end of a camp). QR code at the front desk. Text follow-up with direct review link. Respond within 48h to everything, especially negatives (calm, specific, offline-resolution offer).
- Community-style facilities (CrossFit, F45, Orangetheory pattern) sustain the best ratings because reviews are a byproduct of belonging — a facility with a real community has a structural review advantage. Lean into member success stories.
- Never incentivize or gate reviews (against Google policy; filterable and penalizable).

### Citations & NAP

- Name/Address/Phone must be *character-identical* everywhere. Inconsistency erodes Google's trust in the listing. 62% of consumers say they'd avoid a business over incorrect online info.
- Priority citations: Google, Apple Business Connect, Bing Places, Yelp, Facebook, then niche sports/fitness directories and local chamber/city directories. One-time cleanup + annual audit; don't pay monthly for "citation building" beyond that.

### On-site local pages

- One strong location page: full NAP, embedded map, parking/access details, hours, photos, coach bios, programs, FAQ, LocalBusiness schema.
- Geo-modified service pages only where distinct services exist ("pitching lessons in [city]") — never doorway-page spam of every suburb.
- E-E-A-T through people: coach bio pages with credentials and playing/coaching history, athlete success stories, Q&A/video content from the actual trainers. This doubles as GEO fuel (section 6).

### Local links

Sponsorships (Little League teams, tournaments, school programs), event hosting, partnerships with PT clinics/schools, features in "best training facilities in [metro]" roundups, local news for facility opening. Each of these is simultaneously a link, a citation, a brand mention, and often a customer channel.

### The AI wrinkle in local

45% of consumers now use ChatGPT/gen-AI for local business recommendations; AI users compare ~3.7 businesses and 39% click through to a website. AI local recommendations draw heavily on reviews, GBP data, and third-party "best of" content — one more reason review quality and roundup placement matter. (BrightLocal characterizes AI local visibility as far harder to move than map-pack ranking — treat it as a byproduct of doing local SEO well, not a separate program.)

### 90-day local SEO launch checklist (new facility)

- Weeks 1-2: claim/complete GBP (categories, services, photos, description, Q&A), fix NAP everywhere, build location page with schema.
- Weeks 3-4: top-10 citations, Apple/Bing profiles, review-ask system live (QR + text templates), first 10 reviews from founding members.
- Weeks 5-8: coach bio pages, 3-5 program pages targeting "[service] + [city]" queries, first local sponsorship/partnership secured, weekly GBP posts begin.
- Weeks 9-12: pitch local press/roundups, publish 2-3 genuinely useful local-intent articles (e.g., "youth baseball leagues in [metro]: complete guide"), measure map-pack position for top 10 queries via geo-grid tool, review velocity ≥1/week.

## 5. YouTube search optimization

### The two discovery systems — optimize for both, but know which game each video plays

- **Browse/Suggested** (homepage + up-next): powered by satisfaction signals; spiky, packaging-driven, decays fast. Where viral growth lives.
- **Search**: query-driven, evergreen, compounds for years. Where *durable, intent-driven* views live — and increasingly where Google web results and LLMs pull video answers from.

A healthy channel portfolio deliberately allocates a slice (often 20-30%) to search-intent videos even on an entertainment-first channel, because search views are the annuity: they recruit new viewers steadily and are the videos that surface when someone (or something — see §6) asks a question.

### How YouTube search ranks (Backlinko's 1.3M-result analysis + platform statements)

Two stages: **metadata gets you considered; performance gets you ranked.**

Performance signals (dominant): audience retention (YouTube calls it "a HUGE ranking factor"), total watch time, CTR on the impression, comments, shares, likes, subscribes-after-watching. Longer videos consistently outrank shorter ones for search queries (more watch time to accumulate) — this favors thorough tutorials over 4-minute skims. Blunt truth from the data: "if your video stinks, it won't rank, no matter how optimized."

Metadata signals (gatekeeping): title, description, spoken words (YouTube transcribes and understands audio + visuals now), tags (minor), hashtags, thumbnail. Optimized metadata reportedly earns ~3x more impressions over 90 days than poor metadata — because it determines which auctions you even enter.

### Keyword research for YouTube

1. **YouTube autocomplete** — type seed terms, harvest suggestions (these are literal logged queries).
2. **Competitor mining** — sort niche channels' videos by popularity; their top search-intent videos reveal proven demand.
3. **Your own Traffic Source: YouTube Search report** — the queries already finding you are the cheapest expansion targets.
4. **Video keywords on Google** — prefer keywords where Google itself shows video results (how-tos, tutorials, reviews, fitness/sports demos): you get YouTube views *plus* Google SERP placement. Target ≥100-1,000 monthly Google searches as a floor.
5. **Competition check** — `site:youtube.com "keyword"` result counts, or eyeball whether the current top results are weak (old, low-production, mismatched intent).

### Per-video optimization checklist

- **Say the target keyword out loud** in the first 30 seconds (the transcript is metadata).
- **Title:** ≥5 words, keyword near the front, still human/clickable. Front-load the promise.
- **Description:** keyword in the first 25 words; ≥250 words; keyword 2-4x naturally; include timestamps/chapters (chapters can surface as Key Moments in Google).
- **Tags:** exact keyword first, then variations and related topics — minor ranking weight but helps suggested-video association; borrowing competitor tags aids co-suggestion.
- **Thumbnail:** designed for CTR in *search context* (legible at small size, communicates the answer/outcome).
- **Retention engineering:** deliver the promised answer structure early, preview the payoff, cut dead air; for search videos, answer-first beats slow-burn storytelling.
- **Post-publish:** embed in relevant site pages, answer related Quora/Reddit threads with the video contextually, add to a themed playlist of 4-5 related videos (playlists chain watch time).

### Why this matters double for a facility + media hybrid

Search-intent training videos ("how to fix arm slot," "weighted ball program explained") do triple duty: evergreen channel growth, proof-of-expertise for facility leads searching those exact questions locally, and the strongest single input to AI visibility — YouTube mentions carry the highest measured correlation (~0.737) with brand visibility inside LLM answers. A YouTube-first company doing search-optimized expertise content is accidentally doing best-practice GEO.

## 6. AI search optimization (AEO / GEO)

### Vocabulary

- **AEO (Answer Engine Optimization):** structuring content to be the extracted answer — featured snippets, People Also Ask, voice, AI Overviews.
- **GEO (Generative Engine Optimization):** the broader discipline of getting cited/recommended inside LLM-generated answers (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Claude, AI Overviews). Term originates from the Princeton/Georgia Tech "GEO" paper (KDD 2024).
- Reality check: 92% of marketers say they plan GEO work; ~40% actually do any — early-mover space, and early optimizers report 60-80% higher mention rates than unoptimized competitors.

### What the research says actually earns citations

From the Princeton GEO paper and 2025-26 industry datasets:

| Tactic / trait | Measured effect |
|---|---|
| Adding statistics to content | +41% AI visibility |
| Adding quotations (expert/named) | +28% |
| Citing external sources | up to +115% for lower-ranked pages |
| Long, thorough content (20k+ chars) | 4.3x more citations vs thin (<500 chars) |
| Freshness (updated within 30 days) | 3.2x more citations; 76.4% of ChatGPT-cited pages updated in last 30 days |
| Comparison-format articles | 32.5% of all citations — the #1 cited format |
| Opinion/analysis pieces | ~10% of citations |
| Front-loading (LLMs over-sample intros) | 44.2% of citation text comes from the first 30% of a page |
| Earned media (third-party coverage) | 82% of AI citations are earned, not owned, content |
| Multi-publication distribution | up to +325% citations vs own-site-only |
| Brand mentions (vs backlinks) | correlation with AI visibility 0.664 vs 0.218 |
| YouTube presence | strongest single correlate of AI visibility (~0.737) |

### The ranking/citation disconnect — GEO is not just SEO

- 83% of AI Overview citations come from pages ranking *outside* Google's top 10. LLMs pull from Reddit, YouTube, niche blogs, docs, and roundups that never win the classic SERP.
- Platforms barely overlap: only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity; 97% of Perplexity answers carry citations vs ~16% of ChatGPT answers. Perplexity leans heavily recent web; ChatGPT mixes training-data memory + Bing-flavored retrieval; Gemini/AI Overviews lean Google index. Optimize per-platform only if a platform demonstrably drives your buyers; otherwise do the general playbook.
- Channels compound rather than cannibalize: being cited in an AI Overview lifts adjacent organic CTR ~35% (BrightEdge).

### The practical GEO playbook

1. **Be the source of a number.** Publish original stats from your own operation (e.g., a facility's anonymized velocity-gain data across 200 athletes; a media brand's creator-economy survey). Statistics are the single most portable citation bait, and nobody can copy yours.
2. **Own comparison queries.** "X vs Y," "best [category] for [audience]" — honest comparison pages including competitors are the most-cited format. Uncomfortable but effective.
3. **Answer-first page structure.** Direct 2-4 sentence answer at the top of each page/section (LLMs over-sample intros), then depth. Q&A subheads that mirror real question phrasing. FAQ schema.
4. **Freshness cadence.** Quarterly refresh of your top-20 money pages with updated dates + genuinely updated content; ChatGPT's recency bias makes stale pages invisible.
5. **Earned everything.** Podcast guesting, expert quotes to journalists, niche-publication contributions, getting into "best of" roundups — since 82% of citations are earned media, PR *is* GEO. Note the Muck Rack finding: only 2% overlap between journalists PR teams pitch and sources AI actually cites — target the niche/trade publications LLMs demonstrably pull from in your space (check citations in real answers to find them).
6. **Entity hygiene.** Consistent brand name/description across site, GBP, Wikipedia-adjacent sources, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, schema (Organization/Person/LocalBusiness). LLMs resolve entities; ambiguity kills recall.
7. **Don't block the crawlers** (unless strategically intended): GPTBot, PerplexityBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended in robots.txt.
8. **Measure monthly:** run your 20 highest-value buyer questions through ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, and AI-mode Google. Log: mentioned? cited? recommended first? Track AI referral traffic (referrer strings: chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai) and conversion rate separately — it will likely be your highest-converting source.

### GEO for the two business types

- **Facility:** LLM answers to "best baseball training facility near [city]" synthesize GBP data, reviews, and local roundup articles. The GEO play *is* the local SEO play plus getting into every credible "best facilities in [metro]" listicle and having coaches quoted in local/sports media.
- **Media brand:** sponsors and partners increasingly vet via AI ("top [niche] YouTube channels," "who should sponsor X audience"). Being present in creator-economy roundups, press, podcast circuits, and having a crawlable site that states plainly who you are, audience size, and what you offer determines whether the model can recommend you.

## 7. How the surfaces reinforce each other (the compounding loop)

One expertise engine, four outputs:

1. **Expertise content** (topic-cluster site pages + search-intent YouTube videos) builds topical authority.
2. Topical authority → Google rankings → AI Overview inclusion (3x more likely with strong authority) → brand mentions.
3. YouTube presence directly drives LLM visibility (highest correlation of any channel).
4. Brand mentions + reviews + earned media → local prominence + AI citations → more branded search.
5. Branded search + direct traffic → the strongest signals that survive any algorithm change.

The strategic implication Carl should push: **stop funding channels separately.** Every substantive piece (a study, a method, a client result) should ship as: long-form video (search-optimized), site page (answer-first, stats-rich), short-form clips, newsletter item, and a pitch to one external publication. That single workflow feeds Google, YouTube, local prominence, and LLMs simultaneously.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. **Chasing head terms.** Small businesses targeting "baseball training" or "coffee shop" against entrenched sites lose by default. Long-tail + local + whitespace queries are where small players win.
2. **Content without intent-matching.** Writing blog posts for queries whose SERPs show product pages, map packs, or videos. Always check what format ranks before creating.
3. **Publishing AI-consensus content.** 74% of new content is AI-generated sameness; it earns neither rankings nor citations. No original data/experience = no moat.
4. **Measuring sessions in a zero-click world.** Declining traffic with rising impressions, citations, and branded search can be a *winning* position. Wrong KPI → prematurely killed programs.
5. **Ignoring GBP while paying for content SEO.** For any local business, an incomplete Business Profile with 12 stale reviews wastes every other dollar spent.
6. **Review malpractice:** incentivizing/gating reviews (policy violation), ignoring responses, one-time review blitzes instead of steady velocity.
7. **NAP drift** across directories after a move or rebrand — silently tanks local trust.
8. **Doorway/geo-spam pages** ("personal training in [each of 30 suburbs]") — filtered or penalized.
9. **On YouTube: metadata worship.** Perfect tags on a low-retention video does nothing. Retention and CTR are the ranking; metadata is the entry ticket.
10. **On YouTube: 100% browse-bait portfolios.** Zero search-intent videos = zero evergreen annuity and weaker AI visibility.
11. **Treating GEO as a separate paid program.** It's mostly the same authority/PR/freshness work; buying a "GEO tool suite" before doing the fundamentals is theater.
12. **Cannibalization:** multiple pages/videos targeting the same query cluster splitting signals. One asset per intent cluster; expand rather than duplicate (>45% overlap rule).
13. **Quitting at month 4.** Content SEO's payoff curve is back-loaded (months 6-12); the most common failure is stopping right before the compounding starts.
14. **Technical SEO rabbit-holing.** For small sites, technical work past the checklist has near-zero marginal return; the budget belongs in content, reviews, and PR.

## 9. Questions Carl should ask

**Qualifying the investment**
1. What happens if you get zero new customers from search in the next 6 months — is the business fine? (If no → ads first, SEO second.)
2. What's the lifetime value of one customer? What could you rationally pay to acquire one via a channel that takes 6 months to start?
3. When your best customers found you, what did they actually type/ask, and where? (Google? YouTube? TikTok? ChatGPT? A friend?)
4. Search your top 5 money queries right now — who ranks, and can you honestly out-cover them in 12 months?

**Local (facility)**
5. Is your Google Business Profile claimed, and when did you last add a photo or post?
6. How many Google reviews did you get in the last 30 days (not total), and what % have owner responses?
7. What's your exact primary GBP category? What do the top 3 map-pack competitors use?
8. Is your name/address/phone identical on Google, Apple, Yelp, Facebook, and your site footer?
9. What's your systematic review ask — at what moment, by what mechanism?
10. Which local roundups/"best of" lists exist for your category, and are you in them?

**Media brand / YouTube**
11. What % of your channel views come from YouTube Search (check Traffic Sources)? What are the top queries?
12. Do you have any videos designed to answer an evergreen question, or is everything browse-bait?
13. If a sponsor Googles your brand right now, what do the top 10 results say? Anything stale, missing, or off-message?
14. Does your website plainly state who you are, audience size, and what you sell, in crawlable text a model could quote?

**AI search**
15. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity your 5 most valuable buyer questions right now — are you mentioned? Who is, and why (trace the citations)?
16. What proprietary number could you publish this quarter that your whole niche would have to cite?
17. When were your top 10 pages last substantively updated? (>90 days → invisible to recency-biased engines.)
18. What earned-media placements (podcasts, quotes, roundups, trade press) did you land in the last quarter?

**Measurement**
19. Are you tracking impressions, branded search volume, AI referrals, and citation checks — or just sessions?
20. Which single query cluster, if you owned it completely, would most change the business? Why aren't all resources pointed there?
