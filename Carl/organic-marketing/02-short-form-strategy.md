# Short-Form Strategy: Shorts, Reels & TikTok

## TL;DR

- **Short-form is a discovery engine, not a revenue engine.** Shorts RPM is $0.01–$0.10 per 1,000 views vs. $3–$12+ for long-form. Direct short-form ad money is a rounding error; the payoff is audience acquisition that feeds long-form, newsletter, sponsorship reach, and (for local businesses) leads.
- **The first 3 seconds decide everything.** 50–60% of all viewers who drop off do so in the first 3 seconds. Target: hold >80% retention at the 3-second mark, >60% at midpoint, finish with average percentage viewed >70%. Swipe-away above 40% = hook failure, full stop.
- **The three platforms are NOT the same algorithm in different clothes.** TikTok = interest graph (followers barely matter, every video tested cold). Reels = relationship-weighted (followers see it first; watch time + sends/reach are the top signals per Mosseri). Shorts = hybrid (channel authority and topic consistency matter, plus search/long-tail life).
- **Hybrid channels (shorts + long-form on the same channel) grow ~41% faster** than single-format channels, and since YouTube's "bridge" update, Shorts viewing directly feeds long-form recommendations from the same channel. Beaupré's rule: "Same audience? Same channel. Different audience? Different channel."
- **Cadence benchmark: 3–5 shorts/week per platform, sustained.** Consistency beats volume — 3/week for 6 months beats 7/week for 2 weeks then silence. Burst-then-gap posting is the single most damaging pattern.
- **One long-form episode = 5–20 clips.** Clip selection is an editorial skill: hunt for contrarian takes, story payoffs, complete thoughts, and emotional spikes — not chronological chunks. Every clip needs its own hook; never start mid-setup.
- **Completion rate beats length.** A 45-second video at 70% completion outperforms a 15-second video at 40% completion on every platform. Cut to the natural length of the idea; 15–30s is the retention sweet spot for most content types.

## 1. What short-form is for (strategic roles)

Short-form video does exactly four jobs. Pick which job each piece is doing *before* making it, because the jobs have different success metrics:

1. **Cold-audience discovery (top of funnel).** Short-form is the only organic format where an account with zero followers can reach six figures of strangers in 48 hours. Success metric: reach + new-viewer %.
2. **Funnel into long-form / owned channels.** Clips that make people want the full episode, the newsletter, the channel. Success metric: profile visits, subscriber conversion, long-form views from Shorts viewers.
3. **Frequency/relationship maintenance.** Staying in front of the existing audience between long-form uploads. Success metric: returning-viewer retention, engagement from followers.
4. **Direct response (local/lead-gen businesses).** For a training facility or local service, short-form is a lead channel: DMs, link clicks, bookings. Success metric: inquiries per 1,000 local views — vanity reach is irrelevant.

The classic strategic error is making job-3 content (inside jokes, casual updates) and judging it by job-1 metrics (viral reach), or vice versa.

**Relevance note:** a YouTube-first media company (Mayday-type) mostly plays jobs 1–3; a local training facility (Neptune-type) should play job 4 almost exclusively — a facility short that gets 3,000 views from local parents beats one that gets 300,000 views from out-of-state teenagers.

## 2. Platform-by-platform algorithm mechanics

### TikTok — interest graph, democratic testing, fast death

- Every video is tested independently with a small cold batch on the For You page **regardless of follower count**. Follower count has almost no impact on initial reach; a zero-follower account can hit millions of views.
- If the test batch watches ~70%+ and engages, TikTok widens distribution to a larger pool; repeat until a batch fails. If most of the test batch swipes in the first seconds, the video stalls (often under a few hundred views) and dies.
- Heaviest-weighted signals: **watch time, replays, completion rate**, then shares and saves.
- **Content lifespan: 48–72 hours.** TikTok is the most ephemeral platform; almost all views come in the first 3 days. This is why volume matters more on TikTok than anywhere else.
- Highest engagement rates of the three (~2.8% in 2024, trending toward ~3.15%), and roughly 2x the comments per video vs. Reels (54 vs. 35 avg; Shorts ~20). TikTok is where conversations happen.

### Instagram Reels — relationship-weighted, sends are king

- Reels are shown **first to a subset of your followers** and people who've engaged with similar content; expanded distribution to non-followers (Reels tab, Explore) only follows strong early engagement. Account history and follower relationships matter far more than on TikTok.
- Adam Mosseri has confirmed the three top ranking signals: **watch time, sends per reach (DM shares), and likes per reach.** Watch time is #1; "sends per reach" is the most actionable — content people DM to a friend gets outsized distribution. Design for "who would send this to whom?"
- Meta's internal data: users make the stay-or-scroll decision in **~1.7 seconds** — the fastest of the three platforms.
- **Originality enforcement is real:** original content gets ~40–60% more distribution than reposts; accounts posting 10+ reposts within 30 days get excluded from recommendations entirely. Visible TikTok watermarks are detected and deprioritized. Never cross-post watermarked video.
- Lowest engagement rate of the three (~0.65%) but taps Instagram's brand-advertiser ecosystem and a visual-first audience; best platform for sponsors who care about IG presence.

### YouTube Shorts — hybrid graph + the only platform with a long tail

- Two-phase distribution: phase one tests the video with a small audience selected on **topic relevance, channel signals, and viewer watch history**; phase two scales based on watch time, completion rate, swipe-away rate, and engagement. Channel authority and topical consistency genuinely affect initial reach — unlike TikTok.
- **Shorts have a long tail.** YouTube's search and recommendation system keeps Shorts surfacing for weeks or months (evergreen/educational content especially). TikTok content dies in days; Shorts compound.
- Surprising benchmark: small accounts often get **better absolute reach on Shorts than TikTok or Reels.** Socialinsider (Jan 2024–Aug 2025 data): average views for 1–5K-follower accounts were ~2,600 on Shorts vs. ~660 TikTok / ~600 Reels; for 5–10K accounts, ~14,800 Shorts vs. ~1,420 TikTok / ~1,200 Reels. Shorts is currently the best cold-start platform for small channels on raw views.
- **The Shorts↔long-form bridge (critical):** since the Aug 2022 update announced by Todd Beaupré (YouTube's director of discovery), recommendations consider recent long-form videos from channels a viewer watches in Shorts. Shorts viewing now directly seeds long-form discovery on the same channel. Beaupré's channel-architecture rule: **"Same audience? Same channel. Different audience? Different channel."** (MrBeast split Shorts to a separate channel, then moved everything back to main.)

### Side-by-side reference table

| Dimension | TikTok | Reels | Shorts |
|---|---|---|---|
| Distribution basis | Interest graph, cold test every video | Followers first, then Explore/Reels tab | Hybrid: topic + channel authority + watch history |
| Follower count impact | Near zero | High | Moderate |
| Top signals | Completion, replays, watch time, shares | Watch time, sends/reach, likes/reach | Watch time, completion, swipe-away, viewed-vs-swiped |
| Content lifespan | 48–72 hrs | ~1–2 weeks | Weeks–months (search tail) |
| Engagement rate (avg) | ~2.8–3.15% | ~0.65% | ~0.30–0.40% (rate), but highest raw views for small accounts |
| Decision window | ~3 seconds | ~1.7 seconds | ~3 seconds (slightly more tolerance) |
| Optimal length | 15–60s | 7–30s | 30–60s (15–30s for max retention) |
| Direct monetization | $0.40–$1.00 RPM (Creator Rewards, 1min+ videos) | Bonuses (invite-only, volatile) | $0.01–$0.10 RPM (pooled) |
| Best strategic use | Cold reach + trends + conversation | Sponsor visibility + existing-audience nurture | Funnel to long-form + evergreen search |

## 3. Hooks and retention in under 60 seconds

### The retention benchmark stack (memorize these)

- **3-second retention: >80%** is the target ("swipe or stay" moment). 50–60% of all drop-off happens here.
- **Midpoint retention: >60%.**
- **Average percentage viewed: >70%** is healthy; >100% means replays (excellent); <50% means structural problems.
- **Swipe-away rate (Shorts "viewed vs. swiped"):** <15% exceptional; 15–25% normal/healthy; 25–35% hook problem worth diagnosing; **>35–40% = hook failure regardless of how good the rest is.**
- **Like-to-view ratio (Shorts):** 3–6% healthy; >6% strong; <2% weak.
- **Completion beats length everywhere:** a 45s video at 70% completion beats a 15s video at 40% completion. But also: a 15s Short watched fully (100% retention) beats a 2-minute Short at 50%.

### Length sweet spots by content type (OpusClip retention data)

| Content type | Ideal length |
|---|---|
| Tips / hacks | 15–20s |
| Comedy | 18–28s |
| Tutorials | 25–40s |
| Storytelling | 30–45s |
| Overall retention sweet spot | 15–30s (retention often >80%) |

Rule: cut to the natural length of ONE idea. Stretching a 20-second idea to 55 seconds to "look substantial" is a retention killer.

### Named hook formulas (with mechanics)

1. **Pattern Interrupt.** Break scroll autopilot with contrast — open mid-action with no context, a contradiction ("I made more money after I stopped posting daily"), a rapid zoom/color change, or a 0.3-second flash of the end result before cutting back to the start. The operative principle: *you must be different from the 47 shorts they just scrolled past.*
2. **Direct Promise.** State a specific, measurable outcome: "This 5-second edit doubled my watch time," "3 settings that fix blurry Shorts." The difference between weak and strong promises is **specificity and stakes** — promise a measurable outcome, not "information."
3. **Question Hook / information gap.** "Why do your Shorts die at 40% watch time?" Creates cognitive compulsion to close the gap. Sub-variant — **self-identifying questions** that make the target viewer feel personally addressed: "Are you editing on your phone and feeling limited?"
4. **Combinations.** Contradiction + Promise ("Longer Shorts actually get more views — I'll prove it in 30 seconds"); Question + Visual Proof (pose the question while showing the transformation on screen).

**Delivery standards:** full hook delivered in 2–2.5 seconds; text overlay + spoken words simultaneously; pacing slightly faster than natural speech; zero dead air, zero setup. Practitioners report that fixing hooks alone has moved creators from ~500 to ~50,000 views per Short — the hook is the highest-leverage 3 seconds in the entire content operation.

### Mid-video retention mechanics

- **Cut every 2–4 seconds.** Visual novelty resets attention. Angle change, zoom, b-roll, text pop — anything.
- **Captions boost retention 15–25%.** Non-negotiable; a large share of viewers watch muted (Reels especially — design for silent autoplay).
- **Open loops:** promise the payoff early, deliver it at ~80–90% of runtime, not at the very end (people bail when they sense "it's over").
- **The loop ending:** endings that flow naturally back to the beginning drive replays; even a 10% replay rate meaningfully boosts distribution (replays push average % viewed above 100%).
- **Mobile-first QC:** 95%+ of views are on phones. Check every short on an actual phone screen — text readable, face large enough, key action inside the safe zone (avoid bottom ~15% and right rail where UI overlays sit).

## 4. Clipping long-form into shorts (the repurposing machine)

### Why clip

One 60–90 minute episode yields 5–25 shorts with zero new original content. Documented campaign numbers (Clipping Culture case studies): *Murder With My Husband* — 7.7M views from 7,982 clips; *All In Sports Podcast* — 5M views from 973 clips; *Paul American* — 11M views from 6,141 clips. Note the ratio spread: All In averaged ~5,100 views/clip while Murder With My Husband averaged ~965/clip — clip quality and selection matter enormously; volume alone is a brute-force strategy with diminishing per-unit returns.

### What makes a moment clip-worthy (editorial criteria)

Hunt the transcript/recording for:
- **Contrarian takes** — anything that contradicts conventional wisdom in the niche
- **Story payoffs** — a complete micro-story with setup, tension, and punchline that fits in 45s
- **Emotional spikes** — laughter, genuine surprise, heat, vulnerability
- **Complete thoughts** — a self-contained idea needing zero outside context
- **Specific numbers or claims** — "we spent $40K on X and here's what happened"
- **Frameworks/tips** — a nameable, repeatable how-to

Disqualifiers: anything requiring context from earlier in the episode, mid-sentence starts, inside references, meandering answers. **One clip = one idea.** If a moment contains two ideas, it's two clips.

### The workflow (per episode)

1. **Harvest candidates.** Manual scrubbing takes 4–6 hours/episode; AI clipping tools (OpusClip, Choppity, etc.) generate 20–40 candidate moments in 15–45 minutes. Treat AI output as candidates, not finals — human editorial pass is where the alpha is.
2. **Select 5–7 finals** per 60-minute episode (up to 15–25 for aggressive multi-account campaigns).
3. **Re-hook every clip.** The strongest line of the clip goes first, even if that means reordering. Add a text-overlay hook that works on mute. Never open on the question — open on the most provocative part of the answer, then let context fill in.
4. **Format:** 9:16 vertical, speaker reframed/centered, animated captions, cuts every 2–4s, trim to the natural end of the idea.
5. **Platform-adapt (not just cross-post):**
   - *TikTok:* strongest opening line within 1 second, 20–45s, high-contrast captions, native trends/sounds where relevant.
   - *Reels:* design for silent autoplay, centered framing, clean captions; NO watermarks.
   - *Shorts:* title text matching search intent (Shorts get search traffic), single-takeaway focus.
   - *LinkedIn (if B2B-relevant):* insight-led, operator-story clips.
6. **Schedule across the week** with content-type variety — e.g., bold opinion Mon, framework Tue, story Wed, contrarian take Thu, quick tip Fri.
7. **Review analytics weekly**; feed learnings back into what moments get clipped (see diagnostics, §8).

### The clip funnel model

Two-layer funnel: clips scattered across TikTok/Reels/Shorts catch strangers mid-scroll; the full episode lives on YouTube/Spotify where converted viewers watch 30+ minutes and subscribe. Small-scale documented example: "With The Aims" podcast 5X'd clip output → 20K+ views, 102+ new subscribers. The conversion rate from clip viewer → episode listener is low single-digit percent, which is why clip volume and clip-CTA quality (pinned comment with the link, verbal "full episode on the channel," Shorts' "related video" link) both matter.

## 5. The shorts-to-long-form funnel

### What the data says

- **Hybrid channels grow ~41% faster** than shorts-only or long-form-only channels.
- Since YouTube's bridge update, watching a channel's Shorts feeds that channel's **long-form into the viewer's recommendations**. Shorts also convert subscribers directly (subscribe button on the Shorts player). Documented cases: MacDannyGun +670K subs via a shorts-to-long-form funnel; Pikameo Gameplay +35K subs in 2 months combining formats. (Treat individual creator case numbers as directional, not guaranteed.)
- Pre-2022, viral Shorts famously produced "no extra long-form views, no channel growth" — that complaint is now mostly obsolete *if* the shorts audience matches the long-form audience.

### The audience-mismatch trap (the #1 funnel failure)

YouTube's recommendation system runs on thematic and behavioral consistency. If your shorts attract quick-entertainment viewers who would never watch your 25-minute videos, you poison your own signals: the algorithm learns your channel serves an audience that doesn't convert, subscriber quality drops, and long-form CTR/retention from the polluted audience drags recommendations down. **A viral short on an off-topic gag can be net-negative for the channel.**

Rules of thumb:
- Every short should be something your ideal *long-form* viewer would enjoy. Ask: "would the person who loves our 20-minute videos also watch this short?"
- If you genuinely want to run an off-audience shorts play (pure-reach clip channel, different demographic), use a **separate channel** — Beaupré's "different audience, different channel."
- Shorts that tease/preview a specific long-form video ("full breakdown on the channel") are the highest-intent funnel format; pure-value standalone shorts build reach and familiarity but convert slower.

### Funnel design playbook

1. Define the long-form viewer avatar first; shorts serve *that* person.
2. 70/30 mix: ~70% standalone-value shorts in the niche, ~30% direct teasers of specific long-form uploads (clip the best 30 seconds, cut before the payoff, point to the full video).
3. Use the Shorts **"related video" link** feature to attach the long-form video to the short.
4. Pin a comment with the long-form link; say the CTA out loud in the last 2 seconds (after the payoff, not instead of it).
5. Time teaser shorts within 24–48h of the long-form upload to ride the recommendation bridge.
6. Measure: YouTube Studio → Advanced mode → traffic from Shorts feed to long-form; "viewers who watch Shorts also watch..." audience overlap; new-subscriber source attribution.

## 6. Posting cadence benchmarks

### Per-platform baselines (data-backed consensus, 2025–2026)

| Platform | Baseline | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 3–5/week | New accounts: daily for first ~30 days to accelerate algorithm learning, then settle to 3–5. Diminishing returns past ~5/week. |
| YouTube Shorts | 3–5/week shorts-only; **2–3 shorts/week + 1 long-form/week** for hybrid channels | Hybrid mix is the growth-optimal configuration |
| Instagram Reels | 3–5/week | Higher production bar — 5 low-quality Reels/week is far more damaging on IG than on TikTok. Pair with daily Stories for follower nurture. |

- Posting 2–5×/week delivers up to ~17% more views per post vs. once weekly.
- **Consistency > volume.** Sustainable 3×/week for 6 months dramatically outperforms 7×/week for 2 weeks then silence.
- **Burst-then-gap is the most damaging pattern on all three platforms** — long gaps create a "re-entry tax" on the next posts' distribution.
- Quality floor still applies: 3 excellent shorts beat 7 mediocre ones; mediocre volume trains the algorithm (and audience) that you're skippable.

### Cross-posting protocol

- Record once in 9:16, then **adapt per platform** (hook line, text overlay, caption style) — hub-and-spoke, not copy-paste.
- **Order:** TikTok first (highest cold-reach potential and fastest feedback), then Shorts (search benefit), then Reels. Stagger 2–4 hours between platforms.
- Strip all watermarks. Reels actively deprioritizes watermarked video; TikTok likewise dislikes obvious cross-posts.
- Expect the same clip to perform wildly differently per platform — a TikTok flop can be a Shorts hit (different graphs, different audiences). Judge clips per-platform, not globally.

## 7. Short-form monetization reality

### Direct revenue (the honest numbers)

| Channel | Model | Realistic payout |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube Shorts | Pooled ad revenue share (YPP) | **$0.01–$0.10 RPM**; typical $0.03–$0.06. Real example: a vidIQ short with 468,500 views earned $16.61 (~$0.035 RPM). 1M views ≈ $60. 10M views ≈ $600. |
| YouTube long-form (comparison) | Direct ad revenue | $1–$30 RPM depending on niche; $1,000–$30,000 per million views — **10–100x Shorts** |
| TikTok Creator Rewards | Per qualified view, videos must be 1min+ | $0.40–$1.00 RPM — best per-view short-form payout, but requires 60s+ videos and "qualified" views only |
| Instagram Reels | Invite-only bonus programs | $100–$35K/month for select accounts; volatile, discontinued/reinstated repeatedly — never plan around it |

### YouTube Shorts mechanics worth knowing

- **Eligibility:** 1,000 subs + 10M valid Shorts views in 90 days (or the long-form path: 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours/12mo). Fan funding (Super Thanks, memberships) unlocks earlier: 500 subs + 3M Shorts views/90 days.
- **Pooled model:** YouTube aggregates all Shorts-feed ad revenue → allocates to a Creator Pool after music licensing costs → distributes by each channel's share of total views → creator keeps **45%** of their allocation. Licensed music dilutes your share before your 45% is applied — original/no-music audio earns more per view.
- The $100M Shorts Fund is dead (ended 2023); revenue share replaced it.
- Niche matters even at these tiny numbers: finance/tech RPMs sit at the top of the range, entertainment at the bottom.

### The real monetization math

Direct short-form revenue is a *byproduct*, never a business model. The actual value stack, in order:

1. **Funnel value:** a Shorts viewer converted to a long-form viewer is worth 10–100x more in ad revenue alone, before sponsorship value.
2. **Sponsorship reach math:** short-form views inflate the total-reach numbers in a sponsor deck; brand deals on short-form (dedicated branded shorts, TikTok Shop, IG partnerships) pay orders of magnitude more per view than platform funds.
3. **Owned-channel conversion:** newsletter signups, merch, community — shorts as the top of an owned funnel.
4. **Commerce:** TikTok Shop did ~$15.8B US sales in 2025; for product businesses (merch lines), short-form native commerce is the one place short-form directly drives revenue at scale.
5. **Local lead-gen:** for a facility business, one booked assessment from a short is worth more than a million Shorts-feed views. RPM is irrelevant; cost-per-inquiry is the metric.

Carl's framing for clients: *"If your short-form plan's ROI case rests on platform payouts, you don't have a plan. Short-form buys attention; something else must convert it."*

## 8. Playbooks, checklists, diagnostics

### Weekly short-form operating system (lean team)

1. **Mon:** review last week's analytics (30 min): swipe-away rates, avg % viewed, top/bottom performer, funnel metrics. Log one lesson.
2. **Mon/Tue:** harvest clips from the latest long-form episode (AI pass → human selection of 5–7).
3. **Tue/Wed:** edit batch — re-hook, captions, reframe, platform variants.
4. **Wed–Sun:** publish 3–5 per platform, staggered, TikTok → Shorts → Reels.
5. **Ongoing:** reply to comments in the first 2 hours post-publish (early engagement velocity helps on all three platforms; comments are content).

### Pre-publish checklist (every short)

- [ ] Hook lands inside 2–2.5 seconds; most compelling moment is FIRST
- [ ] One idea only; cut ends when the idea ends
- [ ] Text hook overlay + works fully on mute (captions on)
- [ ] Cut/visual change every 2–4 seconds
- [ ] Payoff at ~80–90% of runtime; ending loops or CTA after payoff
- [ ] Checked on an actual phone; text readable, safe zones clear
- [ ] No watermarks; platform-native caption/hashtags
- [ ] (Shorts) related-video link attached if teasing long-form; title matches search intent
- [ ] Would our ideal long-form viewer watch this? (audience-match check)

### Retention diagnostic decision tree

- **Swipe-away >35% / 3-sec retention <70%** → hook problem. Rewrite opening; front-load the payoff flash; kill preamble. Nothing else matters until this is fixed.
- **Good 3-sec hold, midpoint <60%** → pacing/promise problem. The hook wrote a check the middle isn't cashing; tighten cuts, remove filler, or the clip idea is weaker than its hook.
- **Good retention, low avg % viewed on longer shorts** → too long; cut 20–30% of runtime.
- **Great retention, low views** → distribution/categorization problem (Shorts "0 views" phenomenon is usually the algorithm failing to categorize, not audience rejection): check topical consistency, metadata, whether the content matches anything the channel has trained the algorithm on.
- **Great views, no subscriber/long-form lift** → audience mismatch. The short is reaching people who'll never watch the long-form; revisit clip selection against the long-form avatar.
- **High likes, low shares/sends (Reels)** → passively enjoyable but not send-worthy; engineer "who would DM this to whom?" into concepts.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Easing into the video** — greeting, context, backstory before the point. The single most common and most fatal error. Start at the most compelling moment; add context after, if at all.
2. **Judging shorts by long-form instincts** — importing intros, outros, slow builds, and "production value" that reads as skippable in a feed.
3. **Chasing off-niche virality** — the viral off-topic short that pollutes the channel's audience signals and hurts long-form. Net-negative wins exist.
4. **Watermarked cross-posting** — Reels and TikTok both detect and bury it. Also: identical copy-paste posting with no platform adaptation.
5. **Burst posting then vanishing** — 7 posts one week, zero for three. Worse than steady 3/week on every platform; incurs a re-entry tax.
6. **Stretching content to hit 60 seconds** (or 1 minute for TikTok Rewards eligibility) at the cost of retention. A padded short at 45% completion loses to a tight one at 85%.
7. **Building the business case on platform payouts** — Shorts pays ~$60 per million views. Anyone modeling revenue on Shorts RPM has already failed.
8. **Ignoring the swipe-away metric** — creators obsess over views and likes; the diagnostic gold is viewed-vs-swiped and the retention curve's first 3 seconds.
9. **Editing on desktop for desktop** — unreadable text and tiny faces on the 6-inch screens where 95%+ of viewing happens.
10. **Clipping chronologically instead of editorially** — cutting the episode into sequential chunks rather than hunting for self-contained payoffs, then wondering why clips need context they don't have.
11. **No CTA architecture** — shorts with no related-video link, no pinned comment, no verbal pointer; reach that leads nowhere.
12. **Ignoring analytics entirely** — guessing instead of running the weekly review loop; the platforms hand you a retention curve for every video and most creators never open it.

## 10. Questions Carl should ask a client

**Strategy & intent**
1. Which of the four jobs (cold discovery, funnel, audience maintenance, lead-gen) is your short-form doing? What's the success metric for that job?
2. Would your ideal long-form viewer watch your last 10 shorts? Which ones fail that test?
3. If a short went viral tomorrow to the "wrong" audience, would that help or hurt you? Do you know?

**Diagnostics (pull the data with them)**
4. What's your average swipe-away rate on the last 20 Shorts? Any above 35%?
5. What's your average % viewed? Any shorts above 100% (loops)? What do your top 3 retention curves have in common in the first 3 seconds?
6. On Reels: what are your sends per reach? Which posts got DM-shared, and why would someone send them?
7. How many long-form views / subscribers / newsletter signups can you attribute to shorts last month? (If the answer is "no idea," that's the finding.)

**Operations**
8. What's your actual sustained cadence over the last 90 days — and is it burst-and-gap? What cadence can you sustain for 12 months?
9. How many clips are you harvesting per long-form episode, and who makes the editorial selection call? Chronological chunks or payoff-hunting?
10. Are you adapting per platform or copy-pasting (watermarks included)?
11. Who replies to comments in the first 2 hours, and is that in anyone's job description?

**Monetization & funnel**
12. What percentage of the short-form business case depends on platform payouts? (Should be ~0%.)
13. What is one shorts viewer worth to you after conversion — long-form viewer, subscriber, lead, customer? Have you priced the funnel?
14. (Local business) What's your cost/effort per booked inquiry from short-form, and are you filtering for local reach or celebrating irrelevant national views?
15. Do shorts and long-form target the same audience — and therefore belong on the same channel — or do you actually need a second channel?
