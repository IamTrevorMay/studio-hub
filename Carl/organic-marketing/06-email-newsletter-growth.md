# Email & Newsletter Growth

## TL;DR

- **Email is the only audience you own.** Every platform audience (YouTube, IG, TikTok) is rented; the list is the asset that survives algorithm changes, channel strikes, and platform decline. Treat list growth as a first-class KPI, not a side project.
- **Run all four growth quadrants** (Dan Oshinsky's framework): Owned (site capture, lead magnets), Earned (cross-promos, recommendations, podcast guesting), Algorithmic (social → email funnels), Paid (Boosts/SparkLoop/ads). Best-in-class newsletters operate in all four simultaneously.
- **Lead magnets are the single highest-leverage organic tactic.** Template/checklist-style magnets convert ~2.5x better than long ebooks. A relevant magnet mentioned verbally in the first 60 seconds of a video converts 1–3% of viewers (up to 6% with tight topic match).
- **Judge everything by clicks, not opens.** Apple MPP inflates opens (~43% "average" open rates are fiction). Median click rate ~2.1%, CTOR ~6.8%; media-category newsletters hit ~4% click / ~13% CTOR. Set targets on click rate and reply rate.
- **A welcome sequence is non-negotiable.** ~30% of new subscribers churn in the first 90 days, almost always from weak onboarding. 3–7 emails over the first 1–2 weeks: deliver the magnet, tell the origin story, share best hits, ask a question, make one soft offer.
- **Monetization stacks; don't pick one.** In 2025, sponsorships overtook paid subs as the dominant model (77% of new publications sell ads vs ~2% run paywalls). Realistic stack per 1,000 engaged subs: $100–$2,000/mo combining sponsorships ($20–40 CPM on opens; niche B2B $75–150), products, affiliate, and optionally paid tier (1–5% free→paid conversion).
- **Deliverability is now regulated.** Since Feb 2024, Gmail/Yahoo require SPF+DKIM+DMARC, one-click unsubscribe (RFC 8058), and spam complaints under 0.3% (target <0.1%) for 5k+/day senders. Google moved to permanent rejections in late 2025. List hygiene is not optional.

## 1. Why the newsletter is the strategic center

The core argument, in one line: **subscribers on a platform are a distribution channel; emails are a customer database.**

- **Platform risk.** Channels get demonetized, shadow-throttled, or terminated. The email list is the disaster-recovery plan and the negotiating leverage (a sponsor buying a YouTube integration values a creator more when there's a 50k-person owned list attached).
- **Economics.** Email consistently produces the highest revenue per person of any channel because reach is ~deterministic (you hit 35–45% of the list every send vs. low-single-digit organic reach on social).
- **Flywheel with video.** Emailing the list when a new video drops creates an initial view burst that signals the algorithm to push the video wider → more viewers → more email signups → bigger burst next time. Email and YouTube growth are complements, not competitors.
- **Sponsor product.** A newsletter is a second sellable inventory unit (and often higher CPM than pre-roll). For a media company already selling sponsorships, the newsletter extends every deal ("YouTube integration + newsletter placement" bundles).

*Mayday relevance: the newsletter is both a hedge on the YouTube channels and additional sponsor inventory. For Neptune Performance, a local email list of parents/players is the cheapest bookings channel a facility can own — email a lesson opening and fill it same-day.*

## 2. The Four Growth Quadrants (Dan Oshinsky / Inbox Collective)

The canonical map of every growth tactic. Diagnosis question: "Which quadrants are you actually operating in?" Most stuck newsletters are in one.

### Owned — channels you fully control
- Landing pages, signup modules on every page of the site
- Pop-ups / "toasters" / registration walls (gate a piece of content behind email)
- Lead magnets (see §3)
- Checkout-form opt-ins (merch buyers → list; this is free growth for any store)
- Events: capture emails at every in-person touchpoint (clinics, meetups, camps)

### Earned — relationships and reputation, no payment
- Newsletter cross-promotions and recommendation swaps ("you recommend me, I recommend you")
- Podcast appearances and media interviews (always plug the magnet, not the homepage)
- Guest posts / content syndication
- Word-of-mouth and forward-to-a-friend mechanics

### Algorithmic — third-party platforms with discovery algorithms
- Social content engineered to drive link-in-bio / pinned-comment signups
- Power commenting on bigger accounts in the niche
- SEO: multiple landing pages targeting search intent, each with capture
- Repackaging newsletter content natively per platform (don't just post links)

### Paid — money in, subscribers out
- Meta/Google ads to a lead magnet
- Newsletter ad networks, beehiiv Boosts, SparkLoop partner network
- Giveaways/sweepstakes (dangerous — see Common Mistakes)
- Referral programs (paid-ish: rewards cost money)
- Buying small newsletters outright (underrated at small scale)

**Rule:** to be best-in-class you eventually need all four. Sequence for a small team: Owned first (capture infrastructure), then Algorithmic (you already have social reach), then Earned (swaps once you have something to swap), Paid last (only after retention is proven, or you pay to fill a leaky bucket).

## 3. Lead magnets — the workhorse

### What converts
- **"So good people would pay for it"** is the bar. Thin PDFs don't move numbers anymore.
- **Format matters:** templates, checklists, cheat sheets, and calculators convert ~**2.5x** better than long-form ebooks. Specificity beats comprehensiveness.
- **Emerging genre:** AI-powered magnets (audits, generators, calculators) — interactive tools with an email gate.
- **Topic-match is everything:** a magnet that directly extends the content the person just consumed converts 1–3% of viewers; a tightly matched one (e.g., "the exact prompt sheet from this video") can hit **6%**. Even 100 views with a relevant magnet yields 2–5 subscribers.

### Case study: original-research lead magnet (Ciler Demiralp, via Inbox Collective)
A "2024 Substack Landscape Report" built from scraped data on 75,000 newsletters. Two months of build (scraper + analyst + author). Promotion: 30-day waitlist (200 emails pre-launch), 1,000 personalized DMs, LinkedIn outreach, simultaneous multi-channel launch, creator collabs (300+ signups from partnerships alone), Reddit (160 signups in a week).
- **Results:** 1,100 downloads → 850 emails → 700 newsletter opt-ins → 620 net new after unsubscribes = **+22% list growth** (from a 2,800 base), 89% retention.
- **What failed:** LinkedIn ads at **$111 per signup** (paid social for a free offer rarely pencils); passive tactics like welcome-email replies.
- **Lessons:** original research is a magnet class of its own; creator partnerships with strong audience overlap were the top channel; break big reports into a series of smaller releases next time.

### Lead magnet checklist
1. Pick ONE painful, specific problem your best content already addresses.
2. Choose the shortest format that solves it (checklist > guide > course).
3. Name it with the outcome, not the format ("The 14-Day Velocity Program" not "Free Training PDF").
4. Dedicated landing page: one headline, one bullet list, one field, one button. No nav.
5. Deliver instantly in email #1 of the welcome sequence — and re-deliver the value inside the email itself (don't make the attachment do all the work).
6. Promote it forever: video verbal CTA in first 60s, pinned comments, description links, channel banner, social bio, guest appearances.
7. Build a second magnet only when the first one's conversion is measured and optimized.

*Neptune relevance: facility lead magnets are assessment-shaped — "free movement screen," "velocity assessment," "what college recruiters actually look for (PDF)." These convert parents because they're diagnostic, not content.*

## 4. Referral programs — the Morning Brew playbook

The most-studied case in the category (program built by Tyler Denk, who later founded beehiiv).

**Structure:** every subscriber gets a unique referral link embedded in every issue. Milestone rewards, cheapest first:
- 5 referrals → branded mug/stickers
- 25 → t-shirt
- 100 → exclusive event access / premium content

**Results:**
- Took Morning Brew from ~100k (2017) toward 4.5M subscribers; at launch the program drove **~80% of growth** pre-paid-acquisition, and still ~30% of growth years later.
- Peak months averaged **1,000+ referrals/day**.
- **Cost ~$0.25 per referred subscriber** vs. $4–5 CPA on Facebook/Instagram — a 16–20x efficiency gap.
- 225k+ subscribers referred at least one person; 80k referred three or more.

**Design principles that made it work:**
- The referral module lives *inside every email* with a live progress count ("You're 2 away from the mug") — friction near zero, progress salient.
- First reward attainable by a normal person (5, not 50).
- Rewards are identity goods (branded merch signals membership) — cheaper and stickier than cash.
- Pre-written share copy removes the "what do I say" barrier.

**When NOT to build one:** under ~5,000 engaged subscribers the math rarely works — too few sharers to generate momentum, and fulfillment overhead eats the gain. Prerequisite: strong open/click engagement, or you're amplifying silence. beehiiv/SparkLoop have this tooling built in; don't hand-roll it.

*Mayday relevance: a merch-producing media company has the exact reward inventory (mugs/tees at cost) that made Morning Brew's economics work.*

## 5. Cross-promos and recommendation networks

### Recommendation networks (the biggest structural change of 2022–2025)
- **Substack Recommendations:** publications recommend each other; new subscribers see recommended pubs at signup. Lenny Rachitsky: **"78% of new subscribers now come from other Substack newsletters recommending my newsletter."** Human-curated, free, and compounding — the strongest argument for being on Substack at all.
- **beehiiv Recommendations + Boosts:** same mechanic, plus a paid marketplace (see §7).
- **SparkLoop Partner Network / Upscribe:** platform-agnostic paid recommendations; post-signup "also subscribe to…" screens.

### Manual cross-promos (Earned quadrant)
- Classic swap: you plug them in your issue, they plug you in theirs. Match on audience overlap and rough list-size parity (or trade 2-for-1 if sizes differ).
- Expect roughly **0.5–2% of the partner's list** to convert per placement for a well-matched swap — a 10k-list swap ≈ 50–200 subscribers, free.
- Better formats than a text blurb: guest post exchanges, co-created lead magnets, joint webinars — the Demiralp case found collabs were the single best channel.
- Track: dedicate UTM/subscribe-source tags per partner; kill swaps whose subscribers don't open by email 3.

### Cross-promo pipeline checklist
1. List 20 newsletters with overlapping audiences within ~0.5x–2x your size.
2. Warm intro or short cold email: audience, size, click rate, proposed format, proposed dates.
3. Run one swap per issue max (more reads as spammy and dilutes both).
4. Score each partner on subscribers delivered AND their 30-day open behavior.
5. Repeat quarterly with the top performers; graduate best partners to co-created magnets.

## 6. Substack ecosystem mechanics (state of play, 2025–26)

Substack has evolved from newsletter tool → social network with a subscription-first model. What matters mechanically:

- **In-network discovery dominates:** Substack reported **32M new subscriptions originating inside the app in a single quarter** of late 2025 — readers finding writers within the ecosystem, not from external traffic.
- **The Network drives ~25% of all paid subscriptions**, and 25%+ of subscriptions originate in the apps.
- **In-network readers convert to paid ~3x better** than cold outside traffic — card already on file, one-click checkout vs. cold landing page. This compounds: the platform's paid-conversion advantage is a real moat.
- **Notes is the discovery layer.** For many early-stage publications, Notes drives **90%+ of new subscribers**. The working cadence from practitioners: **one long-form post per week + at least one Note per day**. The post is the product; Notes are the top of funnel.
- **Algorithm shift:** from static profile-matching to *sequential modeling* — "what's the natural next step for this reader right now," which rewards writers who publish consistently inside the ecosystem (the algorithm favors on-platform activity).
- **Recommendations remain the compounding asset** (see §5) — accumulating reciprocal recommendations from adjacent pubs is the closest thing to SEO on Substack.

**Substack trade-offs Carl should name:**
- 10% platform fee on paid subs (plus Stripe) vs. flat SaaS pricing on beehiiv/Kit/Ghost.
- You're renting Substack's network effects — discovery upside in exchange for platform dependence and weak email automation (no real welcome sequences, limited segmentation, no proper A/B tools).
- **Decision rule:** writer-led, essay-shaped, paid-subscription ambitions → Substack (network is worth the fee). Media-brand newsletter monetized by sponsors, needing automations/segments/referral tooling → beehiiv or Kit. Exporting the list is possible either way; the recommendations graph is not portable.

## 7. Paid acquisition economics

- **beehiiv Boosts:** pay per confirmed subscriber (confirmed ≈ opened at least once, didn't immediately unsubscribe). Average CPA ~**$1.63**; $50 minimum budget; beehiiv takes a 20% fee inside marketplace pricing.
- **SparkLoop:** pay only for subscribers meeting your screening criteria; testing shows SparkLoop-acquired subscribers are **~2x more likely** to hit engagement/quality bars than Boosts; Upscribe-sourced subs reportedly retain 10–20% better than organic.
- **Meta/IG ads to newsletter:** Morning Brew's benchmark was **$4–5 CPA** at scale; expect $2–6 for consumer, more for niche B2B. Only viable with downstream monetization math (see §11).
- **The universal failure mode:** paid volume before retention. Paid subscribers churn faster than organic; if 30%+ of organic subs already ghost in 90 days, paid pours water into a cracked bucket and wrecks deliverability (§10).
- **Payback framing Carl should use:** CPA ÷ (monthly revenue per subscriber) = payback months. At $2 CPA and $0.20/sub/mo (typical sponsor-monetized), payback is 10 months — fine if 12-month retention holds, reckless if it doesn't. Some sponsor-heavy operators now break even on paid subs **within 7 days** using co-branded lead magnets and immediate sponsor walls, but that requires ad inventory sold in advance.

## 8. Converting a social/video audience to email

The hardest truth: **platform subscriber counts convert to email at low single digits unless you engineer the funnel.** Tactics ranked by observed effectiveness:

1. **Verbal CTA in the first 60 seconds of the video.** The dominant mistake is a link-in-description mention at the end — 70–80% of viewers are gone by then. Name the magnet, tie it to the video topic, say where the link is.
2. **Topic-matched magnet per content pillar.** Generic "join my newsletter" converts a fraction of "get the exact spreadsheet from this video." 1–3% of viewers baseline, up to 6% with tight matching.
3. **Pinned comment + first description line + channel banner + end screens.** Noah Kagan found ~400x more channel-page conversions than in-video — dress the channel home, not just videos.
4. **Email the list every upload.** Closes the flywheel (view burst → algorithm push → new viewers → new emails).
5. **Community posts / Stories with direct ask** around magnet launches.
6. **The Serein Wu rationale for on-camera framing:** creators who watched peers lose channels frame the newsletter to fans as "the place we can't be separated" — insurance-as-pitch converts loyal viewers who don't need a bribe.

**Realistic conversion expectations:** a channel with 100k views/month running a disciplined magnet funnel should produce roughly 1,000–3,000 emails/month. If actuals are 10x below that, it's a funnel problem (CTA placement, magnet relevance, landing-page friction), not an audience problem.

## 9. Welcome sequences and automation (Matt McGarry's four-sequence stack)

Build in this order — it's the MVP of newsletter automation:

### 1. Welcome sequence (first, always)
- **3–7 emails across the first 7–30 days** (daily newsletters: 2–3; weekly newsletters: 5–10). Common cadence: Day 1, 2, 4, 6.
- Email 1 (instant): deliver the lead magnet, set expectations (what you send, how often), one question to reply to ("what are you working on?" — replies boost deliverability and generate content ideas).
- Email 2: origin story / mission / best-of back catalog — give them a reason this exists.
- Email 3: social proof, FAQ, restate the core benefit.
- Email 4+: one clear conversion push (paid tier, product, booking) with a deadline if honest. Most purchases happen **within 10 days of subscribing** — the sequence must cover that window.
- **Pause the regular broadcast for anyone inside the welcome sequence**, then roll them into normal cadence. Double-sending in week one is a top unsubscribe trigger.
- Write about *them*, not you — self-centered welcome copy is the classic engagement killer.

### 2. Win-back sequence
- Trigger: 14–30 days (daily sends) or ~60–90 days (weekly) without an open/click.
- 1–3 emails max: "still want these?" → one-click stay/leave → then suppress non-responders. This is deliverability maintenance disguised as marketing.

### 3. Trigger sequences
- 1–3 emails fired by behavior: clicked a pricing link, hit a referral milestone, answered a poll, subscriber anniversary. Right message at the exact moment of expressed intent.

### 4. Sales sequence
- 5–15 emails to engaged segments after welcome completes. McGarry's blunt finding: "the more emails you send, the more you'll sell" — creators consistently under-send to buyers. Tae Kim (YouTuber, personal finance) runs a 100+ email nurture behind his welcome sequence as the backbone of launches.

## 10. Benchmarks, deliverability, and list hygiene

### Engagement benchmarks (MailerLite, 3.6M campaigns, Dec 2024–Nov 2025; medians)
| Metric | Overall | Media | Health/Fitness | Author/Blogger |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Open rate | 43.5% | 43.0% | 47.8% | ~43% |
| Click rate | 2.1% | **4.1%** | 1.45% | 2.75–3.0% |
| CTOR | 6.8% | **12.9%** | 4.45% | 7.8–8.6% |
| Unsubscribe | 0.22% | — | — | — |

**Caveats Carl must apply:**
- **Apple MPP auto-"opens" mail for Apple Mail users (~46% of clients).** Open rates are inflated fiction; **click rate is the honest metric**, CTOR second. Never let a client celebrate a 45% open rate without looking at clicks.
- Practical targets for a creator/media newsletter: **click rate ≥3%, CTOR ≥10%, unsub ≤0.3%/send, spam complaints <0.1%**, plus reply volume as a qualitative signal.
- Deliverability threshold pattern: lists sustaining 30–35%+ opens keep strong inbox placement; lists below ~20% face growing placement problems *regardless of technical config*.

### The 2024 Gmail/Yahoo bulk-sender rules (enforced hard since Nov 2025)
Applies at 5,000+ emails/day to Gmail/Yahoo, but treat as universal best practice:
1. **SPF + DKIM + DMARC** on the sending domain (custom domain, never a shared/free one).
2. **One-click unsubscribe** (RFC 8058 List-Unsubscribe headers), honored within 2 days.
3. **Spam complaint rate < 0.3%** hard ceiling; Google recommends **< 0.1%**. Three complaints per 1,000 sends breaches it. Google moved from throttling to **permanent rejections** in late 2025 for violators.

### List hygiene and churn
- **~30% of new subscribers churn within 90 days**, almost always traceable to weak onboarding — if emails 1–3 don't deliver on the magnet's promise, they unsubscribe or (worse) go dormant and rot sender reputation.
- **The churn-deliverability doom loop:** dormant subscribers → lower engagement rates → inbox providers route to spam → engaged subscribers stop seeing mail → engagement drops further. Left alone, it compounds.
- Low-quality acquisition (broad giveaways, unrelated co-reg, purchased lists) churns **2–5x** organic rates. High churn is usually an *acquisition* problem misdiagnosed as a content problem.
- Hygiene protocol: run the win-back sequence, then **suppress (don't delete)** non-responders; watch soft-bounce patterns; never "panic clean" mid-crisis or apply one rule to all segments.
- **Growth vs. net growth:** +500/mo with −400 unsubs is running in place while the list ages and sponsors' results decay. Always report net growth and 90-day retention, not raw signups.

## 11. Monetization models

### The 2025 landscape shift
Sponsorships overtook paid subscriptions as the dominant model: **~77% of new publications pursue advertising vs ~2% running paywalls** (ppc.land). Paid-sub models plateaued outside strong personal brands and must-have niches. Default advice: sponsor-first, paid-tier only with a differentiated premium offer.

### Model 1 — Sponsorships/ads (five pricing structures, per Inbox Collective)
1. **Flat fee** — fixed price per placement. *Recommended default*: predictable, invoice up front. Anchor it to an implied CPM.
2. **CPM** — pay per 1,000 **opens** (not list size): (opens ÷ 1,000) × rate. General range **$20–40 CPM**; consumer broad $15–35; **local newsletters $50–100**; **B2B $75–150**; hard-to-reach niches up to **$200**. Starting-out flat rates: $50–250/placement.
3. **CPC** — $1–2 standard ($1 poor, $2–3 solid, $3+ lucrative; Who Sponsors Stuff: interest niches ~$2, wealthy/finance $3–4, B2B $6–8+). Beware bot clicks (one study: 63% of email clicks were bots) — use it only with click filtering.
4. **CPL/CPA** — pay per lead/conversion; can be hundreds per lead in finance/real-estate but shifts all risk to publisher and depends on advertiser's reporting honesty.
5. **Affiliate** — commission on sales; **never the primary stream** (tracking failures, delayed/zero payment). Supplementary only.
- **Packaging (Who Sponsors Stuff):** publish a rate card **20–25% above target price**, discount into multi-run packages ("buy 5, 1 free") — faster sales cycles, deeper sponsor relationships. Hybrid base-plus-performance deals split risk and are underused.

### Model 2 — Paid subscriptions
- Free→paid conversion: **1–5%** typical (beehiiv cites up to 20% in exceptional cases; plan on low end). $5–15/mo price points.
- Math: 20,000 free subs × 2% × $10/mo = $4,000 MRR with zero advertiser management.
- Requires a real premium promise (extra depth, community, tools, access) — a paywall on the same content converts terribly. Substack's in-network 3x conversion advantage matters most here.

### Model 3 — Own products & services
- Usually the highest ceiling: a $97 course at 5% conversion on 1,000 subs = $4,850 per launch. For a facility: lesson packages, camps, assessments — the newsletter is the booking engine.

### Model 4 — Recommendations revenue
- Getting *paid* to recommend other newsletters (SparkLoop/Boosts supply side): $1–3 per qualified referral. Meaningful at scale, and a nice offset to your own paid acquisition.

### Revenue per 1,000 engaged subscribers (beehiiv, 2025)
| Stream | Monthly range |
|---|---|
| Sponsorships | $20–500 |
| Paid subs | $250–3,000 (at 5–20% conversion — be skeptical of top end) |
| Affiliate/Boosts | $100–1,000 |
| Products/services | $500–2,000 |
| Donations | $50–200 |
| **Stacked 2–3 streams** | **$1,000–5,000** |

Rule of thumb Carl can quote: a well-run niche newsletter monetizes at **$1–3 per subscriber per year** on sponsorships alone, and $5–20+ per subscriber per year with products stacked on top.

## 12. Playbooks

### 0 → 1,000 (months 0–4)
1. Pick platform (Substack if writer-led/paid-sub ambition; beehiiv/Kit if media-brand/sponsor-led). Custom domain + SPF/DKIM/DMARC on day one.
2. One flagship lead magnet tied to your strongest existing content pillar.
3. Capture everywhere you already have attention: video CTAs (first 60s), bios, pinned comments, site pop-up, merch checkout opt-in.
4. Welcome sequence (3–5 emails) before promoting anything.
5. Weekly send, same day/time, no gaps. Consistency is retention.
6. 5–10 recommendation swaps with peer-size newsletters.

### 1,000 → 10,000 (months 4–18)
1. Second and third lead magnets per content pillar; A/B landing pages.
2. Formalize cross-promo pipeline (§5 checklist); consider one original-research magnet (Demiralp playbook: waitlist → collabs → multi-channel launch).
3. If on Substack: daily Notes + weekly post; accumulate recommendations. Else: enroll in SparkLoop/Boosts as *earner* first to learn the marketplace.
4. Add win-back automation; enforce quarterly hygiene.
5. First sponsorships at ~2,500+ engaged subs: flat fee, $50–250, anchored to $25–40 CPM on opens. Build a one-page media kit (audience, click rate, sponsor results).
6. Referral program at ~5,000 engaged (merch rewards; 5/25/100 tiers).
7. Test paid acquisition ($200–500 on Boosts/SparkLoop) only after 90-day retention ≥70%; track cohort engagement per source.

### Sponsorship sales checklist
- Media kit: list size, **click rate** (lead with it, not opens), audience description, past-sponsor results, rate card (+20–25% over target).
- Prospect from adjacent sponsors: who sponsors similar newsletters/channels (Who Sponsors Stuff-style research).
- Sell multi-run packages by default; single runs at premium.
- Report clicks per placement to every sponsor unprompted — renewal rate is the real business.
- Cap ad load: one primary + one classified per issue; sponsor-stuffed issues bleed subscribers.

## 13. Common mistakes

1. **Celebrating open rates.** MPP-inflated; a "45% open" list with 0.8% clicks is a sick list. Clicks and replies only.
2. **Generic "subscribe to my newsletter" CTAs.** No stated benefit = no conversion. Always sell the magnet or the specific value.
3. **CTA at the end of videos.** 70–80% of viewers are gone. First 60 seconds.
4. **Growth before retention.** Paid acquisition or viral giveaways feeding a newsletter with 40% 90-day churn — the money buys deliverability damage.
5. **Broad-appeal giveaways.** iPad raffles produce subscribers who churn 2–5x organic rates and complain-spam you toward the 0.3% ceiling.
6. **No welcome sequence.** The 10-day post-signup window is the highest-intent period a subscriber will ever have; single "thanks for subscribing" emails waste it.
7. **Skipping authentication / shared domains.** Post-2024, missing DMARC or one-click unsub isn't sloppy, it's undeliverable. Permanent rejections since late 2025.
8. **Deleting instead of suppressing; panic-cleaning during a deliverability incident; uniform hygiene rules across segments.**
9. **Affiliate as the primary revenue model.** Broken tracking, delayed payments, zero leverage.
10. **Pricing ads on list size instead of opens/clicks** — sophisticated sponsors reverse-engineer your effective CPM/CPC anyway; naive pricing reads as amateur and invites grinding.
11. **Reporting raw signups instead of net growth** (+500/−400 = stagnation with an aging list).
12. **Treating Substack as just an ESP** — paying the 10% fee without working Notes/recommendations forfeits the only reason to be there.
13. **Self-centered welcome copy.** If subscribers don't see themselves in the emails, they don't engage, trust, or convert.
14. **Referral program too early** (<5k engaged) or with unattainable first tier.
15. **Storing the "list strategy" in someone's head.** Subscribe-source tagging, cohort retention, and sponsor performance must live in a dashboard or the compounding is invisible.

## 14. Questions Carl should ask a client

**Strategy & positioning**
1. If your main platform account vanished tomorrow, how many of your fans could you reach directly? (The list-as-insurance framing.)
2. What does a subscriber get from the email that they can't get from just watching/following you? (If no answer, the newsletter is a change-log, not a product.)
3. Which of the four growth quadrants are you actually operating in this month?

**Funnel diagnostics**
4. What % of your monthly video viewers become email subscribers? (Benchmark: 1–3% of viewers exposed to a relevant magnet; 10x below that = funnel problem.)
5. Where in your videos does the email CTA appear, and is it verbal?
6. What's your lead magnet, and would anyone pay for it?

**Health diagnostics**
7. What's your click rate and reply volume — not open rate?
8. What % of subscribers from 90 days ago still open/click? (Target ≥70% retained-engaged.)
9. What's net growth (signups minus unsubs minus suppressed) over the last quarter?
10. SPF/DKIM/DMARC set? One-click unsubscribe live? Spam complaint rate known and <0.1%?
11. Is there a welcome sequence, and does email 1 arrive instantly with the magnet?
12. When did you last run a win-back and suppress dormants?

**Monetization diagnostics**
13. What's revenue per subscriber per year, and which of the five streams produce it?
14. Are ads priced on opens/clicks with a rate card 20–25% above target, sold in packages?
15. If considering a paid tier: what specifically is behind the paywall that isn't a worse version of the free product? What's the honest expected conversion (assume 1–3%)?
16. For acquisition spend: CPA ÷ monthly revenue per subscriber = how many months to payback, and does retention survive that long?
