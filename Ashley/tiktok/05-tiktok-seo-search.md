# TikTok SEO & Search Optimization

TikTok as a search engine: how videos get indexed and ranked in search results, how to do keyword research natively, where keywords must physically live in a video, what hashtags actually do now, and how to build an evergreen search library alongside FYP content. Strategy-level short-form positioning (platform roles, cadence, clip selection) lives in the Carl docs — this is the execution layer.

## TL;DR

- **TikTok indexes three layers of every video — spoken audio (ASR), on-screen text (OCR), and the written caption — plus hashtags.** To rank for a target keyword, hit all three: say it out loud in the first 3 seconds, show it as a text overlay in the first frames, and write it in the first 50 characters of the caption. One practitioner analysis of 100 top-ranking videos found every single one had the keyword in speech or on-screen text within 3 seconds.
- **Search ranking is a separate system from the FYP.** A keyword-optimized video with modest engagement can outrank a 50K-view viral video for a specific query. Keyword relevance + completion rate + saves decide search position; raw view count does not.
- **Hashtags are capped at 5 per post (TikTok hard limit, rolled out July–Aug 2025) and are now the weakest keyword layer.** Use 3–5: 1–2 broad category tags + 2–3 niche/long-tail tags containing the actual keyword. #fyp/#foryou provide zero algorithmic benefit. Keyword-rich captions out-reach hashtag-heavy captions (~30% more reach in one 2026 multi-account test).
- **Do keyword research inside TikTok, not Google:** search-bar autocomplete (type seed + a/b/c… for variations), Creator Search Insights (official in-app tool — trending topics, content gaps, what YOUR followers search), and Keyword Insights in TikTok Creative Center (ads.tiktok.com). Content gaps — high search volume, few videos — are the highest-ROI targets.
- **Search-optimized videos compound; FYP videos expire in 48–72 hours.** Run a portfolio: roughly 60–70% evergreen search-answer content (how-to, "X vs Y", question-answer) and 30–40% FYP/trend/personality content. Search videos keep pulling views for months.
- **The demand is real but concentrated in question-shaped queries:** ~49% of US consumers have used TikTok as a search engine (Adobe, Jan 2026), 65% of Gen Z. Informational queries dominate (~73% of keywords with higher TikTok-than-Google volume are informational). Optimize for the questions Trevor's audience actually types: "how to throw a slider," "why do pitchers…", "baseball training drills."
- **Search value now feeds the Creator Rewards Program payout** — TikTok officially factors "search value" into monetization, so SEO work compounds into RPM, not just reach (as of 2026).

## 1. Why TikTok search matters (the numbers, as of 2026)

- **~49% of US consumers** have used TikTok as a search engine (Adobe Express survey, Jan 2026, n=807) — up from 41% in 2024.
- **65% of Gen Z** have searched on TikTok; 25% of Gen Z rate it *effective* for finding information (vs 17% millennials, 12% Gen X).
- Reality check (don't oversell it): the share of Gen Z who say they *prefer* TikTok over Google fell from ~8% (2024) to ~4% (2026) per Search Engine Journal's read of the same Adobe data. TikTok search is additive discovery, not a Google replacement.
- Query mix skews informational: **~73% of keywords with higher search volume on TikTok than Google are informational** ("how to," "what is," "best way to"); ~21% commercial (Rise at Seven). Top searched categories: recipes (36%), DIY/how-to (34%), fashion (30%) — how-to is the lane baseball instruction content lives in.
- TikTok reportedly processes **billions of searches per day** (platform-adjacent claims cite 3B+/day as of early 2026 — treat the exact figure as soft, the magnitude as real).
- **Search results also surface in Google.** TikTok videos are indexed by Google and appear in Google's video carousels for how-to queries — a search-optimized TikTok gets two search surfaces for one optimization pass.
- **Monetization tie-in (official):** TikTok's newsroom states that creating search-relevant content "increases potential engagement in the Creator Rewards Program, which factors in search value." SEO is now a revenue lever, not just a reach lever.

## 2. How TikTok search indexing actually works

### The three indexing layers (named mechanisms)

TikTok builds a searchable text representation of every video from three machine-read layers plus explicit metadata:

1. **ASR (Automatic Speech Recognition)** — everything spoken in the video is transcribed and indexed. Every word you say is a potential keyword. This is why talking-head instruction ranks better than music-only montage for search.
2. **OCR (Optical Character Recognition)** — on-screen text overlays, title cards, and burned-in captions are read and indexed. Persistent overlays that state the topic ("How to grip a sweeper") are a direct ranking input.
3. **Caption parsing** — the written caption below the video. First ~50 characters carry the most weight (that's what's visible before the "more" cutoff and what search results display — results truncate around 150 characters).
4. **Hashtags** — explicit category signals, now capped at 5 (see §5).
5. **Visual/AI recognition** — TikTok increasingly classifies objects and scenes in frame (a pitching mound, a bullpen, a batting cage all help topical classification). Weakest, least controllable layer; don't rely on it.

**The stacking effect:** each layer is an independent confirmation of topic. Practitioner testing consistently finds videos that hit ASR + OCR + caption for the same keyword outrank videos with the keyword in only one place. Hootsuite's worked example: the top-ranking "Calgary pizza" video had the phrase in caption, on-screen text, hashtags, AND spoken audio simultaneously.

### Search ranking ≠ FYP ranking

- Search is a **separate ranking system**, not the FYP algorithm with a query bolted on. FYP optimizes "will this person keep watching"; search optimizes "does this answer this query."
- Consequence: a video with strong caption/spoken keyword coverage and a few hundred quality engagements can outrank a keyword-less viral video for that query. Small accounts can win search terms they could never win on FYP reach.
- The two systems feed each other: a video that performs in search generates completion/engagement signals that can re-trigger FYP distribution weeks or months after posting — this is the "old video suddenly spikes" pattern.

### Search-results ranking factors (in rough priority order, as of 2026)

1. **Keyword relevance** across the three indexed layers (spoken, overlay, caption) — table stakes; nothing else matters without it.
2. **Completion rate on the video from search traffic** — did searchers watch to the end? The strongest behavioral signal for holding a search position.
3. **Saves and shares** — saves are the "this answered my question, I'll need it again" signal; heavily weighted for search in 2025–2026.
4. **Engagement velocity** — likes/comments relative to impressions, especially in the first 24–48h.
5. **Early keyword placement** — keyword appearing (spoken or on-screen) inside the first 3 seconds. In one analysis of 100 top-ranking videos, 100% placed the primary keyword in speech or on-screen text within 3 seconds, and 92% used persistent on-screen captions repeating the spoken keyword.
6. **Topical authority of the account** — accounts that post consistently in one topic cluster rank easier for queries in that cluster (TikTok learns "this account = baseball instruction"). Niche-consistent accounts gain followers ~3x more efficiently per video (Sprout).
7. **Recency** — fresh videos get a temporary boost for trending queries; evergreen queries are less recency-sensitive (an 8-month-old video can hold #1 for "how to throw a changeup").
8. **Searcher personalization** — watch history, language, location tilt individual results; you're ranking in a distribution of result pages, not one fixed SERP.

## 3. Keyword research playbook (TikTok-native)

### Method 1 — Search-bar autocomplete mining (free, 10 min)

1. Open TikTok search, type a seed term ("pitching").
2. Record every autocomplete suggestion — these are real, volume-backed queries.
3. **Alphabet-soup it:** append a space + each letter ("pitching a", "pitching b", … "pitching drills for k") to force long-tail variations.
4. Tap through to the results page of promising terms; note the "Others searched for" strip and the related-search chips above results — more validated queries.
5. Study the top 5 ranked videos per term: what's their angle, length, format, caption structure? That's the search intent you must match.

### Method 2 — Creator Search Insights (official in-app tool)

- **Access:** type "Creator Search Insights" into the TikTok search bar, or Profile → Creator Tools → Search Insights (eligible creators).
- **What it gives you (per TikTok's official docs):**
  - **Trending** — most-searched topics platform-wide, filterable by category (Sports is a native category — directly usable for baseball content).
  - **For You** — searched topics related to *your* content type.
  - **Content Gap** — topics searched often but with few videos answering them. **These are the highest-ROI targets on the platform** — demand with no supply.
  - **Follower searches** — what your own followers are searching (requires 1,000+ followers).
  - **Search analytics** — how your existing posts perform in search (7-day / 14-day / custom windows). This is your rank-tracking tool.
  - Popularity scores and "Recommended Topic" labels flag high-engagement-potential terms.
  - Bookmark icon saves keywords to Favorites; camera icon starts a post targeting that keyword.
- Cadence: run a Content Gap + Follower Searches review **weekly**; it feeds the content calendar directly.

### Method 3 — TikTok Creative Center / Keyword Insights (desktop, free)

- ads.tiktok.com → Creative Center → **Keyword Insights**: search-volume-backed keyword data built for advertisers but usable organically — shows keyword popularity, example videos, and phrase variations.
- Creative Center's **hashtag tool**: trending hashtags filterable by industry (16 categories incl. sports) and window (7/30/120 days), with post volume and audience demographics.

### Method 4 — Comment mining & cross-platform import

- Read comments on your own and competitors' videos — questions in comments are literal search queries ("what grip is that?" → "sweeper grip" video).
- Import proven YouTube search terms: queries that work for Trevor May Baseball long-form/Shorts ("how to throw harder," "pitch grips explained") almost always have TikTok twins. Validate each via autocomplete before assuming.
- Google Keyword Planner / Semrush / Ahrefs for volume triangulation — useful for theme discovery, but TikTok phrasing differs (more conversational, question-shaped); always re-verify phrasing in the TikTok search bar.

### Keyword selection filter

For each candidate keyword score three things:
- **Demand:** appears in autocomplete / Creator Search Insights with a popularity score.
- **Winnability:** top results are beatable (thin answers, low production, no keyword stacking) or it's a flagged content gap.
- **Fit:** Trevor can answer it with unique authority (MLB experience beats generic coaching content on any "what's it really like" or "pro-level technique" query — this is the moat; few pitching-content creators have 12 MLB seasons).

Build a running keyword bank (spreadsheet or Studio tool): keyword, source, popularity, intent (informational/commercial/entertainment), status (planned/posted/ranking), video URL, search views.

## 4. Keyword placement spec (per video)

The pre-publish checklist. Target keyword = the exact phrase from research, not a paraphrase.

| Placement | Spec | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoken audio** | Say the exact keyword phrase within the **first 3 seconds** | ASR-indexed; doubles as the verbal hook ("Here's how to throw a slider that actually breaks") |
| **Text overlay** | Keyword as on-screen title card in the **first frames**, ideally persistent | OCR-indexed; 92% of top-ranking videos in one 100-video study used persistent captions repeating the spoken keyword |
| **Caption** | Exact keyword in the **first 50 characters**; natural sentence, then 1–2 secondary keywords later in the caption | Caption limit is 4,000 chars (as of 2026); search results truncate ~150 chars, so front-load. Longer keyword-rich captions do help indexing — use 1–3 natural sentences, not a keyword list |
| **Auto-captions** | Always enable TikTok's auto-generated closed captions; fix transcription errors on your keyword | TikTok explicitly uses caption text for categorization; a garbled keyword in auto-captions is a lost signal |
| **Hashtags** | 3–5, at least one containing the keyword verbatim (#sliderGrip, #pitchingdrills) | See §5 |
| **Comments** | Pin a comment that restates the keyword + adds value ("Full slider breakdown: grip pressure is 80% of it") | Comment text is indexed; also seeds discussion |
| **Sound** | Original audio (your voice) for search content; trending sounds are for FYP content | Voiceover in the first 5 seconds has stronger ranking impact than text alone (Rise at Seven) |

### Profile-level SEO (once, then quarterly review)

- **Username/handle:** keep consistent and brand-matched; a functional word helps if natural (handle changes allowed only every 30 days).
- **Display name:** searchable — "Trevor May | Ex-MLB Pitcher" beats a bare name for "MLB pitcher" queries; display name is indexed in account search.
- **Bio:** primary keywords stated plainly ("Pitching, MLB stories, baseball training"). For Neptune later: geo keywords in bio ("baseball training — [city, state]") because local-intent searches ("baseball lessons near me," "[city] pitching coach") are a real TikTok search behavior for facilities.
- **Consistency:** the account's overall topic cluster is itself a ranking input (topical authority, §2). Every off-topic post slightly dilutes it; every on-cluster post reinforces it.

## 5. Hashtag strategy — the 2025–2026 reality

### What changed

- **Hard limit: 5 hashtags per post**, rolled out mid-July → early August 2025 (upload warning: "Maximum 5 hashtags — use accurate hashtags to get more views"). TikTok only registers the first 5 anyway. The 10–30-tag era is officially dead.
- TikTok's stated rationale: hashtags matter less because recommendation is behavior/interest-driven; accurate tags beat many tags.
- Hashtags are now the **weakest** of the four keyword layers — a categorization assist, not a distribution engine. Data points: keyword-rich captions generated ~30% more reach and ~2x likes vs hashtag-heavy posts in one 2026 multi-account analysis; a 40-account/6-month agency test found tight hashtag sets beat stuffed sets on reach and save-rate, with ultra-niche tags outperforming generic ones.

### The formula (3–5 tags)

- **1–2 broad category tags** — tells the algorithm which interest bucket you're in: #baseball, #mlb, #pitching.
- **2–3 niche/long-tail tags** — the actual search terms, keyword verbatim in at least one: #pitchinggrips, #sliderdrill, #youthbaseballtraining. Sweet spot for niche tags: ~100K–1M posts — enough demand to matter, thin enough to rank.
- **Optional: 1 branded/series tag** if running a series (#MaydayMailbag) — builds a tappable archive, no SEO value on its own.

### What NOT to do

- **#fyp, #foryou, #viral, #foryoupage: zero algorithmic benefit** (repeatedly confirmed by practitioner testing). They waste one of your five slots.
- No trending-tag hijacking on unrelated content — mismatched tags confuse classification and depress distribution.
- Don't duplicate the caption keyword across all five tags; diversity of angle (category + technique + audience) beats repetition.

## 6. Evergreen search content vs FYP content

### The two content economies

| | FYP content | Search content |
|---|---|---|
| Lifespan | 48–72h (nearly all views in 3 days) | Months–years; compounds |
| Success metric | Reach, new-viewer %, follower conversion | Search views, saves, rank held, profile visits |
| Format | Trend, personality, reaction, story | How-to, comparison, question-answer, explainer |
| Audio | Trending sounds fine | Original voice required (ASR) |
| Hook | Emotional/curiosity | Keyword-literal ("How to X") |
| View curve | Spike then flatline | Slow drip, occasional re-spikes when search feeds FYP |

(strategy level — the four jobs of short-form and platform lifespan comparison: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md)

### Portfolio split

Common practitioner recommendation as of 2026: **~60–70% search-optimized evergreen, ~30–40% FYP/trend/personality.** For Trevor specifically the FYP share can run higher on More Mayday-style entertainment clips, while Trevor May Baseball–adjacent TikTok content should skew hard evergreen — instruction is where search demand lives and where his authority moat is widest.

### Formats that dominate TikTok search (2026)

1. **How-to** (15–60s, step-by-step, steps as on-screen text): "How to throw a slider," "How to break in a glove fast."
2. **Comparison / "X vs Y":** "Slider vs sweeper — what's the difference," "Wood bat vs BBCOR."
3. **Question-answer / myth-bust:** "Why do pitchers ice their arms?" "Does long toss actually build velocity?"
4. **Behind-the-scenes real process:** "What a real MLB bullpen session looks like" — BTS is a named top-3 search format in 2026 and is Trevor's cheapest unique asset.
5. **Local/commercial intent (Neptune lane):** "best baseball training facility [region]," "pitching lessons [city]" — low volume, extremely high value per view; a facility video ranking for a local query converts to actual bookings.

### The evergreen library play

Each evergreen video is an asset that keeps collecting search views (the "coin that lands back in your pocket" model — a trend video is a coin spent once). Build systematically:

1. Pick a **cluster** (e.g., pitch grips). List every question in that cluster from autocomplete + Content Gap + comments (grip, arm slot, spin, when to throw it, common mistakes, drills — per pitch type).
2. Ship **one video per question**, each fully keyword-stacked per §4. 8–12 videos per cluster.
3. Cross-link via pinned comments and reply-with-video (answering a comment question with a new keyworded video is both engagement and SEO).
4. Move to the next cluster. 3–4 clusters deep, the account has topical authority and ranks faster for every new video in those clusters.
5. **Refresh, don't delete:** if an old video ranks but is outdated, post an improved version targeting the same keyword and pin a comment on the old one pointing to it. Never delete a ranking video.

## 7. Step-by-step: the per-video SEO workflow

1. **Pick keyword from the bank** (§3) — one primary, 1–2 secondary.
2. **Check the SERP:** search it in-app; watch the top 3 results; note length, angle, what's missing. Plan to be more complete or more credible (MLB-experience angle).
3. **Script the first 3 seconds** to speak the exact keyword phrase as the hook.
4. **Shoot with a persistent on-screen title** stating the keyword; steps/labels as additional overlays.
5. **Caption:** keyword in first 50 chars → one natural sentence of context with a secondary keyword → question CTA to drive comments.
6. **Hashtags:** 1–2 broad + 2–3 niche per §5.
7. **Enable auto-captions**, proofread the keyword transcription.
8. **Post, then pin a keyword-bearing value-add comment.**
9. **48h check:** TikTok analytics → traffic source — what % came from Search? Creator Search Insights → Search analytics for query-level performance.
10. **30-day check:** still earning search views? Save-rate healthy? If ranking but decaying, schedule a refreshed version; if never ranked, diagnose (keyword too competitive → go longer-tail; weak completion → tighten the answer).

## 8. Measurement

- **TikTok Analytics → video → Traffic source:** "Search" as a % of views is the core KPI for evergreen posts. Practitioner heuristic: an evergreen post pulling >15–20% of views from search after week 1 is working; scale that format.
- **Creator Search Insights → Search analytics:** which queries surfaced your posts, 7/14-day or custom windows — the closest thing to a native rank tracker.
- **Saves per reach:** the search-intent quality signal ("I'll need this again"). Compare saves/reach on search content vs FYP content; search content should win.
- **Manual rank checks:** search target keywords from a logged-out or secondary account monthly (results are personalized; logged-out is a cleaner read). Log position in the keyword bank.
- **Compounding check:** monthly, sum views on all evergreen posts >30 days old. That number should rise month over month; if flat, the library isn't compounding — usually a keyword-selection problem, not a production problem.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Optimizing one layer only** — keyword in caption but never spoken or shown. All three layers or you're leaving rank on the table.
2. **Keyword stuffing** — repeating the phrase unnaturally in caption/speech reads as spam to both users and the algorithm; TikTok penalizes it. Natural language, exact phrase once early, secondaries woven in.
3. **Still using #fyp / #viral / 10+ hashtags** — post-Aug 2025 only the first 5 register, and generic tags do nothing. 10+ tags actively read as spam.
4. **Skipping auto-captions for aesthetics** — throws away an explicit categorization input TikTok says it uses.
5. **Trend-only content diets** — 100% trend content leaves zero compounding assets; the account restarts from zero every 72 hours.
6. **Music-only edits for instructional content** — no speech = no ASR layer = invisible to most how-to queries. Voiceover beats text-only for ranking impact.
7. **Paraphrasing the keyword** — saying "how to make your breaking ball nastier" when the query is "how to throw a slider." Match the searcher's exact language; TikTok search is more literal than Google.
8. **Judging search content by FYP metrics at 48h** — evergreen posts often look like flops at day 2 and win at day 60. Different content job, different scoreboard, different review window.
9. **Deleting or privating "underperforming" old videos** — kills accrued search equity; some are quietly ranking.
10. **Ignoring the SERP before shooting** — publishing a 5th identical answer to a saturated query instead of hunting Content Gap terms where demand outstrips supply.
11. **Topic sprawl** — bouncing niches erodes the topical authority that makes every future video rank faster.
12. **Treating TikTok volume assumptions as Google's** — phrasing differs (question-shaped, conversational); always validate phrasing in the TikTok search bar even for imported YouTube keywords.

## 10. Questions Ashley should ask

- What % of views on the last 20 TikToks came from Search (traffic-source report)? Do we even have a baseline?
- Is there a keyword bank? Who owns the weekly Creator Search Insights review (Trending / Content Gap / Follower searches)?
- Which content clusters do we want to own in search — pitch grips? velocity training? "MLB life explained"? — and how deep is each cluster currently (videos published vs. questions identified)?
- Are instructional TikToks currently spoken-word with auto-captions on, or music-cut edits? (If the latter, the ASR layer is dark.)
- What's the current hashtag habit — count and mix? Anything still shipping with #fyp or >5 tags?
- Do captions front-load the exact keyword in the first 50 characters, or lead with a joke/context?
- What's the evergreen : FYP ratio over the last 30 posts, and does it match the ~60/40 target for the baseball-content lane?
- For clips cut from the podcast/YouTube: is anyone re-titling and re-captioning them per-keyword for TikTok, or are captions copied verbatim across platforms?
- Which YouTube search terms already win for Trevor May Baseball, and have their TikTok twins been validated in autocomplete?
- For Neptune (when facility content starts): are local-intent keywords ("[city] baseball training," "pitching lessons near me") in the bio and in dedicated videos? Is there a separate account or a tagged cluster on the main one?
- Are we checking Creator Search Insights' Sports category and Content Gap weekly for baseball-adjacent gaps we can answer same-week?
- Which ranking evergreen videos are >6 months old and due for a refreshed re-shoot targeting the same keyword?
