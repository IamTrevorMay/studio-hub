# TikTok Posting Times & Cadence

Tactical reference for WHEN and HOW OFTEN to post on TikTok. Strategy-level context (what short-form is for, platform algorithm comparison, clip-selection editorial skill) lives in ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md — this doc is the execution layer.

## TL;DR

- **Timing matters less on TikTok than any other platform, but it's not zero.** Distribution can take up to 24 hours to ramp; the correct move is to post 2–6 hours AHEAD of the audience's peak window, not during it. A great video posted at a "bad" time still wins; a mediocre video at the "perfect" time still dies.
- **Default posting window for Trevor's accounts: weekdays 1–6 p.m. ET, with a sports-niche morning slot at 6–8 a.m. ET.** Sprout Social's 2026 data (2B engagements, 307K profiles): Tue–Thu 2–6 p.m. local time is the global peak. Metricool (2.3M posts): 6–9 p.m. activity peak, 8 p.m. highest. Sports/fitness audiences add a distinct 5–7 a.m. morning spike (+35% engagement in pre-workout windows per Later).
- **Frequency data (Buffer, 11.4M posts, Oct 2025): 2–5 posts/week is the efficiency sweet spot** (+17% views/post vs 1x/week). 6–10/week = +29%, 11+/week = +34% — but median views stay flat; extra posts buy lottery tickets (viral ceiling), not baseline reach. TikTok's own guidance: 1–4 posts/day max.
- **Never post two videos within 3–4 hours of each other** (exception: explicit Part 1/Part 2 series, which should post within 10–15 minutes). Closer spacing cannibalizes FYP test batches; 5+ uploads within an hour mimics bot behavior and can trigger spam suppression.
- **Consistency beats bursts, measurably.** A one-week silent gap costs a measurable growth penalty; small accounts (<10K) take 2–6 weeks to recover reach, large accounts (>100K) 4–12 weeks. Five posts/week on a predictable schedule outperforms daily-for-two-weeks-then-nothing.
- **Deleting and reposting does NOT help and usually hurts.** If a video must come down, set it to private — don't delete. The single exception: delete a video that actually violates community guidelines (it's the fastest shadowban recovery path).
- **New accounts need a 7–10 day warm-up before first post** (consume + engage in-niche daily, no posting), then ramp 1/day. A properly warmed account gets 150–300 views on post #1; 0 views on a new account is a trust problem, not a content problem.

## 1. How timing actually works on TikTok (the mechanism)

Get the mental model right first, because most "best time" advice silently assumes the wrong one.

1. **Every video gets a cold test batch on the FYP regardless of when it posts.** The test pool is served over the following hours — TikTok can take up to ~24 hours to fully push a video (as of 2026). So the question is not "when will people see it?" but "when do I want the test batch to be running?"
2. **Early engagement velocity matters, but it's measured against the test batch, not the clock.** A video posted at 3 a.m. gets its test batch when users wake up. It's disadvantaged only slightly (slower first-hour signal), not killed.
3. **Therefore: post AHEAD of peak, not at peak.** If the audience peaks at 8 p.m., posting at 6 a.m. gives the algorithm 14 hours of runway to seed the test batch before prime time; posting at 8 p.m. sharp means the test batch competes with the day's heaviest FYP inventory. Practical rule: post 2–6 hours before the audience's peak activity window.
4. **Timing is a tiebreaker, not a driver.** Every major study (Buffer, Sprout, Metricool) says the same thing in their fine print: hook + completion rate dominate; timing shifts outcomes at the margin. Never let a scheduling debate delay a good video by a day — TikTok content earns ~all of its views in the first 48–72 hours (lifespan mechanics: see Carl short-form doc §2), so a day of delay costs more than a suboptimal hour ever will.

**Weighting for Ashley's advice: content 80% / cadence 15% / clock time 5%.** Spend argument time accordingly.

## 2. Best posting times — the actual study data (as of 2026)

The three biggest datasets disagree on days, which itself is the finding: audience-specific analytics beat global averages. Use these as starting grids, then override with the account's own data within 4–6 weeks.

| **Wednesday** | **1–8 p.m.** (peak) |
| **Thursday** | **1–5 p.m.** (peak) |
| Friday | 3–5 p.m. |
| Saturday | Low — avoid |
| Sunday | Low — avoid |

Headline: **Tue–Thu, 2–6 p.m. local time.** Skews toward brand/business accounts (Sprout's customer base), which explains the weekday-afternoon shape.

| Day | #1 time | Runners-up |
|---|---|---|

| Sunday | 9 a.m. | 1 p.m., 12 p.m. |

Headline: **Saturday is the best day** (directly contradicting Sprout), evenings 6–11 p.m. peak, afternoons 12–5 p.m. weakest. Creator/entertainment content behaves differently from brand content — Buffer's grid is closer to how Trevor's accounts will behave.

### Metricool 2026 — 2.31 million posts, 92,000+ accounts

- Activity concentrates **6–9 p.m.**, with **8 p.m. as the single peak hour** globally.
- Context finding worth knowing: platform saturation is real — views per video fell 31.3%, reach −28.7%, interactions −31.2% YoY in their 2026 study. Baseline expectations should be set lower than 2024-era benchmarks. (44% of sub-100K accounts still grew.)

### How to reconcile the disagreement

- Brand-dataset studies (Sprout) → weekday afternoons. Creator-dataset studies (Buffer) → evenings + weekends. Entertainment/sports content is creator-shaped: **weight Buffer + Metricool over Sprout for Trevor's accounts.**
- The overlap zone across all three: **weekday 1–6 p.m. and evening 6–9 p.m. local-to-audience.** Start there.
- After ~30 posts, pull TikTok Analytics → Followers → "Most active times" and re-center the schedule on the account's own two biggest activity humps, minus 2–4 hours.

### Sports / baseball / fitness niche timing (as of 2026)

- Fitness & sports audiences show a **bimodal pattern**: 5–7 a.m. (pre-workout/motivation scroll) and 6–8 p.m. (post-work/post-practice unwind). Later's research: fitness creators see **~35% higher engagement in pre-workout morning windows** vs midday.
- Hopper HQ niche grid: fitness/wellness best at 6 a.m., 8 a.m., 7 p.m.
- **Baseball-specific overlays (Ashley's own calibration, not study data):**
  - In-season, live MLB games own 7–10 p.m. ET. Don't fight game broadcasts with evergreen content — post the morning slot or 1–4 p.m. ET, OR post reactive content timed to ride post-game conversation (10–11 p.m. ET after a big moment).
  - Youth-baseball/training-content audience (relevant to Trevor May Baseball + Neptune): parents and players check phones after practice, ~8–10 p.m. local, and weekend mornings around tournament schedules. Saturday morning (8–10 a.m.) is a legitimately good slot for training content even though brand studies call Saturday weak.
  - Trevor's audience is US-national with an ET/CT skew (baseball demo). Default the schedule to **ET**, and treat "post at 1 p.m. ET" as also catching West Coast lunch.

## 3. Posting frequency — the hard numbers

### Buffer's 11.4M-post study (150K accounts, fixed-effects regression, published Oct 2025) — the best frequency data that exists

Views per post vs a 1x/week baseline:

| Frequency | Views lift | Median views | 90th-percentile views |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 post/week | baseline | 489 | 3,722 |
| 2–5 posts/week | +17% | 506 | 6,983 |
| 6–10 posts/week | +29% | 487 | 10,092 |
| 11+ posts/week | +34% | 459 | 14,401 |

**Read the median column carefully — this is the most important table in this doc.** Median views are basically flat (489 → 459) across all frequencies. All of the "lift" lives in the 90th percentile: the top-post-to-median ratio expands from 7.6x at 1/week to 31.4x at 11+/week. **Posting more does not make your average video perform better; it buys more lottery tickets for outlier hits.** Buffer's own summary: "posting more helps — but mostly because it increases your chances of getting lucky."

Implications:
- If the account's job is discovery/top-of-funnel (More Mayday clips), volume is rational — outliers are the goal.
- If each video costs real production effort, the marginal post past ~5/week is low-ROI: +12 points of lift for doubling output (5→10/week).
- The steepest gain is 1/week → 2–5/week. Never let an account sit at 1/week.

### Corroborating / conflicting data points

- **1x vs 3x daily:** a 50-account test found once-daily posting earned ~40% higher views *per video* than 3x daily (smaller study, directionally consistent with Buffer's flat-median finding). Meanwhile top-creator analyses claim 2–3x/day posters get ~45% higher engagement and 2.5x faster follower growth — survivorship-biased (accounts that can sustain 3x/day are teams). Both can be true: per-video efficiency falls as total-account output rises.
- **SocialCrawl live pull (31 mega-creators, June 2026):** 4–7 posts/week bucket had the highest engagement rate (10.6%) — but n=2 in that bucket (Charli D'Amelio, The Rock), so directional at best.
- **TikTok official guidance (Creator Portal): 1–4 posts/day**, with "consistency matters more than any number." Treat 4/day as the ceiling, not the target — brands that chased the 4/day benchmark with filler content saw engagement *drop* (Shortimize).
- **>5 posts/day reliably reduces views per video** and risks spam classification.

### Frequency prescriptions by account situation

| Situation | Cadence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| New account (post-warm-up) | 1/day | Builds data + trust without spam signals |
| Established, solo creator, quality-constrained | 3–5/week, fixed days | Buffer sweet spot; sustainable (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md) |
| Established, clip pipeline from podcast/long-form | 1–2/day | Marginal cost per clip is low; lottery-ticket math favors volume |
| Local/lead-gen (Neptune Performance) | 3–4/week | Audience is finite and local; volume adds little, consistency + relevance is everything |
| Pre-launch hype window or news moment | up to 3–4/day, spaced 3–4 hrs | Burst is fine for a bounded event; return to baseline after |

## 4. Spacing rules (same-day multi-posting)

- **Minimum gap: 3–4 hours between posts.** Three stacked mechanisms (as of 2025–26 data):
  1. **FYP test window ≈ 2 hours** — the algorithm needs ~2 hrs to run a video's cold test before a sibling post starts competing for the same inventory.
  2. **User session cycles ≈ 90 min** — 3–4 hr spacing hits different audience sessions; a viewer who sees two of your videos three swipes apart skips the second out of fatigue, tanking your completion metrics.
  3. **Timezone laddering** — spaced posts catch East Coast morning / West Coast lunch / evening as separate windows.
- One cited figure: accounts spacing ≥3–4 hrs see ~22% higher engagement per video vs hourly posting (Koro, 2025 — single-source, treat as directional).
- **Spam thresholds:** 5+ uploads within ~an hour (and certainly 5–10 in 20 minutes) pattern-matches bot behavior → temporary reach suppression while the platform verifies a human. Hard rule: never bulk-dump a batch of finished clips; schedule them out.
- **The one exception — multi-part series:** content explicitly labeled Part 1 / Part 2 should post within 10–15 minutes of each other (or simultaneously). The algorithm links them and viewers actively search for the continuation. This is the only sanctioned back-to-back pattern.
- If running 2/day on Trevor's accounts: morning slot + early-afternoon slot (e.g., 7 a.m. + 1:30 p.m. ET) keeps 6+ hours of separation and both videos get clean test windows before evening peak.

## 5. Consistency vs. burst — what gaps actually cost

- **Going silent for one week produces a measurable growth penalty** (one 2026 analysis quantified it at −0.08 SD of growth). It is not a punitive "penalty" flag — the recommender simply reallocates attention toward accounts generating fresh performance data — but the effect is real either way.
- **Recovery timelines after a silent week (as of 2026):** small accounts (<10K followers) 2–6 weeks to regain prior reach; large accounts (>100K) 4–12 weeks. Bigger accounts fall harder because more of their distribution rides on recent-performance priors.
- **Burst-then-gap is the single most damaging pattern** — worse than a permanently lower steady cadence. 5/week sustained beats 14/week for two weeks then three weeks of nothing. (Strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.)
- **Planned absences playbook** (road trips, playoffs, facility buildout crunch):
  1. Bank 5–10 evergreen clips before the gap; schedule them at the normal cadence via a scheduler (Metricool is already in Mayday's stack).
  2. If banking is impossible, drop to a reduced-but-nonzero cadence (2/week) rather than zero. The data punishes zero, not slow.
  3. On return, resume the prior schedule — do NOT "make up" missed posts with a burst (spam/cannibalization risk compounds the recovery problem).
- **Do not "rest the account to reset the algorithm."** There is no reset mechanic; a deliberate pause just triggers the recovery timeline above. The only pause that helps is the warm-up-repeat protocol for a genuinely throttled/zero-view account (§7).

## 6. Deleting and reposting — settled answers (as of 2026)

- **Does deleting a flopped video and reposting it help? No.** The repost enters a fresh test batch, but the account carries the same content and the same audience — measured outcomes are neutral-to-negative, and repeated delete/repost cycles look manipulative to the platform. The "deleting caused my shadowban" folk belief is a myth, but so is "reposting gives it a second chance at viral."
- **The correct move for a video you want gone: set it to PRIVATE, don't delete.** Privating preserves account history and metrics without the churn signal.
- **The one case where deleting is right:** a video actually flagged for/violating community guidelines. Deleting the offending video is the fastest shadowban recovery path (Loomly's ranked recovery list: 1. delete offending video, 2. appeal — can take weeks, 3. clear cache, 4. keep posting consistently, 5. brief pause).
- **Shadowban detection tactically:** banner "Your video is not eligible for recommendation in the For You feed," or a sudden drop to 0–50 views several hours after posting on an account that normally clears that easily. No fixed duration; severity- and repeat-dependent.
- **Quietly restricted content categories to know (May 2024 guideline expansion, still active as of 2026):** "dieting and extreme fitness," hopelessness/sadness statements, **watermarked content**, and **extremely short videos**. The extreme-fitness one matters for Neptune content — frame training clips as skill development, not punishment workouts / extreme transformation. Never post watermark-bearing crossposts (also an Instagram kill-switch — see Carl doc §2).
- **Legitimate re-use of old content:** don't delete-and-repost the same file. Re-edit (new hook, new caption, new first frame) and post as a new video 4+ weeks later, or use TikTok's native "Repost" button (which is a share, not a re-upload, and carries no penalty).

## 7. New account vs. established cadence

### New-account warm-up (Trevor-relevant for any Neptune Performance account launch)

Consensus protocol across 2025–26 sources (reel.farm, Conbersa, TokPortal):

- **Days 1–3:** create account, then ONLY consume. 20–30 min/day scrolling FYP; search and watch in-niche content (youth baseball, pitching, training). Do not post. Do not blitz-edit the profile on day 1.
- **Days 3–7:** continue 20–30 min/day. Add light, human-rate engagement: like ~1 in 15–20 videos, comment occasionally, follow 1–2 in-niche accounts per session. Fill out bio/avatar mid-week, not all at once. Success check: your own FYP is now serving your niche back to you — the algorithm has profiled the account.
- **Days 7–10:** first post. Low-stakes, native-feeling content matching what the account has been consuming. Expectation for a properly warmed account: **150–300 views**. Near-zero views = trust problem; repeat a consume-only week before posting again.
- **Days 8–14:** ramp to 1/day; test two different posting windows and two formats. Keep 5–10 min/day of consumption/engagement permanently — accounts that "post more than they consume" get throttled; dump-and-leave behavior is a bot signal.
- **Graduation criterion:** consistently clearing 100+ views per post = warm-up done; shift to content optimization and the normal cadence table in §3.
- **Why this matters:** a fresh account that posts immediately, from a new device/IP, with a stuffed-in-one-session profile, pattern-matches spam farms. This is the #1 cause of "my new account gets 0 views."

### Established accounts

- Skip all warm-up logic; the constraint flips to **schedule predictability**. Pick fixed slots (audience trains on them) and protect the floor cadence (never below 2–3/week).
- Established accounts can absorb experiments new accounts can't: an off-schedule reactive post, a 3-post day around an event, a format swing. The recent-performance prior cushions single misses.
- **A dormant established account restarting after months off should run a mini warm-up:** 3–4 days of daily consumption/engagement before the first new post, then 3/week for two weeks before returning to full cadence. Expect the §5 recovery timeline, not instant prior reach.

## 8. Step-by-step: finding THIS account's best times (do this once per account, refresh quarterly)

1. Switch to a Business/Creator account (free) → Analytics → Followers tab → note top territories and the hourly activity histogram.
2. Mark the two biggest activity humps. Subtract 2–4 hours from each → those are the two candidate posting slots.
3. Post consistently in those slots for 3 weeks (≥9 posts minimum for signal).
4. Pull per-video data (Metricool covers TikTok in Mayday's existing stack): views at 24h and 72h, average watch time, completion %, by posting hour.
5. Compare slots on **median** views (not mean — one outlier wrecks the read) and completion rate. Keep the winner, replace the loser with a new candidate slot, repeat.
6. Re-run quarterly and around season boundaries — baseball audience rhythms shift hard between in-season (game nights occupied) and offseason (evenings free).

## 9. Common mistakes

- **Copying a global "best time" chart in the wrong timezone.** Buffer/Metricool grids are audience-local. If 60% of followers are ET, "post at 6 p.m." means 6 p.m. ET, not PT. Check the Followers territory tab first.
- **Posting AT the peak instead of before it** — the test batch needs runway (§1). Peak-hour posting throws the video into maximum competition with zero seeding.
- **Batch-dumping clips.** Editing session ends, 6 clips upload in 40 minutes → spam pattern, cannibalized test batches, fatigued viewers. Schedule them 3–4+ hours apart across days.
- **Chasing 3x/day without the content to back it** — per-video views drop ~40%, filler dilutes account-level completion averages, and burnout creates the gap that does the real damage.
- **Burst-then-gap** — the most damaging cadence pattern in every dataset. Set the cadence at what's sustainable in the worst week, not the best.
- **Deleting flops.** Flops are harmless data points; delete/repost churn is not. Private if it must vanish.
- **Letting a scheduling debate delay a timely video.** With a 48–72h content lifespan, a topical baseball reaction posted a day late at the "perfect time" is worth less than posting it now.
- **Judging a video at hour 2.** Distribution can ramp for 24h+; slow starts sometimes inflect. Judge at 24–72h.
- **Launching Neptune's account and posting day one.** New-account warm-up (§7) is not optional in 2026; skipping it is the top cause of dead-on-arrival accounts.
- **Treating Saturday as dead for a sports audience** because brand studies say so — creator data (Buffer) crowns Saturday, and youth-baseball weekend rhythms make Saturday morning a live slot. Test it.

## 10. Questions Ashley should ask

- What does the Followers activity histogram actually show for this account, and what's the top-territory timezone split? (Before any timing advice.)
- What cadence can the team sustain in its WORST week — travel, playoffs, facility crunch? That number, not the ambitious one, is the schedule.
- Is this account playing for outlier hits (volume logic, More Mayday clips) or for a finite local audience (consistency logic, Neptune)? The frequency prescription differs (§3 table).
- Is it baseball season? Game-night evenings are occupied in-season; does the schedule move to mornings/afternoons April–October?
- How many clips does the current podcast/long-form pipeline yield per week, and are they being scheduled out or batch-dumped?
- When a video flops, what does the team currently do with it? (Catch delete/repost habits early.)
- Are any posts being cross-posted with visible watermarks? (Restricted-distribution category on TikTok, kill-switch on Reels.)
- For a new account: has it been warmed for 7–10 days, and did post #1 clear ~150 views? If not, stop posting and warm it.
- What happened the last time the account went silent for a week+ — and is there an evergreen bank ready for the next unavoidable gap?
- Are we judging slot experiments on median views over ≥9 posts, or reacting to single-video outliers?
