# Sports & Athlete-Creator Content on TikTok

Tactical reference for operating a baseball / athlete-creator TikTok presence. Platform-mechanics strategy (algorithm, cadence, hooks) lives at the strategy level — see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md`. This doc is the niche layer: what wins in *baseball/sports* TikTok specifically, who wins, why, and the exact formats.

## TL;DR

- **The copyright era is over — the access era started Feb 2026.** MLB reversed its takedown-heavy posture; the expanded MLB×TikTok deal gives *selected* creators access to current + archival footage. A retired MLB player is exactly the profile this program wants. Action: pursue formal creator-program access rather than gray-area clip use.
- **Personality beats highlights for individuals.** Survey and league data agree: behind-the-scenes and personality content is what fans follow *creators* for; raw clips are the league's/teams' job. 40% of sports fans follow players more than teams; Bryce Harper's model (baseball + fashion/cooking/travel/family) is MLB's own cited template (as of 2026).
- **Sports is a high-reach, average-engagement category:** ~793K avg views/post but 6.0% median engagement (Kyra, 2.9M posts, Jan 2026). Translation: discoverability is cheap in sports TikTok; differentiation and retention are the battle, not reach.
- **The winning baseball-creator formats are known and copyable:** POV skits of baseball life (Jackson Olson), former-pro teaching with receipts (Robby Rowland), grind documentation (King of JUCO), trend-hijacks "baseball-ified" (Savannah Bananas' daily 4pm trend meeting), reaction splits, and photo carousels (yes — carousels, TikTok's own coaching point to MLB players).
- **Speed rule for clip content: post within 30 minutes of the moment; virality window is ~48 hours.** Raw phone footage beats broadcast cuts. A fan account with a trending sound routinely out-views the official account posting the same clip.
- **Niche-to-broad expansion = person-first, sport-second (Ilona Maher model):** make people care about *you*, then the sport rides along. Keep ~70% niche baseball content / ~30% broad-personality or trend content; expand via crossover verticals (food, family, fitness, business) one at a time, not all at once.
- **LIVE is the sleeper monetization + relationship channel for athletes:** MLB All-Star Geraldo Perdomo hit TikTok LIVE Pro within a year and became one of the highest-earning athlete accounts via streams/gifts — no brand deal required. Gifts unlock at 1K followers.

## 1. The baseball TikTok landscape (as of 2026)

Baseball is one of the fastest-growing sports communities on TikTok: **#MLB posts grew ~60% in 2025**, MLB's global accounts hold **10.7M followers**, and international views spiked during the 2025 World Series (Japan +426%, Korea +710%). The audience is there and growing; the creator supply in baseball is still thin relative to basketball/football — a genuine structural advantage.

### The athlete-creator macro trend (why the timing is right)

- **82% of the world's most marketable athletes** are active on TikTok; all top-10 2025 WNBA draft picks and 6 of 10 NFL top picks arrived with established presences.
- TikTok's What's Next 2026 theme ("Irreplaceable Instinct"): unfiltered stories and behind-the-scenes beat curated perfection; **creator-access clauses are being written into sports rights deals** — creator-led storytelling is becoming a formal sports-media asset class, not a supplement.
- **70% of sports fans follow athletes/teams on social**; fans who see sports content on TikTok are **42% more likely to tune into live games** — leagues now treat creators as promotion, which is what unlocked the rights thaw below.

## 2. MLB content rules on TikTok (as of 2026)

The February 24, 2026 expanded MLB×TikTok partnership changed the operating rules materially:

1. **Creator program with footage access.** "Select creators" get access to MLB's *current and archival* content library to remix, plus behind-the-scenes access at league events. This is the formalized reversal of the takedown era — Manfred publicly admitted the old approach was wrong ("You gotta go where people are").
2. **MLB Hub in-app** (search "MLB" in TikTok): league posts highlights after every game, recaps, standings, ticket links. Implication: *pure* highlight-reposting as a creator strategy now competes head-on with the league's own machine — don't play that game.
3. **Player activation is league policy now.** ~50 players went through TikTok coaching at 2026 spring training (Bregman launched an account there; Butler, Rodriguez, Perdomo participated). The league explicitly coaches players toward **lifestyle mixing** (fashion/cooking/travel/family) and **photo carousels from existing camera rolls** as a low-effort format.
4. **Practical rights guidance for a retired player:**
   - Footage of *yourself* pitching from broadcasts is still MLB copyright — but enforcement against player/alumni personality use is now near-zero and the strategic wind is at your back. Cleanest path: apply/lobby for creator-program access (an ex-player with a media company is the target profile).
   - Content you film yourself (facility, training, podcast, personal archive, minor-league era personal footage) is unrestricted.
   - MiLB and college/JUCO footage norms are looser; amateur baseball clips are effectively open season.
   - Trends note: official, creator, and fan-generated MLB content now perform at near-equal engagement (60–64% band) — the algorithm doesn't privilege official accounts. The moat is personality, not footage.

## 3. Sports-clip vs personality content: the decision framework

Data points that settle the argument for an individual creator:

- Morning Consult (731 TikTok users): 46–49% want game highlights, 42–46% player highlights, 31% behind-the-scenes — but the *follow* decision skews personality: **40% of sports fans follow players more than teams**, and agency 160over90's field rule is that raw/unpolished content "performs the highest nine out of ten times" (their 80/20 raw-to-polished production split).
- The canonical failure case: official account posts a raw 60-second clip → 40K views; a fan account posts the same clip with a trending sound → 2.3M in 48 hours. The clip isn't the asset; the packaging and persona are.
- SportsPro (2026): "what consistently resonates most with fans is personality-driven content that offers genuine access and authenticity" — both cinematic and phone-raw succeed, but access/personality is the common denominator.

**Rule set:**
- Clips are a *supply chain*, personality is the *product*. Use clips as raw material inside personality formats (reaction, breakdown, story), almost never as standalone posts.
- If a video would perform the same posted by anyone else, don't post it. The test: "does this require *Trevor* to exist?"
- Exception — the 0–24h "Moment" layer: when something happens in baseball that Trevor has unique standing on (a pitcher meltdown, a rule change, a former teammate's moment), a fast raw take beats a polished one. Post within 30 minutes when possible; the virality window is ~48 hours.

### The three content layers (TokPortal framework, adapted)

1. **The Moment (0–24h):** real-time reactions to baseball news/highlights. Speed > production. Phone-raw.
2. **The Narrative (1–7 days):** personality, training, behind-the-scenes, day-in-the-life, storytime. Trending audio amplifies. This is the follower-builder.
3. **The Brand (ongoing):** identity/values/community — for Trevor: the Mayday world, Neptune's buildout, the "retired pitcher building something" arc. Drives loyalty and conversion (facility leads, podcast listens).

Healthy mix for an athlete-creator account: roughly 20% Moment / 60% Narrative / 20% Brand.

## 4. Format library (with specs)

Proven sports/athlete formats, ordered by fit for a retired-MLB-pitcher account:

1. **"Mistakes I made in pro ball" / teaching-with-receipts** (Rowland lane). 30–60s, talking-head or demo, hook names the credential in the first line ("I pitched 9 years in the big leagues and did this wrong the whole time"). Text overlay of the credential every video — cold TikTok viewers don't know who anyone is.
2. **Reaction splits** — athlete watching their own (or others') moments, split-screen or green-screen. The single most reliable way to legally-ish leverage highlight footage: the *reaction* is the original content. Works on old Trevor outings, current MLB moments, amateur submissions.
3. **Breakdowns of live moments** — telestrator-style analysis of a pitch/at-bat from last night, posted same-day. 45–90s. This is the "Moment layer" workhorse and the strongest tune-in driver (the 42% live-tune-in stat is built on this genre).
4. **POV skits of baseball life** (Olson lane) — "POV: your pitching coach in 2004," "every bullpen has this guy." Comedy formats travel far beyond baseball fans; 15–30s.
5. **Training secrets / drill content** — "3 things I did every morning in the big leagues." For Neptune: this doubles as facility lead-gen. (Strategy-level note on local lead-gen jobs: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.)
6. **Pre-game / routine content** — rituals, superstitions, what's-in-the-bag. Evergreen; retired-player nostalgia version works ("my game-day routine, 2019 vs now").
7. **Photo carousels** — TikTok's own #1 low-effort recommendation to MLB players in 2026: career photo dumps with story captions and trending audio. Near-zero production cost; strong for nostalgia arcs.
8. **Trend-hijack, baseball-ified** (Bananas method) — take the week's trending format/sound and put a baseball spin on it. Requires a standing trend-review habit (Bananas do it daily at 4pm; weekly minimum for a solo operation).
9. **Series/ranking shows** (Olson × Gametime model) — "ranking every pitch grip," "rating MLB stadiums I pitched in," "grading viewer mechanics." Series create return viewership on a platform with almost none.
10. **Stats visualized** — performance data as visual storytelling ("what 98mph actually looks like from the box"). Baseball's data richness is underexploited on TikTok.
11. **Failure/comeback narratives** — injury, demotion, release stories. Highest share-rates of any athlete genre; authenticity currency.
12. **TikTok LIVE** (Perdomo lane) — Q&A, watch-alongs during big games (85% of fans second-screen on TikTok during live games), bullpen-session streams from Neptune. Gifts unlock at 1K followers; LIVE Pro status is attainable within a year of consistent streaming; engaged LIVE creators at 20–100K followers report $500–$3K/mo from gifts alone (as of 2026).
13. **Fan-interaction formats** — reply-to-comment videos, duets/stitches of amateur pitching clips ("MLB pitcher reacts to your mechanics"). Cheapest engagement flywheel in the niche; builds the coaching-authority ↔ Neptune bridge.

**Packaging specs that recur across every source (as of 2026):** vertical native, first 2 seconds must be eye-catching (sports-specific phrasing of the universal hook rule), trending sound attached *in-app* (the official posting API cannot attach TikTok sounds — schedulers strip them; post natively from the phone), credential text-overlay in frame 1, caption written as a second hook not a description.

## 6. Niche-to-broad expansion playbook

The pattern every crossover athlete-creator follows (Maher, Harper, Olson):

1. **Establish the credential wedge first.** 2–3 months of unmistakably baseball-expert content until the algorithm and audience know exactly what the account is. Broad content from an unestablished account is just noise.
2. **Person-first framing throughout** (Maher's stated strategy from Tokyo 2021: make them care about *you*, the sport follows). Every baseball video should leak personality; every personality video should leak baseball.
3. **70/30 ratio** — ~70% niche baseball, ~30% broad (trends, humor, lifestyle). Broad content earns cold reach; niche content converts it into the *right* followers. Micro-niche depth also protects monetization (niche creators earn ~3.2x more per follower than broad creators).
4. **Expand one adjacent vertical at a time**, chosen by genuine interest (Harper: fashion/cooking/travel/family). For Trevor, candidate adjacencies: building a business (Neptune buildout documentation), dad/family life, fitness-after-pro-sports, food/travel. Test each as a 4–6 video mini-series; keep what holds baseball-audience retention.
5. **Entertainment crossover formats that carry athletes broad:** trend-hijacks with sport twist, comedy skits (POV), collabs/duets with non-sports creators, "athletes doing normal things badly," music/dance moments (the Bananas' Beyoncé dance: 2.7M views), reality-TV-adjacent bits (Olson's viral Bachelor video), and buildout/business documentation ("I'm turning my MLB money into a training facility" is a *broad* entrepreneurship narrative, not a niche one).
6. **Don't abandon the wedge at scale.** The failure mode is going full-lifestyle once broad content pops — engagement quality decays and the monetizable niche audience churns. Maher still posts rugby; Bananas still post baseball.

## 7. Operating playbooks

### Weekly cadence template (solo/small-team athlete-creator, 4–5 posts/wk)

- **Mon:** Narrative — teaching or storytime (from podcast/YT clip pool or fresh)
- **Tue/Wed:** Moment — reaction/breakdown of whatever happened in baseball (flexible slot; if nothing happened, run a carousel or skit)
- **Thu:** Series episode (ranking/grading recurring bit)
- **Sat or Sun afternoon:** Best piece of the week — trend-hijack or comedy (Sunday peak-engagement slot)
- **Weekly 30-min trend review** (Bananas method, scaled down): scroll FYP + saved-sounds list, pick 1–2 trends to baseball-ify.

### Post-game / big-moment pipeline (Moment layer)

1. Standing alert list: MLB rule news, pitcher-specific moments, ex-teammates, Mariners/Twins/Mets story lines.
2. Within 30 min: phone-raw reaction or green-screen breakdown, one take, no edit beyond captions.
3. Attach a relevant trending sound (low volume under voice) in-app.
4. Caption = hot-take hook, 3–5 niche hashtags (#MLB #pitching + moment-specific).
5. 48h later: if it held, cut a follow-up ("part 2 / what everyone got wrong").

### Content-multiplication from existing Mayday assets

- Podcast tapings and Neptune buildout days are "games" in the Bananas sense: pre-plan 3–5 clip moments per session, capture vertical B-roll on a phone *in addition to* the main cameras. Broadcast-frame crops read as repurposed; phone angles read as native. Clip-selection editorial rules: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.
- Every TikTok that pops → check for a Reels/Shorts variant (re-export clean, no watermark) and a long-form expansion topic.

### Neptune Performance note (job-4 content)

Facility content on the main account should be the *story* of the buildout (broad entrepreneurship narrative). Local lead-gen content (drills, tours, athlete results targeting WA-area parents/players) eventually deserves its own account with local hashtags/geo-tags — a facility short seen by 3K local parents beats 300K out-of-state teens. (Strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.)

## 8. Common mistakes (sports/athlete-specific)

1. **Reposting broadcast-quality highlights unedited.** Wrong aspect, wrong pacing, no sound, competes with MLB's own in-app Hub. Always re-package (reaction, breakdown, alternate angle, sound-matched).
2. **Treating TikTok like a press-release channel** — announcements, schedule graphics, "link in bio" energy. TikTok is entertainment or it's nothing; the Bananas' refusal to sell on main channels is *why* they sell out tours.
3. **Assuming the résumé carries the video.** Cold viewers don't know who Trevor May is. Credential must be restated in the first 2 seconds of every video, forever.
4. **Posting via schedulers/APIs** — official API posts can't attach TikTok sounds and lose native features; sounds are a primary distribution lever in sports. Post natively.
5. **Only polished content.** The 80/20 raw rule; TikTok's own coaching to MLB players targeted exactly this hesitation ("feeling like they'd need to produce something polished").
6. **Burst-then-gap posting around events** — algorithm deprioritizes accounts that go dark between "seasons." Retired-player advantage: no off-season excuse. (Cadence fundamentals: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.)
7. **Skipping trends because they feel off-brand.** Trend + sport twist is the single most reliable cold-reach format in sports TikTok; the twist keeps the brand.
8. **Judging by follower count.** Reach per video and profile visits predict trajectory better; sports reach is high and cheap, so follower vanity is extra misleading here.
9. **Ignoring LIVE.** Athletes systematically undervalue it; it's the highest-intimacy, most direct-revenue surface (Perdomo case).
10. **Going broad too early or all-at-once** — lifestyle pivot before the baseball wedge is established produces an account about nothing.
11. **Gray-area footage paranoia (or recklessness).** As of 2026 the rational play is neither fear nor piracy: build on reaction/breakdown/original formats and pursue official MLB creator-program access.

## 9. Questions Ashley should ask

- Is this video's job Moment, Narrative, or Brand — and are we judging it by that job's metric?
- Does this post require Trevor to exist, or could any clip account post it?
- Is the MLB credential visible/spoken inside the first 2 seconds?
- Was this posted natively with an in-app sound, or through a scheduler that stripped native features?
- For a live-baseball moment: are we inside the 30-minute/48-hour window, or has it expired?
- What's the current niche:broad ratio over the last 30 posts — drifting past 30% broad? Under 10%?
- Which recurring series is currently running, and is it on schedule? (No series = no return viewership.)
- Have we applied for / made contact about MLB's TikTok creator-program footage access?
- What did this week's trend review surface, and did at least one trend get baseball-ified?
- Is Neptune content currently doing the *story* job (broad) or the *lead-gen* job (local) — and is it on the right account for that job?
- When did Trevor last go LIVE, and was it anchored to a live-baseball moment (85% of fans are second-screening)?
- Which format above hasn't been tested in the last 60 days?
