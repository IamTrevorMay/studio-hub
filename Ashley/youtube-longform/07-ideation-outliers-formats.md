# Ideation, Outliers & Format Design (Tactical Reference)

Idea-level optimization: how to find proven demand before filming, transplant formats across niches, package before producing, score a backlog, and turn one win into a repeatable series. For the algorithm/packaging/retention strategy layer, see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md` — this doc assumes that context and goes one level deeper into execution.

## TL;DR

- **The idea sets the ceiling.** Paddy Galloway's data point: 90%+ of a channel's growth typically traces to *one* outlier idea that then becomes a repeatable format. Ideation deserves real hours — top creators spend ~30% of total time on ideation + packaging vs ~5% for small creators.
- **Outlier score = video views ÷ channel baseline (use the MEDIAN of the last 20–50 same-format uploads, not the average).** 2x is noteworthy, 3x+ is the professional working threshold, 10x deserves deep analysis, 20x+ usually involves non-replicable factors. One outlier is a clue; **three similar outliers across different channels is a pattern** — only patterns justify production.
- **Copy the logic, not the video.** Extract the viewer desire and the underlying format from an outlier, then rebuild it with your own proof, angle, and packaging. Format transplanting from *unrelated* niches (golf's "Break 50" → baseball, $1 vs $100 → gear/training) is the highest-upside version of this.
- **Packaging-first is non-negotiable:** draft the title + thumbnail *before* filming. If you can't produce a compelling title/thumbnail pair, the idea isn't strong enough to earn clicks — kill or rework it before spending a production cycle.
- **Validate in ~10 minutes before greenlight:** one-sentence test, demand proof (outliers + search + comments), core-audience + new-viewer appeal, and a title/thumbnail path. The worst time to learn an idea was weak is after the edit.
- **Score the backlog, don't vibe it.** A 10-criterion 1–5 scorecard (demand, pain, fit, angle, title, thumbnail, retention, production reality, monetization, strategic upside) with hard thresholds: 45–50 produce now, 38–44 improve first, 30–37 needs proof, <30 kill or park.
- **Formats > one-offs.** A repeatable format compounds: known cost, improving execution, suggested-feed chaining, audience habit. When one works, "run it until they stop you" — retire it on data (declining outlier multiple across 3 consecutive episodes), not boredom.

## 1. Why idea-level work is the highest-leverage hour

Diagnose channel problems in order: **idea → packaging → retention** (strategy level: see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md`). This doc covers the first box only — the tactics for never entering production with a weak idea.

Two numbers to anchor the time budget:

- Small creators: ~95% of time filming/editing, ~5% ideating/packaging. Top creators and strategists (Galloway): ~30% on ideation/packaging. Ed Lawrence (Film Booth) goes further: **~70% planning, ~20% editing**, and works in this order — idea → thumbnail → title → intro → ending → middle → script → film.
- Galloway's volume prescription for a growth-phase channel: **generate ~100 ideas per week** ("when mining for gold, you must dig through some dirt"). Most die at the scorecard; that's the point. For a channel of Trevor's scale, a realistic team version: 20–30 logged ideas/week, 3–5 surviving to packaging drafts, 1–2 greenlit.

MrBeast handbook framing (leaked 36-page onboarding doc, still the best public artifact on industrialized ideation): the goal is "the best YOUTUBE videos possible," not the best-produced videos — and the writer's rule that applies directly to ideation: **"You can't get inspired by things you don't know exist."** An information diet (watching top performers *outside* your niche ~4 hrs/week per Galloway's 12-step process) is an ideation input, not a leisure activity.

## 2. The outlier research method (full playbook)

### 2.1 The math

```
Outlier score = video views ÷ channel baseline views
Baseline      = MEDIAN views of the channel's last 20–50 uploads, same format only
```

- **Median beats average** — one viral video drags the mean up and makes real outliers look ordinary. Every serious tool (1of10, ViewStats, OutlierKit, vidIQ Outliers) uses a multiplier vs typical channel performance.
- **Never mix formats in the baseline.** Long-form, Shorts, livestreams, and podcast clips distribute differently. A podcast channel's clips will fake-outlier against full episodes and vice versa. Compute a separate baseline per format.
- **Views-per-subscriber is a secondary sanity check, not the core metric.** Rough platform norms (as of 2026): a typical upload pulls ~8–14% of subscriber count in views; a 100K-sub channel typically sees 12–15K views per upload. Useful for spotting dead-subscriber channels and for judging small channels, but subscriber counts are lagging/inflated — the multiplier vs recent median is the metric that matters. (OverseerOS's methodology explicitly excludes views-per-sub from the core calculation for this reason.)

### 2.2 Threshold table (working consensus across 1of10 / OutlierKit / OverseerOS, as of 2026)

| Multiplier | Read | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1.2–2x | Slightly above normal / possibly noise | Log it; look for siblings |
| 2–3x | Noteworthy | Track; useful signal in small niches like baseball training |
| **3x+** | **Professional working threshold** | Analyze packaging + format; candidate for adaptation |
| 8–10x | Major outlier | Deep-dive: watch it, read comments, map the retention promise |
| 20–25x+ | Breakout / extreme | Usually involves non-replicable factors — screen hard before acting |

**Pattern rule:** one outlier = a clue. **Three or more outliers with similar topic/format across *different* channels = a pattern** worth building on. Never greenlight off a single data point.

### 2.3 The workflow (weekly, ~60–90 min)

**Phase 1 — Watchlist (set up once, prune quarterly).** 10–30 channels in three tiers: (a) large authority channels in the niche, (b) mid-size active channels, (c) small channels with momentum. *Weight the small ones* — their breakouts are easier to learn from because they won without brand gravity. For Trevor: baseball training channels, athlete-creator channels (Deestroying, Bryson DeChambeau, Jesser-adjacent sports challenge), MLB-media adjacent, plus 5–10 completely unrelated niches for format transplant scouting (cooking, golf, chess, finance — anywhere formats get invented).

**Phase 2 — Baseline each channel.** Last 20–50 same-format videos → median + typical range → note cadence and format mix.

**Phase 3 — Pull outliers by time window.** Buckets: last 30 days / 3 months / 6 months / 12 months / all-time. **Prioritize the 3–6 month window** — recent enough to reflect current viewer appetite, old enough that view counts have settled. A 4-year-old outlier teaches timeless psychology but not current demand; a 3-day-old one hasn't proven longevity.

**Phase 4 — Screen false positives.** Discard or discount when the outlier is explained by:
- **Celebrity/guest leverage** — ask: does this work without that person? (Big trap in the athlete-creator lane — a video outliers because an MLB star showed up, not because the format works.)
- **News timing** — separate "strategy win" from "speed win."
- **Pure controversy** — clicks without satisfied viewers doesn't transplant.
- **Old evergreen search traffic** — check upload date vs. view accumulation.
- **Algorithm-boost extremes** — 20x+ scores are often lightning strikes; study 3–10x for replicable mechanics.

**Phase 5 — Extract the logic (the step everyone skips).** For each surviving outlier, write three lines:
1. **Viewer desire:** what did the click *actually* buy? (not the topic — the feeling: curiosity resolution, vicarious stakes, transformation, authority access)
2. **Format skeleton:** the mechanical structure (tier comparison, countdown, ladder tournament, constraint challenge, expert-reacts, budget-vs-pro)
3. **Your version:** desire + skeleton rebuilt with your unique proof (for Trevor: MLB credibility, real pro-level velocity/stuff, facility access, big-league stories) and an original title/thumbnail — **"Do not copy the video. Copy the logic."**

### 2.4 Tools (as of mid-2026)

- **1of10** — outlier database + Chrome extension; filter by channel size, outlier score range, upload recency. Fastest for volume scanning.
- **ViewStats** (MrBeast team's tool) — Outliers tab; filter subscriber band (e.g., 10K–100K), sort by outlier score.
- **vidIQ Outliers / OutlierKit** — same multiplier concept; vidIQ is already connected to Mayday Studio via MCP (`vidiq_outliers`, `vidiq_breakout_channels`, `vidiq_channel_videos`), so Ashley can run outlier pulls directly rather than sending Trevor to a dashboard.
- **Manual fallback:** channel → Videos → sort "Popular," cross-reference against recent median by eye. Slow but free; fine for a 5-channel niche like baseball training.

**Practical filter set for Trevor's niches:** subscriber band one tier below and one above the target channel; last 6 months; ≥3x; long-form only; exclude clips/compilations channels (their baselines are meaningless).

## 3. Format transplanting across niches

The single highest-upside ideation move: take a format *proven* in one niche and be the **first to run it in yours**. The audience gets a fresh-feeling video; you get a de-risked structure.

### 3.1 Why it works (mechanism)

A format that outliers repeatedly has already proven its retention curve and click psychology on humans — the niche is just the skin. MrBeast built "$1 vs $1,000,000" empire-scale videos after noticing makeup artists and gamers doing cheap-vs-expensive comparisons: he "distilled these ideas into a replicable format and put his own spin on it." Red Bull transplanted drag-race matchup structure into drone-vs-F1. Ian Lauer (astro-photography) went from "I photographed the Milky Way" to "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 minutes, 1 hour, and 24 hours" — a tier-progression transplant that unlocked the channel (Galloway's go-to case study).

### 3.2 Transplant-ready format skeletons (with baseball/athlete-creator translations)

| Proven skeleton | Origin examples | Baseball / Trevor translation |
|---|---|---|
| Tiered comparison ($1 vs $100; 10 min vs 24 hr) | MrBeast, cooking, photography | $30 glove vs $500 glove; bullpen with 10 min vs 24 hr of prep; $100 vs $10,000 swing lesson |
| Break-the-number challenge | DeChambeau "Break 50" (golf) | "Can I still hit 95?" comeback arc; amateur tries to make contact off pro velo; team scores X runs vs constraint |
| Ladder / 1-on-1 gauntlet with cash stakes | Deestroying "1ON1s" ($10K, open entrants) | Open tryout gauntlet at Neptune; hitters climb a velo ladder; winner gets facility membership/cash |
| Pro reacts / grades amateurs | Music producers, chess, golf | MLB pitcher grades submitted mechanics; reacting to worst/best pitching advice on the internet |
| Constraint challenge | Jesser (blindfolded, weak-hand) | Throwing strikes with eyes closed / opposite hand / from knees; knuckleball-only inning |
| Transformation / X days of training | Fitness, chess, language | 30 days to add 5 mph; retired pro rebuilds arm from zero (documented) |
| Access documentary | F1 Drive-to-Survive-likes, farm channels | Neptune Performance buildout series; what a pro offseason actually looks like |
| Hierarchy explainer / "levels of" | Music theory, cooking | Levels of a fastball: HS → college → AA → MLB (demonstrated live) |

### 3.3 Transplant checklist (run before committing)

1. **Proof depth:** has the skeleton produced ≥3 outliers in ≥2 niches? (Pattern rule applies across niches too.)
2. **Novelty in target niche:** search the format phrasing + "baseball" — if 3+ established channels already ran it, you're late; you need a twist, not a transplant.
3. **Asset match:** does the format exploit something only you have? (MLB career, velocity, facility, network of pros.) A transplant without a proprietary asset is a commodity video.
4. **Stakes translate:** does the tension mechanism survive the niche swap? (Cash stakes, physical risk, status, countdown — something must be at risk on screen.)
5. **Packaging survives:** can you draft a title/thumbnail pair for the transplanted version that works *without knowing the original*? If it needs the reference to make sense, it fails.
6. **Repeatability:** score 1–5 — could this be episode 1 of 20? Prefer transplants that seed formats (Section 6) over one-offs.

## 4. Packaging-first ideation workflow

**Rule: the title and thumbnail are drafted before a camera turns on.** If the packaging can't be made compelling, the idea is dead — no amount of editing rescues an unclickable concept. (Ali Abdaal, MrBeast, and effectively every strategist teach this order; Ed Lawrence's sequence starts idea → thumbnail → title before anything else.)

### 4.1 Per-idea packaging sprint (30–45 min per surviving idea)

1. **One-sentence promise.** "Viewer clicks because ___." If the sentence needs a second clause, the idea is fuzzy.
2. **Draft 8–20 title/thumbnail pairs.** Volume matters — the first three are always the obvious ones. (Benchmark from the strategy layer: top channels draft 8–20 pairs per video; see Carl doc for title/thumbnail craft itself.)
3. **Extremity pass (MrBeast principle).** Push each title toward the superlative/unexpected version: "I Don't Like Bananas" → "Bananas Are The Worst Food On Earth"; "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard" → "I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup." For Trevor: "Facing a college hitter" → "I Challenged the Best College Hitter in America to 10 At-Bats."
4. **Honesty gate.** The packaging must be *exactly* deliverable — expectation mismatch destroys retention and (post-2024 satisfaction weighting) actively hurts distribution. MrBeast's yellow-vs-red bouncy castle rule: if the thumbnail shows yellow, the video must show yellow.
5. **Squint test.** Shrink the thumbnail to phone-feed size, check light/dark mode, 1-second read. Vague concept at small size = redo.
6. **Lock the winning pair as a production requirement.** MrBeast treats the locked title/thumbnail as a "critical component" — production exists to fulfill it, and if mid-shoot reality can't deliver the packaging, escalate immediately (change packaging or kill), don't quietly ship a mismatched video.

### 4.2 Why packaging-first also improves the video

Locking the promise before filming gives the shoot a spine: the first 30–60 seconds must pay off the thumbnail (retention architecture: see Carl doc §retention), the b-roll list writes itself from the thumbnail concept, and the edit has a single question to answer. Videos filmed first and packaged later almost always contain a weaker version of their own best idea.

## 5. Idea validation before production

### 5.1 The 10-minute demand check (run at intake, before packaging sprint)

1. **One-sentence test** — explain the idea in one sentence to someone off-team; if you can't, park it.
2. **Dual-audience test** — does it serve the core audience AND have a handle for new viewers? (For More Mayday: baseball fans + "anyone curious what pro sports is really like.")
3. **Outlier proof** — is there ≥1 outlier (ideally the 3-pattern) showing this topic/format wins? Pull from the weekly outlier log (Section 2).
4. **Search/trend proof** — YouTube autocomplete, Google Trends (rising/stable/seasonal/fading?), vidIQ keyword research. Test three demand types: **topic demand, language demand (the exact words viewers use), format demand.**
5. **Comment mining** — read comments on the closest existing videos: what did viewers ask for, complain about, or wish existed? Unanswered questions in comments are pre-validated ideas.
6. Ten minutes here saves a full production cycle on a video "that was never going to get traction" — the worst time to discover a weak idea is after the edit.

### 5.2 Sequential approval gates

Ideas pass gates in order; each gate kills before the next dollar is spent:

**Research gate** (demand proof exists) → **Angle gate** (differentiated from the 3 closest videos) → **Title gate** (a strong title exists) → **Thumbnail gate** (a clear 1-second visual exists) → **Script-brief gate** (hook + retention mechanism outlined) → **Production gate** (cost/feasibility approved).

This is a miniature of the MrBeast pipeline (dedicated ideation team → internal review narrows → feasibility group assesses cost → tiny fraction reaches final consideration). At Mayday scale: Trevor + one producer can run all gates in a weekly 30-minute meeting (Section 6.3).

### 5.3 Hard kill criteria (any one = stop)

- No clearly identified viewer.
- Title requires explanation.
- Thumbnail concept is vague at squint size.
- Same angle as an existing strong competitor video, no differentiation.
- Exists only to feed the upload schedule.
- High production cost + unclear upside.
- **Athlete-lane addition:** depends on a guest/access that isn't confirmed (guest availability is a "critical component" — no confirmation, no greenlight).

## 6. Idea backlog & scoring system

### 6.1 The scorecard (10 criteria × 1–5, max 50)

| # | Criterion | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Demand proof | Outliers/search/comments show viewers want this? |
| 2 | Viewer pain/desire | Solves a real problem or scratches a real itch? |
| 3 | Channel fit | Sits inside a content pillar? (More Mayday vs Trevor May Baseball vs podcast — score against the *specific* channel) |
| 4 | Original angle | Differentiated from the 3 closest videos? |
| 5 | Title potential | A strong clickable title already drafted? |
| 6 | Thumbnail potential | Clear in one second at feed size? |
| 7 | Retention potential | Structure sustains attention past the hook? |
| 8 | Production reality | Team/budget/access can actually execute? |
| 9 | Monetization/business value | Viewer quality, sponsor fit, Neptune funnel value? |
| 10 | Strategic upside | Could become a series, evergreen asset, or pillar? |

**Thresholds:** **45–50 → produce now. 38–44 → improve first** (usually the fix is angle/title/thumbnail, not the topic). **30–37 → risky; needs stronger demand proof or sharper packaging. <30 → kill or park.**

Simpler alternative for fast triage: **ICE** (Impact × Confidence × Ease, each 1–10) — a team can score dozens of ideas in under an hour. Use ICE for the raw intake pile, the 10-criterion card only for ideas that survive to packaging. Don't over-precision either one; scores are for *ranking and forcing conversations*, not truth.

### 6.2 Four-bucket backlog

1. **Produce Now** — scored ≥45, gates passed, slotted into calendar.
2. **Improve First** — good bones, weak packaging/angle; assign an owner + a specific fix.
3. **Park** — seasonal or access-dependent (e.g., spring-training ideas in November; guest not yet booked). Tag with the unlock condition.
4. **Kill** — logged with one-line reason (kill reasons are pattern data).

Store in one shared system, not personal notes (per Mayday convention: Supabase-backed, synced across machines — never local-only).

### 6.3 The 30-minute weekly approval meeting

0–5 min: present idea, viewer, demand proof, title, thumbnail → 5–10: challenge the demand evidence → 10–15: challenge angle differentiation → 15–20: challenge packaging → 20–25: challenge production feasibility → 25–30: score and decide. Every idea leaves with a bucket, an owner, and a deadline.

### 6.4 Monthly backlog review (calibration loop)

- Compare scorecard predictions vs actual results: which high-scored videos underperformed and why? (Usually criterion 7 — retention — is the most-overestimated.)
- Audit killed/parked ideas: did a competitor prove one you killed? Adjust the scorer, not just the score.
- Re-score parked ideas whose unlock condition changed.
- Update pillar definitions as the channel evolves.

## 7. Series & format design for repeatable wins

### 7.1 Why formats compound

A one-off outlier is revenue; a format is an asset. Formats: (a) train the audience to return (episodic structures show 3–4x higher subscriber retention than one-offs in brand-campaign data); (b) dominate the Suggested feed — the highest-value surface for series channels — because episode N is the natural "watch next" for episode N-1; (c) drop production cost per episode as the crew reps the structure; (d) create a nameable show that sponsors buy (per-episode integration is an easier sell than one-offs).

Galloway's rule when a format hits: **"run it until they stop you"** — don't abandon it because *you're* bored or because copycats appear; the copies validate the demand.

### 7.2 Anatomy of a designed format (spec sheet — write this before episode 1)

1. **Name** — two to four words, appears in every title in a consistent slot ("Break 50," "1ON1s"). The name is the brand.
2. **Fixed skeleton** — the beats every episode repeats (stakes → attempt → escalation → verdict). Viewers return for the ritual.
3. **Variable slot** — the one thing that changes per episode (the guest, the constraint, the dollar amount, the opponent). One variable, not three.
4. **Escalation lever** — how episode 10 outbids episode 2 (bigger stakes, harder opponent, higher velocity). Design the ladder up front so you don't cap out at episode 4.
5. **Packaging template** — reusable title formula + thumbnail composition so each episode is recognizable in-feed AND each title works as a standalone search/browse hook (episodes must be independently clickable; never title "Ep. 7").
6. **Stakes mechanism** — what's at risk on camera. Cash, pride, physical challenge, a public number. No stakes, no format.
7. **Cost/effort ceiling** — max production budget per episode, decided in advance, so the format survives a slow month.

### 7.3 Structure choices (as of 2026)

- **Non-serial (any order)** vs **serial (sequential)**: default non-serial — each episode standalone-discoverable, playlist pulls binge sessions. Reserve serial for genuine arcs (the Neptune buildout documentary is a serial; a challenge format is non-serial).
- **YouTube "Shows" feature** (widely available to eligible channels by 2026): packages videos into seasons/episodes with artwork, Netflix-style presentation, supports both structures. Use it once a format has 6+ episodes — it converts a playlist into a destination.
- **Cadence options:** weekly ongoing show (habit-building, heavy lift), finite season of 6–8 episodes (clear arc, natural sponsor package, lower commitment — the right first move for a new format), or evergreen pillar playlist (standalone videos united by topic).
- **Athlete-creator reference cadence:** DeChambeau runs roughly one long-form challenge/collab every two weeks with "Break 50" as the flagship format — a realistic template for a player-turned-creator with other obligations.

### 7.4 Format lifecycle management

- **Validate before committing to a season:** ship episode 1 as a standalone. Greenlight the season only if it beats channel median (≥1.5–2x) or shows unusual satisfaction signals (comments demanding episode 2 are the strongest signal there is).
- **Health metric:** track each episode's outlier multiple vs the *format's own* median. A healthy format holds or grows its multiple as the packaging ritual gets recognized.
- **Fatigue signals (retire/renovate when):** 3 consecutive episodes declining vs format median; returning-viewer share falling; comments describing predictability. Renovate by swapping the variable slot or pulling the escalation lever before killing the name.
- **Portfolio shape:** Galloway's 80/20 — ~80% of uploads inside proven formats/audience expectations, ~20% experiments that scout the next format. Every experiment that outliers is a format candidate; feed it back through the spec sheet in 7.2.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. **Averaging instead of median-ing the baseline** — one old viral video hides every real outlier the channel has had since.
2. **Acting on a single outlier** — one 8x video is a clue; production requires the 3-across-channels pattern.
3. **Copying the video instead of the logic** — shipping "their video but worse" into an audience that already saw the original.
4. **Ignoring false positives** — transplanting a format that only worked because of a celebrity guest, news timing, or a one-time algorithm spike.
5. **Chasing 20x+ extremes** — the replicable teaching lives in the 3–10x band; lightning strikes don't transfer.
6. **Mixing formats in one baseline** — Shorts/clips/long-form have different distribution; blended baselines produce fake outliers both directions.
7. **Filming first, packaging later** — the video ships with a weaker version of its own best idea, and unclickable concepts eat full production cycles.
8. **Packaging that over-promises** — post-2024 satisfaction weighting means a mismatched thumbnail promise now suppresses future distribution, not just this video's retention (see Carl doc).
9. **Making videos to feed the schedule** — "exists only to maintain cadence" is an explicit kill criterion; a skipped week costs less than a sub-30 idea.
10. **Abandoning a working format out of boredom** — retire on data (three declining episodes), not on the creator's fatigue; conversely, riding a dead format because it *used* to work.
11. **Treating scores as truth** — the scorecard ranks and forces arguments; it doesn't predict. Skipping the monthly calibration loop turns it into theater.
12. **Athlete-lane trap: assuming credentials are the idea.** "MLB pitcher talks pitching" is a topic, not an idea. The credential is the *proof asset* inside a proven format — it multiplies a good skeleton, it doesn't replace one.

## 9. Questions Ashley should ask

**Before greenlighting any video:**
- What's the demand proof — which outliers, at what multiple, on which channels? Is it one clue or a three-video pattern?
- What's the one-sentence promise, and does the drafted title/thumbnail pair deliver it without explanation?
- What does this video do that the three closest existing videos don't?
- What's the critical component (guest, access, footage) and is it confirmed?
- Which channel/pillar does this belong to, and would it score differently on the other channel?

**On the outlier log:**
- What are this week's ≥3x outliers in the baseball, athlete-creator, and transplant-scout watchlists?
- Which recurring skeleton keeps appearing across niches that hasn't hit baseball yet?
- Does this outlier survive the false-positive screen (guest? news? controversy? old search traffic)?

**On formats:**
- Which of the last quarter's videos beat channel median hard enough to deserve a format spec sheet?
- For each running format: what's the current episode multiple vs format median — holding, growing, or three-episodes declining?
- What's the escalation lever for episode 10, and what does an episode cost at ceiling?
- Is the 80/20 balance holding, or has the channel drifted to 100% safe formats (or 100% experiments)?

**On the backlog:**
- What's in Improve First with no owner or fix assigned?
- Which parked ideas just had their unlock condition change (season, guest booked, Neptune milestone)?
- Last month: which high-scored video underperformed, and which criterion lied?
