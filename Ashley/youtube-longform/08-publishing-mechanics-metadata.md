# Publishing Mechanics & Metadata (Long-Form)

Execution-level reference for everything that happens between "export finished" and "first hour live." Packaging strategy (idea, title psychology, thumbnails, retention) lives at the strategy layer — see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md`. This doc is the mechanics: what to type into every field, in what order, with what specs, and why.

## TL;DR

- **Upload as private/unlisted 12–24h before publish, never straight to public.** This lets the ad-suitability check, copyright check, HD processing, and auto-dub generation all finish *before* the video goes live — a video that publishes while still processing at 360p or with a pending yellow icon burns its best impressions window.
- **The first ~150 characters of the description are the only part that ranks meaningfully.** Primary keyword in the first sentence (YouTube uses it as the search snippet), 2–3 hashtags on the first line or last line, chapters block next, links *after* the keyword text — never before.
- **Tags are functionally dead (as of 2026).** Official guidance and controlled experiments both confirm they only catch misspellings. Spend ≤2 minutes: 5–8 tags, first one = exact target phrase, done. Reinvest the time in the pinned comment and end screen.
- **Chapters: 3+ timestamps, first at 00:00, each ≥10s, 4–8 word keyword-front-loaded names.** Use them on instructional/segmented content (drills, breakdowns, podcast episodes); skip or minimize on narrative builds where skipping kills the payoff.
- **End screens: 20 seconds, TWO elements max (one video + subscribe), verbally sold with a specific curiosity hook.** The 4-element template grid underperforms. Benchmark 3–8% end-screen CTR; a spoken "Link–Curiosity–Promise" outro has taken channels from ~3% to 15%+.
- **Instant/scheduled publish beats Premiere for almost everything Trevor makes.** Premieres only pay off when you can actually gather a live audience (season announcements, milestone videos, podcast events) — there is no algorithmic bonus, only an event-marketing one.
- **Buffer's 1.8M-video study (Mar 2026): long-form peaks Sunday 10am, mornings 8–11am, Sun/Tue/Mon strongest; Shorts peak Friday–weekend evenings.** Your own audience analytics override this, but it's the right prior for a US baseball audience.
- **Turn auto-dubbing ON and leave it on.** Free, 27 languages, no algo penalty, 6M+ daily viewers watch 10+ min of dubbed content (YouTube, Feb 2026). Baseball is huge in Spanish- and Japanese-speaking markets — this is found money for Trevor May Baseball.

## 1. The master upload checklist (run every time)

The single highest-leverage habit in this doc: a fixed pre-publish sequence so no view-driving step is ever skipped. Order matters — several steps depend on earlier ones.

### T-minus 12–24 hours (upload day, video stays private/unlisted)

1. **Rename the video file before upload** to the target phrase (e.g. `how-to-throw-a-changeup-mlb-pitcher.mp4`). Minor signal, zero cost.
2. **Upload as Private** (unlisted also works; private is safer for leaks).
3. **Title** — final packaged title (strategy: Carl doc §packaging). Mechanics: 100-char hard limit, keep ≤70 to avoid truncation, primary phrase inside first 50 chars (mobile truncates ~50–60).
4. **Description** — full structure per §2 below.
5. **Thumbnail** — upload custom; confirm it reads at 120px wide (phone-size squint test).
6. **Playlist(s)** — add to the correct series playlist(s) at upload time, not later (§10).
7. **Audience setting** — "Not made for kids" (baseball instruction and podcast content is general audience; MFK kills comments, end screens, cards, personalization, and notifications). Set age restriction = no.
8. **Automatic chapters/key moments toggle** — leave ON, but plan to override with manual chapters (§5).
9. **Tags** — 5–8, two minutes max (§3).
10. **Language + captions** — set video language explicitly (feeds auto-dub and translation). If a script exists, upload it as SRT/VTT; otherwise flag auto-captions for review after processing.
11. **Category** — Sports for TMB/game content; People & Blogs or Entertainment for More Mayday vlogs; whichever is *accurate*, not aspirational.
12. **Recording date/location** — fill for event content (games, facility openings); skippable otherwise.
13. **Cards** — place 1–3 per §6.
14. **End screen** — build per §6. Requires video ≥25s; occupies last 5–20s.
15. **Self-certification questionnaire** — answer honestly and precisely (§12). This is what unlocks instant monetization at publish.
16. **Checks tab** — wait for copyright + ad suitability to finish. Green across the board or resolve before scheduling.

### T-minus 0 (publish moment)

17. **Schedule or publish** per §8–9. If scheduling: verify date, time, *and timezone* in the scheduler.
18. **Within 5 minutes of going live: post and pin the pinned comment** (§7). First-hour engagement compounds.
19. **Verify HD is live.** If the watch page still shows ≤480p, processing isn't done — this is the #1 argument for the 12–24h private buffer.
20. **Drop the link** in Community tab / Substack / IG story / X per the cross-promo plan (strategy level: Carl org-marketing docs).

### T-plus 1 hour

21. **Re-check monetization icon.** Self-certified videos can be re-rated by automated systems within ~1 hour of publish (official). Green→yellow flips get appealed immediately (§12).
22. **Reply to the first 10–20 comments.** Also heart 3–5. This is a mechanical task, not optional community warmth — early threads are read as community vitality.
23. **Skim auto-captions** for name/term errors ("Trevor May," "Neptune Performance," pitch names, player names) and fix egregious ones.

Creators who run a written checklist like this consistently report meaningfully higher CTR outcomes simply because nothing gets skipped (View2 checklist analysis, 2026 — treat the exact "20–40%" figure as directional, the mechanism as real).

## 2. Descriptions & keyword indexing

### What YouTube actually reads (as of 2026)

The indexing system reads title, description, spoken transcript, and captions to build semantic understanding of the video. It does not need keyword stuffing — modern NLP flags forced insertion as spam-adjacent. The description's ranking weight is concentrated in the visible-before-"Show more" portion (~first 150 characters on the watch page; first sentence doubles as the search snippet).

### The structure (top to bottom)

```
[Line 1] One natural sentence containing the primary phrase. ≤150 chars.
         e.g. "A former MLB pitcher breaks down how to throw a changeup
         that actually gets swings and misses."
[Line 2] Second hook sentence — what the viewer gets / why it matters.
[blank]
[Chapters block] 00:00 ... (see §5)
[blank]
[Body] 150–300 words of natural context: what's covered, who it's for,
       related terms woven in (grip, arm speed, pitch tunneling — the
       vocabulary the target viewer would search).
[blank]
[Links] Playlist link for the series → other channel → podcast →
        Neptune Performance / Substack → socials. Most-valuable first.
[blank]
[CTA] One line: subscribe framing or next-video pointer.
[Hashtags] 3–5 (see below).
```

### Hard specs

- **Description limit:** 5,000 characters.
- **First ~150 chars** show above the fold on watch page; **first sentence** is the search snippet.
- **200–300+ words of body text** is the practical sweet spot (vidIQ, Spicer, multiple 2026 guides converge here). More is fine if it's genuinely useful (gear lists, sources, guest bios for podcast episodes); padding is not.
- **Links must come after keyword text**, never as the opening lines — links-first is one of the most common indexing self-owns.
- **Don't paste identical boilerplate as the whole description** across videos. A consistent footer block (links, socials) is fine; the top half must be unique per video.

### Hashtag mechanics (distinct from tags)

- 3–5 per video; hard rule: **exceed 15 and YouTube ignores ALL of them**.
- The **first three** hashtags in the description render as clickable links above the title — free real estate that doesn't consume title characters.
- Mix one broad + one niche + one branded: e.g. `#baseball #pitchingtips #TrevorMay` (TMB) or `#mlb #podcast #MaydayPod` (podcast clips).
- Hashtags matter more on Shorts (category inference) than long-form; on long-form they're a minor navigational aid, not a ranking lever.

### Description refresh as a lever

Metadata is editable forever. For evergreen TMB instructionals that pick up search traffic, revisit the description quarterly: update year references, add the newest related video link, fix dead links. Old videos resurface on demand spikes (see Carl doc §1.1) — stale metadata on a resurfacing video is a wasted gift.

## 3. Tags: the verdict (settled, as of 2026)

**They only matter for misspellings. Everything else is folklore.**

Evidence stack:

- **Official docs:** "Tags can be useful if the content of your video is commonly misspelled. Otherwise, tags play a minimal role in your video's discovery."
- **YouTube staff (Todd, Tom — Creator Insider):** repeatedly told creators to spend their time on title, thumbnail, description instead.
- **Controlled experiments (Social Video Plaza):** a video with zero tags ranked fine; unique nonsense tags (`lk0P3fd`, `jop2dsa`) added to live videos returned *zero* search results even after 29 days — tags aren't even indexed for search; identical tags on two videos produced no suggested-video linkage.
- **UI tell:** tags are buried under a collapsed "Show more" in the upload flow. YouTube tells you what matters by what it surfaces.

**Protocol:** 5–8 tags, ≤2 minutes. First tag = exact target phrase. Add genuine misspelling risks ("Trever May", "changup"), channel name, 2–3 topic terms. Stop. Never buy a tag tool for this.

## 4. Titles — mechanics only

Strategy (curiosity, payoff-matching, CTR psychology) is Carl-doc territory. Mechanical constraints to enforce at upload:

- 100-char hard limit; **≤70 chars** avoids truncation in search, **≤50–60** avoids mobile truncation.
- Primary phrase within the first 50 characters when the video targets search (most TMB instructionals do; More Mayday browse-driven videos can prioritize pure curiosity).
- Formulas that survive contact with search: *Keyword + Benefit*, *How to + Keyword + Outcome*, *Number + Keyword + Promise*.
- Titles are editable post-publish — title/thumbnail swaps on underperformers are a legitimate 48–72h play, and YouTube's built-in **Thumbnail Test & Compare** (A/B up to 3 thumbnails) should be on for every upload.

## 5. Chapters

### Specs (hard requirements)

- Minimum **3 timestamps** in the description.
- First one **must be `00:00`**.
- Each chapter **≥10 seconds**.
- Manual chapters **override** auto-chapters; leave the "Allow automatic chapters" toggle on as a fallback but write your own on anything that matters.

### Naming rules

- **4–8 words**, keyword front-loaded: "Changeup Grip: The Circle Change" beats "Part 2".
- **Curiosity over conclusion** — "Why This Grip Fails for Most Pitchers" outperforms "Grip Mistake: Fingers Too Wide" (don't give the answer away in the chapter list).
- Match what's said aloud at the segment start, and make sure the first visual of the chapter matches the label.
- Never generic ("Part 1 / Part 2 / Outro").

### The data, honestly

- A Munch analysis of 1,000+ videos: chaptered videos ran **2.18–2.8× higher like-to-view ratio**; for videos under 20 min, engagement rate 2.96% vs 1.25% unchaptered. (Correlation-heavy — chaptered videos are also just better-produced on average — but the direction is consistent across sources.)
- Chapters surface as **"key moments" in Google Search** (SeekToAction schema) — a second discovery surface, especially strong for how-to queries like "changeup grip." Manually labeled chapters get indexing priority over auto-detected ones.
- **The trade-off is real:** chapters let viewers skip. On instructional/segmented content that's net-positive (they find value instead of leaving). On narrative builds (More Mayday storyline vlogs, challenge videos with a payoff), chapters invite skipping to the end and can hurt retention.

### Decision rule for Mayday channels

| Content | Chapters? |
|---|---|
| TMB drills, mechanics breakdowns, gear reviews | Yes, always — keyword-rich names, this is search fuel |
| Podcast full episodes | Yes — one chapter per topic/segment; doubles as clip map for the edit team |
| More Mayday narrative/challenge videos | No, or 2–3 vague non-spoiler chapters at most |
| Facility/Neptune build-out vlogs | Light chapters (milestone beats), non-spoiler names |

Diagnostic: read the retention graph against chapter starts. Chapters that consistently spike are future standalone-video ideas; chapters that consistently start a cliff get cut or shortened in future formats.

## 6. Cards & end screens

### Cards (the "i" teasers)

- Up to **5 per video**; each = video, playlist, channel, or link.
- Average teaser CTR is **under 1%**; **1–3% is healthy**. Low absolute numbers — but they fire during peak attention, so placement is everything.
- **Placement rules:**
  - **Never in the first 30 seconds** — that's the hook zone, retention is most fragile there, and a card is a distraction you paid for with editing effort.
  - **Sweet spot: the 50–75% mark** of the video, ideally timed to a verbal reference ("I broke down his full outing in another video — card's up in the corner").
  - **Never in the last 20 seconds** — it competes with the end screen and suppresses clicks on both.
- Cards pointing to videos **in the same topic cluster** materially outperform random cross-links (same-cluster linking is also what builds suggested-traffic loops between videos).
- Practical cap: **1–3 cards**, each verbally motivated. Silent cards are near-worthless.

### End screens

**Specs (official):** last **5–20 seconds** of the video; video must be **≥25s**; up to **4 elements** (video, playlist, subscribe, channel, approved-website link for YPP channels). Elements can't overlap; ~20s is the practical default.

**What actually converts:**

- **Two elements beat four.** The best-performing layout across multiple analyses is **one video element + one subscribe button**. The default 4-tile template fragments attention and reads as "the video is over, leave now."
- **Benchmarks:** platform-wide end-screen CTR runs **3–8%**; **>10% is exceptional**; **<3%** means a visibility/scripting/relevance problem. Channels consistently >8% on series content see compounding suggested traffic.
- **The outro must be designed for the overlay:** keep the right side / lower-right clear, keep talking (dead-air outros with music get swiped), and use a branded 15–20s outro template so the edit team doesn't reinvent it weekly.
- **Speak the click.** The "Link–Curiosity–Promise" pattern — name the linked video, open a loop about it, promise the payoff ("Next I test whether a college arm can learn this in one session — the ending genuinely surprised me, it's right here") — has moved channels from ~3% to 15%+ on individual videos. "Check out this video next" converts poorly.
- **Element choice:** use a **specific video** when a true sequel/series-next exists (podcast → next episode; drill video → progression video); use **"Best for viewer"** when there's no obvious sequel and you'd rather let the algorithm pick per-viewer.
- **Maintenance:** end screens are editable post-publish. When a new video supersedes an old "watch next," update the old end screens on your top-10 traffic videos — this is a 20-minute quarterly task that redirects real session traffic.

**Measurement:** Studio → Analytics → per-video → Engagement tab → "End screen element click rate" (and card teaser clicks). Track it per format, not per video.

## 7. Pinned comments

The pinned comment is a free above-the-fold slot most creators waste. Mechanics:

- **Post it within the first 5 minutes of publish** and pin immediately — early engagement compounds, and the pin seeds the thread structure for the whole comment section.
- **Optimize for viewer-to-viewer replies, not replies to you.** Threads (not isolated comments) are what reads as community vitality. Debate-style pins produce threads.
- **Low cognitive load wins:** binary choices, side-picking, one-line specific questions answerable in under 3 seconds of thought. For Trevor this writes itself: "Fastball or changeup — which is harder to master?" / "Would you have swung at that 3–2 pitch?" / podcast: "Whose take was right — me or [guest]?"
- Pinned-comment prompts can lift comment replies substantially (one analysis claims up to ~30%; treat the number as soft, the mechanism as solid).
- **Secondary uses** (rotate, don't stack): correction/clarification (beats re-uploading), the one link that matters this week (Neptune waitlist, Substack post, podcast episode), or pinning a great *viewer* comment (rewards quality comments and visibly trains the section).
- One pin per video; keep it 1–2 lines; you can swap it later (e.g. replace the launch-day question with an evergreen link once the video ages into search traffic).

## 8. Premieres vs scheduled vs instant publish

### The truth about Premieres (as of 2026)

**There is no algorithmic bonus for premiering.** Premieres appear in search/home/suggested like any upload; view count and watch time transfer to the video afterward; the value is entirely *event marketing* — a specific time to promote, live chat + chat replay, Super Chats (if enabled), an optional pre-premiere trailer, and a countdown theme (not included in the final video).

**Costs of premiering:** the video's first hour is gated to real-time watching (early viewers can't skip ahead), a sparse premiere chat looks worse than no premiere, and a weak live turnout front-loads mediocre engagement into the video's most-watched window. Limits: no Shorts, no >1080p output, no 360/VR.

### Decision table for Mayday

| Situation | Publish mode |
|---|---|
| Standard TMB instructional / More Mayday video | **Scheduled publish** (default) |
| Podcast episode, regular cadence | **Scheduled publish** at the fixed weekly slot |
| Big announcement (Neptune opening, season of content, milestone) where you'll actually promote a time and show up in chat | **Premiere** — promote 3–7 days out, seed chat yourself, use the trailer slot |
| Time-sensitive reaction (trade deadline take, playoff reaction) | **Instant publish** the moment checks clear — recency beats slot optimization for news-adjacent content |

Rule of thumb: if Trevor won't personally be in the chat and at least ~50–100 concurrents are realistic, don't premiere.

### Scheduled publish mechanics

- Schedule from the private upload (this is what makes the §1 checklist work — checks finish while private, video flips public at the slot).
- Scheduled publish sends subscriber notifications normally and is monetization-safe *if* self-certification and Checks completed first.
- Double-check the timezone in the scheduler every time; it's the most common "why did this go live at 3am" failure.

## 9. Time-of-day / day-of-week (long-form)

### The best current large-N data — Buffer, 1.8M videos, published March 2026

- **Long-form #1 slot: Sunday 10 a.m.** "by a wide margin" (engagement factor 0.95), then Sunday 9 a.m. (0.80), Friday 12 p.m. (0.70).
- **Best window: mornings 8–11 a.m.** Best days ranked: **Sunday, Tuesday, Monday** — a *reversal* of the old "Wed–Fri afternoons" folklore.
- **Worst:** Wednesday/Thursday generally; Thursday 2 p.m. was among the lowest measured (0.11).
- **Shorts are the opposite:** Friday 4–7 p.m. peak, evenings 6–9 p.m., Fri/Sat/Thu best days. Never copy the long-form slot to Shorts.
- Buffer's own caveat: posting time is a real but second-order lever — "not a magic bullet."

### How to apply it (mechanics)

1. **Publish 1–2 hours before your audience's peak**, not at it — YouTube needs time to process, notify, and run the first-viewer test before the wave hits. For a Sunday-10am-ET audience, an 8–9 a.m. ET flip is right.
2. **Override population data with your own:** Studio → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube." Baseball audiences skew to game-adjacent rhythms (weekday evenings in-season, weekend mornings offseason) — check this per channel, TMB and More Mayday audiences will differ.
3. **Consistency beats slot-hunting.** A fixed day+time trains the audience and stabilizes first-hour engagement, which matters more than a theoretically optimal wandering slot. Pick one slot per channel per format and hold it for a quarter before judging.
4. In-season baseball nuance: never publish into the first pitch of a marquee game your audience will be watching; late morning or right-after-game windows work with, not against, the sport's calendar.

## 10. Playlists

Playlists are the cheapest session-length lever on the platform, and (as of 2026) increasingly a discovery surface in their own right — YouTube indexes playlists as standalone search results, and viewers can filter search to playlists only.

**Mechanics that matter:**

- **Add to playlist(s) at upload time** (it's a checklist field, not an afterthought).
- **Series playlists** (Studio setting): mark one playlist per true series (e.g. "Pitching 101," podcast season). Series playlists tell YouTube the canonical order and bias "up next" toward the next episode.
- **Order deliberately:** instructional playlists in learning order (not upload order); podcast in episode order; a "Start Here" playlist for each channel front door, best-performers first.
- **Title playlists like search queries:** "How to Pitch: Complete Mechanics Series" beats "My Pitching Videos." Playlists get their own descriptions — fill them (a few sentences with the topic vocabulary).
- **Link the playlist URL, not the bare video URL,** in descriptions/pinned comments/socials when the video belongs to a series — same click, longer session. Use the `&list=` watch URL so the viewer lands in playlist context.
- **One video, multiple playlists** is fine and good (a changeup video sits in "Pitch Grips," "Pitching 101," and "Most Popular").
- Playlists compound with end screens: end screen playlist element → the exact binge session you designed.

(Strategy level — why series/formats dominate suggested traffic: see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md` §formats.)

## 11. Localization, multi-language audio & dubbing

### State of the feature (as of 2026)

- **Auto-dubbing is available to all creators, 27 languages** (fully rolled out Feb 2026; multi-language audio tracks opened to all creators Sept 2025).
- **"Expressive Speech"** (carries the creator's tone/energy/intonation into the dub) live in 8 languages: English, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Portuguese, **Spanish**.
- Scale signal: **6M+ daily viewers watched 10+ minutes of auto-dubbed content** (YouTube, Dec 2025 figure, announced Feb 2026). A lip-sync pilot (reanimating mouths to match the dub) is in testing.
- **Officially discovery-neutral:** auto dubs have "no negative impact on your original video's discovery." Dubs surface to viewers by their language preference; viewers can always switch to original audio.
- Creators can review/reject individual dubs, supply their own tracks, or disable entirely. The system auto-skips content unsuited to dubbing (music-heavy, near-silent).

### The upside case

- Creators using custom multi-language tracks report **25%+ of watch time from non-primary languages**. MrBeast's canonical example: a video dubbed into ~12 languages went from ~15M to ~200M views — and, critically, his team found **early AI dubs tanked retention** and switched to human voice actors, which held retention at near-parity with English. Lesson: auto-dub is a free floor, human dubbing is the ceiling.

### Playbook for Mayday

1. **Enable auto-dubbing on both channels today** (Studio → Settings → Upload defaults → Advanced). Zero cost, zero algo risk.
2. **Spot-check the Spanish dub** on each big TMB upload (Expressive Speech supports Spanish) — baseball's second language is Spanish (Dominican, Venezuelan, Mexican, Puerto Rican audiences); pitch-term mistranslations are the main risk. Reject bad dubs per-video rather than disabling globally.
3. **Watch the signal:** Analytics → Audience → subtitle/audio-track usage + geography. If Spanish watch time on TMB instructionals crosses ~10%, that justifies step 4.
4. **Escalation path:** human-voiced Spanish tracks (uploaded as custom multi-language audio) on the top-10 evergreen TMB videos, and eventually Japanese (huge baseball market, not in Expressive Speech yet — auto-dub only, so QC harder).
5. **Metadata localization rides along:** add translated titles/descriptions (Studio → Subtitles → Add language) for any language exceeding ~5% of watch time; multi-language *thumbnail text* is a reason to keep thumbnail text minimal or visual-only on instructionals.

## 12. Monetization checks pre-publish

### How the system works (official, as of 2026)

- Every upload gets an **ad-suitability review** against advertiser-friendly guidelines, which apply to the video content **and the thumbnail, title, description, and tags** — a clean video with a violent/edgy thumbnail still gets limited.
- **Self-certification:** monetizing channels rate their own uploads via a questionnaire. Creators with a **high accuracy rating** get their own rating used as the initial decision — publish fully monetized instantly — though automated systems can re-rate **within ~1 hour** of publish. Low-accuracy or new channels wait on automated checks. Consistent honest answers → YouTube defers to you more. Gaming it destroys the rating; never "all clean" a video with real profanity.
- **Icons:** green = full ads; **yellow = limited/no ads (typically a 50–90% revenue haircut on that video)**; "checking" = in review.

### The workflow (this is why §1 says private-first)

1. Upload private → fill self-certification honestly → **Checks tab runs ad suitability + copyright while private**.
2. Yellow while private? You can: request **human review** (worth it whenever you believe it's wrong — reviews of borderline sports content frequently flip green), trim the offending segment (Editor works pre-publish), or re-cut and re-upload with zero public cost.
3. Only schedule/publish once green (or once you've consciously accepted yellow on a video that's worth publishing anyway).
4. Re-check at T+1h post-publish (the automated re-rate window).

### Mayday-specific tripwires

- **Profanity:** the current guideline regime keys hardest on the **first ~7 seconds** and on density/title/thumbnail profanity. Podcast episodes with guests are the main risk surface — keep the cold open clean, bleep or dip the first minute, and self-certify the profanity question accurately.
- **Copyright ≠ ad suitability:** MLB game footage triggers **Content ID claims** (revenue to claimant or block), a separate system from the yellow icon. The Checks tab surfaces both pre-publish — another reason for the 12–24h buffer. Commentary-over-stills or short transformed clips are the standard mitigation; a claim on a TMB breakdown should be caught and disputed/trimmed *before* the video is public.
- **"Dangerous acts"** flags occasionally hit training content (weighted-ball programs, max-effort stunts). Framing as professional instruction in title/description helps; request human review on any flag.
- Betting/DFS sponsor reads live in a sensitive category — keep sponsor language measured and check the icon before publish on any episode containing one.

## Common mistakes (field-observed, ranked by cost)

1. **Publishing straight to public** — video goes live at 360p, unchecked for claims, with a "checking" monetization icon, into its best impression window.
2. **Links as the first lines of the description** — burns the only description real estate that ranks.
3. **Four-element end screen template with a silent music outro** — attention fragments, nobody clicks, session ends.
4. **Premiering everything** — gates early viewers to real-time watching and showcases an empty chat, for zero algorithmic benefit.
5. **Cards in the first 30 seconds or last 20** — sabotages the hook / cannibalizes the end screen.
6. **Chapter names that spoil the payoff** on narrative videos — invites skip-to-end behavior.
7. **>15 hashtags** — silently voids all of them.
8. **Identical boilerplate descriptions across the channel** — indexer reads the channel as thin/duplicative.
9. **Agonizing over tags** — 2013 behavior; measurable ranking effect is ~nil.
10. **Setting "Made for kids" defensively** — kills comments, end screens, cards, notifications, and personalized serving; only use when legally accurate.
11. **Never updating old end screens/descriptions** — top evergreen videos keep pointing at stale "next" videos for years.
12. **Timezone errors in the scheduler** — the classic 3 a.m. accidental launch.
13. **Pinning the comment hours late (or never)** — misses the first-hour thread-seeding window.
14. **Ignoring the T+1h monetization re-check** — a quiet green→yellow flip goes unappealed through the whole revenue-relevant view window.

## Questions Ashley should ask

**Diagnosing an underperforming upload (mechanics pass, after ruling out idea/packaging — Carl doc order: idea → packaging → retention):**
- Was it public before processing/checks finished? What did the first hour look like in real time?
- What's the end-screen CTR and card teaser CTR on this format vs the channel norm?
- Is the primary phrase in the first description sentence, and is the first line links-free?
- Did it publish into the audience's dead zone (check "When your viewers are on YouTube," not Buffer's averages)?
- Was there a pinned comment in the first 5 minutes, and did it produce threads?

**Auditing the standing setup (quarterly):**
- What do the top-10 evergreen videos' end screens point to — is anything newer a better "next"?
- Which chapters spike on retention graphs (→ standalone video ideas)? Which cliff (→ cut the segment from the format)?
- What % of watch time is non-English per channel, and does anything cross the human-dub threshold?
- What's the self-certification accuracy rating, and did any video flip yellow post-publish this quarter without an appeal?
- Are series playlists set as *series* in Studio, ordered for learning/watching (not upload order)?

**Before a big launch (Neptune announcement, season premiere):**
- Is this genuinely a Premiere-worthy event — will Trevor be in chat, and are 50+ concurrents realistic?
- Is there a trailer for the premiere slot? Is the countdown promoted 3–7 days out across IG/X/Substack?
- Has the video sat private through full checks, with a human review already requested on any borderline flag?
- Is the pinned comment drafted, and is the end screen pointing at the single next action we want (waitlist link video? subscribe?)?
