# Community Tab, Posts & Non-Video Reach Levers (2026)

Non-video distribution: everything that reaches viewers between uploads — community posts, the subscriptions feed, bell notifications, memberships/Join, and Hype. This is the tactical layer; algorithm/packaging strategy lives in the Carl docs (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md).

## TL;DR

- **Community posts are a distribution surface, not a bulletin board.** Posts surface in subscribers' Home feeds and the Subscriptions tab, and (as of 2026) image posts can additionally surface in the **Shorts feed** — high-engagement posts leak to non-subscribers with similar watch histories. Treat every post as a reach asset with a hook, not a channel-page footnote.
- **Polls win on raw interaction** — practitioner consensus and a 2025 Vidooly study put poll posts at **3–4x the interactions of text-only posts**. Default format hierarchy: poll/image-poll > quiz > image+question > video share with commentary > bare text.
- **Cadence sweet spot: 2–3 posts/week, hard ceiling ~1/day.** More than ~3 polls/week triggers audience fatigue; sporadic posting (long gaps) is worse than none because feed placement builds on recent engagement history. Distribution of a post peaks in the first 24–48 hours.
- **Use image polls as a free pre-production thumbnail/idea lab**: 2-option thumbnail votes and 3–4-option topic polls before scripting. Caveat: poll voters are your warmest fans, not the cold browse audience — treat results as directional, then confirm with post-upload Test & Compare (native A/B, up to 3 thumbnails, decided on watch-time share).
- **The bell is nearly dead as a reach lever.** Only subscribers with "All" + account-level + device-level notifications on get pushed; YouTube caps channels at ~3 notifications per 24h and is testing engagement-based filtering even for "All" opt-ins (as of 2025–26). Optimize the subscriptions-feed impression (packaging) instead of chasing bell CTAs.
- **Memberships convert 0.5–3% of subscribers** (education 2–4%, entertainment 0.5–1%); most successful setups use 3 tiers around $2.99/$4.99/$9.99; YouTube keeps 30%. ~20% of new members join after seeing a members-only content promotion in feed (YouTube's own stat). Members-only community posts are the cheapest sustainable perk.
- **Hype (launched globally Aug 2025) is a real discovery lever for sub-500K channels**: every viewer gets 3 free hypes/week, videos are hypeable for 7 days, smaller channels get a points multiplier onto regional leaderboards that feed browse recommendations. Both More Mayday and Trevor May Baseball likely qualify — build the 30–60s ask into videos and a day-2 community-post reminder.

## 1. Why non-video reach matters (framing, 30 seconds)

Between uploads, a channel's relationship with its audience decays or compounds. Non-video surfaces do four jobs: (1) keep the channel present in subscriber feeds so the next upload's first-24h velocity is higher, (2) generate zero-production-cost engagement signals, (3) de-risk production by testing packaging/ideas before you shoot, (4) monetize the warmest 0.5–3% via memberships. Practitioner data points worth holding loosely (single-source, directional): channels posting community content ≥3x/week report **18–25% higher subscriber retention**, and active-community weeks outperform quiet weeks by **15–30% on new-video launches** (Spicer). Nobody serious claims posts directly boost video ranking; the mechanism is warmer subscribers → better early response → better distribution.

## 2. Community posts: mechanics and surfaces (2026 state)

### Eligibility & access
- The old 500-subscriber threshold was **eliminated in 2023**; as of 2026 essentially all channels have posts, provided: advanced features enabled, no active Community Guidelines strikes, channel not set "Made for Kids," not a supervised account.
- Posts tab appears within 24–48h of meeting requirements. In early 2025 YouTube renamed the public-facing tab from "Community" to **"Posts."**

### Where a post can appear (each is a distinct reach surface)
1. **Channel page** — Posts tab + optional Posts shelf on the Home tab (feature via channel customization).
2. **Subscriber Home feed** — inserted between video recommendations for subscribers; this is where most impressions come from.
3. **Subscriptions feed** — chronological placement alongside uploads.
4. **Shorts feed** — official: "image posts may additionally show on the Shorts feed" (as of 2026). This makes image posts the only post type with a vertical-feed discovery path.
5. **Non-subscriber Home feeds** — when a post's engagement-per-impression is high, YouTube shows it to non-subscribers with similar watch histories. Home feed ranks on relevance, not subscription status.
6. **Notifications** — some subscribers get post notifications (subject to the same filtering as video notifications).
7. **Google Search** — through 2025–26 Google has been indexing more social content; community posts can appear for some queries in select markets. A post with a clear topic + a link to the video can surface alongside the video itself.

### Distribution shape
- Feed distribution **peaks in the first 24–48h**, then decays to channel-page/search discovery. Post when ~50–67% of your audience is active (Studio → Audience → "When your viewers are on YouTube"), typically 1–2 hours before peak.
- Early engagement (first-hour votes/comments) drives broader push — same expand-on-response logic as videos. Reply to early comments to compound it.
- **Overposting throttles**: exceeding ~1–2 posts/day can reduce post distribution and reportedly trigger temporary posting restrictions.

### Post types and specs (as of 2026 — verify current limits in Studio, these shift)
| Type | Spec notes | Engagement rank | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Text poll** | Up to ~5 options; voting is one tap, results shown instantly (a built-in return-visit loop) | Highest | Topic selection, opinions, audience research |
| **Image poll** | Question + one image per option (up to 4) | Highest | Thumbnail votes, gear/uniform/matchup picks |
| **Quiz** | Up to 4 answers, one correct, optional explanation text | High (competitive pull — people return to check answers) | Baseball trivia, rules knowledge, "guess the pitch" |
| **Image(s)** | Multi-image carousel (up to ~5) | Med-high **only when paired with a question/CTA** | BTS, facility build progress, milestone screenshots, memes |
| **Video share** | Your video or others', with commentary | High when framed ("the moment at 4:32 nobody's talking about") | Resurfacing evergreen catalog; never repost bare links |
| **GIF** | Reaction-style | Mid | Filler/humor between heavier posts |
| **Text only** | Keep to 2–3 sentences ending in a direct question | Lowest | Quick updates only; always add a question or attach media |

### Members-only posts
Any post type can be gated to members — the lowest-effort recurring membership perk that exists (see §6).

### Communities (distinct feature, don't confuse)
Since late 2024 YouTube has been rolling out **"Communities"** — a Discord-like space where *viewers* can start threads on a channel, separate from creator Posts. Opt-in, moderation-heavy. Worth enabling only once there's demonstrated comment-section culture; it does not have the feed-reach properties of Posts.

## 3. Cadence playbook

- **Baseline: 2–3 posts/week. Ceiling: 1/day.** Fewer than 1/week breaks momentum and feed placement; more than ~3 polls/week measurably fatigues voters.
- **Content mix (Spicer's 40/30/30)**: 40% engagement (polls/quizzes/questions), 30% value (tips, BTS, insights), 30% promotional (upload announcements, teasers). If the tab reads as an ad board, engagement rates collapse and feed placement follows.
- **Sample weekly rhythm** (adapt, don't copy):
  - Mon: BTS image + question (facility build, edit bay, bullpen session)
  - Wed: preference poll tied to the content calendar (next video topic / thumbnail vote)
  - Fri: discussion prompt or hot take ("Agree or disagree: ___")
  - Sun: niche quiz (trivia, "guess the exit velo," rules edge cases)
- **Mine the comment section for poll ideas** — recurring debates in comments are pre-validated poll questions.
- **Timing**: late afternoon/early evening in the audience's dominant timezone is the generic answer; the real answer is Studio's audience-activity chart minus 1–2 hours.
- **Consistency beats volume.** Give any community-tab push **4–6 weeks** before judging — feed placement builds on engagement history.

### Engagement benchmarks
- Post engagement rate (interactions ÷ impressions): **2–5% is healthy under 50K subs; 1–3% for larger channels** (practitioner benchmark, not official).
- Poll posts ≈ 3–4x interactions of text posts (Vidooly 2025). Two-option polls generate the highest vote counts; cap at 4 options.

## 4. Using posts to test thumbnails and ideas before production

This is the highest-ROI use of the tab for a production-heavy operation like Mayday. (Packaging-first workflow strategy: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md — this section is only the pre-production testing mechanics.)

### Thumbnail testing via image poll
1. Draft 2 finalist thumbnails (poll fatigue kills 4-way votes; run a bracket over two polls if you have 4).
2. Post an image poll: neutral framing — "Which one makes you want to click?" not "Which do you like?" (fans "like" the creator's face; clicking is a different question).
3. Run 24 hours max, harvest votes + read the comments (the *why* comments are worth more than the vote split).
4. **Bias correction — critical**: poll voters are subscribed superfans; the browse audience that decides a video's fate is cold. A thumbnail that wins with fans on recognition/inside-jokes can lose on browse. Use the poll to eliminate obvious losers and surface objections, then let **native Test & Compare** (up to 3 thumbnails post-upload, winner decided by *watch-time share*, not CTR) make the final call on real cold traffic.
5. Bonus: the poll itself pre-markets the video — voters return to see which thumbnail "their" pick got and whether they were right.

### Idea/topic validation
- **3–4-option topic poll before scripting**: "Which one first?" with real candidate titles (not vague themes — test the actual packaging language). Directionally more reliable than keyword tools for an existing audience; it measures *your* audience, which is exactly what the sub-feed and early-push algorithms measure.
- **Conditional commitment posts**: "If this hits 500 votes, I'll film ___" — converts validation into a co-created event and lifts launch-day turnout.
- **Hot-take text posts as demand probes**: post the video's core claim as a one-liner ("Hot take: velocity training is ruining youth arms. Agree?"). Comment volume + heat ≈ demand signal for the long-form treatment. Screenshot the best replies for the video itself.
- **Title-language A/B in the wild**: run the same question with two different phrasings a few weeks apart; the framing that draws more engagement is usually the stronger title stem.

### What polls can't tell you
- Ceiling for *new-audience* appeal (poll sample is 100% warm).
- Retention. A topic can poll great and die at minute 2. Polls de-risk the click, not the watch.
- Anything with n < ~100 votes — below that, treat as anecdote.

## 5. Subscriber feed & notification realities

### The mental model
Subscribing does **not** guarantee a subscriber sees your uploads in Home — Home is relevance-ranked, and inactive subscribers stop being shown your content. Three delivery paths, in descending reliability:
1. **Subscriptions feed** — chronological, always complete, but requires the viewer to open that tab (a minority behavior).
2. **Home feed** — algorithmic; subscription is one signal among many.
3. **Push notification** — only for the narrow opt-in slice below.

### The bell, precisely (Studio: Analytics → Audience → "Subscriber bell notifications" card)
- Default subscribe = **"Personalized"** notifications (algorithm decides). The bell = **"All."**
- To actually receive a push, a subscriber must have **all three**: bell set to All + YouTube notifications enabled at the Google-account level + enabled at the device level. The Studio card shows both percentages; the triple-opt-in number is typically a small single-digit fraction of subs.
- **Per-video "Bell notifications sent"** (video → Reach tab) shows: notifications sent, **notification CTR** (expect it *lower* than impressions CTR — people get pinged while at work/cooking, not while browsing), and views from notifications (immediate clicks only).
- **Delivery caps**: YouTube sends **max ~3 notifications per channel per 24h**; rapid multi-publishing, subscriber-count fluctuation, or privacy changes mid-send all reduce delivery.
- **Engagement-based filtering (2025–26)**: YouTube has confirmed testing that suppresses notifications *even for "All" opt-ins* who habitually ignore them. Translation: the bell is opt-in *and* behavior-gated. Do not build strategy on it.

### Tactical consequences
- **Stop spending video time on "ring the bell" CTAs.** The triple-gate + filtering makes the yield tiny; the seconds cost retention. A once-a-quarter mention for superfans is plenty.
- **The subscriptions feed is your cleanest quality read.** YouTube's own guidance: watch how the video performs with subscription-tab traffic — it's your core audience's reaction "without any algorithm involvement." Check traffic-source = Subscriptions AVD/CTR in the first 24h as an early verdict, before browse data matures.
- **Never burn the 3-per-day notification budget on low-value posts.** A community post can consume a notification slot; if an upload is coming within hours, hold the post.
- **Premieres and live streams also notify** — a premiere converts an upload into a notify-able event twice (schedule + go-live), useful for tentpole videos; don't premiere routine uploads (waiting rooms with 12 people read as failure).

## 6. Memberships & Join-button optimization

### Eligibility & economics (as of 2026)
- YPP member, 1,000+ subs, 4,000 public watch hours (12mo) or 10M Shorts views (90d), 18+, supported country, not Made-for-Kids, no significant claimed music.
- Up to **6 tiers**, priced **$0.99–$49.99/mo**; higher tiers inherit all lower perks. **YouTube takes 30%** (creator keeps 70% — vs ~88–95% on Patreon/Substack; factor this into where you route superfans).
- **Conversion benchmarks**: 0.5–3% of subscribers overall; education 2–4%, entertainment/gaming 0.5–1%, lifestyle ~1–2%. A 100K-sub channel realistically supports 500–3,000 members. Trevor's teaching-adjacent baseball content should model toward the education band; More Mayday entertainment content toward the lower band.

### Tier design that works
- **3 tiers ≈ $2.99 / $4.99 / $9.99** is the dominant successful pattern. Six tiers = choice paralysis; one tier = no anchor. The middle tier should be the obvious buy (name it accordingly and stack the visible value there).
- Perks ranked by sustainability (cost-to-serve vs perceived value):
  1. **Members-only community posts** (near-zero effort, recurring touch) — extra BTS, early thumbnail votes, facility-build updates
  2. **Badges + custom emoji** (zero recurring effort, high visibility in live chat/comments)
  3. **Members-first videos** (release to members 48–72h early — zero extra production)
  4. **Monthly members-only live stream / Q&A** (bounded effort, highest retention value)
  5. **Members-only videos** (real production cost — only if the above prove demand)
- **YouTube prohibits**: 1:1 meetings, member-exclusive contests, content downloads as perks. Don't design tiers around them.
- Community features across platforms correlate with **~50% higher member retention** — the badge/emoji/members-post layer is the retention engine, not the "exclusive content."

### Promotion playbook
- **YouTube's own stat: ~20% of new members join after seeing a members-only content promotion** on Home, watch-next, search, or channel page — publishing *some* members-only content is itself acquisition.
- In-video: a 10–15s tier mention when delivering the kind of value the membership extends ("the full breakdown of this drill progression is in this month's members stream"). Contextual asks convert; generic outro begs don't.
- Pin a comment with the Join link on videos that spike; run a community post announcing each members-only stream (the locked-post impression is an ad).
- **October 2025 change — know the tradeoff**: YouTube now surfaces members-only videos in *public* feeds with a lock stamp. Upside: free membership promotion. Downside: widely reported viewer annoyance and creators (Linus Tech Tips, Freakin' Reviews) documenting depressed public-video impressions when members-only content floods the feed. Tactical rule: keep members-only *videos* ≤ ~1 in 5 uploads; keep the members funnel weighted toward posts/streams that don't paywall the feed.
- Launch checklist: (1) name the membership (identity > "channel member"), (2) 3 tiers with middle-tier anchor, (3) seed 2–3 members-only posts before announcing, (4) launch video segment + pinned comment + community post, (5) first members-only stream within 2 weeks, (6) monthly cadence commitment stated publicly.
- **Neptune angle**: membership perks can preview facility content (build progress, program philosophy) without gating the marketing itself — the facility funnel should stay public.

## 7. Hype (launched globally Aug 2025) — the newest non-video reach lever

- **Mechanics**: every viewer gets **3 free hypes/week** (refresh weekly); a video is hypeable for its **first 7 days**; one hype per viewer per video. Hyped videos earn points toward **regional leaderboards** that feed browse/home recommendations to *new* audiences. Live in 39 countries.
- **Eligibility**: YPP channels between **500 and 499,999 subscribers**. Channels graduate out at 500K. **Both More Mayday and Trevor May Baseball likely qualify — confirm current sub counts and use the window.**
- **Multiplier**: fewer subs → more points per hype (500–10K strongest, 200–500K near-base). A mid-size channel needs roughly **5–10% viewer participation** for consistent leaderboard presence.
- **Playbook**:
  1. One-time 30–60s in-video explainer of Hype (most viewers still don't know they have 3 free/week, as of 2026).
  2. Standing description line: "You get 3 free Hypes a week — using one here helps this channel get discovered."
  3. **Day-2/3 community post reminder** per major upload — leans on the 7-day urgency window and is the cleanest posts↔Hype synergy on the platform.
  4. Publish Mon–Wed so the video's hype window overlaps two weekly hype refreshes for most viewers.
  5. Cross-hyping with overlapping athlete-creator channels is legitimate and effective.
- **Paid hypes**: testing in Brazil/Turkey since late 2025, broader rollout expected through 2026 — fans buying extra hypes as "discovery investment." Watch for US availability.
- Don't beg every video. One structural ask + per-upload post reminder; begging reads as engagement bait and burns goodwill.

## 8. Measuring all of this (Studio locations + what to actually track)

- **Posts analytics**: Studio → Content → **Posts chip** (appears once you've posted): impressions, likes, votes, comments, top posts by type. Aggregate view under Analytics → Content; Advanced Mode has post-level dimensions including **subscribers gained per post**.
- Track monthly: (1) post impressions trend, (2) engagement rate (interactions ÷ impressions, target 2–5%), (3) subs gained from posts, (4) poll vote counts on pre-production tests (n ≥ 100 to act), (5) new-video first-24h views in active-posting weeks vs quiet weeks.
- **Notifications**: Audience tab bell card (channel-level %) + per-video Reach tab (sent / CTR / views). Expect small numbers; watch the *trend*, not the level.
- **Memberships**: Studio → Earn → Memberships: joins, cancels, MRR, per-tier distribution. Watch churn after each members-only stream (streams are the retention lever).
- **Hype**: per-video hype counts in Studio when eligible; correlate leaderboard appearances with browse-traffic spikes in the same week.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Treating the tab as an announcement board** — 100% promo posts crater engagement rate, which craters feed placement, which makes the promos themselves invisible.
2. **Bare text posts and bare video reshares** — lowest-performing formats; always attach a question, image, or framing commentary.
3. **Overposting** (>1–2/day) — measurably reduced distribution and reported temporary restrictions; also burns notification-slot budget near uploads.
4. **Sporadic posting** — worse than not posting; feed placement is momentum-based. Commit to 2–3/week for 4–6 weeks or don't start.
5. **Taking fan-poll thumbnail winners as final** — warm-audience bias; polls eliminate losers, Test & Compare on cold traffic picks winners.
6. **Ignoring poll results** — running "which video next?" and shipping something else torches the co-creation contract. Only poll options you'll actually honor.
7. **Not replying to post comments** — replies drive the early-engagement push; a poll with 40 unanswered comments wastes its own distribution.
8. **"Smash the bell" CTAs in 2026** — triple-gated, capped at 3/day, behavior-filtered. Wasted retention seconds.
9. **Flooding public feeds with members-only videos** post-Oct-2025 — documented impression drag and viewer resentment; keep locked videos a small minority of output.
10. **Engagement-bait mechanics** ("like this post for no reason," vote-farming) — falls under YouTube's fake-engagement/spam policy; posts can be removed and strikes applied.
11. **Launching memberships with no perk backlog** — announcing Join with an empty members tab produces day-one churn; seed content first.
12. **Ignoring the Shorts-feed path for image posts** — image posts are the only post type with vertical-feed reach; strong stills (game photos, facility build shots) with a question are free Shorts-adjacent impressions.

8. Which recurring comment-section debates (velo training, pitch design, MLB takes) can be converted into a standing weekly poll/quiz franchise?
