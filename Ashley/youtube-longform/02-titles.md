# Title Craft & Testing — The Complete Playbook

Tactical execution reference for writing, formatting, and testing YouTube long-form titles. Algorithm mechanics, CTR benchmarks, and packaging time-allocation strategy live at the strategy level (see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md) — this doc is the how-to-write-the-actual-title layer.

## TL;DR

- **Write the title before you shoot.** If you can't write a clickable title for the idea, it's not an idea yet (Paddy Galloway / MrBeast doctrine). Draft 10-30 candidates per video; pick the packaging first, then produce to it.
- **Shorter wins for browse-driven content.** A 61,838-video study (ViewsKit, 2026) found titles ≤30 characters earn roughly 2x the median views of 80-100-character titles, monotonically declining ~1.5% of expected views per character past 20. Target ≤50 chars for entertainment; the "70-100 chars for SEO" advice only applies to search-first tutorial content.
- **One title = one emotion.** Every strong title runs on curiosity, fear/loss-aversion, or desire (Jake Thomas / Creator Hooks). If you can't name which one your title triggers, rewrite it.
- **Title and thumbnail must never say the same thing.** Split the message: thumbnail carries the emotion/image, title carries the context/stakes. Word-for-word repetition is wasted real estate and kills the curiosity gap.
- **YouTube's native Test & Compare now does title A/B tests** (wide rollout Dec 2025): up to 3 titles, 3 thumbnails, or 3 combos per video; winner picked by **watch time per impression, not CTR** — so overpromising titles lose tests even when they win clicks. Needs Advanced Features, desktop Studio, ~1,000-5,000 impressions per variant, up to 2 weeks.
- **Numbers, selective caps, and brackets are measurable CTR levers** — numbers lift CTR ~15-30% in split tests, bracketed clarifiers earned +38% clicks in HubSpot's classic study — BUT brackets underperform in entertainment/sports, where short bare titles beat elaborate ones.
- **Calibrate the curiosity gap to a 6-8 out of 10, and always close it in the video.** A gap the video doesn't pay off tanks retention and satisfaction signals, which since 2024-25 actively suppress distribution.
- **For sports/athlete content, the highest-leverage title ingredient is credentialed proximity**: "MLB Pitcher," "Ex-Pro," "I Faced," "What It's Actually Like." Trevor's credential is a permanent title asset most competitors can't copy.

## 1. The job of a title (per surface)

A title has ~1 second of scan time and does a different job depending on where the impression happens:

| Surface | Viewer state | Title's job | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home/Browse | Idle, scanning a menu | Stop the scroll; add stakes/context to the thumbnail | Shortest, punchiest titles. First 3-5 words carry everything. |
| Suggested | Mid-session, just watched something adjacent | Promise continuation or escalation | Reference the topic cluster explicitly ("Part 2", "vs", the named subject). |
| Search | Intent-driven, typed a query | Match the query language | Keyword-first phrasing, longer titles acceptable (up to ~70-100 chars). |
| Subs feed / notifications | Already a fan | Signal format/series | Franchise tags OK here ("Ep. 12") — but put them at the END of the title. |

Rule of thumb: decide **before writing** whether the video is a browse play or a search play. Most More Mayday content is browse/suggested; most Trevor May Baseball instructional content has a real search component. These need different title styles (see §4).

Eye-tracking data shows viewers process only the **first 3-5 words** of a title while scanning (ViewsKit, 2026). Whatever wins the click must live there.

## 2. Title length — the real data (as of 2026)

Two camps of data exist and they're both right for different content:

**Camp A — short wins (browse/entertainment):** ViewsKit's 61,838-long-form-video study (API-sampled 2006-May 2026, median views per length bucket, min n=1,570 per bucket):

| Title length | Median views | vs ≤20-char baseline |
|---|---|---|
| ≤20 chars | 3.97M | — |
| 21-30 | 3.26M | -18% |
| 31-40 | 2.75M | -31% |
| 41-50 | 2.08M | -48% |
| 51-60 | 1.70M | -57% |
| 61-70 | 1.63M | -59% |
| 71-80 | 1.55M | -61% |
| 81-100 | 1.20M | -70% |

Caveats the study itself flags: observational, popularity-biased sample (big entertainment channels favor short titles), and the mechanism is CTR → algorithmic promotion, not the character count itself. But the correlation is monotonic across all eight niches tested and "bigger than any single title formula."

**Camp B — longer wins (search/tutorial):** Multiple 2026 SEO-oriented analyses claim 70-100-character titles outperform for search-heavy content by 10-14%, because longer titles carry more query-matching surface and front-loaded keywords lift search rankings ~20%. This applies to "how to" instructional content, not entertainment.

**Operational rules:**
- Hard platform limit: 100 characters. Truncation with "..." starts around **35-50 characters on mobile/TV**, ~60-70 on desktop.
- **Entertainment/browse video (More Mayday):** target ≤50 chars, ideal ≤40. Every word must fight for its place.
- **Search/instructional video (Trevor May Baseball drills, "how to throw a changeup"):** keyword phrase in the first 30 characters, total up to ~70 chars; the clickable hook still lives in the first 40.
- **Podcast episodes:** guest name or the single most quotable claim first; episode number and show branding last (YouTube's official guidance: branding/episode numbers at the end).
- Cost model to hold in your head: past ~20 characters, each extra character costs ~1.5% of expected views on browse-driven content. Cut ruthlessly.

## 3. Curiosity gap calibration

The mechanism (Loewenstein's information-gap theory): a gap between what the viewer knows and wants to know creates a deprivation feeling only the click can close. MrBeast's applied version: **trigger curiosity, then satisfy it** — packaging must match the product.

### The calibration scale

Score every draft title 1-10 on gap width:

- **1-3 (too closed):** Title answers its own question. "I threw 95mph at 34 by long tossing daily" — no reason to click, the payload is in the title. Fix: withhold the mechanism or the result, not both.
- **4-5 (mild):** Fine for search content where clarity > intrigue.
- **6-8 (target zone for browse):** Specific enough to feel real, open enough to demand the click. "The Pitch MLB Hitters Told Me They Hated" — names the category (a pitch, real hitters), withholds the answer.
- **9-10 (toxic/clickbait):** Gap the video can't close or was never going to close. Spikes CTR, tanks retention in the first 10 seconds, and post-2024 satisfaction weighting means the algorithm actively suppresses it. YouTube's Test & Compare judging by watch-time-per-impression is explicitly designed to make this style lose.

### Four levers to widen a gap without lying

1. **Withhold the object:** "This One Pitch Ended My Career" (which pitch?)
2. **Withhold the outcome:** "I Challenged a D1 Hitter to 10 At-Bats" (who won?)
3. **Contradict an assumption:** "Velocity Doesn't Matter (From a Guy Who Threw 98)"
4. **Signal insider info:** "What Pitchers Actually Say on the Mound Visit"

### The payoff rule

Whatever the title opens, the video must close — and the open loop should be re-stated in the first 30 seconds ("confirm the click"). If the video can't deliver the title's promise, change the title, not the standard. (Strategy-level retention mechanics: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/01-youtube-growth-strategy.md.)

## 4. Keyword vs click balance

- **Browse-first title (default for entertainment):** written for humans, zero keyword obligation. The algorithm learns topic from transcript, description, and viewer behavior.
- **Search-first title:** exact query phrase in the first 4-5 words, natural language after. "How to Throw a Slider (MLB Pitcher Explains)" — query match + credential + bracket clarifier.
- **Hybrid (most Trevor May Baseball videos):** keyword phrase up front, curiosity/credential payload after a colon, dash, or bracket. Pattern: `[query phrase]: [twist or credential]` or `[query phrase] ([credential])`.
- **Never keyword-stuff.** "Best Pitching Drills Pitching Velocity Drills at Home" reads as spam to humans AND confuses topic classification. One primary phrase, used once, naturally.
- **Front-load always.** Both search ranking and human scanning reward the first 30 characters. Episode numbers, channel branding, and "| Mayday! with Trevor May" style suffixes go last or get cut.

## 5. Formatting mechanics: caps, numbers, punctuation, brackets

Data-backed effects (as of 2026):

| Element | Effect | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Numbers/digits** | +15-30% CTR in multiple split tests; digits read as facts and create pattern interrupts | Use digits, not words ("7" not "seven"). Specific beats round: "2hrs 26mins" outperforms "2.5 hours"; "$127" beats "$100+". |
| **One ALL-CAPS word** | Adds emphasis, measurable CTR lift | Cap ONE emotionally loaded word max: "I NEVER Throw This Pitch Anymore". |
| **Full ALL-CAPS title** | Reads as spam; excessive caps is a clickbait signal that can reduce distribution | Never. YouTube's official guidance: "Limit ALL CAPS and emoji." |
| **Brackets/parens** | +38% clicks (HubSpot's classic large-N headline study); adds context without cluttering the main claim | Best for tutorials/finance/education: "(Step-by-Step)", "(Full Breakdown)", "(2026)". **Weakest in entertainment and sports** — there, short bare titles beat elaborated ones because the thumbnail does the format-signaling. |
| **Question marks** | Fine when the question is the hook | Don't ask questions the viewer doesn't care to answer. |
| **Exclamation points / ellipses** | Overuse reads spammy | One "!" max, and usually zero. Ellipses only for a deliberate trailing hook ("...and then he charged the mound"). |
| **Title case vs sentence case** | No hard data winner; title case reads more "produced," sentence case more personal/native | Pick one per channel and stay consistent — mixed casing across a channel page looks sloppy. |
| **Emoji** | YouTube says use sparingly; near-zero use among top sports channels | Skip for Trevor's brands. |
| **Year tags ("2026")** | Signals recency, boosts short-term CTR on advice content | Short shelf life — evergreen titles compound longer. Use only when recency IS the value ("2026 MLB Rule Changes"). |

## 6. Title-thumbnail interplay rules

The pair is one unit of packaging; write them together, never sequentially in isolation.

1. **Never repeat.** If the thumbnail text says "50 HOURS," the title must NOT say "I Spent 50 Hours..." — it should add the missing dimension ("I Got Buried Alive for 50 Hours"). Titles that complement (add context the image can't convey) consistently outperform titles that duplicate.
2. **Division of labor:** thumbnail = emotion + one visual question; title = context + stakes. MrBeast's formula: one face, one object, one question in the image; ≤4 words of thumbnail text; the title carries the rest.
3. **The combined unit must create ONE gap, not two.** If thumbnail asks one question and the title asks a different one, the viewer is confused, not curious.
4. **Test the pair at 10% zoom.** If the thumbnail isn't readable small, cut elements; if the title's first 4 words don't carry the hook once truncated, rewrite.
5. **Reframe before you re-shoot.** Galloway's Ian Lauer case: same footage, packaging changed from "I photographed the Milky Way" to "Photographing the Milky Way in 10 Minutes, 1 Hour and 24 Hours" — channel went from 2-3K views/video to 1M+. Tiered/comparative structure created both a visual story (3-panel thumbnail) and a title gap.
6. **When the thumbnail is strong, the title can be quiet** — and vice versa. A jaw-dropping image ("pitcher mid-collision") needs only a calm factual title; a plain talking-head thumbnail needs the title to do the heavy lifting.

## 7. Proven title structures — library with examples

Jake Thomas's meta-rule before any formula: identify the emotion (curiosity / fear / desire), then borrow a proven structure from ANY niche and transplant it. The formula is the vehicle; the emotion is the engine.

### 7.1 Universal high-performers (data-backed, Humble & Brag 2026 outlier analysis)

| Formula | Structure | Example | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Compression** | [Big value] in [small time] | "30 Years of Business Knowledge in 2hrs 26mins" | 80x channel-average multiplier |
| **Blueprint** | My [system] for [specific result] | "My Full Blueprint for Scaling to 100K Subs" | 100x multiplier class |
| **Identity** | [Statement about viewer] + [challenge] | "You've Consumed Enough. It's Time to Start Creating." | 100x multiplier |
| **Authority** | [Credential] + [claim] | "ACCOUNTANT EXPLAINS: Money Habits Keeping You Poor" | 61x multiplier; strongest in expertise niches |
| **Novelty** | The NEW way to [result] | "The NEW Way to Win on Social Media in 2026" | 11x; short shelf life |
| **Versus** | A vs B | "$25K vs $25M Vacation" | Binary frames drive clicks; 3-tier versions add a story arc |
| **Warning / loss-aversion** | Stop doing [common thing] (it's [cost]) | "Stop Using This Strategy (It's Killing Your Channel)" | Fear-driven; strong across niches |
| **Transformation** | How I went from [A] to [B] in [time] | "How I Made $3,000/Month in Under 6 Months" | Personal results > generic promises |
| **Do this + outcome** | Do this and [outcome] | "Do This and You Will Never Hit a Fat Iron Shot" | Jake Thomas's cross-niche workhorse |
| **Negative/confession** | I regret / I stopped / The truth about | "I Stopped Lifting Heavy. Here's What Happened." | Vulnerability = curiosity + trust |

### 7.2 Sports / baseball patterns (calibrated to Trevor's lane)

Patterns that dominate baseball/sports YouTube (Jomboy Media, JM Baseball, Talkin' Baseball, pro-athlete channels):

- **The Breakdown:** `[Moment], a breakdown` — Jomboy's franchise ("Umpire has a meltdown, a breakdown"). The lowercase-suffix format itself became a brand. Lesson: a repeated title syntax can BE the format signal. (Strategy level on formats/series: see Carl 01.)
- **Insider credential:** "MLB Pitcher Explains...", "What Pros Actually Do...", "Ex-Big Leaguer Reacts to...". Trevor's strongest permanent asset — competitors teaching pitching without the credential can't use it.
- **Proximity/access:** "What It's Really Like to [insider experience]" — clubhouse, mound visits, getting traded, facing a specific star. Access is the product.
- **Confrontation/challenge:** "I Challenged [X] to [stakes]", "Can a 40-Year-Old Ex-Pro Still Throw 90?" — built-in stakes + outcome gap.
- **Named-star gravity:** titles containing a famous player's name inherit that player's search and suggested traffic ("Facing Shohei Ohtani", "What deGrom Told Me About His Slider"). Use real names whenever the content earns it.
- **Reaction/take:** "[Event] Is Worse Than You Think", "My Honest Reaction to [trade/rule change]" — timeliness play; short shelf life, fast suggested-feed pickup.
- **Experiment:** "I Tried [method] for 30 Days", "I Rebuilt My Mechanics Using Only [constraint]" — the sports version of the challenge video.
- **Ranking/tier:** "Ranking Every Pitch I Threw in the Big Leagues" — list + credential + completeness promise.
- **Fear/development (for the training-facility audience):** "The #1 Mistake Killing Youth Pitchers' Velocity", "Why Your Kid's Pitching Lessons Aren't Working" — parent-targeted loss aversion; directly relevant to Neptune Performance content.

### 7.3 Athlete-creator hybrid patterns

The athlete-creator lane (retired/active pros as creators) has its own title grammar:

- **Myth-buster from inside:** "What Baseball Movies Get Wrong (From Someone Who Lived It)"
- **Money/business transparency:** "What MLB Players Actually Spend Their Money On", "My First Contract vs My Last" — finance-curiosity crossover, huge for general audiences.
- **Second-act narrative:** "Why I Retired at 34", "Life After Baseball: The Honest Version" — the personal-story gap only the athlete can close.
- **Crossover collision:** athlete + non-sports domain — "MLB Pitcher Tries [other sport/skill]", podcast guests from entertainment. The title should name BOTH worlds so both audiences see themselves in it.

### 7.4 Entertainment-crossover patterns

For broad-appeal More Mayday content: strip jargon entirely. A general viewer must understand the title with zero baseball knowledge. Test: would a non-fan understand the stakes? "I Faced the Fastest Pitcher Alive" passes; "Facing a 70-Grade Heater" fails.

## 8. Writing 10+ variants — the playbook

Galloway's benchmark: 30-50 title drafts for big videos, then crowd-test to a top 3. Minimum viable process for every upload:

**Step 1 — Write the core promise in one plain sentence.** ("Trevor throws a bullpen for the first time in two years and has his velocity measured.")

**Step 2 — Generate 10+ variants using the angle matrix.** Force at least one title per row:

| Angle | Lens | Example from the bullpen video |
|---|---|---|
| Curiosity/withhold | Hide the result | "I Threw My First Bullpen in 2 Years" |
| Stakes/number | Quantify it | "Can I Still Throw 90 MPH at 36?" |
| Fear/loss | What could go wrong | "This Might Be the Last Time I Ever Pitch" |
| Desire/aspiration | What viewer wants | "How Much Velocity Do You Really Lose After Retiring?" |
| Authority | Lead with credential | "Ex-MLB Pitcher Tests His Velocity After 2 Years Off" |
| Identity/direct address | Talk to the viewer | "You Never Forget How to Throw. Or Do You?" |
| Contradiction | Break an assumption | "Retired Pitchers Don't Lose Velocity. I Proved It." (only if true) |
| Named entity | Attach a star/place | "Throwing a Bullpen at [famous facility] After 2 Years" |
| Short/bare | ≤30 chars, thumbnail does the rest | "2 Years Without Pitching." |
| Question | The viewer's own question | "How Hard Can I Still Throw?" |

**Step 3 — Kill weak variants with the checklist:**
- [ ] Emotion identifiable in one word (curiosity/fear/desire)?
- [ ] First 4-5 words carry the hook?
- [ ] Survives truncation at 40 characters?
- [ ] Zero jargon (for broad content)?
- [ ] Gap the video actually closes?
- [ ] Doesn't repeat the thumbnail text?
- [ ] Would this stop YOU scrolling on a competitor's channel?

**Step 4 — Get 3-5 outside votes** (team, Discord, group chat) on the top 3-5. Show title + thumbnail together, at small size, mixed in with real competing videos if possible.

**Step 5 — Ship one, queue two.** Keep the two runners-up for a Test & Compare experiment or a post-publish swap (see §9).

**Variant-generation accelerators:** feed your best draft to an LLM and ask for "shorter," "more emotional," "withhold the outcome," "add a number," "as a question" — then human-edit. Steal structures from Creator Hooks' weekly outlier breakdowns and from unrelated niches (Jake Thomas's core method: a golf title formula works for pitching; a finance formula works for contract stories).

## 9. A/B testing titles

### 9.1 Native Test & Compare (as of 2026 — the primary tool)

Rolled out to thumbnails in 2024, expanded to **titles and title+thumbnail combos December 2025**, now available to all creators with Advanced Features enabled (YPP membership NOT required).

**Exact specs:**
- Up to **3 variants**: titles only, thumbnails only, or full title+thumbnail packages.
- Desktop YouTube Studio only (no mobile).
- Eligible: public long-form videos, podcast episodes, live archives, post-Premiere videos. Ineligible: Shorts, scheduled lives/Premieres, Made for Kids, age-restricted, private.
- Winner metric: **watch time share per impression — NOT CTR.** YouTube's docs: "we optimise tests for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through rate." A clickbaity title that wins clicks but loses retention loses the test.
- Duration: up to 2 weeks; needs roughly **1,000-5,000 impressions per variant** for significance.
- Outcomes: Winner (auto-applied), Performed the Same (you choose), Inconclusive (defaults to first-uploaded variant).
- A control group of viewers sees only the default version and is excluded from calculations.
- **Manual title/thumbnail edits outside the tool kill a running test.** Don't touch packaging mid-test.
- Thumbnail variants below 720p force ALL variants down to 480p — always upload ≥1280x720 images even in title-only tests with a thumbnail attached.

**Testing protocol:**
1. **Test angles, not synonyms.** Three variants should be three different psychological framings (e.g., authority vs curiosity vs stakes), not word-shuffles of one idea. Word-level tweaks won't reach significance.
2. **Prioritize videos with strong retention but weak CTR** — that pattern means the content is good and the packaging is the bottleneck; biggest upside per test.
3. Run high-traffic videos first (they hit impression thresholds fastest). Low-traffic videos often end Inconclusive.
4. Let tests run the full window — cutting early is vulnerable to weekday/weekend traffic noise.
5. **Keep a testing log**: video, variants, angle of each, impressions, result. After ~10 tests you have a channel-specific title playbook — which emotions and structures YOUR audience clicks and stays for.
6. Third-party option: TubeBuddy still offers CTR-optimized (rather than watch-time-optimized) sequential testing if you specifically want a CTR read; vidIQ is keyword/research-oriented, not true A/B.

### 9.2 Post-publish title swaps (the older lever — still works)

- Changing a title on an underperforming video can relaunch it: the algorithm re-evaluates packaging, and an old video with a new gap can take off weeks later (Galloway's public case: a video re-packaged after launch went from "8/10 worst" to fastest-to-1M on the channel).
- Swap when: CTR is below channel norm AND retention is solid AND the video is >72h old (let the initial impression wave finish first).
- Swap ONE element at a time (title OR thumbnail) if diagnosing; swap both if relaunching a dead video.
- Don't churn titles on a video that's actively climbing — you reset viewer-facing consistency for no gain.

### 9.3 Pre-publish validation (cheapest test of all)

- Community-poll the top 2-3 title/thumbnail pairs (Community tab, Discord, X).
- Paddy Galloway's team method: mock the packaging into a fake YouTube homepage screenshot next to real competitor videos and ask "which would you click?"
- Score drafts against your own channel history: which past titles beat channel-average CTR, and what emotion/structure did they use?

## 10. Applying it to Trevor's properties

- **More Mayday (entertainment/browse):** ≤45-char bare titles, no brackets, no jargon, credential or named-star gravity where possible. Thumbnail carries emotion; title carries stakes. Every upload: 10-variant matrix, minimum.
- **Trevor May Baseball (instructional/hybrid):** keyword phrase first 30 chars + credential clarifier. Brackets earn their keep here: "(MLB Pitcher Explains)", "(Full Drill Progression)". Parents-of-players fear/desire titles feed the Neptune Performance funnel.
- **Podcast ("Mayday! with Trevor May"):** never title episodes "Ep. 47 — [Guest Name]". Lead with the single most clickable claim or moment from the episode: "[Guest] on the Trade That Broke Him | Mayday Ep. 47". Guest name early if famous, claim early if not. Clip titles ≠ episode titles — each clip gets its own gap.
- **Neptune Performance content:** authority + loss-aversion aimed at parents/players: "The Velocity Mistake Every 14-Year-Old Makes", "What College Scouts Actually Look For (From an MLB Vet)".

## 11. Common mistakes

1. **Titling after the edit.** The title is the idea. Writing it last produces descriptions, not hooks.
2. **Describing instead of selling.** "Bullpen Session Vlog #12" describes; "Can I Still Throw 90?" sells.
3. **Answering the question in the title.** If the payload is in the title, there's no click left to earn.
4. **Two ideas in one title.** One video, one promise, one emotion. Compound titles ("...AND we tour the facility!") dilute the gap.
5. **Keyword stuffing.** Repels humans and confuses topic classification. One natural phrase.
6. **Toxic clickbait.** Gap the video can't close → 10-second bounces → satisfaction-signal suppression. The watch-time-per-impression test metric now makes this measurably losing behavior, not just ethically iffy.
7. **Franchise branding first.** "MAYDAY PODCAST EP 47: ..." wastes the only 40 characters that matter. Branding last, always.
8. **ALL-CAPS everything / emoji soup.** Spam signals; YouTube explicitly warns against both.
9. **Repeating the thumbnail text.** One message, split across two surfaces — never duplicated.
10. **Testing synonyms instead of angles.** "I Tried" vs "I Attempted" will never reach significance; authority-frame vs stakes-frame will.
11. **Judging a title by first-48-hour CTR alone.** Browse CTR is naturally low (2-5%); read CTR per traffic source and against the video's own retention. (Benchmarks: see Carl 01.)
12. **Insider jargon on broad-audience videos.** "70-grade heater," "FIP," "pronation" — instant filter that shrinks the clickable audience to hardcore fans.
13. **Copying a formula without its emotion.** Structures transplant across niches; the emotion has to be re-earned in your context (Jake Thomas's core warning).

## 12. Questions Ashley should ask

Before titling any video:
- What's the one-sentence promise of this video, in plain English a non-fan understands?
- Is this a browse play or a search play? (Different length and keyword rules.)
- Which of the three emotions is this title running on — curiosity, fear, or desire? Can you name it in one word?
- What does the thumbnail say, and does the title ADD to it or repeat it?
- Does Trevor's MLB credential belong in this title, or is it stronger implied?
- Would this title work with a famous name in it — and does the content earn that name?
- Rate the curiosity gap 1-10: can the video fully close it?
- What are the other 9+ variants? (Never accept the first title.)

For testing and iteration:
- Which recent videos have above-average retention but below-average CTR? (Those are the Test & Compare queue.)
- What do the last 10 title tests say about which angle wins for THIS audience?
- Which past titles beat channel-average CTR, and what structure/emotion did they share?
- Has the title been re-evaluated 72 hours post-publish against its traffic-source CTRs?
- Is there a dormant back-catalog video whose topic just became timely and deserves a re-title relaunch?
