# Facebook Groups & Sports Communities

Groups are the single highest-leverage Facebook surface for the Neptune Performance side of the business, because the youth-sports parent demographic — the people who write checks for lessons, memberships, and travel ball — organizes itself on Facebook Groups more than on any other platform surface. For Mayday Media, groups are a retention/superfan tool, not a discovery tool. This doc covers the mechanics, the build-vs-borrow decision, and the operational playbooks.

## TL;DR

- **Group posts out-rank Page posts for the same audience.** Facebook's ranking treats group activity as a "meaningful social interaction" signal; a member of an active group sees far more of that group's content than a Page follower sees of that Page's (Page organic reach ~2–5% of followers; third-party estimates put typical group-post reach at 20–40% of members, 50%+ for high-engagement posts). If the goal is *reaching people you've already captured*, a group beats a Page every time.
- **Borrow before you build.** For Neptune: 90 days of genuine, helpful presence in existing local youth-baseball / travel-ball / parents groups (comment-led, zero pitching) before launching an owned group. Trevor's "retired MLB pitcher" identity is a legitimacy cheat code in these rooms — but only if the first 50 interactions contain no ask.
- **Membership questions are a lead-capture form.** 3 questions on join: (1) segmentation (player age/level), (2) pain point (what are you trying to fix), (3) "Where should we send [free resource]?" → email. Tools like Groupboss/Group Leads auto-sync answers to an email list/CRM. This is the highest-converting email capture mechanism on Facebook (as of 2026).
- **Comments are the ranking currency, not likes.** Posts that generate back-and-forth comment threads get the strongest distribution inside the group feed and into members' main Feeds. Design posts as questions/polls/debates; reply to every comment within the first hour of posting.
- **"Give in public, sell in private."** Never pitch in the group feed. Value posts → commenters/question-askers get a helpful reply → move to DM ("happy to look at his video, send it over") → offer assessment/booking. The group is the trust engine; the DM is the sales floor.
- **Moderation must cost <30 min/day.** Admin Assist auto-rules (keyword declines, link blocks, membership-length gates), keyword alerts, a daily digest, and 1–2 deputized parent moderators. If a group takes more than that, the automation isn't set up right.
- **Platform state (as of 2026):** community chats inside Groups were killed Oct 5, 2025; private→public conversion (with history protected) shipped Nov 2025; Local tab surfacing Groups/Events/Marketplace is rolling out in major US metros; optional Group AI answers repeat questions. Anonymous "nickname" posting exists — expect it in parent groups where people ask sensitive coach/team questions.

## 1. How group content ranks (mechanics, as of 2026)

Group content flows through three distinct surfaces, each with its own ranking:

### 1a. The member's main News Feed
- Group posts compete in the main Feed alongside friends/Pages/reels. Facebook's four-step process applies: **Inventory → Signals → Predictions → Relevance score**.
- Group posts win Feed slots primarily via predicted **comment probability** and **interaction history with that group**. A member who has commented in your group recently is dramatically more likely to be shown the next post.
- Practical implication: engagement is compounding and *per-member*. Every member you get to comment once becomes a member who sees the next five posts. Cold members decay out of distribution. This is why onboarding (Section 5) matters more than growth.

### 1b. The dedicated Groups Feed (Meta Transparency Center, official)
Meta publishes the actual model inputs for the Groups Feed AI. It scores ~200 candidate posts per session and orders by relevance score. Named prediction models and their inputs:
- **Meaningful engagement**: how many group posts the user has seen vs. commented on; prior interactions with suggested posts.
- **Reaction likelihood**: group context, time spent viewing, click history.
- **Viewing duration**: time spent on group posts, overall Feed usage.
- **Post dismissal**: how many suggested posts the user has hidden, *by post type and by author* — repeatedly hidden authors get suppressed personally.
- **Comment engagement**: comment count, photo clicks, time spent reading comments.
- **Video watching**: time spent on suggested group videos.
- Users can **prioritize a group** (pins it to the top of Groups Feed, newest-first), snooze groups for 30 days, or leave. Getting members to prioritize your group is the single strongest distribution unlock a member can give you — put it in onboarding.

### 1c. Notifications
- Default for most members is **"Highlights"** — Facebook decides which posts merit a notification (friend posts, spiking comment threads, rising activity). "All posts" exists but even then the feed itself is still ranked; members can miss things.
- A post whose comments spike quickly can trigger highlight notifications to *inactive* members — this is the resurrection mechanic. One genuinely hot thread per week re-activates lapsed members better than any volume of average posts.
- Onboarding ask #2 (after "prioritize this group"): turn on group notifications. Give members the exact tap path (Group → ⚙ → Manage notifications → All posts).

### What the algorithm rewards inside a group (ranked by leverage)
1. **Back-and-forth comment threads** — 20 real replies beats 200 likes. Reply to every comment in the first 60 minutes; each admin reply re-fires distribution.
2. **Saves** — elevated as a quality signal in 2025–26 ranking; reference/checklist-style posts ("12U arm-care checklist") earn saves.
3. **Poll participation** — lowest-friction comment substitute; polls reliably out-distribute plain text.
4. **Time-on-post** — longer thoughtful posts and native video hold attention; link-dump posts (instant bounce off-platform) rank worst.
5. **Photo/video clicks** — a real photo of a real kid's swing beats a branded graphic every time in parent groups.

## 2. Platform changes to know (2025–2026)

Date-stamped because this stuff churns:

| Change | Date | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Community chats inside Groups discontinued** | Oct 5, 2025 | Real-time chat moved out of Groups. Don't build programming around group chats; use the feed + Events, or Messenger Communities for real-time. |
| **Private → public conversion** | Nov 2025 | Admins can flip a private group public. Pre-conversion posts/member list stay visible only to prior members + admins; 3-day cancel window; reversible. Lets you build in private (trust) then open up (discovery) without exposing history. |
| **Facebook Local tab** | Rolling out from Oct 2024, major US metros | Aggregates local Groups, Events, Marketplace into one discovery surface. Local groups + facility Events get a new organic discovery channel. Name/describe the group with city + sport keywords so it surfaces. |
| **Messenger Communities** | Testing since Oct 2024 | Topic-based chat spaces *without* a Facebook Group. Good lightweight option for a per-cohort space (e.g., "Neptune Winter Camp — Parents"). |
| **Group AI** | Limited US/CA rollout | Optional admin-enabled bot that answers members' repeat questions by linking prior posts. Turn on when available — it's free FAQ moderation. |
| **Nickname (anonymous) posting** | 2025 | Members can post under a nickname. Expect anonymous posts in parent groups on touchy topics (coach complaints, team politics). As admin you can allow/disallow; allowing it usually increases post volume in parent communities. |
| **Admin Assist Daily Digest** | 2025 | Daily summary of what automation handled. Skim it instead of living in the queue. |

## 3. Build vs. borrow: the decision framework

**Borrow** (participate in groups you don't own) when: you need reach *now*, you're pre-launch, the audience already congregates somewhere, or the content is local-discovery (facility awareness). Cost: no data ownership, rules constrain you, admin can delete you.

**Build** (own the group) when: you have a recurring reason for people to return, you can commit 30+ min/day for 6 months, and you want the email list + DM pipeline the group generates. Realistic expectation per Sprout: meaningful traction takes **3–6 months** of consistent effort.

**The right sequence for a facility (Neptune):** borrow for 90 days → soft-launch owned group seeded from borrowed relationships + first customers → keep borrowing (the two compound). Never skip the borrow phase; a 12-member ghost-town group is negative social proof.

**For Mayday Media (creator side):** a fan group is a *retention* play only — the equivalent of a Discord for the FB-native segment of the audience (older, parent-heavy, exactly the crossover with Neptune's demo). Only build it if someone owns programming for it; otherwise the Page + existing channels suffice. Do not build a group hoping it grows the channel — groups don't do cold discovery for creators the way short-form does (see Carl short-form doc for the discovery stack).

## 4. Borrowing groups: the youth-baseball parent playbook

### Where the demographic actually lives
The youth/travel baseball parent world on Facebook is organized in overlapping layers — map all of them for the facility's metro:
1. **State/regional travel-ball groups** — e.g., "Alabama Youth Travel Baseball," "Chicago Travel Baseball," Georgia Travel Baseball/NWBA. Nearly every state has one or several; primary use is tryout announcements, "looking for players/teams," tournament chatter. These are the highest-intent rooms.
2. **National parent communities** — e.g., Travel Ball Parents (~78K followers on the page side, plus group). Good for listening/content research, too diffuse for local lead-gen.
3. **Local league/community groups** — Little League parents' groups, "[Town] Youth Sports," school-community groups.
4. **General local groups** — "[Town] Moms," neighborhood groups, "[Town] Community," buy/sell/trade groups. Youth-sports questions ("anyone know a good hitting coach?") get asked here constantly. UMich research on mom-to-mom groups: these run on **visible-transaction trust** — everyone sees everyone's behavior, reputation is the currency, and recommendations carry near-referral weight.
5. **Coach-side groups** — youth coaching groups, pitching/hitting instruction groups. Peer credibility + referral source (coaches recommend facilities).

### The 90-day borrowed-authority plan
- **Days 1–7 — Join & lurk.** Join 8–12 groups across the layers above (personal profile, not Page — groups are person-to-person; many groups bar Page participation). Read each group's rules *before anything else*: note promo-day policies (many allow business posts one designated day/week), link policies, and tone. Save the rules.
- **Days 8–30 — Comment-only.** Answer questions in your lane: arm care, velocity myths, when to start offspeed pitches, recruiting timelines, "is my 10U throwing too much?" No links, no mention of the facility. For Trevor specifically: answer as a former MLB pitcher and a dad-aged peer, not as a business. 3–5 substantive comments/week per priority group.
- **Days 31–60 — Original value posts** where rules allow: a myth-busting take, a free checklist, a "ask me anything about pro pitching development" (clear with the admin first — admins usually *love* being asked and will often pin it). Introduce the facility only when directly relevant and permitted ("full disclosure, I run a training facility in [town]").
- **Days 61–90 — Convert attention.** People will start DMing *you*. Also use promo days properly: one clear offer (free assessment day, open house, camp) with a real photo, on the sanctioned day only. Befriend 2–3 admins of the biggest local groups — offer them something exclusive for their members (group-only discount code, free clinic slot). Admin partnership is the only sanctioned way to "advertise" in most groups.

### Comment-led lead gen (evergreen tactic)
Monitor target groups for trigger phrases — "hitting lessons," "pitching coach," "looking for a trainer," "[town] baseball" — and be the best answer in the thread. Best practice per multiple practitioner sources: give the complete, genuinely useful answer *in the comment*, then let interested parents come to you. Manual monitoring is fine at small scale; a saved daily 10-minute sweep of the top 5 groups covers it. Never DM someone cold from their group post unless they invited it — that's the fast lane to a spam report.

### Hard rules for borrowed groups
- Rules-violation is one-strike in most parent groups; a ban also burns the reputation that took 90 days to build.
- Never post the same content across multiple groups the same day (members overlap; it reads as spam and Facebook's systems detect cross-group blasting).
- Never argue with a parent in public. Ever. Take heat to DMs or let it go.

## 5. Building the owned group (Neptune Performance playbook)

### Setup specs
- **Name = search phrase, not brand cleverness.** Members find groups by keyword search and (increasingly) the Local tab. Formula: `[Metro/Region] + [audience] + [topic]` — e.g., "Tampa Bay Baseball Parents — Player Development" beats "The Neptune Fam." Brand can live in the description and cover photo.
- **Privacy: Private + Visible.** Private drives candid parent conversation (the product); visible keeps it searchable. As of Nov 2025 you can flip public later with history protected, so private is the no-regret starting move.
- **Cover photo:** real facility, real kids training, Trevor's face. Description front-loads keywords: town, baseball, softball if applicable, ages served.
- **Rules (write your own, don't use Facebook's stock set), keep to ~5:** be respectful; no team/coach bashing by name; no self-promo without admin approval; keep it baseball/development-focused; what happens in group stays in group. Custom rules signal a real community and give Admin Assist something concrete to enforce.

### Membership questions = the lead form
Three questions, mirroring the pattern practitioners (e.g., Gemma Gilbert's 0→500-in-3-months build) validated:
1. **Segmentation:** "What age/level is your player?" (routes future content and offers)
2. **Pain point:** "What's the #1 thing you want to improve for your player this year?" (content mine + sales intel)
3. **Email capture:** "We'll send you our free [12U–16U velocity & arm-care guide] — best email?"

Wire Groupboss or Group Leads (browser extensions) to auto-save every applicant's answers + email to Google Sheets/Mailchimp/ActiveCampaign/GoHighLevel before you click Approve — otherwise Facebook discards the answers after approval and the emails are gone. Answer data is only stored if you capture it at approval time. (As of 2026 this remains the standard workflow; Facebook has no native export.)

### Pre-launch seeding
- Don't launch at zero. Seed 30–50 members before "opening": current facility customers, friends/teammates, and interest raised in borrowed groups ("thinking of starting a group for [metro] baseball parents — who'd want in?"). Gemma Gilbert's build had 30+ pre-launch members from exactly this move.
- Growth curve expectation from that case study: ~200 members month 1 → 350 month 2 → 500 month 3, with the group becoming the #1 client source. Local facility numbers will be smaller and that's fine — 300 local parents is worth more than 30,000 randoms.

### First-7-days member onboarding (the retention lever)
Groups with deliberate onboarding show materially higher long-term engagement; the first week decides whether a member ever engages. Pipeline:
1. **Batch-approve + welcome post** tagging the week's new members (native "Write welcome post" feature), with one low-friction prompt: "Drop your player's age and what position he's chasing."
2. **Pinned start-here post** (Featured): what the group is, the rules, the free guide, and the two asks — *prioritize this group* and *turn on notifications* (exact tap paths).
3. **Personal DM or comment** to each new member within 48h. At facility scale this is minutes/day and it's the single thing big groups can't do.

### Content engine (cadence + formats)
2–5 strong posts/week beats daily filler. Themed weekly skeleton so it runs without creative energy:
- **Mon — Poll/debate:** "Should 12U pitchers throw curveballs?" (polls = cheapest comments; debates = comment threads = distribution)
- **Wed — Trevor value drop:** 60–90s native video, one concrete tip. Native upload, not a YouTube link (link posts bounce users off-platform and rank worst; a *comment* with the YouTube link after the video posts is the compromise).
- **Fri — Community thread:** wins ("post your player's PR"), tournament check-ins, photo threads. Rotate formats — top-health groups mix video/text/poll weekly on a predictable schedule.
- **Monthly — Live Q&A/AMA** with Trevor (announced 1 week out, Event created); **member-milestone posts** (player achievements, "member spotlight") — recognition posts are a consistent marker of top-decile groups.
- **80/20 rule minimum** on value:promotion. In practice the feed should be ~95% value; the selling happens in DMs and on the sanctioned monthly offer post.
- Reply to every comment, fastest in the first 60 minutes. Admin responsiveness within ~4 hours is the strongest common trait reported across high-health groups.

### Facility-specific power moves
- **Events for everything:** camps, open houses, assessment days as Facebook Events attached to the group → members get reminders, Events surface in the Local tab, and RSVPs are retargetable.
- **UGC engine:** post-session clips of kids (with parent permission collected at signup) tagged/shared by parents = organic reach into every parent's friend graph — the highest-trust impression a local business can buy, for free.
- **Off-season = group season:** winter is when parents plan spring; run the group hardest Oct–Feb (velocity programs, tryout prep threads) when competitors go quiet.

## 6. The Group → Page → DM funnel

The funnel shape (for both Neptune and any Mayday community play):

```
Borrowed groups (reach)             Page + short-form (air cover)
        \                                /
         → Owned group (trust engine) ←
                    |
        Membership Qs → email list (owned)
                    |
   Value posts → comments/questions → DM
                    |
     Assessment / booking / camp signup
```

- **Page's role:** credibility layer + ad account + Events host + link hub. Parents who hear about Neptune in a group will check the Page before booking; keep it alive (2–3 posts/week is enough) but don't expect reach from it. Link Page ↔ group so the group is discoverable from the Page.
- **DM protocol ("give in public, sell in private" — ACE):** trigger = any member asking a question with buying intent ("how do I fix his arm slot?"). Answer publicly and fully → close the comment with an invitation, not a pitch: "If you want, DM me a video of him throwing and I'll take a look." → In DM: genuinely diagnose → *then* offer the paid next step ("this is exactly what our assessment covers — want me to hold a slot?"). Conversion happens because the free value already proved the paid value.
- **Direct sales pitches in-feed are the documented failure mode** (Sprout, Buffer, every practitioner source): they depress group engagement, train members to ignore you, and get screenshot-mocked in parent groups. One structured monthly offer post + DM selling covers all commercial needs.
- **Email is the escape hatch.** The membership-question email list is the only asset that survives a Facebook ban/algorithm change/group nuke. Sync it continuously; mail it monthly minimum. (User data must live in owned systems — a group is rented land.)
- **Tripwire pattern** (Group Leads playbook): free guide (membership Q) → email nurture → low-price offer (assessment $, single lesson, camp early-bird) → core program → upsell (membership, team training). Group challenges ("30-day velocity challenge") work as live-launch mechanisms inside the group.

## 7. Moderation efficiency (target: <30 min/day)

- **Admin Assist rules to set on day one:**
  - Auto-decline pending posts containing links (except allowlisted domains) or promo keywords ("DM me," "check out my," "% off," crypto/MLM vocabulary).
  - Auto-hide comments with links/images from members < 2 weeks tenure.
  - Auto-suspend/mute members who trip multiple rules.
  - Keyword *alerts* (not auto-actions) on volatile terms for a parent group: coach names, "abuse," "lawsuit," team names during drama seasons — you want eyes, not auto-deletion, on those.
  - Moderation Assist catches variants automatically ("spam" also catches "SPAM," "s.p.a.m.," "sp4m").
- **Daily Digest:** skim the automation summary once daily instead of living in the queue. Facebook may auto-flag false positives ("Flagged by Facebook"); admins can restore those posts.
- **Post approval: ON for the first ~6 months** (quality control while culture forms), then off for members with clean history once norms are self-enforcing. Membership approval stays on forever (it's the lead form).
- **Deputize 2 parent moderators** by month 3 — pick the two most helpful organic contributors, give them mod status + a free perk. Recognition converts superusers into free staff, and multiple admins is a standard best practice.
- **Group AI:** enable when available — it auto-answers repeat questions ("what ages do you take?", "where's the tryout post?") by linking prior posts.
- **Time budget:** 10 min queue/digest + 15 min comment replies + 5 min borrowed-group sweep = the whole daily job. Batch welcome posts weekly.

## 8. Benchmarks & measurement

Group Insights (native, free) is the dashboard. What to track monthly:

| Metric | Healthy signal | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Active members (monthly) | 50%+ of members active; elite groups report 70–90% | The #1 health metric per Sprout; third-party figures on the high end are directional, not audited |
| Posts/comments/reactions trend | Up or flat MoM | Watch comments specifically — likes are noise |
| Top contributors | Same 5–10 names + rotating new ones | All-same-names = clique forming; recognize and recruit them as mods |
| Membership requests | Steady trickle | Spikes after borrowed-group activity or events = attribution signal |
| Email capture rate | 60–80% of approvals answer the email question | If lower, reword Q3 to emphasize the free asset |
| Popular days/times | Use Insights, post accordingly | Weekday 11am–6pm is the generic global window; parent groups often spike 8–10pm (post-bedtime scroll) |
| DMs/bookings sourced from group | Count manually | The only number that ultimately matters for Neptune |

Directional reach expectation (third-party estimates, as of 2026): a typical group post reaches ~20–40% of members and a hot one 50%+, vs. ~2–5% follower reach for a Page post. Treat as order-of-magnitude, not gospel.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Launching an owned group with no seed members and no daily owner.** A dead group is public proof nobody cares. Borrow first, seed 30–50, assign an owner.
2. **Pitching in borrowed groups before earning standing** (or ignoring promo-day rules). One-strike bans plus reputation damage in the exact rooms Neptune needs.
3. **Approving members without capturing membership-question answers/emails.** Facebook discards the data at approval; without Groupboss/Group Leads (or manual copy) the lead is lost forever.
4. **Posting links as the post.** Link-dumps (even to your own YouTube) are the worst-ranking format. Native video/photo/text first; link in comments.
5. **Judging the group by member count.** 300 engaged local parents > 5,000 randoms. Distribution is per-member engagement; inactive members actively dilute nothing but vanity.
6. **Broadcasting instead of hosting.** Admin-as-lecturer kills groups. The admin's job is prompts + replies; the members' job is content. If >50% of posts are yours after month 3, the culture failed.
7. **Letting a coach/team-bashing thread run.** In parent communities one nasty named-coach thread can define the group and create real local blowback for the facility. Keyword alerts + a hard rule + fast admin action.
8. **Selling in the feed weekly.** Trains members to unfollow mentally; tanks comment rates, which tanks distribution of everything else.
9. **Building programming on deprecated surfaces** — e.g., group community chats (dead as of Oct 2025). Check current feature status before designing rituals around a surface.
10. **Treating the group as owned infrastructure.** It's rented. Emails, phone numbers, and booking history live in owned systems from day one.

## 10. Questions Ashley should ask

- Which metro/radius is Neptune actually drawing from? (Defines which local groups to borrow and how to name the owned group.)
- Has anyone mapped the existing local FB groups — state travel-ball, town leagues, moms/community groups — and their rules/promo days? If not, that's the first 2-hour task.
- Who is the daily owner of the group (queue, replies, welcomes)? If the answer is "Trevor when he has time," scope it down to borrowing-only until there's an owner.
- Is Groupboss/Group Leads (or equivalent) wired to the email platform *before* the group opens?
- What's the free lead magnet for membership Q3 — and does it exist yet? (Age-banded arm-care/velocity guide is the obvious one.)
- What's the monthly conversion event cadence — assessment day, open house, camp — that the group funnels into?
- For Mayday Media: is there a named owner and a programming plan for a fan group? If not, park it — the Page + short-form stack covers the creator side (see Carl short-form doc).
- Are parent media-consent forms in the signup flow so kid training clips can fuel the UGC engine?
- Is Trevor participating as himself (personal profile) in borrowed groups — and is he blocking 15 min/day for it during launch season?
- When the owned group hits critical mass: does flipping private→public (history-protected, as of Nov 2025) serve discovery, or does private candor matter more?
