# Facebook Pages, Cross-Posting & Format Strategy

Tactical execution reference for running Facebook as a distribution surface for Mayday Media (More Mayday, Trevor May Baseball, the podcast) and Neptune Performance. Strategy-level short-form doctrine (hooks, clip selection, cadence philosophy) lives at ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md — this doc covers the Facebook-specific machinery.

## TL;DR

- **Never use Instagram's "Share to Facebook" toggle for anything you care about.** Agorapulse's controlled test: FB-native posts averaged **72.28% higher reach** than IG-crossposted posts (86% / 72% / 58% across three pages), and up to 140% more likes. Post natively to FB via scheduler (Metricool) with FB-specific copy. (As of 2026 this remains the single highest-leverage FB fix.)
- **Reels are the only format gaining engagement on FB** (0.15% ER in Q1 2026 per Socialinsider, the sole format that rose YoY) and the only format with meaningful unconnected/discovery distribution. Everything else mostly reaches existing followers.
- **Links in the post body are radioactive.** Link posts run **0.05% ER — roughly one-third of every other format** — and Meta's own transparency data says ~98% of U.S. feed views come from no-link posts. As of Dec 2025 Meta is even testing a **2-links-per-month cap** for non-Verified professional accounts. Put links in the first comment (Meta itself now surfaces this advice in the Professional Dashboard) or drive to DMs.
- **Text/status posts still punch way above their effort** for connected reach and comments — the best comment-driver per Socialinsider's page-size tables — making them ideal for Trevor's takes/questions between video posts. Question/poll-style posts are the highest engagement-per-effort format on FB in 2026.
- **Organic page reach is 1–6% of followers; up to ~50% of the average feed is now unconnected (recommended) content.** So the game is: Reels for unconnected discovery → status/albums for connected depth → weekly "invite engaged non-followers to follow" sweeps (20–40% conversion is common) to bank the audience.
- **FB is the older-audience platform** — heaviest in 25–54, with 54% of US 50–64-year-olds on daily. That's dads, coaches, and facility-paying parents: arguably Neptune Performance's single best organic surface, more so than Mayday's youth-skewing content.
- **In-stream ads / Ads on Reels / Performance Bonus all ended Aug 31, 2025**; monetization now runs through the unified **Facebook Content Monetization** program. Payout is a why-bother, not a why — treat FB as reach + local funnel, not revenue (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).

## 1. What Facebook is for in this operation

Facebook does three jobs here, in priority order:

1. **Neptune Performance local funnel.** FB's demo (parents 35–54, local Groups culture, Marketplace/Events habits) is exactly who writes checks for baseball training. A Neptune page + owner presence in local youth-baseball Groups is a lead channel, not a vanity channel.
2. **Zero-marginal-cost redistribution of Mayday short-form.** Clips already cut for Reels/TikTok/Shorts get a second life as FB Reels — but posted natively, not via the IG toggle (see §5).
3. **Older-fan relationship surface.** The MLB-era fanbase that remembers Trevor pitching skews older than TikTok; FB text posts and albums are where they actually comment.

What FB is *not* for: link-driven traffic (Substack, YouTube links) from the post body — the penalty numbers in §4 kill that play.

## 2. The 2026 distribution model (how a Page post travels)

As of 2026, FB organic reach has two distinct lanes:

- **Connected distribution** — followers and people with an existing relationship to the Page. Governed by classic feed ranking: relationship strength, engagement quality, format.
- **Unconnected distribution (recommendations)** — AI-recommended content to non-followers. Zuckerberg has stated ~30% of feed posts are AI-recommended; some analyses put total unconnected content at up to ~50% of the average feed. **Reels are the primary vehicle for unconnected reach**; status/image/link posts get little of it.

Ranking signals that matter in 2026 (per Fanpage Karma's 2026 analysis + Meta statements):

- **Watch time & completion** — the dominant video signal; why Reels get preferential treatment (completion is measurable at scale).
- **Meaningful engagement** — substantive comments > emoji comments > reactions. Shares and saves are heavily weighted "silent signals."
- **Relationship recurrence** — repeated interactions from the same users boost future delivery to them (this is why comment-reply discipline compounds).
- **Negative feedback** — hides/unfollows carry outsized down-weight; clickbait captions that don't pay off generate exactly this.
- **UTIS (User True Interest Survey), launched Jan 2026** — Meta now injects in-feed surveys ("How well does this video match your interests?") into Reels ranking. Practical effect: engagement-bait that games clicks but doesn't genuinely interest people decays faster than it used to. Content that a niche truly wants (baseball mechanics breakdowns) is advantaged over generic engagement-bait.
- **Caption-content alignment** — captions that overpromise cause early drop-off, which now reads as a negative signal.

**First-hour rule:** FB decides distribution breadth from early engagement velocity. Practitioner consensus (2026): the first hour determines the bulk of a post's ceiling. The strongest controllable lever is replying to every early comment with a real sentence (Buffer's 52M-post 2026 study measured a **+9.5% engagement lift from replying** on FB — smallest of all platforms but real).

**Benchmarks to calibrate against (as of Q1 2026):**
- Average Page organic reach: **1–6% of followers** (below 2% for 1M+ pages).
- Average FB engagement rate (per-follower, Socialinsider, 25M posts / 130K pages): **0.15%**.
- Buffer's per-reach median ER: **5.6%** (up 11% YoY). *Note the denominators: Socialinsider divides by followers, Buffer by reach. Use per-follower for "is our page healthy," per-reach for "was this post good."*
- Sports-team pages are the top-engaging category on FB at ~0.3% per-follower ER — baseball content plays on easy mode here.

## 3. Format hierarchy: what still earns organic reach (as of 2026)

Socialinsider Q1 2026 per-follower engagement by format:

| Format | ER 2025 | ER Q1 2026 | Trend | Distribution character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reels | 0.18% | **0.15%** | **only format up** | Connected + unconnected (discovery engine) |
| Status (text) | 0.20% | 0.13% | down | Connected; best comments-per-post at every page size |
| Albums (multi-photo) | 0.18% | 0.13% | down | Connected; best *views* per post at every page size |
| Images | 0.15% | 0.13% | flat | Connected |
| Links | 0.05% | **0.05%** | flat at the bottom | Actively suppressed (§4) |

(Buffer's per-reach data shows a flatter spread — images 5.20%, video 4.84%, text 4.76%, links 4.43% — i.e., once FB *shows* a post, formats engage similarly. The big format differences are in *how many people FB shows it to*. That's why the play is format-per-job, not format-monoculture.)

### Format playbook

**Reels — the discovery format**
- Specs: 9:16, 1080×1920, MP4/H.264, 30fps+, ≤4GB. Since June 2025, *all video uploads on FB are Reels* — the separate "video post" type is gone; longer uploads just become long Reels.
- Length: 15–60s performs best for completion; **90s is FB's sweet spot for shares/comments** (Socialinsider: 90s FB Reels average ~4K views, ~21 shares, 0.13% ER). Note this is ~30s longer than the IG optimum (~60s) — FB's older audience tolerates longer clips. When a clip has a natural 75–90s cut, that's the FB cut.
- FB Reels vs IG Reels raw numbers (Socialinsider): IG wins on ER (0.70% vs 0.13%) and views (~5.7K vs ~4K) — but FB Reels reach *a different audience* (25–54) and FB Reels reach grew ~13% in 2025. It's additive, not either/or.
- One practitioner-reported claim (unverified, treat as hypothesis): same-day-created Reels may get meaningfully more distribution than older-file uploads — freshness metadata may matter. Low cost to comply: upload clips the day they're cut when possible.

**Status/text posts — the conversation format**
- Highest comments per post at every page size (Socialinsider tables). Zero production cost.
- Best uses for Trevor: hot takes on MLB news, "coach's question of the day" for Neptune, behind-the-scenes one-liners, polls/questions (highest engagement-per-effort format on FB in 2026).
- Write them like a person, not a brand — 1–4 sentences, one clear reaction hook.

**Albums (3–10 photos) — the reach-per-effort sleeper**
- Highest *views* per post at every page size in Socialinsider's tables and the top engagement format for smaller pages. FB's older audience still browses photo albums in a way no other platform's audience does.
- Uses: facility buildout progress dumps (Neptune), shoot-day BTS, podcast guest photos, "week at Mayday" recaps.

**Single images** — fine, stable, unremarkable. Use when there's one strong image; otherwise prefer album or text.

**Link posts** — see §4. Default: don't.

**Live video** — still earns notification-driven connected reach and is the vehicle for Stars. Low priority unless there's a natural event (Neptune open house, watch-along).

## 4. The link penalty — exact state of play (as of mid-2026)

The evidence this is real, not folklore:

- Meta's Widely Viewed Content Report (Q1 2025): **~97–98% of U.S. feed views are on posts without links**; links appear in <2% of top-performing posts.
- Socialinsider: link posts flat at **0.05% ER** — half or less of every other format — for two straight years.
- Dec 2025: Meta began testing a **limit of 2 link posts/month** for professional accounts and Pages unless subscribed to Meta Verified ($14.99/mo; higher tiers $50/$150/$500). Per TechCrunch: affiliate links exempt, links *in comments* exempt, links to Meta properties (FB/IG/WhatsApp posts) don't count, publishers currently excluded. Over-limit posts get minimal reach rather than being blocked. Test status — watch whether it ships broadly.
- Meta has itself surfaced guidance in the Professional Dashboard recommending **links in the first comment** instead of the post body.

### The link playbook

1. **Default: no URL in any post body.** Ever. Including YouTube links.
2. **Link in first comment**, posted immediately after publishing (Meta-sanctioned, unlimited, uncounted against the test cap). Reference it in the post: "Full video linked in the comments."
3. **For YouTube episodes:** post a native 60–90s Reel of the best moment + first-comment link, rather than a link post with the thumbnail. The link post version is reaching ~1/3 the people before anyone even clicks.
4. **Comment-trigger → DM flow** for anything conversion-oriented (Neptune assessments, Substack): "Comment ASSESS and I'll DM you the details." Mari Smith's 2026 read: Meta is actively privileging Messenger flows — "get people into the DMs, because that's what Meta wants." Links inside Messenger are unrestricted.
5. **Meta Verified:** Mari Smith's advice, endorsed here — **skip it; spend the $15/mo on ads instead**, unless tracked conversions from body links demonstrably pay for it.
6. Cross-links to Instagram/Facebook posts are safe (don't count as external links).

## 5. IG→FB crossposting: mechanics and verdict

### How the native toggle works (mechanics, as of 2026)

- Requires linking in **Meta Accounts Center**: IG creator/business account + FB Page (+ a personal FB profile with admin on the Page). IG Profile → Edit Profile → Public business information → Connect.
- Once linked, the composer shows a **"Share to Facebook"** toggle per post/Reel/Story; Accounts Center can also auto-share every post.
- What carries over: caption (including IG-style hashtags), media, and the Reel itself. IG surfaces the FB post's reactions/comments in IG notifications.
- Known failure modes: Reels occasionally crosspost as a still image (long-standing bug — another reason to post to each platform's endpoint independently); analytics get conflated (FB plays/likes partially reported through IG), making per-platform performance reads muddy in Meta Business Suite.

### The performance verdict

**Agorapulse Social Media Lab controlled test** (3 pages × 7 native FB posts vs 7 IG-crossposted posts):

| Page | Native FB reach advantage |
|---|---|
| Agorapulse | +86.44% (1,316 vs 706) |
| Space Walk of Central Texas | +58.24% (1,076 vs 680) |
| Grace Bible Church | +72.19% (291 vs 189) |
| **Average** | **+72.28%** |

Likes: +140% and +100% for native on two of three pages. Sharing the IG URL as a link post tested *even worse*. Their conclusion, verbatim: "Do not recommend posting from Instagram to your Facebook business page — unless you want to risk having lower reach."

Small-N study (3 pages, 42 posts) — but directionally consistent with everything else: Meta rewards native posting, and crossposted Reels carry IG-shaped metadata/captions that fit FB worse.

### Decision rules

- **Reels you care about → post natively to FB** via Metricool (already in the stack) with an FB-specific caption: no hashtag walls (1–3 max on FB), older-audience framing, first-comment link if needed. Marginal cost per post: ~2 minutes.
- **Prefer the 90s cut on FB** when one exists; the 60s cut on IG. (One clip, two trims.)
- **Stories: the toggle is acceptable.** Stories are ephemeral, low-stakes, and the reach penalty matters least there. Auto-share IG Stories → FB is a reasonable default to keep the FB page "alive" daily at zero cost.
- **Feed photos: borderline.** If it's throwaway, toggle is fine; if it's a tentpole (announcement, milestone), post natively with rewritten copy.
- **Never crosspost anything with a TikTok watermark** to FB Reels — same detection/deprioritization as IG (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).
- **Audit quarterly:** Meta Business Suite → compare reach on crossposted vs native posts for your own pages; the 72% figure is someone else's audience, verify against yours.

## 6. Page vs Professional Mode — setup decisions

Two ways to run a public FB presence as of 2026:

| | Professional Mode (personal profile) | Facebook Page |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Creator layer on the personal profile; public followers without friend requests | Standalone business entity |
| Reach character | Often better — people follow *people*; public posts get Page-like distribution incl. recommendations | Standard Page distribution (1–6% of followers) |
| Monetization | Stars, content monetization (5,000+ followers, consistent niche) — but **no subscriptions** | Full suite incl. subscriptions |
| Ads | **Cannot run ads** (no ad account attachment) | Full Ads Manager / Business Manager |
| Team access | None — one person's login | Multiple admins/editors (VA, editor, agency) |
| Best for | Solo creator identity | Business with staff and ad spend |

**Recommended architecture for Trevor:**
- **"Trevor May" personal profile in Professional Mode** — the athlete-creator identity. Personal-feeling posts, takes, BTS. This is where FB's people-over-pages distribution advantage lives, and where an ex-MLB name accrues followers fastest.
- **Neptune Performance as a Page** — it's a business: needs team admin access, ad capability (local FB ads are the obvious Neptune paid channel), Events, reviews, and a Google-indexable public presence.
- A Mayday Media / More Mayday Page only if the team wants scheduled clip distribution with staff access; otherwise the professional-mode profile can carry the creator content.

**Professional Mode setup (30 seconds):** profile → three-dot menu under cover photo → "Turn on professional mode" → category (Public Figure or Creator — pick one and keep it; niche consistency is a monetization eligibility factor). Turning it off pauses monetization but preserves followers.

**Page setup checklist (Neptune):** category = Sports & Fitness/local business subtype; complete address + hours + service area (local recommendation eligibility); "Book Now"/"Send Message" CTA button; username claimed; About section written with searchable terms ("baseball training," city name — FB search and Google both index it); reviews enabled; linked WhatsApp/Messenger for inquiry handling; Meta Business Suite access granted to whoever handles scheduling.

**Monetization reality check (as of 2026):** In-stream Ads, Ads on Reels, and Performance Bonus **all ended Aug 31, 2025**, folded into the invite/eligibility-based **Facebook Content Monetization** program (unified payouts across Reels, videos, photos, text). Eligibility roughly: professional mode/Page, 5,000+ followers, consistent output, clean standing — check the Professional Dashboard for actual status. Expect trivial revenue relative to YouTube; FB monetization is a byproduct, never a goal that shapes content decisions.

## 7. Follower growth mechanics (2025–2026)

FB page follower growth actually accelerated recently — Socialinsider: average page growth **doubled from 12.2% (2024) to 23.2% (2025)**, with 10–50K pages growing fastest (38.2%). The mechanics that drive it:

1. **The invite loop (highest-leverage, most-skipped).** Every post surfaces non-followers who reacted. Page → post → reactions list → "Invite" button next to non-followers. Practitioner-reported conversion: **20–40%** of invited reactors follow. Make it a weekly 10-minute VA task on every post with meaningful reactions. This is the FB-specific compounding tactic with no equivalent on other platforms.
2. **Reels cadence.** Reels are the fastest follower-conversion format because they're the unconnected-reach format. 2026 growth baseline from multiple practitioner sources: **3–4 Reels + 1–2 other posts per week**; aggressive: 4–6 Reels/week + daily Stories. (Cadence philosophy: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md.)
3. **First-hour comment discipline.** Reply to every comment within the hour, with sentences not emojis — velocity feeds distribution, and repeat interactions raise those users' future delivery.
4. **Timing.** Buffer 2026 (161K profiles): FB winners cluster **8–11am weekdays, peaking Thursday ~9am**. Verify against Page-specific "audience active times" in the Professional Dashboard — sports audiences also spike evenings/weekends around games.
5. **Groups as the reach cheat code.** Group posts aren't throttled like Page posts. For Neptune: participate personally (not as the Page) in local youth-baseball/travel-ball parent Groups — answer questions, be the resident pro — and consider an owned Group ("[City] Baseball Development" style) once the facility opens. The 2026 pattern among effective FB marketers: **Groups for organic community, Pages for ads and indexable presence.**
6. **Cross-pollination from owned channels.** Podcast/YouTube CTAs mentioning the FB presence specifically for the older-fan segment ("the good arguments happen on my Facebook").

- Per-follower ER benchmark: 0.15% average; 0.3% = sports-category good; link posts 0.05%.
- Organic reach: 1–6% of followers (connected); Reels only reliable unconnected lane.
- Feed composition: ~30% AI-recommended (Zuckerberg), up to ~50% unconnected total.
- Link test cap: 2 body-link posts/month for non-Verified pro accounts (Dec 2025 test); comments/affiliate/Meta-property links exempt.
- Crosspost penalty: −72% reach avg (Agorapulse, small-N).
- Reply lift: +9.5% engagement (Buffer).
- Best windows: weekdays 8–11am, Thursday 9am peak (Buffer) — verify per page.

- Demo: heaviest 25–54; 54% of US 50–64 daily; oldest-skewing major platform = the parent/coach/legacy-MLB-fan surface.

## Common mistakes

1. **Using the IG "Share to Facebook" toggle for Reels** — eats the ~72% native-reach premium and occasionally posts a still image instead of the video.
2. **Putting the YouTube/Substack URL in the post body** — converts a 0.13–0.15% ER format into a 0.05% one before a single click happens; may soon count against a monthly link cap.
3. **Hashtag walls on FB.** IG caption habits crosspost badly; FB users read hashtag stacks as spam. 0–3 max.
4. **Treating FB as a mirror of IG** — same cut, same caption, same audience assumption. FB's audience is 10–20 years older; the 90s cut, plainer captions, and question-posts fit it.
5. **Posting and ghosting.** No first-hour replies = ceded distribution; the reply lift and relationship-recurrence signals are free.
6. **Never running the invite-reactors sweep** — leaving a 20–40%-conversion follower tap closed on every post.
7. **Chasing FB monetization** — building content around a payout program that restructured in 2025 and pays a rounding error vs YouTube.
8. **Buying Meta Verified for the link allowance** without tracked ROI — spend it on local ads for Neptune instead.
9. **Judging FB by IG benchmarks** — 0.15% per-follower ER on FB is *normal*; comparing to IG's 0.70% Reels ER and concluding "FB is dead" misses that it's a different audience acquired at zero marginal cost.
10. **Neptune relying on the Page alone and ignoring Groups** — Page posts are throttled to 1–6%; local parent Groups are where the actual reach is.
