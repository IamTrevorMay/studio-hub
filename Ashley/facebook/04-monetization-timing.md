# Facebook Monetization, Posting Times & Cadence

Tactical reference for deciding how much Facebook effort Mayday Media should spend, how to get paid for it, and exactly when/how often to post. Strategy-level short-form doctrine (hooks, clipping, platform algorithm differences) lives at ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md — this doc covers the Facebook-specific money and scheduling mechanics.

## TL;DR

- **One program now: Facebook Content Monetization (CMP).** In-stream ads, Ads on Reels, and Performance Bonus all ended August 31, 2025. CMP pays performance-based on *all* eligible public content — Reels, long videos, photos, text posts — from a single Insights tab. If Trevor's page is still thinking in "in-stream ads" terms, that mental model is dead (as of 2026).
- **Check Creator Fast Track eligibility immediately (launched March 18, 2026).** 100K+ followers on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube = $1,000/month guaranteed; 1M+ = $3,000/month, for 3 months, for posting 15 original Reels per 30 days across 10+ distinct days — content Mayday already produces. It also grants instant CMP access (normally invite-gated) and a persistent reach boost. This is near-free money for a clipping operation.
- **RPM reality: entertainment/sports content earns $0.30–$1.50 per 1,000 monetized views; Reels-heavy pages net $0.02–$0.20 per 1,000 raw plays.** Long-form (3+ min) video is the only format that earns YouTube-adjacent rates ($1–$5 RPM). Facebook direct payouts are a bonus layer, not a business — but at scale (Meta paid creators ~$3B in 2025, +35% YoY) it's real found money on content that already exists.
- **30–50% of views won't pay.** Ineligible views (fast scrolls, non-ad geos, unlicensed audio, unoriginal content flags) are the #1 gap between expected and actual payout. Original upload + Meta Sound Collection audio + US-skewing audience is the controllable part.
- **Post midweek, midday-to-evening.** Sprout (2B engagements, 307K profiles, 2026): Mon 12–1pm, Tue/Wed 12–8pm, Thu 12–2pm local. Facebook is unique in that engagement holds into the *evening* — schedule the day's best clip at 12–1pm and a second piece 6–8pm. Sunday is the worst day on every study.
- **Cadence floor: never a zero week.** Buffer's 4.8M channel-week analysis found a "no-post penalty" — accounts that skip a week underperform their own baseline growth. 1–2 posts/week massively beats zero; brand median is 2–6 posts/week (~39/month); 10+/week correlated with +32 followers/week vs silent weeks.
- **Verdict for Mayday: Facebook is a syndication-plus platform, not a native-first one.** FB's strong 35–65 demo = baseball parents and older MLB fans = podcast clips, Trevor May Baseball instructional content, and eventually Neptune Performance local lead-gen. Run a repackage pipeline (~2–4 hr/week), take the CMP/Fast Track money, and invest zero in FB-only formats until the data argues otherwise.

## 1. The 2026 monetization landscape (what exists, what died)

### Dead as of August 31, 2025
- **In-stream ads** (standalone program)
- **Ads on Reels**
- **Performance Bonus** (the old invite-only bonus)
- **Reels Play Bonus** (killed even earlier)

Any guide, checklist, or creator advice referencing these as live programs is stale. Their *eligibility thresholds* still echo in how Meta screens invites (see §2), but the payout programs are gone.

### Alive as of mid-2026

| Program | What it pays for | How to get in |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook Content Monetization (CMP)** | Performance-based payouts on eligible public Reels, longer videos, photos, text posts | Was invite-only beta (Oct 2024, ~1M creators); open enrollment rolled out through 2025; instant access via Creator Fast Track |
| **Creator Fast Track** (launched 3/18/2026) | $1K–$3K/mo guaranteed × 3 months + reach boost + CMP access | Apply at creators.facebook.com/creator-fast-track with 100K+ followers on IG/TikTok/YouTube |
| **Stars** | Viewer tipping — $0.01 per Star to creator, on video/live/some posts | Separate onboarding in Professional Dashboard; engaged communities report $100–$1,000/mo |
| **Branded content / Paid partnerships** | Sponsor deals disclosed via the paid-partnership tool | No gate beyond compliance; Meta facilitates but doesn't pay |
| **Subscriptions (fan supporters)** | Monthly recurring from superfans | Invite/eligibility-gated; low priority for a syndication play |

### How CMP actually pays
- **Performance-based model** inherited from the three legacy programs: earnings are tied to "engagement, views and plays your eligible public content receives on Facebook" (Meta's words). There is no published rate card.
- One enrollment covers **all formats**. About two-thirds of legacy-monetizing creators were only in one program — CMP's whole pitch is monetizing the photo posts and text posts that used to earn nothing.
- **Consolidated Insights tab** (Professional Dashboard mobile / Meta Business Suite desktop) shows per-post earnings. In 2026 Meta added metrics showing *which views qualified*, approximate earnings rate, and why views were disqualified — use this monthly, it's the only ground truth.
- **One-way door:** once enrolled in CMP you cannot return to legacy programs (moot now, but explains why some pages migrated late).
- **Payouts:** monthly, around the 21st for the prior month; $100 minimum threshold; 18+ and tax/identity verification required before anything transfers.

### Creator Fast Track — the immediate action item (as of 2026)
Requirements and terms, precisely:
- **Eligibility:** 100,000+ followers on Instagram, TikTok, or YouTube (Tier 1 = $1,000/mo) or 1M+ on any one of them (Tier 2 = $3,000/mo). Facebook follower count irrelevant.
- **Work required:** ≥15 Reels on Facebook per 30-day window, posted across ≥10 distinct days. Content need NOT be exclusive — cross-posted is fine — but must be **original to the creator** (AI-assisted allowed if it's yours).
- **Duration:** 3 months of guaranteed pay. Reach boost on eligible Reels reportedly persists after the guarantee ends ("in perpetuity" per Engadget's coverage).
- **Kicker:** immediate CMP enrollment, skipping the invite queue.
- **Ashley's math for Trevor:** 15 Reels/month is ~4/week — below the clip volume a weekly podcast + two YouTube channels already generate. If any Mayday-connected account clears 100K on IG/TikTok/YouTube, this is $3,000–$9,000 total for scheduling clips that already exist. Check eligibility for both the personal brand accounts and any channel-branded accounts; apply on the strongest one.

## 2. Eligibility playbook: zero → paid

Meta's screening blends the legacy thresholds with new CMP criteria. Build to all of them; the invite/approval logic is opaque and the strictest number governs.

**Structural requirements (checklist):**
- [ ] Page or **professional-mode profile** (professional mode on a profile is the fastest route for a personal brand like Trevor's)
- [ ] Page/profile ≥ 90 days old (pages under 90 days or recently recategorized get auto-blocked)
- [ ] Creator is 18+, in an eligible country (US ✓ — full program access)
- [ ] Payout account: bank name exactly matching page owner, current tax forms (W-9), identity verification complete — do this *before* earnings accrue; mismatches freeze visible-but-untransferable money

**Audience/volume thresholds (legacy numbers that still shape screening):**
- [ ] ~5,000–10,000 followers on the Facebook page itself (bonus tracks reached down to 5K in beta; 10K is the safe target)
- [ ] Video watch: 600,000 total minutes viewed in past 60 days, incl. 60,000 minutes from 1-minute+ views (legacy in-stream bar)
- [ ] Reels track: ~600,000 Reel plays in past 60 days (cited in 2026 guides)
- [ ] ≥5 videos published in past 30 days (activity signal)

**Conduct requirements:**
- [ ] Zero Community Standards removals in past 90 days
- [ ] No account warnings/restrictions in past 60 days
- [ ] No copyright strikes; no reused/unoriginal content flags
- [ ] Compliant with Partner Monetization Policies + Content Monetization Policies (check Professional Dashboard → Monetization → Policy Issues monthly)

**Fastest sequence for a page starting cold:**
1. Enable professional mode / confirm Page setup; complete payout + tax info day one.
2. Apply to Creator Fast Track off the external follower count (skips most of the above if accepted).
3. If not Fast Track eligible: run the syndication cadence in §6 for 60–90 days to build the watch-minute base, keep the record clean, then express interest in CMP via the Professional Dashboard prompt.

## 3. RPM benchmarks & revenue math (as of 2026)

No official rate card exists; these are converged practitioner/aggregator numbers. Treat ±50% as normal variance.

### By format
| Format | Payout per 1,000 views | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Long-form video (3+ min) | **$1–$5 RPM** | Only format with mid-roll ad economics; 3–8+ min enables mid-rolls; longer = more ad slots |
| Videos under 3 min | well under $1 | Pre-roll only in legacy terms; CMP pays but thin |
| Reels | **$0.02–$0.20 per 1,000 plays** (median ~$0.05–$0.10) | Volume game; "$0.30–$1.50 RPM" figures you'll see quoted are per *monetized* view, not raw plays |
| Photos / text posts | pennies, engagement-scored | New under CMP; treat as rounding error that rewards posting anyway |
| Stars | $0.01/Star | $100–$1,000/mo reported for engaged communities; live streams are the main Stars surface |

### By niche (Reels/video RPM, 2025–2026 reported ranges)
| Niche | RPM |
|---|---|
| Finance/business | $2.00–$5.00 (outliers to $8–$10) |
| Health/wellness | $1.00–$3.00 |
| General interest | $0.50–$2.00 |
| **Entertainment/sports (Mayday's lane)** | **$0.30–$1.50** |

### By geography (advertiser CPM feeding the pool, 2026 Lebesgue benchmark)
US ≈ $16.08 CPM · Canada/Australia ≈ $11.5 · UK/W. Europe high tier · Brazil $2.63 · Mexico $3.92 · India $1.36. **A US-majority audience is worth ~10x an India-majority one per view.** Baseball content naturally skews US/CA — a structural RPM advantage for Trevor vs. generic entertainment pages.

### Worked examples (calibrate expectations)
- Reference case from 2026 data: finance page, 100K followers, 500K monthly views ≈ **$1,500/mo** at $3 RPM. Same audience size on Instagram direct payouts: ~$0–$200/mo.
- **Mayday realistic case:** 2M monthly Reel plays at $0.05–$0.10/1,000 = **$100–$200/mo** from Reels alone. Add 200K views/mo on 3-min+ podcast segments at ~$1 RPM = +$200. Add Fast Track = +$1,000–$3,000/mo for 3 months. Conclusion: **Fast Track + long-form segments are where the actual dollars are; Reels payouts are a vanity line.**
- Seasonality: Q4 (Oct–Dec) RPM peaks with advertiser demand; expect a Jan trough. Also relevant: MLB playoffs (Oct) = peak baseball CPMs *and* peak topical volume — stack heavier posting there.
- Earnings volatility is real: beta testers reported the same view count paying $500 one period and $5 another depending on traffic geography and originality scoring. Judge RPM on 90-day averages, never single posts.

## 4. Eligible views: where 30–50% of the money leaks

Creators consistently report **30–50% of raw views are payout-ineligible**. The 2026 Insights update shows qualified vs disqualified views per post — audit it monthly. Disqualifiers:

1. **Fast scrolls / sub-threshold watches** — a "view" registers at ~1 millisecond for the vanity metric, but doesn't pay. Hook quality (see Carl short-form doc §hooks) is a *revenue* lever here, not just reach.
2. **Non-ad-serving geographies** — views from countries without ad delivery pay $0. You can't control it, but US-topical content (MLB) self-selects.
3. **Unlicensed audio** — any music outside the **Meta Sound Collection** can zero a post's monetization and, worse, flag the page. Podcast/interview voice content is inherently safe; never add trending-audio overlays to monetized FB uploads.
4. **Unoriginality flags** — visible TikTok/YouTube watermarks, unedited re-uploads of others' content, or "minimal edits" recycling. Cross-posting your own content is fine (Fast Track explicitly allows it) but strip watermarks and upload native files.
5. **Policy-flagged content** — one flagged post can pause earnings across *all* formats on the page (cascade risk, see §8).

## 5. Posting times: what the 2026 data actually says

Three big studies disagree on hours because they measure differently — reconcile by understanding what each measures:

| Study | Sample | Finding | What it optimizes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sprout Social 2026** | ~2B engagements, 307K profiles (Nov 2025–Feb 2026), local time | Mon 12–1pm · **Tue/Wed 12–8pm** · Thu 12–2pm & 8pm; Mon–Thu best, weekends worst | Total engagement (when the most people scroll) |
| **Buffer 2026** | 14M FB posts | **Thursday 9am** single best slot; weekday mornings > afternoons | Engagement *rate* (low-competition windows) |
| **Hootsuite 2025** | 1M+ posts, 118 countries | Peaks 5–8am, top Tue 5am | Engagement rate, global blend |

**Consensus that survives all three:** Tuesday–Thursday >> Friday–Sunday; **Sunday is the worst day on every study**; Facebook engagement uniquely **stretches into the evening** (unlike LinkedIn/X which die after work hours) because it's a leisure-scroll platform.

**Ashley's operating rule for Mayday (audience: US baseball fans/parents, ET-heavy):**
- **Primary slot: 12–1pm ET Tue/Wed/Thu** — lunch scroll, the highest-consensus window.
- **Secondary slot: 6–8pm ET** — Facebook's evening tail; also when parents are done with practice pickup. Good for the day's second post or discussion-bait text posts.
- **Game-adjacent exception:** reactive MLB content posts when the moment happens, not on schedule. A take on last night's game posted 8–10am next morning beats a "perfect" Tuesday slot 4 days later.
- **Weekend caveat:** general data says avoid weekends, but *live baseball happens on weekends*. Test Saturday morning (youth-tournament scroll time) for Trevor May Baseball instructional content specifically; judge against the page's own baseline, not global averages.
- Once the page has ~8+ weeks of data, Meta Business Suite's "when your followers are online" beats every third-party study. Global studies are the cold-start default, nothing more.

## 6. Cadence & content-type mix

### Frequency benchmarks (2026)
- Brand median: **2–6 posts/week** (Rival IQ); average ~39 posts/month (Socialinsider, down 22% YoY — the market is posting *less*, which lowers competition).
- Buffer (4.8M channel-weeks, 161K FB profiles): **no magic number exists**, but (a) top performers post more than median, (b) the **"no-post penalty" is real** — zero-post weeks underperform the account's own baseline growth, (c) 10+ posts/week ↔ +32 followers/week vs silent weeks, (d) even 1–2/week strongly beats 0.
- Translation: **the floor matters more than the ceiling.** Never a dark week; scale volume only if the pipeline is automated.

### Format performance (know both scoreboards)
- **Engagement rate per post** (Socialinsider, 25M posts): overall 0.15%; status/text posts 0.20% > albums 0.18% > Reels 0.15% > images 0.13% > links 0.05%. **Links are the worst format on Facebook — put URLs in the first comment** (Mari Smith's standing tactic).
- Buffer's median-rate view: images 5.20% ≥ video 4.84% ≥ text 4.76% ≥ links 4.43% — format matters *less* on Facebook than any other platform. Don't over-engineer format choice; over-engineer the content.
- **Reach/discovery** is a different scoreboard: Reels get the strongest algorithmic push and were the only format with a Q1 2026 engagement uplift; ~50% of feed content is now from unconnected sources (Mari Smith), so follower count ≠ reach ceiling. Video gets shared far more than other formats.
- **Replying to comments = +9.5% engagement** (Buffer; 54% of pages did better when replying). Fifteen minutes of replies after each post is the cheapest lever on the platform.
- Image specs: 4:5 aspect ratio outperforms square. Color-background text posts (question prompts) 1–2×/week are a proven cheap engagement format. Stories are the most under-used free reach surface — use them to test angles before committing a post.

### Mayday weekly template (~2–4 hrs/week, all repurposed)
| Day | Slot (ET) | Post | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | 12pm | Reel — podcast clip (best moment of last ep) | Mayday! podcast |
| Tue | 12–1pm | **Native 3–8 min video** — podcast segment or TMB instructional cut | Pods/YouTube — the RPM play |
| Tue | 7pm | Text/color-background post — discussion question ("Who's your NL Cy Young right now?") | 5 min native |
| Wed | 12–1pm | Reel — Trevor May Baseball drill/teaching clip | TMB channel |
| Thu | 12pm | Reel — reactive MLB take or More Mayday clip | Whatever's topical |
| Thu | 7pm | Photo/album — BTS, facility progress, card-collecting | Camera roll |
| Sat (test) | 9–10am | TMB instructional Reel aimed at parents/coaches | TMB |

That's 6–7 posts/week incl. 3–4 Reels — clears the Fast Track quota (15 Reels/30 days over 10+ days) with margin, stays inside the 2–6/week+ brand norm, and mixes formats per the engagement data. Reactive game content overrides the calendar whenever news breaks.

### Content-mix ratios
- ~50% Reels (discovery + Fast Track quota) · ~20% native 3min+ video (the actual RPM) · ~20% text/photo engagement posts (cheap, highest ER, monetized under CMP) · ~10% Stories/live tests.
- Every 3min+ upload must be **native** (uploaded file), never a YouTube link — links get 0.05% ER and zero monetization.

## 7. Native effort vs. syndication: the decision for a sports/entertainment brand

(Strategy level — platform prioritization and short-form doctrine: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md. This is the FB-specific call.)

**The case against native-first:** entertainment RPM is bottom-tier ($0.30–$1.50); organic page reach for small pages is brutal (Socialinsider: a 10–50K page averages ~215 views on a status post); Mayday's growth engine is YouTube and the team has no spare production capacity for a fourth native platform.

**The case against ignoring it:** Facebook's demo (strong 35–65) is *exactly* baseball's — dads, coaches, older MLB fans who remember Trevor pitching, and critically the **parents who will write checks to Neptune Performance**. Crossposting carries no algorithmic penalty (only watermarks and audience-mismatch do). Meta is actively paying for exactly this behavior (Fast Track, ~$3B creator payouts in 2025, +35% YoY). Digiday-documented precedent: Business Insider scaled a whole YouTube channel off its Facebook video library — repackaging between these two platforms works because the long-ish-video audiences overlap.

**Verdict: "syndication-plus."**
1. **Syndicate everything** via the §6 template — native uploads, watermark-free, FB-written captions (never bare cross-post captions with TikTok-isms).
2. **Plus** three genuinely native cheap layers: (a) first-comment links + 15 min of comment replies per post, (b) 1–2 text/question posts weekly (highest ER format, ~5 min each), (c) reactive-MLB timing overrides.
3. **Take every Meta check:** Fast Track now if eligible; CMP enrollment; Stars on when live.
4. **Escalate only on evidence:** if within 6 months the page shows (a) >$500/mo CMP earnings, (b) >20% of reach from non-followers, or (c) measurable Neptune inquiries from FB — then consider FB-native formats (live Q&As, parent-focused groups). Facebook Groups + local targeting become a *separate, Neptune-specific* play when the facility opens (facility lead-gen ≠ media reach; see Carl doc §1, job-4 content).
5. Cap: if after 6 months the page can't beat ~$100/mo + negligible funnel effect, drop to pure auto-syndication (Metricool scheduling, zero native effort) and revisit annually.

## 8. Common mistakes

1. **Optimizing for raw views instead of eligible views.** 30–50% of views don't pay. The Insights "qualified views" breakdown (added 2026) is the real scoreboard.
2. **Posting YouTube links instead of native uploads.** 0.05% engagement rate, zero monetization, algorithmically buried. The single most common syndication error.
3. **Leaving watermarks on cross-posts.** Triggers unoriginality flags that can pause monetization *page-wide*, not per-post.
4. **Any music outside Meta Sound Collection.** One trending-audio Reel can freeze earnings across all formats. Voice-only clips (podcast, instruction) are inherently safe — Mayday's advantage.
5. **The cascade error: appealing before cleaning up.** A policy flag pauses everything; remove the offending content *first*, then appeal. Appealing with violations still live entrenches the strike.
6. **Payout plumbing left for later.** Bank-name mismatch, stale (>12 mo) tax forms, or incomplete ID verification = earnings visible but frozen; routinely misdiagnosed as a policy problem. Set up day one.
7. **Burst-then-dark posting.** The no-post penalty means one zero week costs baseline growth; 15 Reels crammed into week one also fails Fast Track's 10-distinct-days rule. Automate the floor.
8. **Judging FB by single-post RPM.** Geographic traffic mix and originality scoring make per-post earnings swing 100x. 90-day averages only.
9. **Chasing a global "best time" over own-page data.** Studies conflict by methodology; after ~8 weeks, Meta Business Suite audience-activity data wins. Also: scheduled-slot dogma that makes reactive sports content wait 3 days for a "good" time slot.
10. **Ignoring unlabeled AI-content rules (2026).** AI-assisted content is monetizable but unlabeled synthetic content triggers "inauthentic content" flags. Label it, add original voice/edits.
11. **Treating a page recategorization as harmless.** Recategorizing or renaming a page can reset eligibility screening (90-day-style holds). Don't churn page identity while pursuing monetization.
