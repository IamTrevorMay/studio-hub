## TL;DR

1. **Trevor May Baseball is a 179K-sub, 29.3M-lifetime-view channel growing ~16% YTD** (153.8K → 179K subs, Jan 1 → Jul 12, 2026), with 849 total videos since Nov 2019. It is ~11x the size of More Mayday (16.6K subs, 3.37M views) but MM is growing 4x faster in percentage terms (+300% subs YTD).
2. **Momentum peaked in May and has been sliding since:** monthly views went 1.23M (Apr) → 1.58M (May) → 836K (Jun) → 281K in the first 10 days of July (~28K/day, roughly June's pace). May's spike was outlier-driven, not cadence-driven.
3. **Median recent long-form video does ~47K views; the winners are 2–5x that and share one DNA:** first-person MLB insider access. "Behind the scenes of MLB Travel" (231.8K, **5.0x median**), "Hilarious History of Cheating in MLB" (158.8K, 3.4x), "I pitched in a game for the FIRST time since MLB" (134.4K, 2.9x), "10 brutal truths about Pro Baseball" (134.2K, 2.9x). The losers (0.4x) are news-cycle commentary ("Prediction Market Deal" 18.0K, "Ins & Outs for 2026" 20.2K).
4. **Retention is genuinely strong — 40–55% average view percentage on 13–30 minute videos** (YouTube Analytics via studio DB) — so the constraint is packaging + topic selection, not the content itself.
5. **Search is nearly untapped: only 3.2% of lifetime views come from YouTube search** (897K of 27.9M in the 2026-05-25 lifetime snapshot), on a channel perfectly positioned for evergreen "explained by a real MLB pitcher" queries. Browse/subscriber feed = 44.6%, Shorts feed = 31.2%, Suggested = 10.8%.
6. **Shorts bring 31% of views but almost zero subscribers:** long-form outliers gained 263–368 subs each, while similarly-viewed Shorts gained 0–8. Shorts are reach, not conversion, and there is no visible funnel from them.
7. **Podcast-length episodes ("Mayday!", 68–81 min) do 2.1K–5.3K views at 0–28% retention** — 0.1x median — and are diluting the uploads feed and channel averages.
8. **Engagement on recent long-form is healthy: ~2.7% median like rate** (range 1.3–4.7%, RSS like counts / views on the last 14 uploads).

## 9. Packaging observations

From titles/durations on the last ~80 uploads (both tabs). Thumbnail analysis was not possible (vidIQ score_thumbnail unavailable; no CTR data — `impressions`/`impressions_ctr` are 0 in the DB because YouTube impressions only arrive via manual CSV upload in this stack).

- **Winning titles are first-person or insider-framed:** "I pitched…", "Behind the scenes of…", "…according to players", "Ex-MLB Player attempts…". Losing titles read like sports-radio segments: "Who Is Going To Be The AL Cy Young??", "Let the MLB negotiations begin!", "the Ins & Outs for the 2026 MLB Season" (lowercase "the" — copyediting slips ship regularly).
- **The credential is the differentiator but is inconsistent in titles:** ~1/3 of titles carry "MLB pitcher / Ex-Major leaguer / since MLB"; the rest could be any baseball channel.
- **Inconsistent casing and punctuation:** doubled "??", ALL-CAPS words vary video to video; older Shorts still carry hashtag-stuffed titles (#baseball #mlb #wtf) that look spammy in feeds.
- **Vague curiosity titles underperform without a stake:** "I have a confession" (20.8K, 0.4x) — curiosity gap with no baseball payload signal.
- **Podcast episodes ship with member-cast packaging** ("… | Mayday!") into the same feed as flagship videos.

## 10. Top opportunities (ranked)

1. **Reallocate the calendar toward the proven 2–5x formats and away from news commentary.** Roughly 12 of the last 30 long-form videos were news/prediction pieces averaging ~25K (0.5x); insider-access and first-person videos averaged ~120K. Concretely: 3 of every 4 long-form slots should be insider/BTS ("Behind the scenes of…", "…according to players", "what X is really like") or comeback/challenge episodes ("I pitched…" is a nascent series — serialize it with numbered episodes and a season arc). Push news reaction to More Mayday, which exists for exactly that.
2. **Build a search/evergreen layer.** Search is 3.2% of lifetime views on a channel that can own "explained by a real MLB pitcher" queries (the existing "Pitcher DECEPTION, explained" at 55.6% retention proves the format holds). Target 1–2 evergreen explainers/month with keyword-first titles (pitch grips, velocity training, "why lefties…", scouting/contract mechanics). *Blocked measurement:* run `vidiq_keyword_research` on candidate terms once credits reset to size and rank the list.
3. **Fix the Shorts → subscriber funnel.** 31% of views convert to ~0 subs. Every Short should be cut from (or point to) a long-form parent via related-video links, pinned comments, and matching titles ("full breakdown on the channel"); the 189K "Relievers are weirdos" Short had a natural parent in "The 14 Perks of being an MLB closer" (112K) — that pairing should be deliberate, every time. Also raise end-screen usage (currently 0.2% of traffic) and suggested-traffic chaining with series playlists.
4. **Stabilize cadence at a fixed weekly slot + measure packaging.** Browse/subscriber feed is 44.6% of views, and June's 8–12-day gaps line up with the 47% June view drop. Lock one flagship slot/week (plus MM's 3/week). In parallel, close the packaging measurement hole: upload the YouTube Studio impressions/CTR CSV monthly (the sync can't fetch impressions via API) and re-run `vidiq_score_title` / `vidiq_score_thumbnail` on the next 8 uploads when credits reset — right now CTR is invisible, so title/thumbnail decisions are unmeasured.
5. **Get the podcast out of the flagship feed.** Five 68–81-minute "Mayday!" episodes at 2.1–5.3K views and 0–28% retention sit in the uploads feed between 100K+ videos, dragging browse performance signals and cluttering the channel page. Options: move to More Mayday, a dedicated podcast channel/playlist-only surface, or chop into 8–15-min topic cuts (its best segments are exactly the "unhinged takes/mailbag" material that does 80K as standalone videos).
