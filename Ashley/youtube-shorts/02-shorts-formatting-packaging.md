# Shorts Formatting & Packaging

Tactical execution reference: specs, hook craft, loops, captions, titles, length, sound, and vertical-crop workflow for YouTube Shorts. Strategy-level context (what Shorts are *for*, platform-by-platform algorithm comparison, cadence, clip-selection editorial strategy) lives at ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md — this doc assumes that and goes one level down into the craft.

## TL;DR

- **The swipe decision happens in ~1.3–1.8 seconds.** Frame zero must be visually legible, mid-action, and mute-readable (3–6 words of on-screen text max). If the verbal hook hasn't landed by second 3, the Short is dead — target >80% retention at the 3-second mark.
- **Design the ending before the beginning.** Seamless loops (last frame = first frame) push retention past 100%, which is the strongest single distribution signal; film or cut the ending shot first, then match the opener to it. But note: since March 31, 2025, loops inflate raw "views," while only **engaged views** count for monetization — loops help distribution, not RPM.
- **Default length: 20–35 seconds.** Under-25-second Shorts capture the majority of all Shorts views; 15–20s single-idea clips can hit 85–100%+ completion. Use 35–60s only for narrative/tutorial payoffs, and 60–180s only when retention data proves the audience holds — and beware the >60s Content ID global block (any claimed track = Short blocked worldwide).
- **Captions are non-negotiable and have exact specs**: middle 50–60% of frame, 2 lines max, ~5–7 words per line, bold sans-serif, 4.5:1 contrast minimum, appear 0.1–0.3s before the audio. Captioned Shorts run ~12–15% higher completion (most Shorts viewing is sound-capable but caption-dependent).
- **Titles matter for search/shelf surfaces, not the feed.** Front-load the promise in the first 40–50 characters; the feed itself only cares about the video's first seconds. Thumbnails for Shorts are frame-picks, not uploads (as of 2026) — choose the frame deliberately because it lives forever on the channel grid and in search.
- **From landscape masters (podcast, long-form), never center-crop blindly.** Speaker-tracked punch-ins or stacked split-screen for two-person conversation; centered crops that lose faces are the worst-performing reframe pattern. Re-hook every clip — the original conversational lead-in is never the hook.
- **Benchmarks to hold Ashley's judgments against** (82k-account data, as of 2026): ≥70% average percentage viewed = healthy, >100% = strong; swipe-away 10–30% healthy, >40% = hook failure; like-to-view 3–6% healthy.

## 1. Format specs & what counts as a Short (as of 2026)

| Spec | Value |
|---|---|
| Aspect ratio | 9:16 vertical (square also classifies as a Short) |
| Resolution | 1080 × 1920 px (export at this; platform downscales) |
| Max length | **3 minutes (180s)** for uploads on/after Oct 15, 2024 (Official Artist Channels: on/after Dec 8, 2025) |
| Classification rule | Square-or-vertical + ≤3 min = Short, automatically. To force long-form classification, use 16:9 |
| Export | MP4, H.264, 8–12 Mbps for 1080p30 |
| Music in Shorts | Most licensed songs usable up to **90 seconds** within a 3-min Short; some tracks capped at 60s or 30s |
| Content ID trap | Any Short **over 60s** with an active Content ID claim of any type is **blocked globally** — unplayable, unrecommendable, unmonetizable. Under 60s: no such block |
| Revenue share | 45% to creator via Shorts revenue pool; 3-min Shorts (post-Oct 15, 2024) are eligible |

Classification is automatic — there is no "publish as Short" toggle. If Trevor cuts a 3:20 vertical, it becomes a badly-framed long-form video; trim to 2:59 or under.

**View counting (changed March 31, 2025):** a "view" is now any start or loop — no minimum watch. **Engaged views** (watched past the initial seconds / interacted) are what count for YPP eligibility and revenue. Practical consequence: raw view counts are inflated and cross-era comparisons on the More Mayday dashboard should treat pre/post-March-2025 numbers as different currencies.

## 2. First-frame and first-second hook craft

### The physics
- Viewer decides in **~1.3–1.8 seconds** on Shorts (Reels is ~1.7s per Meta's internal data — same muscle).
- By second 3 the viewer must know **what they gain by staying**. Retention target at 3s: **>80%**.
- Todd Sherman (YouTube Shorts product lead): Shorts views come from the feed with no conscious choice — the video *is* the thumbnail. The algorithm seeds a small test audience; if they hold, distribution expands (explore → exploit). A weak first second kills the seed test and the graph flatlines.

### Frame-zero checklist (the literal first frame)
1. **Subject already in frame and in motion** — never fade in, never a static logo, never a slate. Start mid-action (mid-throw, mid-sentence, ball already in flight).
2. **Mute-readable**: 3–6 word text overlay stating the premise, on screen at 0:00 — not appearing at 0:01 (text landing after the 1-second mark loses ~half the swipe decisions).
3. **Visually anomalous**: unusual framing, motion, or object (pattern break). For baseball content: the grip close-up, the radar gun readout, the slow-mo release frame — something a scroller hasn't seen 40 times today.
4. **No ambiguity**: viewer should never spend the first second figuring out *what they're looking at*.
5. **Face or ball early**: human face with expression, or the object of curiosity, in the first frame — not an establishing wide.

### Verbal hook formulas (first line of audio)
- **Result-first**: show/state the payoff, then rewind. "This pitch ended a guy's career — here's the grip." (strategy-level hook taxonomy: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md)
- **Power words** (Jenny Hoyos): "banned," "secret," "free," "$1," "illegal," "nobody" — concrete, high-contrast words in the first sentence. Hoyos will "change the idea of the entire video for a strong hook."
- **But/So chaining** (Hoyos): every beat introduces tension or consequence. "Chick-fil-A has the best sandwich, **but** I'm not paying $6, **so** I'm making it for $1." Never chain with "and then" — chain with "but" and "so." Baseball version: "Every kid throws a curveball wrong, **but** it's not their fault, **so** here's what actually happens at release."
- **Foreshadow the ending in the first 3 seconds**: state the conflict + the promised payoff. The hook is a contract; the ending is the payment.
- **Insider-credential hook** (athlete-creator specific): "I threw 98 in the big leagues — this is the drill nobody told me about until AAA." Trevor's credential is a hook asset most Shorts creators would kill for; spend it in the first sentence, not the description.

### Hoyos retention doctrine (the most-cited Shorts practitioner, ~10M avg views/Short)
- Retention target: **90%+** average percentage viewed.
- Hook ≤3 seconds, then immediate foreshadowing of conflict + payoff.
- Transitions exist to move the story forward without breaking pace — no decorative transitions.
- **End on the emotional peak**: "the viewer decides how they felt about the video based on the intensity of the emotion at the end." Put the punchline/twist/payoff in the final 2 seconds, then hard-cut. Never trail off, never outro, never "follow for more" tail (it's the #1 late-video drop trigger).

### Pacing spec after the hook
- New visual element (cut, zoom, angle, prop, text change) every **1–2 seconds** for fast formats, every **3–7 seconds** minimum for longer/narrative Shorts.
- Trim every pause; conversational Shorts should have silence-stripped audio (remove breaths and gaps — 10–20% runtime reduction is normal from a raw podcast clip).
- Micro-payoffs every **5–8 seconds** in anything over 30s (a reveal, a number, a joke) — each one re-earns the next stretch.

## 3. Loops and replays

### Why loops matter mechanically
- Shorts autoplay-loop by default. A viewer who watches twice = 200% retention on that view; average percentage viewed **can and should exceed 100%** on the best Shorts. >100% APV is the strongest "exploit-phase" expansion signal.
- Retention-graph spikes usually mean rewatch of a specific moment — either strong replay appeal (good) or confusion (bad). Check comments to tell which.
- Post-March-2025 caveat: loops pump *views* (distribution/social proof) but not *engaged views* (money). Loops are a reach play, not a revenue play.

### Seamless-loop construction (step by step)
1. **Write the last shot first.** Decide the final frame, then design an opening frame that matches it (same framing, same position, same motion vector).
2. When filming: **shoot the ending shot, then immediately shoot the opener** to match it physically (Mario So method).
3. In edit: end the sentence *grammatically incomplete into the opening line* — e.g., final line "...and that's why —" cuts to opening "— this pitch is illegal in Little League." The audio splice is the loop seam.
4. Best loop candidates are **under ~15–25 seconds** — short enough that a second lap is frictionless.
5. Test: play the exported file on loop 3 times; if you can feel the seam, re-cut it.

### Loop patterns that fit Mayday content
- **Circular action**: pitch wind-up → release → ball hits mitt → cut back to wind-up (motion-matched).
- **Question loop**: open with a question whose answer is only fully parseable if you watch again ("Did you catch the grip change?" — most viewers rewatch).
- **List loop**: end "…and that's only the third-weirdest one" → loops into item #1.
- **Podcast-clip loop**: cold-open on the punchline reaction shot, run the setup, land on the same punchline — the reaction now reads differently, prompting a rewatch.

## 4. Length optimization now that 3-minute Shorts exist

### Performance bands (as of 2026)
| Length | Use case | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| 15–20s | Single idea, one reveal, loopable | 85–100%+ completion achievable; best loop economics |
| 20–35s | Default band — versatile sweet spot | Majority of high-performing Shorts live here; under-25s clips take the majority of all Shorts views |
| 35–60s | Tutorial, story with a real payoff | 45–55% APV is solid at this length; needs a mid-video re-hook |
| 60–180s | Mini-video: story arcs, breakdowns | Only with proven retention; needs a beat every 3–7s; **Content ID block risk over 60s**; music capped at 90s |

- The algorithm rewards **completion over duration**: a 25-second Short most people finish beats a 55-second Short half the audience abandons; a 3-minute Short at 40% APV loses to a 45-second Short at 90%.
- vidIQ's 2026 analysis flags **~13s and ~60s** as high-performing cluster points — very short loopers and "full-thought" clips.
- Rule of thumb Ashley should apply: **cut to the natural length of the idea, then cut 15% more.** If a clip "needs" 90 seconds, first ask whether it's actually a long-form topic or a 2-part Short.

### When to actually use 2–3 minutes
- A story with a genuine arc (Trevor's MLB stories, prospect-to-debut narratives) where the 60s version would gut the payoff.
- Multi-step teaching content where the steps are the value (pitch-design breakdowns) — but consider whether that belongs on Trevor May Baseball as long-form instead.
- Never for padded versions of a 40-second idea. There is no separate distribution pool rewarding longer Shorts; it's the same completion math with a harder curve.
- Audio discipline: over 60s, use YouTube Audio Library / owned audio only, or run clean of music, to avoid the global Content ID block.

## 5. Text overlay & caption conventions

### Safe zones at 1080 × 1920 (as of 2026)
- **Bottom ~25% (bottom ~480px): off-limits** — title snippet, channel name, sound attribution, and engagement prompts render here.
- **Right ~12% (right ~130px): off-limits for text** — like/comment/share/remix rail.
- **Top ~15–20% (top ~290–384px): risky** — system UI, search, "Shorts" label. YouTube is more forgiving up top than TikTok, but cross-posted files must survive TikTok's harsher top overlay, so keep the conservative zone.
- **Working canvas: middle ~55–60% of the frame, center ~80–90% of width.** Hook text slightly above center; captions at or just below center.
- Cross-platform conservative spec (one export that survives Shorts + Reels + TikTok): 15% top margin, 20% bottom margin, 10% right margin clear.

### Caption specs (burned-in)
| Parameter | Spec |
|---|---|
| Position | Center third; top of caption block at ~35–45% of frame height |
| Lines on screen | Max 2 (never 3+) |
| Line length | 32–42 characters (~5–7 words) |
| Font | Bold/semi-bold sans-serif (Montserrat, Helvetica, Arial class) |
| Size | 48–72px at 1080×1920 (≈18–24pt; 20–22pt sweet spot) |
| Contrast | ≥4.5:1 (WCAG AA); white text + black semi-transparent box (60–80% opacity) is the reliable default |
| Timing | Caption appears **0.1–0.3s before** the audio it matches; blocks display 1.5–3s; ~0.3s per word + 0.5s buffer |
| Style | Word-by-word or phrase highlight (karaoke-style) is the dominant 2026 convention; keep one accent color, don't rainbow every word |

- Burned-in captions on the *video* + uploaded SRT/VTT in YouTube's caption field = both the retention benefit (**~12–15% higher completion**) and search indexing. Auto-captions alone miss names ("Wheeler," "sweeper," "Driveline") — always correct baseball jargon and player names.
- **Hook text ≠ captions.** The 3–6 word premise overlay at frame zero is a separate, larger element (roughly 2× caption size), positioned upper-middle, and it should disappear or change by ~3s so the frame doesn't feel static.
- Text hierarchy per frame: one hook/headline element + one caption line-pair maximum. Three simultaneous text elements = clutter = swipe.

## 6. Titles, thumbnails, and metadata for Shorts — what actually matters

### Titles (yes, they matter — but not where people think)
- **In the vertical feed the title is barely visible** (small snippet bottom-left). The feed test is won by the video's first seconds, not the title.
- Titles matter on every *other* surface: YouTube search, the Shorts shelf on Home, channel page grid, subscriptions feed, suggested. These surfaces drive the long tail — Shorts (unlike TikTok's 48–72h lifespan) keep collecting search views for months.
- Spec: up to 100 characters allowed, **truncates around ~60 in most views** — put the keyword + promise in the **first 40–50 characters**. Write it as a sharp promise, not a description: "The Grip That Ended My Career" not "Trevor talks about his splitter grip and injury."
- Include the topic keyword early ("curveball," "MLB," "pitching drill") — Shorts rank in regular YouTube search, and baseball instructional queries are evergreen search traffic (a real edge for Trevor May Baseball).
- `#shorts` in title/description: harmless, mildly useful for surfacing; not a ranking lever. 1–3 topical hashtags max; hashtag stuffing does nothing.
- Description: first 1–2 lines restate the promise with keywords; links are non-clickable in Shorts descriptions/comments (removed platform-wide as of 2025) — funnel via pinned comment text, the "related video" link, or channel page.

### Thumbnails (frame-picks, not uploads)
- As of 2026, Shorts thumbnails are **selected from a frame of the video** (mobile, at upload) — no custom image upload, and **not changeable after publishing** for most channels. Plan for it *before* export.
- The thumbnail never shows in the swipe feed, but it shows **everywhere else**: channel grid, search results, Shorts shelf, playlists, homepage. Older Shorts get most of their views from these surfaces.
- Tactic: **engineer a thumbnail-frame into the edit** — a deliberate 2–3 frame moment with a clean expressive face + readable 3-4 word text in the upper 2/3 (bottom of the frame gets covered by the title overlay in grids). Put it early or at the peak moment, then pick it at upload.
- Grid consistency: on the channel page, Shorts thumbnails sit side by side — consistent text placement/color across Shorts reads as a branded shelf and lifts channel-page click-through.

## 7. Sound selection

- **Voice-first content (Trevor's lane) wins on YouTube.** Shorts is more talk/education tolerant than TikTok; trending-audio-driven Shorts matter most for montage/highlight formats, not commentary.
- **Trends page**: in the Shorts feed → Trends tab (worldwide as of 2025-2026) shows currently-hot sounds; sounds can be saved and reused legally through YouTube's own music tools. YouTube Charts also publishes a daily Top Shorts Songs list. Note: Shorts trending audio is its own ecosystem — 2025's top Shorts songs did *not* mirror YouTube's overall top songs.
- Licensing mechanics (as of 2026): licensed music usable up to 90s in a 3-min Short (some tracks 60s/30s); **any Content ID claim on a >60s Short = global block**; revenue share is 45% *regardless of music use* — using licensed music no longer costs extra revenue share on Shorts.
- Music-bed craft for talking Shorts: bed at **-18 to -25 dB under voice**, pick tracks with steady energy (no big drops mid-clip unless you cut to them), and beat-sync cuts where possible (YouTube's editor now has beat-sync assist).
- Highlight/montage Shorts (Neptune facility clips, bullpen slow-mo, glove-pop compilations): trending or high-energy sound carries retention — pick the best 5–15 seconds of the track and cut the action to its transients. Glove pop, bat crack, and cage ambiance are premium native sounds — keep them high in the mix; sound design *is* the hook on sensory baseball content.
- Original audio (Trevor's voice) becomes a reusable asset: other creators remixing his sound = free distribution; name audio clearly.

## 8. Vertical crop craft from landscape footage

Most Mayday Shorts start life as 16:9 (podcast cams, long-form b-roll, broadcast-style baseball footage). The reframe is a craft step, not a batch export.

### Reframing patterns, ranked
1. **Speaker-tracked punch-in** (single subject): crop to a 9:16 window that follows the active speaker's face; eyes in the top third. Auto tools (Opus Clip, Descript, Riverside) get ~90% there — always QC for the ball/prop leaving frame.
2. **Stacked split-screen** (two-person podcast): both faces stacked vertically, each in a ~9:8 half; active speaker can get subtle scale emphasis. Use when the exchange itself (reaction shots) is the content.
3. **Cut-switching** (two-person, single-cam or multicam): hard-cut the 9:16 window between speakers on dialogue turns — reads faster than split-screen, adds free pacing cuts.
4. **Centered blind crop: last resort only.** Center crops that lose faces are the worst-performing reframe pattern measured. Never batch-export center crops of podcast footage.
5. **Letterboxed 16:9 with headline space**: keep the landscape shot full-width in the middle, hook text above, captions below. Acceptable for footage where the wide frame *is* the content (full pitch flight, stadium shots, telestration frames) — fill top/bottom with blurred-clip or brand color, never black bars alone.

### Baseball-footage specifics
- Pitch footage: the ball travels horizontally — a static vertical crop loses either release or catch. Options: keyframed pan following the ball; speed-ramp so the flight fits the frame; or letterbox the flight and punch in vertical for the reaction.
- Broadcast-style footage: score bugs and chyrons will sit outside a vertical crop or get half-cropped — crop them out fully or cover with your own text, never leave half a graphic.
- Slow-mo mechanics content (release frames, hip-shoulder separation): vertical is actually the *better* frame for a pitcher's body — full wind-up fits 9:16 naturally. Shoot new Neptune facility content **vertical-native** where possible instead of reframing.
- Shoot-for-the-crop rule for new long-form: frame interviews and demos with 9:16 crop lines in mind (subject center-weighted, no critical action at frame edges) so every long-form shoot yields clean Shorts crops.

### Conversion workflow (podcast/long-form → Short), step by step
1. Pull the clip moment (editorial selection criteria: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).
2. **Re-hook it**: cut the conversational lead-in; open on the most quotable/visual second, even if that's from the *middle* of the moment, then let the setup play after.
3. Reframe per patterns above; QC every cut for faces/props leaving frame.
4. Strip silences and breaths; tighten to natural idea length, then −15%.
5. Burn captions to spec (§5); add frame-zero hook text.
6. Engineer the thumbnail frame (§6); note its timestamp.
7. Music bed if montage/energy content (§7); native audio forward if talk content.
8. Export 1080×1920 H.264 8–12 Mbps; **strip all platform watermarks** before cross-posting (watermarked video is deprioritized on Reels/TikTok).
9. Title with front-loaded promise; 1–3 hashtags; keyworded first description line.
10. Upload from mobile if the frame-pick thumbnail matters (frame selection is mobile-only as of 2026); select the engineered frame.

## 9. Retention benchmarks & diagnostics (as of 2026)

| Metric | Healthy | Strong | Failing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Average % viewed (APV) | ≥70% | >100% (looping) | <50% |
| 3-second retention | >80% | — | <70% |
| Midpoint retention | >60% | — | — |
| Swipe-away rate ("viewed vs swiped") | 10–30% | <10% | >40% |
| APV, Shorts <30s | >55–60% | 70–80%+ | — |
| APV, Shorts 30–60s | ~45% | 55%+ | — |
| Like-to-view ratio | 3–6% | >6% | <2% |

(82,000-account benchmark set, Humble & Brag 2026, plus Shortimize/vidIQ analyses. Shortimize's caveat is right: compare against *similar-length* Shorts in YouTube Studio, not one universal number.)

**Diagnostic tree Ashley should run on any underperforming Short:**
1. Swipe-away >40% → first-frame/first-second problem (or wrong-audience seeding). Fix frame zero, not the middle.
2. 3s retention fine, midpoint <60% → the hook wrote a check the content didn't cash, or pacing sagged; add a mid-video re-hook / cut 20%.
3. APV fine but views flat → seed audience mismatch (topic drifted from channel's Shorts identity) or metadata failing search surfaces; check title/keywords.
4. Retention spike in the graph → find the rewatched moment; if it's confusion, clarify; if it's delight, build the next Short around that beat.
5. High views, low engaged views (post-3/31/25 analytics) → passive loop inflation; fine for reach, don't expect revenue.

## 10. Common mistakes

- **Text landing after the 1-second mark.** Half the swipe decisions are already made. Hook text on frame zero, always.
- **Captions or hook text in the bottom 25% / right 12%** — hidden behind the title bar and engagement rail. The single most common self-inflicted formatting wound.
- **Starting the clip at the conversational beginning** of a podcast moment instead of re-hooking on the payoff line.
- **Center-cropping landscape podcast footage** and losing faces half the time.
- **Outros, "follow for more," logo stings** — every second after the emotional peak bleeds APV; hard-cut at the peak.
- **A 90-second Short with a licensed song and a Content ID claim** → globally blocked. Check claims *before* publishing anything over 60s with music.
- **Padding a 30-second idea to 2 minutes** because 3-minute Shorts exist. Same completion math, harder curve, no bonus pool.
- **Judging Shorts by raw views after March 2025** — loops inflate views; engaged views are the honest number.
- **Ignoring the thumbnail frame** because "Shorts don't have thumbnails" — the frame-pick lives on the channel grid and in search forever, and it can't be changed after publish.
- **Uploading TikTok-watermarked exports** to Shorts/Reels (detected and deprioritized — see Carl doc for the cross-posting rules).
- **Three text elements on screen at once** (hook + captions + CTA sticker) — clutter reads as spam and triggers swipes.
- **One-size caption style batch-exported across platforms** without checking each platform's safe zone — the conservative cross-platform zone (§5) exists precisely to make one export safe.

## 11. Questions Ashley should ask

Before a Short is made:
- "What's the frame-zero image, in one sentence? Would it stop *you* mid-scroll on mute?"
- "What's the first spoken line, and does it contain the promise? Where's the 'but'?"
- "What does the ending pay off, and can the last frame cut back to the first frame cleanly?"
- "Is this a 20-second idea, a 45-second idea, or actually a long-form topic wearing a Shorts costume?"
- "Talk content or montage content — and therefore voice-forward or sound-forward?"
- "Is this vertical-native or a reframe — and if a reframe, which pattern (§8), and who's QC-ing the crop?"

Before publish:
- "Which frame is the thumbnail, and is its text in the upper two-thirds?"
- "Over 60 seconds — is the audio 100% clean of Content ID risk?"
- "Do the first 45 characters of the title carry the keyword and the promise?"
- "Is every text element inside the middle 60% of the frame?"

After publish (48–72h):
- "Swipe-away rate vs. the 10–30% band — hook problem or audience-match problem?"
- "APV vs. same-length Shorts on this channel — and is anything looping past 100%?"
- "Engaged views vs. raw views — real watch or loop inflation?"
- "Where's the retention spike/cliff, and what happens at that timestamp?"
- "Did this Short's topic match the channel's Shorts identity, or did it confuse the seed audience?"
