# Shorts Cadence, Timing & Publishing Mechanics

Tactical execution reference for WHEN and HOW to publish Shorts — frequency, timing, spacing around long-form, scheduling workflow, metadata, thumbnails, and cross-linking mechanics. For the strategic "why short-form at all / which platform / clip selection" layer, see the strategy doc (../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).

## TL;DR

- **Cadence target: 3–5 Shorts/week per channel, sustained for 90+ days, published at roughly the same times of day.** Sustained rhythm beats bursts — "3/week for 3 months beats 20 in one week then silence" is the most repeated finding across every 2025–2026 dataset. Only scale to daily once quality holds at the lower rate.
- **Shorts peak in the evening; long-form peaks in the morning — nearly opposite.** Buffer's 1.8M-video study (published March 2026): top Shorts slots are Friday 4pm, 6pm, 7pm; general Shorts peak is 6–11pm; worst window is weekday 12–5pm (except Friday). Long-form peaks 8–11am. Never assume one channel-wide "best time."
- **Timing is a second-order optimization, not a driver.** A great Short at a mediocre time beats a mediocre Short at the perfect time. Get frequency and hooks right first; then tune times against the channel's own "When your viewers are on YouTube" heatmap in Studio.
- **The 24–48-hour spacing myth is officially debunked** (Rene Ritchie, YouTube Creator Liaison, Nov 2025): the recommendation system learns only from live audience behavior, so pre-publish waiting periods do nothing. Same-day Shorts + long-form does not cannibalize — but staggering (long-form AM, Short PM) captures two consumption modes.
- **Hashtags: 3–5 max, in the DESCRIPTION, not the title.** First 3 description hashtags surface as clickable links above the title anyway. Over 60 total hashtags = YouTube ignores all of them. Title budget (100 chars) should be spent on a keyword-rich human hook, never tags.
- **Shorts thumbnails don't show in the swipe feed — but they DO show in 5 placements** (channel Shorts tab, homepage Shorts shelf, search results, subscriptions feed, recommended rail). Custom upload (1080×1920, <2MB) rolled out via desktop Studio through 2025–2026. No A/B "Test & Compare" for Shorts as of 2026.
- **The related-video link is the single highest-leverage publishing field on a Short.** Set it on every Short (desktop Studio only). Expect ~0.03–0.05% click-through at normal volumes (a 15K-view Short sends ~50 long-form views; a 10M-view Short can send 3K+) — but converted viewers carry ~40% higher lifetime value.

## 1. Posting frequency — what the data actually shows

### The benchmarks (as of 2026)

| Situation | Cadence | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Floor to stay "algorithmically warm" | 2–3 Shorts/week | Below this, the channel loses cold-seed consistency and each Short is re-tested from scratch |
| Standard growth cadence (Mayday default) | 3–5/week | Consensus across Social Champ, Miraflow, AIR Media-Tech 2025–2026 guidance; sustainable from podcast + long-form clipping alone |
| Aggressive growth push | 1–2/day | Only when a repeatable clip pipeline exists; channels posting 2–3/day showed 2–3× faster sub growth **at equal quality** — the qualifier is doing all the work |
| Faceless/volume channels | 3–10/day | Not the Mayday model; quality collapse at this rate kills retention, the metric that actually drives distribution |

### Key mechanisms behind the numbers

- **YouTube grades channel rhythm, not raw volume.** Daily-ish uploads at approximately the same time of day get better cold seeding than sporadic 5-in-a-day bursts (2026 practitioner consensus). The algorithm builds a categorization + audience model of the channel; erratic posting keeps resetting it.
- **The first 20–30 Shorts on a channel are training data.** The algorithm needs a body of same-niche Shorts to learn who to seed to. This means: (a) never judge a Shorts strategy on fewer than ~25 uploads, and (b) topic consistency within the Shorts stream matters more early than any individual banger.
- **Volume without quality is now actively negative.** Metricool's 2026 study (7.3M videos, 82K accounts): brands raised Shorts output to an average 16.21/month but engagement fell 36% and reach fell 31% — output up, results down. The spray-and-pray era ended; each Short must independently clear the retention bar.
- **Breaks don't kill channels.** Confirmed pattern in 2025–2026 analyses: the algorithm rewards long-term engagement patterns, not streaks. A 1–2 week planned pause after a sustained run does not reset distribution. What DOES damage is the burst-then-gap sawtooth (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).

### Ramp protocol (when increasing cadence)

1. Establish baseline: 3/week for 4 weeks. Record median engaged views, avg % viewed, swipe-away rate.
2. Increase by +1/week per month, NOT per week. (3 → 4 → 5.)
3. At each step, compare the new marginal Shorts' retention against baseline median. If the added Shorts run >5 points worse on avg % viewed, the pipeline is diluting — hold or drop back.
4. Never fill a slot with a filler Short to "keep the streak." A skipped slot costs less than a 35%-retention upload that teaches the algorithm your Shorts are skippable.

### Mayday-specific calibration

- **More Mayday / podcast clips:** one podcast episode reliably yields 5–20 usable clips (strategy doc covers selection). At 3–5 Shorts/week, ONE episode per week fully funds the Shorts calendar — cadence should never be the bottleneck; clip QC should be.
- **Trevor May Baseball:** instructional/baseball-IQ Shorts have search long-tail life (drills, mechanics, "how to throw X"), so slightly lower cadence (2–4/week) with heavier metadata optimization outperforms volume here.
- **Neptune Performance (when live):** cadence rules barely apply — it's a local lead channel. 2–3/week is plenty; judge on local inquiries, not reach (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md §1).

## 2. Time-of-day and day-of-week effects

### Buffer 1.8M-video study (published March 2026 — the largest current dataset)

Shorts and long-form have **almost opposite** peak windows:

**Shorts — best slots by day (engagement-weighted):**

| Day | Best times |
|---|---|
| Monday | 8pm, 5pm, 6pm |
| Tuesday | 8pm, 9pm, 7pm |
| Wednesday | 7pm, 8pm, 9pm |
| Thursday | 7pm, 8pm, 9pm |
| **Friday** | **4pm, 6pm, 7pm (top 3 slots of the entire week)** |
| Saturday | 7pm, 11am, 6pm |
| Sunday | 7pm, 8pm, 5pm |

- Best days ranked: **Friday > Saturday > Thursday.** Weakest: Tuesday, Monday.
- General Shorts pattern: **6–11pm evenings**; worst window is weekday **12–5pm** (Friday late-afternoon is the exception).
- Long-form for contrast: mornings 8–11am, top slot Sunday 10am, worst Thursday 2pm. This is the mechanical basis for the stagger strategy in §3.
- Buffer normalized the data so the times apply in the creator's local/audience timezone rather than one fixed zone.

### Corroborating / diverging datasets (as of 2026)

- **Hootsuite 2025:** highest YouTube engagement 12–3pm weekdays, 9am–12pm weekends, *audience-local time* — skews earlier than Buffer because it blends formats. Treat Buffer's Shorts-only split as more format-accurate.
- **Aggregate of Buffer/Sprout/Adobe (FlowShorts roundup, 2026):** Tue–Thu 3–6pm ET as a consensus mid-week window; Wednesday 4pm flagged as a single strong slot. Weekend mornings 9–11am also index well.
- **Late-night anomaly:** one Metricool 2025 slice showed ~1am posts averaging 3,184 views/Short — a competition-gap effect, worth testing only for channels with international audiences.
- **Weekends get materially more Shorts consumption** (one scheduler dataset claims ~60% more) — consistent with Shorts as leisure-mode content.

### How much does timing actually matter?

Less than everything else. Every serious study self-caveats: content quality, hook, and retention have "an outsized impact" relative to slot choice; Shorts also have a longer shelf life than Reels/TikTok (re-tested over weeks, search long-tail), which further dampens acute timing sensitivity. Timing is worth ~one settings decision per Short, not a strategy meeting.

### Operational method — stop guessing, read the heatmap

1. YouTube Studio → Analytics → Audience → **"When your viewers are on YouTube"** (28-day heatmap, shown in local time).
2. Schedule Shorts **30–60 minutes BEFORE** the darkest band begins (the Short needs to be live and gathering its first impressions as the audience arrives — not published into the middle of the peak).
3. Re-check monthly; the heatmap drifts seasonally (baseball audience: expect evening peaks to shift with game broadcast windows Apr–Oct, and weekend-morning youth-baseball-parent bands year-round).

### Baseball/sports-niche timing notes

- General sports content indexes to **evenings around live-game windows** — a Short posted 6–7pm ET on a game night rides elevated baseball-mode app usage; postgame morning windows (8–10am) catch highlight-checking behavior.
- Youth-baseball parents (Trevor May Baseball + Neptune audience) over-index **weekend mornings** (tournament/practice culture) — Saturday 7–9am local is a test-worthy slot the generic studies underweight.
- In-season vs off-season is a bigger variable than day-of-week: run separate heatmap reviews for April and December; don't average across them.

## 3. Spacing Shorts around long-form uploads

### The settled facts (as of 2026)

- **Shorts do not algorithmically harm long-form on the same channel.** YouTube evaluates each video individually, not a channel average. Hybrid channels grow faster, and the post-2023 "bridge" update makes Shorts viewing feed long-form recs from the same channel (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).
- **The 24–48-hour wait-between-uploads myth is dead.** Rene Ritchie (YouTube Creator Liaison), Nov 2025: "The recommendation system is largely based on audience behavior. So until your video goes live… there's no audience behavior data," and "Waiting 24 to 48 hours is no different than waiting 24 to 48 seconds or weeks or months." There is no cooling-off timer, no shared "distribution budget" between uploads, and no penalty for same-day publishing.
- Nothing in current guidance suggests two same-day Shorts split each other's reach — each is tested against its own audience pool. The real constraint on same-day multiples is production quality, not algorithm mechanics.

### The stagger pattern (recommended default)

Because Shorts peak evenings and long-form peaks mornings, the natural same-day layout is:

- **Long-form: morning slot (8–11am audience-local).**
- **Short: evening slot (6–9pm) — ideally a clip FROM that long-form, with the related-video link set to it** (§7). The Short then works as an evening trailer for the morning upload during its most important first-24h window.

### Weekly grid — worked example for More Mayday (podcast + long-form, 4 Shorts/week)

| Day | Publish | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Tue | Long-form/podcast episode, 9am | Buffer: Tue is a top-3 long-form day |
| Tue | Short #1 (best clip of new ep), 7pm | Related-video link → new episode |
| Thu | Short #2 (2nd clip), 7–8pm | Thu is a top-3 Shorts day |
| Fri | Short #3 (evergreen/baseball-IQ clip), 4–6pm | Friday 4pm = #1 Shorts slot of the week |
| Sat or Sun | Short #4 (personality/crossover clip), 7pm | Weekend evening; rotate related link to back-catalog video that needs traffic |

Principles encoded in the grid: episode-day Short doubles as promo; Shorts never occupy the long-form morning slot; Friday's premium slot gets the strongest standalone clip (not necessarily the newest); weekend Short is allowed to be broader/entertainment-lane since weekend Shorts audiences skew leisure-mode.

### Multi-Short days

If running 2/day: split morning (11am-ish) + evening (7–9pm) to hit two different session peaks; don't stack both in the same hour. Keep the two Shorts on different sub-topics so they seed to different audience pockets rather than competing for the identical viewer.

## 4. Scheduling mechanics (workflow, as of 2026)

- **Shorts schedule exactly like regular videos:** desktop Studio → upload → Visibility → Schedule → date/time. Schedulable up to 12 months out. Mobile app scheduling exists but desktop is required anyway for related links and custom thumbnails, so build the workflow on desktop Studio.
- **No Premieres for Shorts.** Premiere is long-form-only; don't plan a Shorts "premiere event."
- **Scheduled ≠ processing head start on recommendations.** Per the Ritchie debunk, the clock starts at public. Scheduling buys operational consistency (same slots every week — which the algorithm's channel-rhythm grading rewards) and lets uploads process fully (HD/4K versions ready at publish), nothing more.
- **Set EVERYTHING before it goes live:** title, description + hashtags, related-video link, thumbnail, playlist. Editing metadata in the first hours after publish is safe but sloppy; publishing bare and "fixing it later" wastes the highest-impression window.
- **Never delete-and-reupload a slow Short.** Shorts get re-tested over weeks and have search long-tail; a Short that dies at 400 views can resurface later. Reuploading resets everything and duplicates content on the channel. Only pull a Short for actual errors (wrong cut, factual/legal problem).
- **First 24–72 hours = active-decision window.** Shorts "live and die within their first 24 to 72 hours" for their initial feed push. During it: reply to every comment (comments in hour 1–6 compound), share the Short link externally, and don't bury it by publishing the next Short into the same window if the current one is climbing. If a Short is visibly running (views accelerating hour over hour), it is legitimate to slide the next scheduled Short back a day.
- **Third-party schedulers (Metricool etc.)** publish Shorts fine via API, but as of 2026 the related-video link and Shorts custom thumbnail are Studio-only fields — anything scheduled externally needs a manual Studio pass to set them. For Mayday: schedule through Studio directly, or add a "Studio finish pass" step to the Metricool workflow.

### Publish-day checklist (per Short)

- [ ] Title ≤100 chars, keyword-first, zero hashtags in it
- [ ] Description: first 125 chars = natural-language keyword summary; 3–5 hashtags at the end
- [ ] Related-video link set (desktop Studio)
- [ ] Thumbnail: custom 1080×1920 uploaded, or clean frame selected
- [ ] Added to the correct playlist(s)
- [ ] Scheduled 30–60 min ahead of the target audience peak
- [ ] Not classified wrong: vertical/square AND ≤3:00 → it WILL be a Short (see §8)
- [ ] Calendar reminder to check comments at +2h and +24h

## 5. Hashtags, titles & descriptions for Shorts

### What metadata does and doesn't do (as of 2026)

YouTube's official position: Shorts rank primarily on **performance and viewer relevancy**, not metadata. Metadata's real jobs: (1) initial topical categorization (who gets the cold seed), (2) search + Suggested long-tail (where Shorts, unlike TikToks, keep earning views for months), (3) the clickable-hashtag feed placements.

### Title (100-character limit)

- Keyword phrase early, then hook. The title shows in the Shorts player, search, and every thumbnail placement.
- **No hashtags in the title.** Each tag character steals from the hook, looks spammy, and measurably drags CTR in the grid placements. Description hashtags carry identical categorization weight.
- Pattern that works for the baseball niche: `{searchable skill/moment} — {curiosity turn}` e.g. "How MLB pitchers grip a sweeper — it's not what coaches teach."

### Description

- **First 125 characters are the preview** — write them as a natural sentence containing the primary keyword, not a tag dump.
- 1–3 more sentences of context (feeds search indexing; Shorts search traffic is the compounding asset here).
- Then hashtags, last.

### Hashtags — exact rules

- **3–5 total.** Formula: `#Shorts` + 1–2 niche tags (`#baseball`, `#pitching`, `#MLB`) + optionally 1 trending/branded tag (`#TrevorMay`, `#MoreMayday`).
- Put them **in the description**; the first 3 auto-surface as clickable links above the title.
- **Hard limit: >60 hashtags anywhere = YouTube ignores ALL of them** (long-standing policy, still true 2026).
- `#Shorts` is no longer required for format classification (aspect ratio + length decide that, §8) but still feeds the hashtag browse pages — cheap to keep, first position.
- Do NOT rotate trending-but-irrelevant tags for reach; miscategorization seeds the Short to low-intent viewers, tanks retention, and hurts more than the tag helped.

## 6. Thumbnails for Shorts

### Where they appear — this decides how much effort to spend

Shorts thumbnails are **invisible in the swipe-up feed player** (where most Shorts views happen) but appear in **five placements**: channel Shorts tab grid, homepage Shorts shelf, YouTube search results, subscriptions feed, homepage recommended rail. Translation: the thumbnail doesn't affect feed virality at all, but it directly affects **search CTR** (big for Trevor May Baseball instructional content), **channel-page browse**, and how professional the Shorts tab reads to a potential subscriber auditing the channel.

### Specs (as of 2026)

- **1080 × 1920 px, 9:16 vertical.** Not 1280×720 — 16:9 uploads get letterboxed/cropped badly in the vertical grid.
- **< 2 MB.** JPG / PNG / GIF / BMP.
- **Safe zone:** keep faces/text/logos inside a central ~900×1160 window. UI eats roughly: top 380px (channel info), bottom 380px (title/subscribe), right 120px (like/comment column), left 60px.

### The three ways to set one

1. **Mobile frame-pick (always available):** pencil icon on the upload details screen → scrub to a frame → Done. Minimum viable: pick a frame with a clear face/action, never a motion-blur transition frame.
2. **Desktop custom upload (rolled out through 2025–2026, still expanding by region/account):** studio.youtube.com → Content → pencil → Thumbnail → Upload. The clean path when available.
3. **Bake-in fallback:** design a 1080×1920 title card, insert it as the first frames in the edit, frame-select it. (Costs a beat of hook time — use only if custom upload isn't enabled on the account.)

### Practical rules

- **Thumbnail must match the content.** A bait thumbnail that wins the search click then mismatches the video spikes swipe-away rate, which is a primary negative distribution signal in 2026.
- **No A/B testing:** YouTube's "Test & Compare" does not support Shorts (as of 2026). Iterate by convention instead: pick one visual system for the Shorts tab (consistent type treatment, Mayday colors) so the grid reads as a series.
- Effort budget: ~2–5 minutes per Short. Instructional/search-oriented Shorts (drills, mechanics) deserve custom thumbnails; ephemeral reaction clips can use frame-pick.

## 7. Related-video links, playlists & collections

### The related-video link (highest-leverage field on the upload form)

- **What it is:** a creator-set link to one of your own long-form videos, shown as a tappable pill above the Short's title in the feed. YouTube's only sanctioned direct Shorts→long-form bridge.
- **How to set it:** desktop Studio → open the Short → "Add related video" → pick the long-form video → Save. **Desktop only** (as of 2026). Editable anytime, per-Short.
- **Realistic conversion math:** ~0.03–0.05% of Shorts views click through at normal volumes — a 10–20K-view Short sends ~50 long-form views; a 10M-view Short can send 3,000+. Small per-Short, but it compounds across every Short forever, and Shorts-converted viewers carry ~40% higher lifetime value than average.
- **The Flood Method:** point EVERY Short on the channel at the same long-form video for a period (e.g., the newest episode, or one video being pushed toward a milestone), then rotate. Manual per-Short updates, tedious but effective for concentrating momentum. Mayday usage: flood the current week's podcast episode with all that week's clips, then leave each Short permanently pointed at its source episode.
- **Make the link earn clicks:** the pill alone converts poorly. Stack it with (a) a verbal CTA ("full story on the pod — linked right up there"), (b) a point-up gesture at the pill location, or (c) a text overlay "full video ↑". And use **open loops**: start the story in the Short, resolve it only in the long-form — then make sure the long-form pays off the promise immediately (no 90-second intro before the moment).
- **Secondary bridge — pinned comment** with the long-form URL: measurably weaker than the related link but free; use both.

### Playlists / collections for Shorts

- Shorts can be added to playlists exactly like long-form (Studio: edit → Playlists; mobile: ⋮ → Save to playlist). Since the 2024–2025 updates this works cleanly.
- **What playlists do for Shorts:** organize the channel page into browsable series ("Pitching Grips," "Pod Moments," "Cage Work"), add a session-watch-time surface, and give search another indexed entity. They are a **channel-page and search asset, not a feed-distribution lever** — the swipe feed ignores playlist membership.
- Tactical setup for Mayday: one playlist per recurring Shorts format/series, plus mixed playlists that interleave Shorts with their source long-form episodes (a viewer who enters via a clip can lean back into full episodes without leaving the playlist).
- Series-name the Shorts consistently (e.g., "Pitch Grips #12") — recurring formats train both the audience and the algorithm's categorization, and make playlist growth automatic.

## 8. Format classification & metric mechanics that affect publishing

Publishing-relevant platform rules, all date-stamped because they've changed recently:

- **What counts as a Short (since Oct 15, 2024, reaffirmed in Dec 2025 Help-doc updates):** any upload that is **square or vertical AND ≤3 minutes** is auto-classified as a Short. There is no checkbox. Consequence: a 2:45 vertical podcast clip IS a Short; if a clip is meant to live as long-form (e.g., a 3-minute highlight for the main feed), export it 16:9. Check classification before publish — misclassification is irreversible without reupload.
- **3-minute ceiling ≠ 3-minute target (as of 2026):** 30–60s Shorts dominate distribution; sub-15s Shorts have collapsed in reach because they can't accumulate enough absolute watch time even at 100% retention. 30–45s is the current sweet spot; use 1–3min only when the content genuinely holds (retention curve, not vibes, decides).
- **Views metric split (since March 31, 2025):** "Views" now counts every play/replay/scroll-by (TikTok-style vanity number); the old metric survives as **"engaged views"** — which still governs YPP eligibility and Shorts revenue-share math. **All cadence/timing benchmarking must use engaged views**; raw views inflated overnight when this changed and will make any pre/post comparison lie.
- **The distribution predictors in 2026 are completion rate and swipe-away rate,** not raw views. When judging whether a cadence increase is working (§1 ramp protocol), those two are the health check.
- **Original audio bonus (reported March 2026):** original voiceover/audio out-earns trending sounds for sub-50K-sub channels. Publishing implication for Mayday: podcast-voice clips are already optimal; never swap in trending audio beds on clips.
- **TikTok watermarks are actively demoted** (as of 2026). Publishing pipeline must export clean vertical masters and post natively per platform — never route the TikTok export to Shorts.

## 9. Common mistakes

1. **Burst-then-gap posting.** 10 Shorts this week, zero for three weeks. The single most damaging cadence pattern; resets algorithmic rhythm every cycle.
2. **Chasing "best time" charts instead of the channel's own heatmap.** Buffer's Friday-4pm is a population median; a baseball audience on game nights and tournament weekends has its own shape. Charts are the starting hypothesis, Studio's heatmap is the answer.
3. **Publishing bare, "optimizing later."** Missing related link/hashtags/thumbnail during the first 24–72h window where the Short earns most of its feed impressions.
4. **Hashtags in the title / hashtag stuffing.** CTR drag, spam signal, and >60 tags voids all of them.
5. **Deleting and reuploading underperformers.** Kills the re-test and search long-tail that make Shorts different from TikToks.
6. **Judging a cadence experiment in under ~25 Shorts** or on raw views instead of engaged views + swipe-away rate.
7. **Filler Shorts to protect the streak.** A low-retention upload actively teaches the algorithm to seed the channel less.
8. **Letting a 2–3 minute vertical clip auto-classify as a Short unintentionally** (or exporting a wanted Short at 16:9 so it doesn't classify).
9. **Same-hour stacking of multiple Shorts,** competing for one audience session instead of splitting AM/PM.
10. **Flooding related links to an irrelevant video.** The link converts only when the Short's audience matches the long-form's promise; mismatched floods produce clicks that bounce and poison the long-form's retention stats.
11. **Post-and-ghost.** No comment replies in the first hours; comments are cheap engagement fuel exactly when the Short is being decided on.
12. **Copying the TikTok/Reels posting calendar onto Shorts.** Shorts' longer shelf life, search tail, and evening peak make it a different scheduling animal (platform-difference strategy: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md §2).

## 10. Questions Ashley should ask

**Before setting a cadence:**
- How many QC-passed clips does one podcast episode / long-form shoot actually yield right now? (Cadence must be funded by the pipeline, not willpower.)
- Which channel is this Short for, and what job is it doing — discovery, episode promo, or Neptune lead-gen? (Different cadence and timing rules per §1.)
- Can this cadence survive a road week / season crunch? Set the floor at what survives the worst week.

**Before picking times:**
- What does "When your viewers are on YouTube" show for THIS channel in the last 28 days — and has it been re-checked since the season changed?
- Is the audience US-broad, or concentrated in specific timezones (youth-baseball regions)? Whose 7pm are we targeting?
- Is a long-form upload landing that day? If yes: long-form AM, Short PM, related link pointed at it.

**Before each publish:**
- Is the checklist in §4 complete — especially the related-video link and correct format classification?
- Is the current Short still climbing? If yes, does the next one need to slide 24h?
- Does the thumbnail read at grid size, inside the safe zone, and match the actual content?

**When reviewing results (monthly):**
- Engaged views (not raw views) per Short vs last period, at the same cadence?
- Did marginal Shorts from any cadence increase hold retention parity with baseline (±5 pts avg % viewed)?
- Which slots actually over-indexed on engaged views + completion — and does that justify moving a weekly slot?
- What % of long-form new viewers arrived via Shorts/related links (traffic sources report)? Is the bridge working or is Shorts growth siloed?
