# Shorts Monetization & Sports-Niche Performance

Tactical reference for deciding how much money Shorts actually make, how to price them into sponsor packages, and where league footage and brand-safety rules bite. Strategy-level context (Shorts as discovery engine, hybrid-channel growth, clip selection) lives at ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md — this doc is the numbers layer.

## TL;DR

- **Shorts AdSense is a rounding error; treat every Short as $0 direct revenue when planning.** Realistic 2026 Shorts RPM: $0.03–$0.10/1K engaged views for most niches; sports commentary with an older US male audience can hit $0.25–$0.50. Even the best case is ~$250–$500 per **million** views — one mid long-form video's ad revenue.
- **Sports is one of the better Shorts niches** — American-football commentary Shorts have been benchmarked around ~$0.50 RPM (highest observed niche figure as of 2026), and baseball commentary with an adult US audience should plan at $0.10–$0.25 base / $0.25–$0.50 upside. Trevor's demo (25–54, US, male-skewing, advertiser-safe) is the high end of the pool.
- **Real Shorts money is in sponsor packages, not the Creator Pool.** Price dedicated Shorts at 20–40% of the long-form rate; bundle 1 long-form + 3–5 Shorts at 130–160% of the long-form rate alone. Never give Shorts away free inside a long-form deal — itemize them.
- **Music kills the pool share.** One licensed track = ~50% of the Short's revenue diverted to music licensing before your 45% cut; two tracks = ~67% diverted. Sports commentary needs no licensed music — always use original audio/voice.
- **MLB footage is the single biggest monetization risk.** MLB runs Content ID on broadcasts and historically claims (revenue-diverts) even transformative commentary. Claimed Shorts still *count* for reach but earn $0. Keep clips short, commentary-dominant, and know the claim/dispute playbook below. MLB's 2025 Jomboy investment signals a friendlier creator posture — but Content ID is still automated and does not care.
- **The March 2025 view-count change means "views" ≠ money.** Public Shorts view counts now increment on any play (no minimum watch); monetization and YPP thresholds still run on **engaged views** (Analytics → Advanced Mode). Report engaged views to sponsors and in ROI math, never the vanity number.
- **Gambling brand-safety rules (effective March 19, 2025 + Nov 2025 update) directly affect baseball creators:** no logos, verbal mentions, or links to non-Google-certified gambling services; betting-adjacent content can be age-restricted (which guts Shorts feed distribution). DraftKings/FanDuel are certified in most US states, but integration wording still matters — playbook below.
- **ROI rule of thumb:** a Short is worth making if (a) it costs <30 min of editor time from existing footage, (b) it serves a named funnel job (long-form bridge, Neptune leads, sponsor deliverable), or (c) it's part of a paid bundle. A Short made *only* for Creator Pool revenue is never ROI-positive at Mayday's scale.

## 1. How Shorts ad money actually works (as of 2026)

Shorts are **not** monetized per-impression like long-form. YouTube uses a four-step pooled model (official: YouTube Help, Shorts monetization policies):

1. **Pool:** All ad revenue from ads shown *between* Shorts in the feed is pooled monthly, per country.
2. **Creator Pool allocation with music deduction:**
   - Short with **no licensed music** → 100% of its associated revenue enters the Creator Pool.
   - **1 licensed track** → 50% to Creator Pool, 50% to music licensing.
   - **2 tracks** → ~33% to Creator Pool, ~67% to music licensing.
3. **Share by engaged views:** Each monetizing creator gets a slice of the Creator Pool equal to their share of all eligible engaged views among monetizing creators in that country. (5% of engaged views = 5% of pool.)
4. **Revenue split:** Creator keeps **45%** of their allocated amount (vs. 55% on long-form). YouTube Premium Shorts revenue also pays at 45%.

**Consequences Ashley should reason from:**
- Your RPM is partly determined by *other people's* Shorts. Q4 pools are richer; January is the annual trough.
- Views in low-CPM countries dilute your per-view take. A viral Short that travels globally can *lower* channel-level Shorts RPM.
- Original audio (voice, ambient) is strictly better than any trending sound for a commentary channel. Trevor talking = no music deduction, ever.

### Eligibility & ineligible views
- **Full ad-revenue tier:** 1,000 subs + 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days, OR 1,000 subs + 4,000 long-form watch hours (More Mayday qualifies via watch hours — the 10M Shorts bar is irrelevant once in YPP).
- **Fan-funding tier:** 500 subs + 3M Shorts views / 3,000 watch hours (Super Thanks, memberships — no ad revenue).
- **Views excluded from the pool:** non-original/unedited reuploads, artificial traffic, and views on content violating advertiser-friendly guidelines. Compilation/clip-dump Shorts with minimal added commentary are routinely flagged ineligible — this is the enforcement lever that hits lazy podcast-clip channels.

### The March 31, 2025 view-count change
- Public "views" now count **any play or replay, no minimum watch time** (TikTok/Reels parity). Most channels saw raw view counts jump 20–30% with zero revenue change.
- The old metric survives as **engaged views** (YouTube Analytics → Advanced Mode → Engaged views). YPP eligibility and Creator Pool math still use engaged views only.
- **Tactical:** any historical Shorts benchmark that straddles March 2025 is apples-to-oranges. When Metricool or the studio dashboard shows Shorts "views," label which metric it is. Sponsor reporting: quote engaged views + average % viewed, and say so — it builds trust and pre-empts the "your views are inflated" objection.

## 2. Shorts RPM reality by niche (2026 benchmarks)

Cross-source consensus ranges (Satura, vidIQ, Influencer Marketing Hub, Lenostube, YT Money Calculator; all 2026-dated):

| Niche / audience profile | Shorts RPM (engaged views) | Per 1M views |
|---|---|---|
| Broad entertainment, memes, viral clips, pets | $0.01–$0.05 | $10–$50 |
| Typical mid-niche (cooking, lifestyle, general) | $0.05–$0.10 | $50–$100 |
| Healthy creator niche — original, advertiser-safe, US-heavy | $0.10–$0.25 | $100–$250 |
| Older US/UK audience, educational (history/military style) | $0.25–$0.50 upside | $250–$500 |
| **American football commentary (highest observed sports figure)** | **~$0.50** | ~$500 |
| Finance / B2B (ceiling reference, not Trevor's lane) | $0.15–$0.45 | $150–$450 |

Long-form comparison on the same channels: $3–$12+ RPM — i.e., **Shorts pay 30–100x less per view** (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md).

### Where baseball/athlete-creator content lands
No source publishes a "baseball Shorts RPM" line item, but triangulate from the drivers:
- **Audience age & gender:** baseball skews older and male — a 45-year-old US male is a premium ad impression. This is the exact profile behind the ~$0.50 football-commentary figure.
- **Geography:** MLB content is US/Canada-heavy — top-2 RPM countries (US $32.75 CPM / Australia $36.21 CPM on long-form per Lenostube; the same geo gradient applies to the Shorts pool).
- **Advertiser safety:** insider analysis/instruction is clean content; betting-adjacent content gets haircuts (Section 5).
- **Original commentary vs. clip-dump:** a Trevor face-on-camera breakdown is pool-eligible at full weight; a raw highlight reel risks ineligibility entirely.

**Planning numbers for Ashley (as of 2026):**
- **Trevor May Baseball / More Mayday commentary Shorts:** plan $0.10–$0.25 RPM; treat $0.25–$0.50 as achievable when the Short is talking-head analysis with older-skewing pull.
- **Podcast clip Shorts (entertainment-leaning, younger crossover):** plan $0.05–$0.10.
- **Rule:** 1M engaged Shorts views ≈ **$100–$250** for Mayday. That is the entire direct-revenue upside. Every ROI conversation starts there.

### Seasonality & trend
- RPMs have climbed every year since Shorts revenue sharing launched (Feb 2023, from a ~$0.01–$0.02 baseline) — driven by advertiser demand growth, not YouTube changing the split.
- Q4 (Oct–Dec) RPM can run 30–60% above the annual average; January is the trough. Baseball adds its own curve: playoff October = high demand + high sports ad spend (peak month); offseason January–February is a double trough. **Schedule Shorts-heavy pushes for March (season preview) through October; don't judge RPM experiments run in January.**

## 3. Shorts in sponsor packages — pricing playbook

This is where Shorts actually make money for Mayday. 2026 market data (InfluencerFee, InfluenceFlow, Creators Agency, Descript):

### Rate anchors
- **Dedicated Short = 20–40% of the same creator's long-form integration rate.** (Some sources say Shorts pay 40–60% *less*; same range from the other direction.)
- **By channel size (dedicated Short, market-wide):**

| Tier | Subs | Dedicated Short | Long-form equivalent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro | 10K–50K | $100–$400 | $300–$1,200 |
| Mid | 50K–100K | $400–$900 | $1,200–$2,500 |
| Macro | 100K–500K | $900–$4,000 | $2,500–$10,000 |
| Mega | 500K–2M | $4,000–$15,000 | $10,000–$40,000 |

- **Athlete premium:** named pro-athlete creators price above these grid rates — micro/mid athlete-influencers run $1,500–$10,000/post and the athlete-creator lane carries a trust premium (OpenSponsorship/InfluencerFee 2026). Trevor's MLB pedigree justifies pricing at the top of his view tier, not his sub tier.
- **CPV benchmarks for Shorts deals:** mainstream $0.003–$0.01 per view; premium niches $0.03–$0.08. Sports sits mainstream-plus — quote **$0.01–$0.02 per *median* Short view** as a sanity check against the flat rate.
- **Price on median recent views, not subscribers.** Pull the median engaged views of the last 10 Shorts; outlier virals excluded. Sponsors' agencies do this math anyway.

### Integration formats (descending rate)
1. **Dedicated Short** — whole Short about the brand (full rate). Converts worst organically; charge accordingly.
2. **Verbal integration** — 10–20s mention inside a real Short (40–60% of dedicated rate). Best value for both sides in commentary content.
3. **Overlay/text or pinned-comment link** — lower rate, good bundle sweetener.
4. **End-card CTA** — weakest; throw in free within bundles rather than pricing standalone.

### Bundle structures that work
- **Anchor bundle:** 1 long-form integration + 3–5 supporting Shorts = **130–160% of the long-form rate alone.** This is the default Mayday sponsor package: the long-form carries conversion, Shorts carry frequency/reach, and the bundle premium is nearly pure margin because Shorts are cut from footage that already exists.
- **Series deal:** 4–8 Shorts/month with 20–30% volume discount — right shape for a recurring baseball-brand partner (training equipment, apparel) and for Neptune's own future sponsors.
- **Whitelisting/usage rights:** if the brand wants to run the Short as paid media (Partnership Ads / their own channels), charge +30–100% of the content fee for 90-day usage; don't include usage by default.
- **Performance kickers:** Shorts deals increasingly use per-click/per-view components. Accept a hybrid (floor fee + kicker) — never pure performance, since Shorts distribution variance is enormous.
- **Reporting deliverable:** commit to engaged views, average % viewed, and click/promo-code data. Shorts sponsorship opportunities grew ~180% YoY per YouTube 2025 creator data — brands are buying, but their measurement literacy is low; clean reporting wins renewals.

### Disclosure mechanics (checklist)
- Toggle **"Paid promotion"** on every sponsored Short (Details → checkbox) — required, and it does *not* meaningfully suppress reach.
- Verbal or on-screen "#ad / paid partnership" within the first 3 seconds for FTC safety — an end-of-Short disclosure fails the "unmissable" standard on a swipe format.
- If the sponsor is a betting brand, run the Section 5 checklist before contracting deliverables.

## 4. League footage: MLB Content ID reality

The core tension: baseball Shorts want game footage; MLB owns it and runs Content ID against full broadcasts.

### How it hits you
- **Content ID is automated pattern-matching — it does not evaluate fair use.** An exact audio/video match auto-files a claim; ~98% of YouTube copyright actions are Content ID, not takedowns.
- Rights-holder options on a match: **block**, **monetize (revenue diverted to MLB)**, or **track**. MLB has historically defaulted to monetize-claiming even clearly transformative commentary (documented pattern: Techdirt 2019 on fair-use breakdown videos, including Jomboy-style content).
- A **claim ≠ strike.** Claims cost you the money on that video only; the channel is unharmed and the Short still distributes. A **DMCA takedown/strike** is the dangerous one (3 strikes = channel termination) — MLB used takedowns aggressively circa 2010 but the modern posture is claim-and-monetize.
- **On a Short specifically:** a music/footage claim removes its engaged views from your Creator Pool allocation. Reach survives; revenue doesn't. For funnel-job Shorts this is an acceptable cost; for sponsor-deliverable Shorts it's not (a blocked Short = missed deliverable — contract for redelivery, not refunds).

### The 2025–26 thaw — and its limits
- MLB took a **minority equity stake in Jomboy Media (2025, via its $1B+ BELP fund)** — its first direct investment in a creator company. MLB EVP Kenny Gersh: "We are focused on helping amplify content creators' voices," with intent to partner with more baseball creators. Jomboy got MLB event access, IP access, and sponsorship connections.
- **Read for Trevor:** the league now *wants* a creator ecosystem, and a former MLB pitcher is exactly who they'd greenlight. The endgame is a **direct MLB content/IP relationship** (media credentials, licensed footage access) rather than fair-use roulette. Ashley should keep "pursue formal MLB creator relationship" on the BD radar — it converts the biggest content risk into a moat.
- **But:** Content ID doesn't know about the vibe shift. Until footage is licensed, every broadcast-matched Short can still be auto-claimed.

### Practical footage playbook (unlicensed)
1. **Commentary-dominant structure:** Trevor's face/voice is the video; footage is the exhibit. Aim for footage ≤ ~30–40% of runtime and individual broadcast clips **≤ 5–7 seconds** — short clips are both more fair-use-defensible and less likely to trigger a match.
2. **Transform the frame:** picture-in-picture, telestration (Mayday already has the Telestrator tool), zoom/crop, speed changes, and graphic overlays reduce match probability and strengthen transformation. Never lift broadcast **audio** — mute it and talk over; audio fingerprinting is the easiest match.
3. **Prefer non-broadcast sources:** Trevor's own playing-days footage he has rights to, self-shot bullpen/facility video, MLB Film Room embeds for reference (not ripping), public-domain/CC alternatives, or licensed stock (Getty/Storyblocks ~$30/mo covers generic b-roll).
4. **Dispute selectively:** dispute claims only on clearly transformative analysis where the money matters (long-form). On Shorts, disputing a $2 claim isn't worth the strike-escalation risk if MLB rejects and escalates. Credit lines and disclaimers provide **zero** protection — don't rely on them.
5. **Never build a sponsor deliverable on unlicensed broadcast footage.** If the Short can be blocked, it can't be sold.
6. **Player NIL vs. league footage are different rights.** Trevor can always use his own name/image/story; what he can't freely use is the *broadcast recording*. Recreations, whiteboard/telestrated stills, and first-person storytelling ("what I was thinking 3-2 to Judge") deliver the same content value with zero claim surface — this is the structurally safest baseball-Short format and happens to be the one only an ex-player can make.

## 5. Brand safety: gambling, supplements, and the sports-adjacent minefield

### YouTube gambling policy (effective March 19, 2025; expanded Nov 2025 — date-stamped, verify before big bets)
- Creators **may not show logos of, verbally mention, or link to gambling services not certified by Google** — URLs, embedded links, logos, spoken references, and visual displays all count. Certification is country-by-country and requires the operator to be licensed.
- Content **promising guaranteed returns** from gambling can be removed even when the operator is Google-certified.
- Online-gambling content can be **age-restricted** (18+, signed-in only). An age-restricted Short is effectively dead — it's excluded from most feed distribution and from the ad pool. The Nov 2025 update extended coverage to digital-goods gambling (skins/social casinos).
- Depictions of **in-person betting and legacy sportsbook coverage** are treated more leniently than directing viewers to online gambling.

**Tactical rules for Mayday:**
1. DraftKings/FanDuel-class sponsors are Google-certified in legal US states — the *sponsor* is fine; the *execution* can still trip the policy. Keep integrations brand-level ("presented by"), avoid odds-reading, "lock of the day," or any guaranteed-win framing, and never link to promos in Shorts descriptions/pinned comments for non-certified operators.
2. **Quarantine betting content from Neptune-adjacent content.** Neptune serves youth athletes and parents; a feed that mixes betting integrations with youth-training content is a brand-safety self-own regardless of platform policy. Different channels/handles for betting-sponsored content if it ever becomes material.
3. Watch for **"limited ads" (yellow icon)** on anything betting-adjacent — on Shorts this shows up as pool-ineligible views. Spot-check monetization status on the first Short of any new sponsored series before cutting the rest.
4. Supplements/CBD (common athlete offers): supplements are fine if claims stay compliant (no medical claims); CBD ad certification is state-limited — treat like gambling: certified-partner-only, soft-mention execution.

### League-relationship considerations
- Retired players aren't bound by MLB personnel rules, but a future formal MLB/team/RSN relationship (broadcasting, Jomboy-style partnership, Neptune pro-partnerships) will carry its own sponsor-category restrictions (competing sportsbooks, alcohol, crypto). **Keep sponsor contracts short (≤12 months) and include a league-conflict out-clause** so a media opportunity never gets blocked by a legacy Shorts deal.

## 6. When Shorts effort is ROI-positive — the decision framework

Direct revenue math for Mayday-scale channels (as of 2026): 1M engaged views ≈ $100–$250. A part-time editor cutting Shorts costs more than the ad revenue those Shorts will ever generate. So a Short must justify itself on one of four jobs (strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/02-short-form-strategy.md); this section is the accounting.

### ROI-positive cases (make the Short)
1. **Sponsor deliverable or bundle filler.** A Short inside a 140% bundle at, say, a $4,000 long-form rate carries ~$300–$500 of incremental price for ~30 min of editing. Highest-ROI Short that exists.
2. **Long-form bridge from existing footage.** Since the Shorts→long-form "bridge" update, Shorts viewers get recommended the same channel's long-form. Marginal cost ≈ editor time on footage already shot. Threshold test: the clip must contain a complete thought + its own hook in <30 min of edit work, else skip.
3. **Neptune lead-gen Shorts.** A facility Short is direct-response: judge on inquiries/DMs per 1,000 *local* views, not reach. 3,000 views from Puget Sound-area parents beats 300K national teens. These are ROI-positive at tiny view counts because a single membership/lesson package is worth more than 5M Shorts pool views.
4. **Sponsorship-reach inflation.** Consistent Shorts output raises total monthly impressions, which raises the media-kit numbers the *long-form* rates are negotiated against. This is real but slow — count it as supporting rationale, never the sole justification.

### ROI-negative cases (skip it)
- Shorts produced *de novo* (shot specifically as Shorts, not cut from existing production) with no sponsor attached and no Neptune angle — production cost can't be recovered by pool revenue or plausible funnel value.
- Clip-dump volume plays ("post 3 raw highlights daily") — pool-ineligibility risk + zero funnel value + MLB claim exposure. Worst possible Shorts strategy for this operation.
- Chasing a viral general-audience format that pulls non-baseball, non-US viewers — inflates vanity views, dilutes channel-level RPM and audience quality signals.

### The quarterly Shorts P&L check (run this)
1. Pull 90-day Shorts data: engaged views, Shorts ad revenue, subs gained from Shorts, long-form views with Shorts as prior touchpoint (Advanced Mode → traffic/new-viewer reports).
2. Cost side: editor hours × rate attributable to Shorts.
3. Value side: Shorts ad revenue + (Shorts-line items in sponsor deals) + (subs from Shorts × channel's revenue-per-sub proxy = last-12-mo total channel revenue ÷ avg subs) + Neptune inquiries attributed.
4. If value < cost two quarters running, cut Shorts volume to sponsor-deliverables-only — don't cut to zero (cadence collapse damages the whole channel's momentum; see Carl doc on burst-then-gap).

## 7. Common mistakes

- **Judging Shorts by ad revenue and concluding "Shorts don't work."** They never pay directly; the failure is expecting them to. Equal-and-opposite mistake: making Shorts with no assigned job because "reach is good."
- **Using post-March-2025 raw view counts in revenue math or sponsor decks.** Any-play views run 20–30%+ above engaged views; sponsors' agencies will catch it and the credibility cost exceeds the vanity gain.
- **Trending audio on commentary Shorts.** One licensed track halves the Short's pool contribution before the 45% cut. A talking channel should have ~0% licensed-music usage.
- **Broadcast audio left in the clip.** Audio fingerprinting is the fastest Content ID match. Mute it, talk over it.
- **Selling a sponsor a Short built on claimable footage.** A block or claim mid-campaign is a redelivery scramble and a relationship dent.
- **Pricing Shorts by subscriber count.** Shorts reach is algorithmic; price off median engaged views of the last 10 Shorts.
- **Bundling Shorts into long-form deals for free.** Every Short in a package needs a line-item price, even when discounted — free Shorts today set the renewal anchor at $0.
- **Reading odds / "guaranteed value" in betting-sponsored content.** Post-March-2025 this risks removal or age-restriction, which kills the Short and irritates the sponsor. Brand-level mentions only.
- **Disputing every MLB claim on principle.** Claims on $2 Shorts aren't the hill; escalated disputes can convert claims into strikes. Save disputes for high-revenue long-form with strong transformation.
- **Running Shorts experiments in January and extrapolating.** Annual RPM trough + baseball offseason = worst-case data.

## 8. Questions Ashley should ask

**Diagnostics (data pulls):**
- What is the median engaged-view count of the last 10 Shorts on each channel — and what's the gap vs. raw views?
- What's the current Shorts RPM per channel (Analytics → Revenue, Shorts filter)? Is More Mayday commentary landing in the $0.10–$0.25 band the demo predicts, or lower (audience-quality signal)?
- What % of published Shorts currently carry a Content ID claim, and who is the claimant? What's the total revenue being diverted?
- What % of Shorts views come from the US/Canada? (Geo dilution check.)
- How many subs and long-form views did Shorts feed last quarter (new-viewer + traffic-source reports)?

**Sponsor packaging:**
- Do current/prospective sponsor deals itemize Shorts? At what implied rate vs. the 20–40%-of-long-form anchor?
- Is there a recurring baseball-brand partner suited to a 4–8-Shorts/month series deal?
- Are usage rights (brand re-running Shorts as paid media) being charged for, or given away?

**League & risk:**
- Has Mayday explored a formal MLB creator relationship post-Jomboy (footage license, event access, credentials)? Who at MLB is the entry point — the Gersh/creator-partnerships track?
- Is any planned content betting-adjacent, and does the execution pass the March-2025 gambling checklist (no non-certified mentions/links, no guaranteed-return framing)?
- Are Neptune-facing Shorts fully quarantined from betting-sponsored content?

**ROI hygiene:**
- What's the actual editor-hours cost per Short right now, and when was the last quarterly Shorts P&L check run?
- Which of the four ROI-positive jobs is each planned Short assigned to — and what's its success metric?
