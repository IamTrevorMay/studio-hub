# The Master Posting-Time & Cadence Matrix

Tactical reference for WHEN to post and HOW OFTEN, per platform, for a US-based sports-entertainment audience run from Pacific Time. Benchmarks below aggregate the five major 2025–2026 studies (Buffer 52M posts, Sprout Social ~2B engagements, Hootsuite 1M posts, SocialPilot ~7M posts, Metricool multi-million-post per-platform studies) plus the one sports-vertical timing study that exists (Comscore/Shareablee). All platform mechanics claims are date-stamped; treat anything older than ~12 months as suspect.

## TL;DR

- **Timing is a multiplier, not a maker.** It affects the first-hours engagement-velocity signal every algorithm uses to decide wider distribution. It will never rescue weak content, and below ~1K followers/subs on a platform it barely moves the needle — above ~10K, timing optimization is worth real percentage points (SocialPilot measured 20–40% first-24h view lift on 10K+ YouTube channels).
- **Anchor the clock to Eastern Time, not Pacific.** ~48% of the US audience is in ET and ~29% in CT; only ~14% is in PT. "Noon" for Trevor's audience means 9 AM PT. Every scheduled time in Mayday's calendar should be chosen in ET and back-converted.
- **Consensus windows (audience-local time, as of 2026):** IG feed/Reels — Tue–Thu, 9 AM–12 PM or 6–9 PM (sports-vertical data says weight the evening slot); TikTok — weekday 4–8 PM + weekend mornings, Saturday best day; YouTube long-form — Sunday 8–10 AM or weekday 2–4 PM (2–3h before evening peak); YouTube Shorts — Thu–Sat, midday 12–2 PM or 6–8 PM; Facebook — Tue–Thu 9 AM–2 PM. Long-form and Shorts have near-OPPOSITE optimal windows — never put them on the same schedule.
- **Sports content skews evening, harder than the all-industry averages show.** The only vertical-specific study (Comscore/Shareablee, US Sports & Recreation IG data): 8–10 PM was the best window every day of the week, and 8 PM posts earned ~5x the engagement of 8 AM posts. Evening TV game windows drag sports-fan attention (and second screens) into the night.
- **Your own analytics beat every study within ~6 weeks.** YouTube Studio "When your viewers are on YouTube" heatmap, IG Insights "Most active times," TikTok Studio Followers tab, and Metricool's per-account best-times heatmap (already in Mayday's stack) each give account-specific data. Run the 4-week derivation protocol below, then re-check quarterly.
- **The "before-peak" rule:** publish 2–3 hours BEFORE the audience-activity peak, not at it — the platform needs lead time to index, test, and build early signals so the content is already circulating when the peak hits. At peak = too late.
- **Sports-calendar overlay:** post AROUND live-game windows, not into them (exception: real-time reaction content during games — 45–80% of sports fans second-screen). MLB weekday slates eat 4–7 PM PT / 7–10 PM ET; publish analysis mornings-after and previews late-morning ET.
- **Cadence floors that matter more than exact times:** TikTok 2–5/wk (up to +17% views/post vs 1/wk; 11+/wk = +34% and ~4x the viral ceiling — Buffer, 11.4M posts), IG 3–5 feed posts/wk + ~2 Stories/day, YouTube 1 long-form/wk/channel + 2–3 Shorts/wk, FB 3–5/wk (mostly crossposts). Never raise cadence past the point quality drops — 2026 algorithms reward satisfaction, not existence.

## 1. How much does timing actually matter? (Read this before obsessing)

### The mechanism: early engagement velocity

Every major feed algorithm runs a test-and-expand loop: show the post to a seed audience, measure response rate in the first minutes-to-hours, expand or bury accordingly. Posting when your audience is online maximizes the seed audience's response rate. Named signals as of 2025–2026:

- **Instagram** (Mosseri, Jan 2025): the three heaviest ranking signals are **watch time, sends-per-reach (DM shares), and likes-per-reach**. Feed still weights recency — posts less than a few hours old get priority; Stories are heavily recency-ranked. First 30–60 minutes of engagement determines follower-graph push.
- **TikTok**: highest recency sensitivity of any platform. A video is actively tested on the FYP for roughly its first 48 hours (Hootsuite, 2025); early completion/share rate in the first hours drives expansion. Timing matters MOST here.
- **YouTube long-form**: LOWEST timing sensitivity. Browse/suggested distribution runs on satisfaction and CTR×AVD over days-to-weeks — but videos gaining traction in the first 2–4 hours get homepage push, so timing matters for the launch spike while a good video recovers from a bad slot. (Strategy level on CTR×AVD diagnostics: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/12-analytics-experimentation.md)
- **YouTube Shorts**: behaves like TikTok — feed-tested, recency-sensitive, evening/weekend scroll-driven.
- **Facebook**: moderate; the algorithm leans on early reactions/shares within the follower graph before wider distribution.

### Timing sensitivity ranking (as of 2026)

| Rank | Platform | Sensitivity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | TikTok | High | ~48h FYP test window; recency is a ranking input |
| 2 | IG Stories | High | Near-chronological surface |
| 3 | YouTube Shorts | High | Scroll-feed tested like TikTok |
| 4 | IG feed/Reels | Medium-high | Recency-weighted feed + first-hour signal |
| 5 | X | Medium-high | Fast decay; ~1–4h post half-life |
| 6 | Facebook | Medium | Slower feed decay |
| 7 | YouTube long-form | Low-medium | Evergreen surfaces dominate after week 1 |
| 8 | Podcast / Substack | Low | Subscription delivery; consistency > clock time |

### Size threshold

SocialPilot (301K videos, 27K channels, 2026): under ~1,000 subscribers, timing impact is negligible — the seed audience is too small to generate a measurable velocity signal. Above ~10,000, timing shifts produced 20–40% first-24-hour view improvements. One documented case: a channel moving uploads from noon to 4:30 PM gained +29% impressions with zero content changes. **Practical rule: More Mayday and Trevor May Baseball are both above threshold — timing discipline pays there. A brand-new Neptune Performance account should spend the effort on content, not clock-watching, until ~1K followers.**

Notes on reading the matrix:

- **Long-form vs Shorts are near-opposites** (mornings vs evenings; Sunday vs Friday). SocialPilot calls this out explicitly: "publishing both formats on the same schedule is likely costing you performance on one of them." This is the single most actionable row-level insight in the matrix.
- **The 2026 evening shift:** Buffer's 2026 data shows peak windows moving later in the day vs 2025 across IG/LinkedIn/TikTok — evening (6–11 PM) engagement now beats working hours on entertainment-leaning platforms.
- **The sports-vertical override:** the only niche-specific timing study available (Comscore/Shareablee, US Sports & Recreation, total IG actions — data from March 2022, so directionally trust it, don't quote it as current) found 8–10 PM local was the best window EVERY day, 8 PM ≈ 5x the engagement of 8 AM, best days Tue/Wed/Thu. Sports fans' evenings are organized around televised games, and their social engagement follows. For Mayday's IG, this means when the generic matrix offers "9 AM–12 PM OR 6–9 PM," take the evening slot as the default hypothesis and let the §3 test settle it.
- **Where studies flatly disagree (e.g., TikTok Saturday-best vs weekends-worst):** Buffer/Hootsuite/Metricool datasets skew creator/entertainment (weekends and evenings win); Sprout's skews brand/B2B customers (weekday office hours win). For Mayday — a creator/entertainment operation — weight Buffer, Hootsuite, and Metricool over Sprout.
- **Sunday paradox:** Sprout says Sunday is the worst day overall across platforms, yet Sunday morning is the single best long-form YouTube slot (Buffer: Sun 10 AM). Both are true: Sunday is bad for feed-scroll platforms, great for lean-back long-form viewing. Sports adds NFL/MLB Sunday viewing on top (see §4).

## 3. Deriving YOUR times (beats every benchmark within ~6 weeks)

Every study above says the same thing in its fine print: published benchmarks are a starting grid, not an answer. Account-specific data wins. Here is where it lives per platform and how to convert it into a schedule.

### Per-platform: where the data lives (as of 2026)

**YouTube (both channels):**
1. YouTube Studio → Analytics → **Audience** tab → scroll to **"When your viewers are on YouTube"** heatmap (shown in YOUR local time = PT — no conversion needed).
2. Darkest purple = most audience online. Find the darkest contiguous block per day.
3. **Count back 2–3 hours** from the darkest block start → that's the upload time. Uploading AT peak wastes the indexing/testing lead time.
4. Do this separately per channel — More Mayday and Trevor May Baseball audiences will differ (baseball-training viewers skew younger + evening/weekend practice hours).
5. Caveat: the heatmap shows when viewers are on YouTube at all, not when they watch YOU — cross-check in Advanced Mode by grouping videos by publish hour and comparing first-24h views.

**Instagram:** Professional dashboard → Insights → Total followers → scroll to **"Most active times"** (toggle hours/days). Mosseri has confirmed active-hours posting matters most for Feed. Cross-check with Metricool's IG heatmap.

**TikTok:** TikTok Studio → Analytics → **Followers** tab → **"Most active times"** graph. Requires Business/Creator account, 100+ followers. Note it has reported in **UTC on some views (as of 2025)** — verify which timezone the graph is using before trusting it.

**Facebook:** Meta Business Suite → Insights → Audience → active times. The FB audience for an ex-MLB-fan demo skews older and earlier in the day than IG/TikTok — expect the heatmap to confirm morning/lunch windows.

**Metricool (already in Mayday's stack):** Planner view → **best-times heatmap overlay** (darker = better), toggle exact percentages. Per-account, computed from Mayday's own follower activity, covers all connected platforms in one screen, free tier. **Default tool for this job — check it before any of the native dashboards.**

**X:** no useful native active-times analytics as of 2026 — derive manually from top-post publish times, or accept the benchmark (8–11 AM + 12–6 PM ET).

### The 4-week derivation protocol (per platform)

1. **Week 0 — baseline:** pull the platform's active-times heatmap + Metricool heatmap. Pick the top TWO candidate windows: (a) the heatmap peak minus 2–3h, (b) the matrix consensus window from §2. If they agree, you're done — schedule there and skip to step 4.
2. **Weeks 1–2 — A slot:** publish everything for that platform in window (a). Log first-24h views/reach + engagement-per-reach per post in the decision log.
3. **Weeks 3–4 — B slot:** same content mix in window (b). Same logging.
4. **Compare medians, not means** (one outlier post wrecks a mean). Need ~6–8 posts per window minimum before concluding anything (SocialPilot's threshold: 6–8 uploads).
5. **Adopt the winner, re-audit quarterly** — audience composition drifts, and platform-wide behavior shifted measurably between 2025 and 2026 (evening migration). Also re-run after any follower spike (viral post, playoff run, facility launch press).

Confounders to control: keep content type constant within the test (don't compare a Reel at 9 AM to a carousel at 6 PM), avoid holiday weeks, and note any live-sports calendar collisions (see §4).

## 4. Timezone strategy for a US/PT-based sports-entertainment audience

### The population math

US audience distribution by timezone: **ET ≈ 48%, CT ≈ 29%, MT ≈ 6%, PT ≈ 14%** (remainder AK/HI). A PT-based operator's intuition ("post at lunch" = 12 PM PT = 3 PM ET) systematically posts 3 hours late for ~77% of the audience. **Every calendar decision anchors to ET; PT is the operator's clock, not the audience's.** Verify the actual split per account: YouTube Studio → Audience → top geographies; IG Insights → follower locations. If an audience is genuinely PT-heavy, flip the anchor for that account.

### Two distinct audiences, two anchors

- **National entertainment audience** (More Mayday, podcast clips, IG @trevmay65, TikTok/X): anchor ET, per the §2b cheat-sheet.
- **Local facility audience** (Neptune Performance, once live): anchor PT literally — local parents check phones 7–8 AM PT (school run), 12 PM PT, and 8–9 PM PT (after kids' practice). Local-intent content ignores the national matrix entirely. The two audiences cannot share a calendar.

### The live-sports overlay (where Mayday differs from generic advice)

Sports fans' attention is scheduled by the league calendar, not by generic engagement curves. As of 2025–2026, 45–80% of sports fans second-screen during live games — they're ON social during games, but attention is on the game, not on discovering new content. Rules:

1. **Don't drop long-form or effortful posts into live-game windows.** MLB weekday slates: ~4–7 PM PT (7 PM ET East-coast starts) and 6:40–10 PM PT (West games). A 5 PM PT TikTok on a full-slate night competes with the games themselves.
2. **Exception — real-time reaction content wins DURING games.** X posts, IG Stories, and fast TikToks reacting to a live moment (pitching-mechanics take on a live start, playoff-moment reaction) ride the second-screen wave. This is Trevor's structural edge: credentialed instant reaction. Speed requirement: inside ~30–60 min of the moment or the wave is gone.
3. **Morning-after window (9 AM ET / 6 AM PT):** analysis/breakdown of last night's games hits the commute + coffee sports-talk habit. Strongest recurring slot for baseball-analysis content in-season.
4. **The 8 PM ET sports-prime slot (5 PM PT):** per the Comscore/Shareablee finding, sports-fan IG engagement peaks 8–10 PM — but split it by night: on light-slate or off nights it's the best slot of the day; on marquee-game nights it belongs to reactive content only.
5. **Sunday flip:** in NFL season (Sep–Jan), Sunday 10 AM–5 PM PT is a dead zone for non-football content; Sunday morning before 10 AM PT still works (and remains YouTube long-form's best slot). During MLB-only months, Sundays are safer all day.
6. **Season calendar beats — plan cadence surges around:** Spring Training report dates (mid-Feb), Opening Day (late Mar), trade deadline (Jul 31), September races, playoffs/World Series (Oct), free agency + Winter Meetings (Nov–Dec). Baseball-content demand roughly doubles at these peaks and craters in January; shift publishing volume with it rather than holding flat cadence year-round.
7. **Trevor's own appearance schedule is a timing input:** any broadcast/podcast guest spot creates a 24–48h attention spike — have a scheduled post land within hours of the appearance airing.

## 5. Cadence: how often, per platform (2025–2026 data)

Frequency benchmarks with the hard numbers behind them:

| Platform | Floor | Sweet spot | Ceiling worth chasing | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TikTok | 1/wk | **2–5/wk** (+17% views/post vs 1/wk) | 11+/wk (+34% views/post; 90th-percentile post views 3,722 → 14,401 from 1/wk → 11+/wk) | Buffer, 11.4M posts, 150K accounts, fixed-effects regression, 2025 |
| IG feed + Reels | 2/wk | **3–5/wk** (Instagram's own recommendation) | ~7/wk before quality risk | Meta guidance + Dash Social 2026 |
| IG Stories | 3/day-equiv./wk | **~2/day** | 4+/day for launch weeks | Meta guidance 2025 (ties to the existing "Do this more" IG-story goal widget) |
| YouTube long-form | 2/mo per channel | **1/wk per channel** | 2/wk only with dedicated editor | Industry consensus 2025–26; retention > volume |
| YouTube Shorts | 1/wk | **2–3/wk** | 1/day | SocialPilot/Buffer 2026 |
| Facebook | 2/wk | **3–5/wk** (crossposts fine) | 1/day | SocialPilot 2026 |
| X | 3/wk | **1–2/day** (incl. replies/QTs) | 3–5/day in-season | Fast decay makes volume cheap here |
| Substack | 2/mo | **1/wk, fixed day** | 2/wk | Consistency of send day > count |
| Podcast | 2/mo | **1/wk, fixed drop day+time** | — | Subscription habit formation |

Two governing laws over that whole table (both 2026-consensus):

- **The quality-floor law:** frequency lifts only hold while per-post quality holds. Algorithms now score satisfaction (watch-through, shares, "not interested" clicks) — 10 mediocre posts/week actively teach the algorithm your content is skippable. The right cadence = the highest rate sustainable WITHOUT quality dropping, for 12 months, with the current team.
- **The ceiling-not-floor law:** Buffer's TikTok data shows median views barely move with frequency (~459–506 across all tiers) — what frequency buys is more lottery tickets: the 90th-percentile ceiling roughly quadruples from 1/wk to 11+/wk. Cadence is a viral-sampling strategy, not a per-post booster.

Cadence at these numbers is only achievable via a one-to-many repurposing pipeline — one podcast/long-form recording feeding the short-form slots. (Strategy level: see ../../Carl/organic-marketing/04-content-strategy-pillars-repurposing.md)

## 7. Common mistakes

1. **Scheduling in PT because you live in PT.** The single most common error for a West Coast operator; ~77% of a national audience is ET/CT. Anchor ET.
2. **Posting AT the audience peak instead of 2–3h before it.** The velocity test needs runway; at-peak = distribution starts as attention is already leaving.
3. **Running long-form and Shorts on one schedule.** Their optimal windows are near-opposites (Sun AM vs Fri PM). Costs measurable performance on whichever format got the wrong slot.
4. **Treating study benchmarks as answers instead of priors.** Buffer/Sprout/Hootsuite disagree with each other because their datasets differ; your account data outranks all of them after ~6–8 posts per window.
5. **Copying B2B-skewed data for entertainment content.** Sprout's weekday-office-hours findings come from a brand-heavy customer base; creator/entertainment engagement lives evenings + weekends (Buffer 2026 evening shift; Comscore sports 8–10 PM). Know which dataset a recommendation came from.
6. **Dropping planned content into live-game windows** — then concluding "TikTok is down" when the audience was watching the Dodgers.
7. **Double-posting after a missed slot,** splitting early engagement between two posts so both fail the velocity test.
8. **Chasing frequency past the quality floor.** 2026 algorithms punish skip-signals; the marginal 8th weekly TikTok that's filler damages the account, not just itself.
9. **Ignoring the size threshold** — micro-accounts (new Neptune handles) burning hours on timing optimization that can't register below ~1K followers.
10. **Set-and-forget schedules.** Platform-wide windows shifted materially 2025→2026 (later/evening migration); audience composition shifts with every growth spurt and every baseball-season phase. Quarterly re-audit or drift silently.
11. **Trusting a heatmap without checking its timezone.** TikTok analytics has reported UTC on some surfaces (as of 2025); a "5 PM peak" read as PT when it's UTC is a 7–8 hour error.
12. **Comparing timing-test results on means.** One 10x outlier post makes the wrong window look like a winner; medians only.
