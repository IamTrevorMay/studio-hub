# IG Posting Times, Cadence, Trial Reels & Grid Strategy

Tactical execution reference for @trevmay65 and any Mayday/Neptune IG accounts. Strategy-level context (algorithm mental model, format roles, sends-per-reach, DM automation) lives in `../../Carl/organic-marketing/03-instagram-organic.md` — this doc is the when/how-often/test-loop/profile-layer playbook.

## TL;DR

- **Default posting windows (as of 2026, Buffer 9.6M-post study):** Wednesday 12pm/6pm, Thursday 7–9am, Tuesday 3–7pm — all *local audience time*. Evenings (6–11pm) beat mornings on most days; Friday/Saturday are the weakest days. Sports/fitness audiences add a 5–7am window.
- **Frequency has a measured dose-response** (Buffer, 2.1M posts): 3–5 feed posts/week = 2.2× the follower growth of 1–2/week; 6–9/week = 3.7×; 10+/week = 5.5×. Zero-post weeks actively *shrink* the account (−0.08 SD vs own baseline). The biggest marginal win is getting from 1–2 to 3–5.
- **Trial reels are the platform's built-in A/B lab**: shown to non-followers only, insights at ~24h, optional auto-share to everyone if it performs within 72h, schedulable since April 2026. Hard limits: professional account, ~1,000+ followers, max ~5 trials/day, no near-duplicate spam.
- **Compare trials only to other trials** — Mosseri himself: trials "almost always get less reach" than normal reels because followers are excluded. Judge on avg watch time + follows per reach, not raw views.
- **The grid is 3:4 now** (rolled out 2025). Ship everything at 4:5 (1080×1350), keep text inside the center 3:4 zone, and use "Adjust Preview" to fix crops. Old square posts get top/bottom-cropped on the profile.
- **Pin 3 posts deliberately** — best proof-of-credibility content, a "who this account is for" post, and a best-performing entry-point reel. Practitioners report 15–25% lifts in profile-visit→follow conversion from a strategically pinned top row vs an unpinned grid.
- **One weekly template to start**: 4 reels (1 run as a trial first), 1–2 carousels, 1–2 Stories/day weekdays, timing per the windows above. Then let 8 weeks of Instagram Insights / Metricool data override the generic studies — audience-specific data always wins.

## 1. Posting times — what the large-N studies actually say (2026)

### The headline data (Buffer, 9.6M posts, 200K+ accounts, Jan 2024–Dec 2025, median engagement rate)

| Day | Best slots (local time) | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Wednesday | 12pm, 6pm, 8am | Best day overall |
| Thursday | 9am, 8am, 7am (morning anomaly) | Best |
| Tuesday | 7pm, 3pm, 5pm | Strong |
| Monday | 6–8pm | Moderate |
| Sunday | 8–10pm | Moderate |
| Friday | weak all day (10pm least-bad) | Worst |
| Saturday | weak (11am, 8–10pm least-bad) | Worst |

- Evenings **6–11pm dominate** every day except Thursday (morning). Overnight 1–5am is dead universally.
- Buffer normalized across timezones — treat all times as *the viewer's local time*. For Trevor's largely US audience, weight toward ET/CT (roughly 2/3 of US population); a 6pm ET post also catches 3pm PT afternoon scroll.
- Hootsuite (1M+ posts, 118 countries) broadly agrees: engagement peaks "early mornings and early evenings, especially early in the week"; Mon 3–9pm, Tue 5–8am + 3–7pm, Wed 5pm, Thu 4–5pm.
- A MeetEdgar meta-analysis of nine studies found **Wednesday named top day in 8 of 9** — the single most replicated finding in this literature.

### Sports/fitness niche windows (closest proxy to Trevor's audience)

- Fitness/wellness content peaks **5–7am** (workout-planning scroll), **12–2pm** (lunch), and **5–7pm** (post-work gym window); Monday–Wednesday are the strongest days; weekend engagement drops because the audience is out being active (SocialPilot 7M-post data + CreatorFlow aggregation).
- **Baseball-specific overlay Ashley should apply (reasoned, not study-backed):** in-season, live games own 7–10pm local — baseball fans are second-screening the game, which is *good* for reactive/commentary content posted mid-game and *bad* for evergreen content that needs full attention. Post evergreen/training content in the morning windows in-season; post game-reaction content within 30–60 min of the moment. Off-season, the standard evening windows apply cleanly.
- Coaches/educators windows (relevant for Trevor May Baseball training content): 6–9am, 12–2pm, 8–10pm.

### How much does timing actually matter?

Rank-order of leverage: **content quality ≫ frequency > timing**. Timing decides the size of the *initial test batch* audience and early-velocity signals; it cannot save a weak hook. Buffer's own caveat: these are aggregated medians — an account's real curve can differ substantially. Treat study times as the starting grid, then within ~8 weeks switch to account data (below). Never let scheduling-perfectionism delay a post by more than a day. (Strategy level — why early velocity matters at all: see `../../Carl/organic-marketing/03-instagram-organic.md`.)

### Finding the account's *own* best times — 30-minute quarterly workflow

1. **Instagram Insights** → Total followers → "Most active times" — note the top 2 hours per day.
2. **Metricool** (already wired into Mayday Studio) → Instagram → Best hours heatmap — cross-check against Insights; Metricool plots audience-online + historical engagement together.
3. Pull the account's **top 10 posts by reach in last 90 days**; log their publish day/hour. Look for clustering.
4. Pick **3 primary slots** (e.g., Tue 6pm, Wed 12pm, Thu 8am ET) and 2 backups. Hold them constant for 4 weeks.
5. A/B by slot: same content class (e.g., pitching-breakdown reels) alternated between two slots over 2–4 weeks; compare median non-follower reach. Only conclude from ≥4 posts per slot.
6. Re-run quarterly and at season boundaries (Opening Day, All-Star break, playoffs, off-season) — baseball audience rhythm shifts hard with the calendar.

## 2. Frequency — the dose-response data

### Buffer 2026 study (2.1M posts, 102K accounts; photos/reels/carousels, Stories excluded; fixed-effects vs each account's own baseline)

| Feed posts/week | Weekly follower growth | Multiple vs 1–2/wk | Reach per post vs 1–2/wk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **negative** (−0.08 SD below own baseline) | — | — |
| 1–2 | +0.12% | 1× | baseline |
| 3–5 | +0.26% | **2.2×** | +12% |
| 6–9 | +0.44% | 3.7× | +18% |
| 10+ | +0.66% | 5.5× | +24% |

Read it correctly:

- **Reach per post goes UP with volume, not down** — posting more does not cannibalize; each post also earns more (+12–24%). The "algorithm punishes posting too much" folk belief is falsified at feed-post frequencies.
- **Biggest marginal jump: 1–2 → 3–5.** Returns per added post shrink after 6–9. Buffer's 52M-post engagement study shows the reach-lift curve flattening after 6–9/week.
- **The no-post penalty is real**: a zero week doesn't hold ground, it loses it. Minimum viable cadence during travel/facility-buildout crunch weeks: 2 posts, even if they're repurposed podcast clips.
- Quality guardrail (Mosseri, 2025): more volume only helps if quality holds; 3–5 strong posts beat 7–10 rushed ones, and creator burnout reads through in the content.

### Format-level cadence norms (as of 2026)

- **Reels:** 3–5/week is the growth zone; accounts posting 4–7 reels/week see ~2.3× the reach of 1–2/week (industry aggregations). Below 2/week the account never builds testing volume.
- **Stories:** 1–2/day minimum for tray placement (Buffer); engagement drops off sharply after the ~5th frame in one session — spread 3–5 frames across the day rather than dumping 10 at once. Stories don't count toward the feed-post dose-response but drive the relationship signals that decide connected reach. (Strategy: see Carl doc §Stories.)
- **Carousels:** 1–3/week; highest save rates of any format — use for training breakdowns, "5 things" lists, photo dumps from starts/facility.
- Ceiling rule: never publish more than ~2 feed posts/day on a personal-brand account; beyond that, cannibalization of Story attention and follower fatigue outweigh gains.

## 3. Trial reels — the built-in testing loop

### Mechanics (as of mid-2026)

- **What it is:** a reel shared *only to non-followers* — it does not hit followers' Feed/Reels tab, your grid, or your Reels tab. Launched Dec 2024.
- **Eligibility:** professional (Creator/Business) account; ~1,000+ followers commonly reported as the floor; check Help Center per account.
- **How:** create reel normally → toggle **Trial** on before sharing. **Turn OFF "Also share to Facebook"** and any cross-post toggles or the trial leaks publicly and the test is ruined.
- **Insights:** ~24h after posting — views, likes, comments, shares, plus a comparison vs your previous trials. Found under Profile → Reels tab → "Drafts and trial reels."
- **Auto-share:** optional setting; Instagram promotes the trial to everyone if views in the **first 72h** clear its internal bar. Toggle-able anytime. Manual "Share with everyone" also available.
- **Scheduling:** trials can be scheduled in advance (added April 2026) — slot them into the peak windows from §1 like any other post.
- **Leakage caveat:** followers can still encounter a trial via DM shares, the audio page, or location pages. Trials are private-ish, not private.
- **Volume limits:** community-reported ceiling ~**5 trials/day**; exceeding it risks a 30-day feature ban. Instagram's duplicate detection kills near-identical re-uploads — only the first copy gets distribution. One existing regular reel may be re-run as a trial without duplicate penalty.
- **Platform-level results:** Instagram (June 2025): 40% of trial-reel users started posting more often; trial reels contributed to an **80% increase in reels reach among non-followers** platform-wide.

### How to read trial results (calibration)

- **Benchmark against other trials only.** Mosseri: "Because it's not going to your followers... it's going to get less reach, almost always." One practitioner baseline: regular reels 1.5–5K views vs trials ~180 views on the same account — that's normal, not failure.
- Primary metrics, in order: **avg watch time → shares → follows per reach → likes**. Raw views are noisy at trial scale.
- Kapwing's controlled study (same videos posted as trial + regular): trend-based content got **2.3× more views** as a trial (2,258 vs 949) but lower ER (0.7% vs 1.6%); product/promotional content did *worse* as a trial (802 views, zero engagement) than to followers. Their 1.4M-view trial converted only **130 followers** from 8,600 profile visits. Lessons: trials are for *discovery-genre* content, not promos; and virality without a converting profile is wasted (see §4).
- Sample discipline: minimum 3–5 trials per hypothesis before concluding. Change **one variable per test** (hook OR audio OR length — never two).

### The Mayday trial-reel testing loop (weekly)

1. **Pick one hypothesis** per week. Rotation that fits Trevor: hook phrasing ("Every pitcher does this wrong" vs "I threw 97 and still made this mistake"), face-first vs footage-first open, trending vs original audio, 15s vs 35s cut, MLB-story angle vs coaching angle on the same clip.
2. **Cut 2–3 variants** of one strong piece (podcast clip, bullpen breakdown, facility BTS). Keep caption, hashtags, location tag, and posting window identical across variants — only the test variable differs. Trial content ideally shows its hook in the first 3s.
3. **Schedule trials into peak windows** (§1) on consecutive days — never dump all variants the same hour.
4. **Read at 24–72h.** Winner = best avg watch time and shares vs your trial baseline.
5. **Ship the winner** — two schools: (a) "Share with everyone"/auto-share the trial itself (official flow, simplest), or (b) re-post the winning version as a fresh regular reel a few days later — practitioners (Modern Millie) argue converted trials can arrive "pre-aged" to followers and a clean re-post performs better. Test both once, keep whichever wins on this account; don't do both with identical files (duplicate detection).
6. **Log it.** Keep a running sheet (hypothesis → variants → watch time/shares → verdict). Ten weeks in, this is a proprietary map of what makes baseball audiences stop scrolling — feed conclusions back into the main-feed content, which is the entire point.
7. Secondary uses: safely test off-vertical content (Neptune Performance facility content, broader entertainment crossover) without confusing the existing follower base; re-run archive winners to new audiences; revise-and-retrial flops with a new hook before writing the idea off.

## 4. Grid & profile conversion — the 3:4 era

Reach means nothing if the profile doesn't convert the visit. Discovery flow: reel → profile tap → 2–3 second judgment on photo + bio + top row → follow or bounce. Kapwing's cautionary number again: 1.4M views → 8,600 profile visits → 130 follows (1.5% visit→follow). A tuned profile is the cheapest growth lever on the platform.

### Grid specs (as of 2026)

- Profile grid thumbnails are **3:4 vertical** (rolled out from Jan 2025), replacing 1:1 squares. Grid preview renders ~1015×1350.
- **Create everything at 4:5 — 1080×1350** for feed posts (max vertical feed space; nearly matches the 3:4 crop). Reels/Stories remain 9:16 (1080×1920); a 9:16 reel loses its top and bottom in the grid thumbnail.
- **Safe zone:** keep faces, text overlays, and hook text inside the center 3:4 region of any 4:5 or 9:16 asset so the grid crop doesn't behead it.
- **Fix bad crops:** post → ⋯ menu → **"Adjust Preview"** → drag to reposition within the 3:4 frame, or fit with a border. Audit legacy square posts near the top of the grid — they were auto-cropped top/bottom.
- Reel **cover images**: upload a designed 3:4-safe cover (readable 3–5-word title text) so the grid reads like a menu of episodes, not random freeze-frames.

### Pinned posts (top row = the storefront)

Up to 3 pins. Framework (Marketing Examined "discovery funnel" + practitioner consensus; reported 15–25% visit→follow lift vs unpinned):

1. **Credibility/intro post** — who Trevor is in one asset: 9-year MLB career, what this account gives you. Best format: a high-production reel or carousel with social proof baked in.
2. **"Who this is for" / best-of-value post** — the single most useful, saveable piece for the target follower (e.g., the best-performing pitching-mechanics breakdown). It answers "what do I get by following?"
3. **Best-performing entry-point reel** — the account's proven top converter; keeps working on every new visitor. Swap pins when something new outperforms; review monthly.

Rules: bio and pins must tell one coherent story (bio promise → pins prove it). Never pin promos as all three slots. When a big reel is spiking, temporarily pin it so profile visitors land on the thing that brought them.

### The rest of the conversion surface (checklist)

- **Bio line 1**: searchable identity keywords, not cleverness — "Ex-MLB pitcher" / "9 yrs in the big leagues" (name field is indexed for IG search + Google as of 2025 — strategy: see Carl doc §SEO/keywords).
- **Bio line 2–3**: one clear value statement (what a follower gets) + one CTA/link. Avoid the vague "creator/athlete/entrepreneur" stack.
- **Highlights**: 3–6 max, covers in a consistent style, ordered by what a new visitor needs: Start Here / Podcast / Training (Neptune) / Story. Highlights are proof assets, not an archive.
- **Top 9–12 grid tiles**: at a squint, do they look like one show? Mixed-purpose accounts (Trevor: MLB stories + coaching + podcast + facility) should enforce a visual system — consistent cover template per content pillar — so the grid communicates range without reading as chaos.

## 5. Weekly cross-format cadence templates

Anchored to the dose-response data (§2) and windows (§1). All times local-audience (weight ET).

### Template A — Baseline (sustainable floor, ~5 feed posts/wk)

| Day | Slot | Post |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | 6pm | Reel — MLB story / podcast clip (relatable, send-worthy) |
| Tue | 7pm | **Trial reel** — this week's test variant #1 |
| Wed | 12pm | Carousel — training breakdown or photo dump (save-bait) |
| Thu | 8am | Reel — coaching/mechanics (morning fitness window) |
| Fri | — | rest day (weakest day; Stories only) |
| Sat | 11am | Reel — lighter/entertainment crossover or trial variant #2 |
| Sun | 8pm | Reel or carousel — week's best repurposed moment |
| Daily | spread | 2–4 Story frames (BTS, polls, reshare of the day's post) |

### Template B — Growth push (6–9 posts/wk → 3.7× tier)

Baseline + a second daily-window post on Tue/Wed/Thu (one extra reel, one extra carousel, one extra trial). Cap at 2 feed posts/day. Run in 4–6 week sprints (launch windows: podcast season, Neptune opening, playoff runs), then drop back to baseline — the data rewards sustained consistency over heroic weeks followed by zero weeks.

### Seasonal overlay (baseball calendar)

- **In-season:** shift evergreen posts to morning windows; reserve evenings for reactive game-commentary content posted near the moment; expect weekend engagement dips.
- **Off-season (Nov–Feb):** standard evening windows fully apply; training-content demand rises (players in offseason programs) — weight Trial reels toward Neptune/coaching content here.
- **Tentpoles** (Opening Day, trade deadline, playoffs, HOF votes): pre-plan reactive slots; timeliness beats the optimal-hour chart on news days.

## 6. Common mistakes

- **Posting into dead zones by habit** — Friday night and Saturday are measurably the worst slots; overnight 1–5am is dead everywhere. Don't burn A-material there.
- **Zero-post weeks** — the no-post penalty means silence loses followers. Keep a 2-post emergency floor of repurposed clips for crunch weeks.
- **Judging trial reels against regular reels** — trials exclude followers and "almost always get less reach" (Mosseri). Panic-deleting a 200-view trial that had great watch time throws away the win.
- **Testing multiple variables per trial** — new hook + new audio + new length = unreadable result. One variable, 3–5 trials, then conclude.
- **Leaving "Also share to Facebook" on for a trial** — publicly posts the test and invalidates it.
- **Exceeding ~5 trials/day or re-uploading near-duplicates** — feature bans and duplicate suppression.
- **Running promos as trials** — Kapwing's data shows promotional content underperforms with cold audiences; trials are for discovery-genre content.
- **Ignoring the 3:4 crop** — text/faces in the top or bottom of 9:16 or square assets get chopped in the grid; legacy square posts near the top of the profile look broken until Adjust Preview is used.
- **No pinned posts, or pinning stale announcements** — the top row is the highest-traffic real estate on the profile; leaving it to chronology wastes every profile visit a viral reel generates.
- **Chasing generic study times over own Insights after month 2** — the studies are priors, not truth; 8 weeks of account data beats 9.6M strangers' posts.
- **Interpreting a platform-wide ER decline as personal failure** — baseline ER is structurally falling (~−24% YoY); benchmark vs own trailing 90 days (strategy: see Carl doc §benchmarks).
